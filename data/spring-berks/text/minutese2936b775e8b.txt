Continue to Provide Residents with Quality Police and Fire Services

Fire Department: Chief Hackman addressed the Board, stating that Fire Rescue Services
continues to follow the Township Board of Supervisors’ vision to provide high-quality, yet safe,
fire rescue services for the residents and guests of the Township. Providing high-quality services
relies on adequate staffing, training, equipment, apparatus, and public relations/community
outreach. Chief Hackman stated that the Department continually evaluates operations and
efficiency based on response times, total personnel per incident following NFPA 1710
Organization and Deployment of Fire Suppression Operations, Emergency Medical Operations,
and Special Operations to the public by Combination Departments, saved property, and ISO
(Insurance Services Office) rating. The Department currently has an ISO Rating of 3Y.

The Fire Department continues to provide public relations /community outreach through the
children’s book reading monthly at libraries, providing a Fire Prevention Program for the entire
second grade class of the Wilson School District on education of the use of 911 and evacuating
from a house filled with smoke, attending community events like National Night Out, Township
Playground programs, Touch-a-Truck, maintaining the Township’ Smoke and Carbon Monoxide
Detector Program, attending fire drills at the schools and numerous other requests totaling 1,892
hours in 2025.

Staffing: Fire Rescue is currently staffed with 20 full-time employees and 1 part-time employee,
including a Fire Chief, Deputy Fire Chief, Fire Marshal, Fire Inspector, 16 full-time
Firefighters/EMTs, and 1 part-time Fire Inspector. Two (2)employees entered the DROP on April
30", 2026. There are 26 Volunteers, broken down into four categories: Interior Qualified —6 ,
Exterior Qualified — 9, Fire Police — 4 & Support — 7.

Fire Marshal’s Office: The Fire Marshal’s Office continues to complete plan reviews and
inspections. In 2025, the OFM completed 836 Inspections and 35 Fire Investigations. To date, the
OFM has achieved 98% fire code compliance with all commercial occupancies in the Township.

Responses: 2025 ended with 2,548 responses, with 3,430 hours spent on incidents and 4,398
hours spent on training. 2026 to date responses: 982 / 60, less than 2025 to date. 2026 Personnel
per Incident: 4.8 , 2026 Dispatched to En route: 1.22 , 2026 Dispatched to On Scene: 7.31

Grants: The Department continues to apply for grants through OSFC (PEMA), FEMA, PA
American Water, and UGI. The Department is currently submitting three (3) grants to FEMA for
hoses, nozzles, AEDs, thermal imaging cameras, and air monitoring equipment.

Fire Rescue’s Vision

The Fire Department’s continued vision for Fire Rescue is to maintain high-quality services to the
residents and visitors of the Township, staffing, maintaining the current station, which is 21 years
old, maintaining the current apparatus, and replacement following NFPA 1901 Standard for
automotive fire apparatus, and NFPA 1910 Standard for the inspection, maintenance,
refurbishment, testing and retirement of in-service emergency vehicles and marine vessels.

Police Department
Chief Brock stated that the Board received a report that is a Year-to-Date Activity Report to

highlight comparisons with 2025. Chief Brock reviewed the report, highlighting offenses that
increased, noting an average 10% increase in calls from last year. Six (6) new officers have
started since 2025. There have been no sustained citizen complaints or out-of-policy uses in

force in 2025-26. The Department continues to maintain a strong relationship with the
community, including events such as High School Graduation, National Night Out, Coffee with a
Cop, Career Days, and Touch-a-Truck. Chief Brock stated that the Department is looking to
increase staffing by 4-5 officers in 2027-2028 to account for the increased population and call
volume in the northern end of the Township.

Maintain Quality Roads

Staff is currently working on a 5-year street plan. The 2026 Roadwork budget is $1,200,000.00,
with additional sewer funds used to improve the roads affected by pipe replacement projects. The
Township has spent over $8,000,000.00 on road improvements since 2021.

Harvard Boulevard Road Restoration

The Township received a Multi-Modal Grant for basic walkability in the amount of $250,000.00.
Proceeds will be used to install up-to-date handicap accessibility ramps and a portion of curbing
around the ramps. Property owners will be required to pay the cost of curb, sidewalk, and tree
removal. Residents will have the option to have the work done themselves or have the Township
complete and assess the property. Staff completed a skim coat in 2025 to prepare for road
restoration in 2028 and to allow residents to replace their curbs and sidewalks. The Township
plans to reapply for an LSA Grant in 2027 for road restoration in conjunction with bike lanes to
benefit transportation at the new elementary school. Staff completed an assessment of sidewalk
conditions and updated the overall cost estimate to reflect the impact on affected residents. A
Town Hall Meeting with residents is scheduled for August 17", 2026.

Howard Street Sidewalks
The Township plans to apply for a Multimodal Walkability Grant in 2027. Any planned
upgrades should include a street study to identify “one-way” streets.

Updates from Topics from the October 17th, 2025 Vision Meeting

Werner Farmhouse-Historical Status

The Parks and Recreation Department has copies of all deeds dating back to 1858. The farm does
not meet the criteria for centennial or bicentennial farms. Staff have not discovered anything that
is of notable "historical significance" that would qualify the farm for a “historical site”
designation. Estimates were received for demolition of the farmhouse and smokehouse, and an
estimate has been received for restoration of the smokehouse. The farmhouse was tested for lead
paint and asbestos and is awaiting test results.

Emergency Pre-emption Devices

Directive was to evaluate intersections without devices and upgrade those with devices.
Township staff and 3™ party consultants performed these tasks and identified 29 intersections.
Mr. Murray reviewed the Green Light-Go Grant, noting that backplates, LED lighting, and
pedestrian crossings were the main items included in the grant; however, 27-30% was permitted
for Pre-emption devices. Since the Township has so many intersections listed in the grant
application, they were included in the grant funds received, assisting our emergency services.
The total grant applied for was over $1,250,000,00 and the grant was an 80/20 split, with the
Township’s obligation at 20%.
