B. Parks and Recreation Department
Mr. Stuck questioned the status of the demolition of the farmhouse at Werner Farms.
Ms. Houck said the Department received test results for asbestosis and lead paint, and that
there is no asbestosis, but it is heavily contaminated with lead paint. Ms. Houck will contact
the contractor to determine how that affects the quote and said that if the Board decides to
proceed, it will be over the bid limit, and the demolition will need to be bid out.

Mr. Royer suggested starting the bidding process for removal of the farmhouse. Mr. Stuck
stated that he would like to start a conversation about what the Township plans to do at the
site and asked whether there are any thoughts on erecting a building that the Township could
rent for indoor events. Ms. Houck indicated that if the Board would like to hold events there,
parking would need to be addressed.

Ms. Houck confirmed for Mr. Royer that the site was dedicated as recreation land when the
development was built. Ms. Houck stated that the Parks and Recreation Board discussed
constructing a facility to allow the Department to maintain and store its environmental
supplies, and to utilize the site for environmental education. The site has a wetland and a
spring, and the Department was very interested in using that area for environmental
education. Ms. Houck said she will bring the quote received for restoring and removing the
smokehouse back to the Board.

Motion to direct staff to look into the cost of building a facility to hold indoor events and
programming at the Werner Farmhouse Location was made by Mr. Stuck and seconded by
Ms. Robertson. Upon roll call, all Supervisors present voted affirmatively. MOTION CARRIED

The Zoning Officer met with the applicant on-site and informed them that not only would a
permit be necessary, but also a Zoning Variance request due to the height of the structure
being 16’-10” (maximum permissible height; 15-feet). The applicant has applied to the
Zoning Hearing Board seeking relief from Section 327(C)(1)(f)(3)(i), the maximum
permissible height, 15 feet.

After further review, it appears that the applicant will need additional relief due to the
structure installed across the property line immediately adjacent to the northwest, along
Dwight Street. The adjoining parcel appears to be owned by the applicant’s father, who
passed away in 2013. Mr. Reichert indicated that ownership of the parcel must be resolved,
the structure must be removed or relocated outside the required setbacks, or an easement
must be required and an additional Zoning Variance relief for setback encroachment.

At their Public Meeting on Thursday, June 4", 2026, the Planning Commission voted to
recommend that the Zoning Hearing Board deny the application for zoning relief. It was
noted that there are multiple items that should be resolved beyond the height issue.

Mr. Becker expressed his concern about whether the property was properly subdivided.

Mr. Reichert reviewed the exhibits with the Board. After questions and review,

Motion to recommend to the Zoning Hearing Board denial of the application for a Variance
for Zoning relief for 30 Howard Street was made by Mr. Stuck and seconded by Mr. Wertz.
Upon roll call. All Supervisors present voted affirmatively. MOTION CARRIED

2) Giuliana’s Restaurant — 776 Fritztown Rd Revised Final Plans
Mr. Reichert stated that staff have been working with the applicant’s consultant on issues
with the final Land Development Plans, including the unpermitted, unapproved parking lot
expansion, which has raised stormwater concerns. Revised plans were received in April
2026, which included updated stormwater management improvements. Mr. Reichert
reviewed the plans, noting that the applicant will install an underground stormwater system
to capture runoff from the parking lot expansion, and that staff will work with the
Solicitor’s office on an agreement related to the stormwater feature. A cost estimate for
installing the stormwater feature was received, and Mr. Reichert indicated that the
Township would receive an escrow from the applicant for that.

Mr. Reichert stated that there was a review of sewage usage for this property. Mr. Reichert
reviewed the billing history and said that a sewage analysis was prepared and that the
monthly billing has been updated to reflect the changes.

3) 231 Wheatfield Rd — Minor Subdivision Revised Final Plans
Mr. Reichert reviewed the submission, noting that the applicant was granted Conditional
Use approval to subdivide their property at the Public Hearing held on January 27", 2025,
and received Conditional Final approval on February 10", 2025. When the Applicant
worked through the conditions of approval to record the plans, the property owners
discussed the matter with the Assessment Office, and it was determined that, to avoid tax
issues related to Clean and Green, the area of lot 2 would need to be reduced.

Mr. Reichert stated that the new lot configuration complies with both the minimum lot size
and lot width requirements of the Zoning District, and the structures continue to meet the
setback requirements to the property lines. The new lot configuration does not appear to
affect the Conditional Use approval, as a single-family dwelling is still proposed.

At their Public Meeting held on Thursday, June 4", 2026, the Planning Commission voted
to recommend that the Board of Supervisors reaffirm the Conditional approval of the Final
Subdivision Plan.

Motion to reaffirm Conditional approval of the Minor Subdivision Revised Plans for 231
Wheatfield Rd. was made by Mr. Stuck and seconded by Mr. Wertz. Upon roll call, all
Supervisors present voted affirmatively. MOTION CARRIED
