Mohns Hill Road — Stor-4-You
It was decided to review Stor-4-You first since the majority of attendees at the PC meeting were
present for that project.

The subject property is located on the north side of Mohns Hill Rd, between State Route 222 and
Chapel Hill Rd, approximately 800 ft west of the Gouglersville Interchange. The subject
property is located within the Planned Highway Interchange (PHI) District where a Self-Storage
Facility is a use permitted by right.


The proposed project is for the addition of 2 new self-storage buildings at the northeast corner of
the property and to rectify the conversion/development of the area that was to remain a meadow
listed on the 2006 recorded Land Development Plans. Presently this area has been converted into
a gravel parking area for vehicle storage without prior planning and stormwater approvals. The
Township had received concerns regarding storm water runoff from the property. Upon
investigation and using aerial imagery it was concluded that between 2010 and 2018, the entire
2.33-acre meadow that was to remain, was converted to a gravel vehicle storage area adding to
the impervious coverage of the property.

In response to the Township notice of violation, the Applicant submitted Land Development
plans in February of 2025. The first plan submittal showed the total impervious coverage of the
lot being 73%. The maximum allowable impervious coverage according to Section 316(H) of
the Zoning Ordinance is 60%. After revising the plan to reduce the amount of impervious
coverage, the applicant found that the property was at 66.3% still exceeding the 60% maximum
coverage. The applicant applied to the Zoning Hearing Board for a variance and was denied
relief from the 60% maximum coverage requirement at the Public Hearing dated January 21,
2026.

The applicant has since revised the plans to show the purchase of an adjacent 8.28-acre parcel
which borders the Stor-4-You property and the annexation of 2.75 acres in order to meet the 60%
maximum coverage requirement and provide additional stormwater improvements to the site.
Ms. Bensinger stated that even with the annexation of the 2.75 acres, the property is at its
maximum allotment for pervious/impervious coverage. No additional impervious areas may be
added to the property. A waiver of plan scale and request for a single-stage approval are the
current waivers waiting for review.

Mr. Zaborowski asked if the meadow area was going to remain impervious or returned to
meadow. Mr. Chuck Frantz of C2C stated that the parking will remain gravel instead of reverting
to meadow.

Mr. Ulrich asked if the original detention basin on the original plan was going to be updated
since it is not functioning. Mr. Frantz stated that the water from the meadow area will be
collected and directed to the new stormwater basin at the north east corner of the property and
the water that will go to the existing basin will meet the original design criteria from the existing
plans. By creating the second basin it will alleviate the additional flows going to the existing
basin from the meadow area.

As part of the process the owner had to apply for an NPDES permit since this was a major
change to the plan, which is issued by the Berks County Planning Commission and the
Pennsylvania DEP. This involves an intense study of the plan, how it will work and if the new
stormwater numbers are supported by the plan.

310 Mail Route Road

The property is located on a 12.36-acre parcel on the west side of Mail Route Rd between
Kocher Rd and Vinemont Rd within the RHA Zoning District. The property was developed in
May of 2001 as a residential 4-bedroom Single-Family detached dwelling with a well and on-lot
septic system. Shortly after the house was built, the applicant constructed a 2,500 sq. ft. horse
barn, accessory to the single-family detached dwelling meeting the requirements of the 25ft side
yard setback to the lot line.

In 2011 the applicant applied for a 13,000 sq. ft. barn addition for equipment storage, hay and
feed storage, a riding and training ring, breeding area, and a wash area. Upon review of the
application, the Zoning officer interpreted this Use to fall under the category of horse farm
which falls under the definition of General Agricultural in the Township Zoning Ordinance.
With the approval of the permit, the Use of the property became Agricultural General, and
the Single-Family Dwelling is incidental to the Horse Farm Use. The 2011 permit was
approved with the following conditions;

e The addition to the original barn must be 75 feet from exterior property lines
in accordance with Section 321(B)(2)(b) of the Zoning Ordinance.

e The addition to the original barn cannot involve animal husbandry activities
due to required setbacks.

The development of an Ordinance for Data Centers

A committee was created to work through the drafting of an Ordinance for Data Centers. After
three weeks of meetings and discussions, a draft Ordinance has been developed and should be
ready for the PC to review at the next meeting.

A resolution was adopted by the Board of Supervisors which gives the Township 180days to
develop Ordinance standards pertaining to Data Centers. Without an Ordinance in place the
Township cannot enforce certain conditions and performance standards. The Ordinance is being
drafted to protect the Township and have legal standards all applicants must adhere to. The
Ordinance must be reviewed by the Township Planning Commission and the Berks County
Planning Commission prior to adoption.

Data Centers have effects on water usage, power consumption, and noise among other things.
Many areas of our Township are not prepared for the massive amount of electricity required and
water required to run a center. Having an Ordinance in place will set the standards that must be
met for a center to be approved.
