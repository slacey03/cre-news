AGENDA AMENDMENTS: None 
OPEN TO THE FLOOR: 
Todd Hiester, 508 Albert Drive, read a letter to the Board regarding the sinkhole issue on Albert 
Drive, noting his wishes and that the Board of Supervisors will agree with the Township 
Manager and Assistant Township Manager to take action on the issue as soon as possible, to do 
everything possible safely to get this area of the road and the road of neighboring properties open 
again. Mr. Hiester thanked the Managers for their positivity and respectful consideration of 
residents' needs. 
Lisa Gallagher, Bainbridge Circle, requested that the Township decline to enter into any 
agreement with the U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement and requested that any such 
decision affecting the Community would first require a vote of the Board of Supervisors in a 
public meeting, the Township publicly state whether it has or is considering a 287(g) or a similar 
agreement, and if the Township would consider adoption of a resolution affirming that the role of 
the Township’s police department is community based, not federal immigration enforcement. 
Ms. Gallagher noted other communities that opted to decline collaboration with ICE, and noted 
recent violent, disturbing behavior and racial profiling by ICE.

OPEN TO THE FLOOR: (Continued) 
Mr. Royer stated that the Township’s Police Chief is committed to the safety of Township 
residents. Ms. Robertson stated that she would meet with Ms. Gallagher. 
 
Chris Light, 128 Intervilla Avenue, addressed the Board stating that he had gone camping 
recently, leaving his residence on Thursday, August 6th. The next day, Friday, August 7th, 
the Township posted a no-parking sign in that area because Township staff would be paving  
on Monday. The Township attempted to contact Mr. Light, and he said that he did not have cell 
service and was in the Poconos. Mr. Light said he doesn’t believe he should be penalized for 
leaving his house for the weekend and requested reimbursement for the $339.00 towing charge 
he had to pay to pick up his truck. 
 
Mr. Royer stated that Township internal paving sometimes happens on a quicker timeline, and he 
and the other Supervisors agreed to reimburse Mr. Light for the towing cost of his vehicle. Since 
the request was not on this evening’s agenda, it will be moved to the next Board of Supervisors 
Meeting, on September 14th, for approval. Mr. Light thanked the Board for their understanding. 
 
Matt Varone, 407 Harvard Boulevard, said they were attending this evening to find out the plan 
for paving Harvard Boulevard, noting that the Town Hall meeting the Board had originally 
scheduled for August was moved to November.  
Mr. Royer stated that the Board is committed to this project and explained the complexities 
involved. 
Jess Varone stated that she and her husband were out for a walk and noticed that Stanford 
Avenue was paved and questioned why the Township spent money paving a road that wasn’t in 
bad shape. Ms. Varone stated that they had lived there for eighteen (18) years, the road was 
terrible the entire time and said she is embarrassed when they have people over with the road all 
torn up. After discussion, the Board noted that the Town Hall Meeting for residents for Harvard 
Boulevard is scheduled for November 30th, 2026.

9. Entech Engineering Work Order-Clint Davis of Entech Engineering reviewed the 
Engineering Work Order (EWO) for bid services for the Police Department Site  
Improvements and Building Safety Upgrades, including Construction Document 
revisions, Scope refinement, and bid reservices. The cost of the EWO from Entech 
Engineering for approval by the Board is $15,000.00. 
  Mr. Davis briefed the Board on items he is looking to reduce significant costs by   
  removing the parking lot mill and overlay, purchasing the camera security system   
  through COSTARS, removing the 1st floor security window film, and simplifying the   
  rear drainage in the building, as well as a few other areas, and simplifying the contract. 
  Mr. Davis reviewed costs to the Township, explaining the previous markups for the   
  camera system. The main project will be put out to bid through PennBid, and Mr.   
  Davis negotiated a cost through COSTARS with Verkada for the camera security   
  system licensing for $30,000.00, a significant cost savings. Mr. Davis confirmed that   
  each camera has its own separate server, that data is saved for 60 days and then goes to 
  the cloud-based system, and that this includes a three (3) year license. After review   
  with staff, it was the best option of the two (2) companies that submitted quotes.

OLD BUSINESS: Albert Drive –Mr. Conrad updated the Board regarding a hole that opened up 
in the area of the sinkhole on Albert Drive. Crews quickly addressed the hole by installing 
flowable fill. Mr. Conrad stated that staff would like to proceed with further engineering and 
investigation, noting that the initial lidar investigation only penetrated down 6-8 ft. Mr. Conrad 
stated that he is seeking approval for up to $50,000.00 to investigate further and speed up the 
remediation process. Mr. Murray added that the investigation will encompass surrounding areas.  
Motion to authorize staff to utilize engineering services, not to exceed $50,000.00 to address the 
sinkhole on Albert Drive, was made by Mr. Wertz and seconded by Mr. Zeoli. Upon roll call, all 
Supervisors present voted affirmatively. MOTION CARRIED
