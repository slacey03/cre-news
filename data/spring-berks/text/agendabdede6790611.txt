ZONING REQUESTS:

4006 and 4010 Beaver Road (Heritage Stone) -Special Exception

The project is located at the end of Beaver Road, in the RHA Rural Holding Area Zoning
District.

The project is the redevelopment of an existing, non-conforming, multi-use property.
Previously the property included the following;

a. Residential home (occupancy unknown)

b. Retail establishment (vacant)

c. Construction office and storage facility (occupancy unknown)


The Zoning Officer met with the property owner previously and issued a formal
Nonconforming Use Letter (dated February 2025 attached) detailing to the owner what is
necessary to bring the property to a corrected permittable state. The letter references a
baseline date in 2004 where the Uses are established as nonconforming, and then in 2021
where it can be noted that unpermitted improvements have occurred. The applicant will
be required to return the site to the 2004 condition.

The applicant has submitted a special exception application requesting approval to go from one
non-conforming use to another. Based on the current submittal, the applicant would like to keep
the construction office as is, the house as is but change the retail store into a landscape stone
business. There will be pallets of stone stored outside. The processing of stone will not be
allowed. The land has never been subdivided and is listed in a single deed.

The current speculation is how the increased truck traffic will affect the road conditions.

A motion was made by Mr. Stuck and seconded by Mr. Himelright to recommend the Zoning
Hearing Board approve the Special Exception with the condition that no industrial processes
including the processing of stone can be performed on the property. Upon roll call all members
voted affirmatively. MOTION CARRIED.

802 Snyder Road — Annexation

The subject property 802 Snyder Rd is a .330-acre (14,386 sq ft) lot located on the south side of
Snyder Road between Gring Drive and Wassner Drive in the Berkshire Greens Development
which was later renamed to Whitfield Sec 1. 2303 Wassner Drive is a .245-acre (10,698 sq ft)
lot located adjacent and perpendicular to 802 Snyder Rd.

The applicant proposes to subdivide 6,386 sq feet from the existing 14,386 square foot lot and
Annex it onto the property known as 2303 Wassner Drive located perpendicular to 802 Snyder
which is owned by the same owner as 802 Snyder Rd.

802 Snyder Rd will be left with an 8,000 square foot lot which is the minimum lot size
requirement in the Moderate Density Suburban (MDS) District. 2303 Wassner will go from a
10,898 square foot lot to a 17,084 square foot lot after the annexation. Both lots meet the
minimum lot size, lot width, and the lot depth to width ratio requirements of the MDS District.

It should be noted that because the depth of the lot of 802 Snyder Rd has been reduced, if 802
Snyder Rd intends to construct an addition or large deck to the rear of the house in the future,
zoning relief may be required. As currently proposed, the house will only be 4-6 feet from the
30-foot rear yard setback line.

The maximum lot coverage by buildings is limited to 45% of the total lot area and the total
impervious coverage is limited to 60% total coverage of the lot. Both properties comply with the
building coverage and lot coverage requirements of the MDS District.

The plans indicate an above ground pool on the property of 802 Snyder Rd. There is a plan note

near the pool that states “Pool TBR”. The Township interprets this to mean Too be Removed.

Please be advised that this structure does not have Zoning or Building Code Permit approval. If

DATA CENTER ORDINANCE REVIEW

A committee was created to work through the drafting of an Ordinance for Data Centers. After
three weeks of meetings and discussions, a draft Ordinance has been developed and is ready for
review.

The key purpose tonight is to decide on areas in Township that have developable land, larger
pieces of land that have access to roadways and have similar uses. Some ideas have been put
together as desirable zones for this type of construction. Right now, an Ordinance is being

created to protect the Township interests but also accommodate for this particular use. At this
time, no one can apply for a permit to build a center since the Board of Supervisors adopted a
Resolution giving the Township 180 days to develop Ordinance standards pertaining to Data
Centers. Without an Ordinance in place the Township cannot enforce certain conditions and
performance standards. The Ordinance is being drafted to protect the Township and have legal
standards all applicants must adhere to. The Ordinance must be reviewed by the Township
Planning Commission and the Berks County Planning Commission prior to adoption.

Data Centers have effects on water usage, power consumption, and noise among other things.
Many areas of our Township are not prepared for the massive amount of electricity and water
required to run a center. Major points of the Ordinance are:

1. Energy, where it is coming from and how will it affect the power grid and the consumers.
Some Ordinances require data centers to supply their own electricity but that has yet to be
determined as to its’ legality. The infrastructure of the energy grid must be adequate to
supply the center but not at the cost of the consumer. A trend toward a requirement that a
percentage of energy is supplied by renewable sources such as wind or solar is also being
considered.

2. Water supply, The Commonwealth has introduced legislation requiring a municipal source
of water supply and it must be a loop system where the water is recycled and reused, which is
also a component of the proposed Township Ordinance. Ground water is not to be used.

3. Noise and vibration modulation which comes with the operation of the Center. How will this
be enforced to accommodate the best interest of Township of Spring residents.

Comparing a Data Center to a Warehouse there is less vehicle traffic than a warehouse because
there are less employees required and truck deliveries are few.

Mr. Becker stated that Spring Township is not an ideal place to design a center because of the
lack of open space (20 acres minimum) and steep slopes in the southern end of the Township, but
areas must be selected so the Township is not considered exclusionary. The possible zones that
have been looked at are the PHI (Planned Highway Interchange), PHT (Planned Highway
Transitional), MI (Manufacturing and Industrial), EI (Extractive Industry) and FS (Fuel Storage)
Zoning Districts. A large section of the PHT district is owned by the Metropolitan Development
Group, which contains flood zones and wetlands. The PHI district currently consists of mostly
residential dwellings and businesses. MI is off Fritztown Road near the former Steel Co
Property. FS is the site of the fuel tanks and underground lines, and the EI District is the Old
Grings Hill Quarry now Storage World self-storage facility.

It was suggested that the Township could find larger tracts of land at the southern end of the
Township, but it would require breaking up an existing district to create a new overlay district.
That would require a map change, which is a more involved process and excessively more
expensive.

The reason the Township was looking at the above-mentioned areas is they are already existing
