Recycling in Parks

Township representatives met with Waste Management, which reported that other customers
attempt recycling in parks but are not successful, and that they have no viable options for a
Township of our size separate from staff collection, transport, and storage to the Township’s
containers at the garage for pickup. Mr. Groller stated that Waste Management said the entities
that succeed in recycling are larger amusement parks and organizations with waste-management-
type staff whose daily responsibility is managing recycling. A pilot program was initiated at
Breneman Park on weekends when the pavilion is rented. The recycling containers are rarely
used, and what is recycled is contaminated with non-recyclables and garbage and must be
disposed of by traditional methods. Ms. Houck confirmed that the pilot recycling program at
Breneman Park will continue another month until the end of the season in October.

1. Renewable Energy: Mr. Zeoli stated that he would like to see renewable energy broken down
into two (2) ideas, with,

1) Plastic Bag Ban: Getting in touch with the organization that attended a meeting previously to

2) Solar Panels: Mr. Zeoli stated that he would like to see potential areas scoped out to determine
what Township-owned structures would be viable for solar panels, requesting a scope of the
project this quarter and quotes and details for spring/summer of next year. Mr. Royer agreed,
noting that with the planned garage expansion, he would like to see solar panels in the specs for
that project. Mr. Royer noted that the environmental argument resonates with him, and that the
real benefit is the cost savings. Mr. Stuck added that, when evaluating areas for solar panels, they
should also evaluate the remaining life of the roofs for planned installation. Mr. Royer noted that

3. Food Trucks: Mr. Zeoli stated he would like to reaffirm his interest in food trucks in the
Township as they are in surrounding areas at parks and events. Mr. Zeoli asked whether the
Township’s insurance requirements are a deterrent and whether the cost is higher in the
Township than in surrounding communities, noting his desire to support food trucks in the
community.

Ms. Houck stated that the Township’s Insurance Company requires the Township to receive a
certificate of insurance from the vendor, and that the Township is listed as an additional insured,
noting that the Township receives this from anyone who rents a facility in the Township.

Mr. Becker confirmed that this is standard practice across all municipalities.

Chief Hackman added that the Health Certificate is required under the Fire Code, and they
require it at inspection, which is done once a year; the cost is $80.00. When the Fire Department
inspects for the Food Certificate, they also inspect the safety of the gases in the truck to prevent
explosions or fire hazards.

Ms. Houck stated that the Township additionally confirms that all food trucks on Township
property follow State Requirements and hold a license from the Department of Agriculture and
provide automobile insurance. The Township process involves Codes, the Fire Department, and
Parks and Recreation Department (if on Township Parks property).

VISION DISCUSSION TOPICS

Harvard Boulevard

Mr. Reichert opened the topic by addressing the status of Harvard Boulevard and the need for a
new roadway, curb replacements, and sidewalk repairs or replacements. Mr. Reichert said that
the proposed plan for Harvard is a full reconstruction, which will allow the Township to
complete a lot of work in that area. Mr. Reichert reviewed the status of the entire area on
Harvard Boulevard. He stated that after final staff review, the recommendation is a full
restoration of Harvard Boulevard, including full restoration of curbs per Township specs, and
bringing the curbs in 2ft on both sides. The current cartway width is 40ft, and a typical cartway
width is 33ft. Mr. Reichert said that a wider road encourages speeding and is more road to
maintain.

Mr. Reichert stated that staff recommends the Township cover the project cost because the
Township is relocating the roadway and will incur the cost of the new curbs in this situation. Mr.
Reichert confirmed that everyone’s planting strip will be increased by 2ft; the Township will
backfill and seed it, and after that, the residents will be responsible for maintaining it. Residents
will be responsible for their curbs in the future, including any maintenance or replacement needs.
Property owners must address sidewalks that need repair or replacement.

Mr. Reichert discussed issues with some very large trees on Harvard Boulevard, noting that
some of the trees are in the grass strip, which is not permitted in the Township. One tree’s root
system has grown over the curb, and the gutter line has tree roots. Mr. Reichert stated that
driveway aprons will need to be extended, and Mr. Conrad said storm drains will need to be
moved, and additional storm features added. Costs, (Including the grant the Township received)
were discussed. After a thorough discussion, the Board agreed with staff’s recommendation. The
Town Hall Meeting for Harvard Boulevard residents is scheduled for November 30" at 6:00 p.m.
