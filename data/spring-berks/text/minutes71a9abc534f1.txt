Reedy Road Speeding Concerns

Dan Wozniak, a 34-year resident at 2959 Reedy Road, addressed the Board regarding speeding issues on
Reedy Road, noting it is a dangerous situation with all the children and pedestrians in the area, as well as
a nearby park. Mr. Wozniak suggested that the Township consider installing speed bumps, adding that
due to truck restrictions on Wilshire Boulevard, Reedy Road has become a major truck route. In addition

and down the road and expressed concern for children and residents in the neighborhood.

Chief Brock stated that only the State Police in PA are permitted to use radar, noting that the speed
timing devices the Department uses have the same accuracy. Mr. Royer said that speed bumps have been
discussed at the Infrastructure Committee (IC) and Transportation Advisory Committee (TAC)
Meetings. Mr. Reichert confirmed that the Township has received additional information from
PennDOT regarding traffic calming, and this may be discussed at the next TAC Meeting. Mr. Stuck
requested that, during that review, the Committee consider traffic-calming solutions for Gelsinger Road.

1) 310 Mail Route Road-Zoning Variance Request

Ms. Bensinger updated the Board on the history of a 12.36-acre parcel located on the west side of Mail
Route Road between Kocher Road and Vinemont Road within the RHA Zoning District. The property
was developed in May of 2001 as a residential 4-bedroom Single-Family detached dwelling with a well
and on-lot septic system. Shortly after the house was built, the applicant constructed a 2,500-sq.-ft. horse
barn on the property. The building predates the Township property files, so there are no records of its
approval or of how it came to be at its setback location.

In 2011, the applicant applied for a 13,000-sq.-ft. barn addition. Upon review of the application, Mr.
Wozniak interpreted this Use to fall under the category of a horse farm, which falls under the definition
of General Agricultural in the Township Zoning Ordinance and requires that the barn be set back a
minimum of 75 ft. from the property line. The original application and drawing showed a 79-ft.-4-inch
setback; therefore, the zoning permit was approved. Agricultural structures in the State of Pennsylvania
are building code exempt; the horse barn was not reviewed for compliance with the building code.

It was later discovered that the structure was constructed only 21.9 ft. from the rear lot line (not 79 ft-4
inches as indicated on the application), thereby violating the 75-ft. requirement.

The farm property is currently for sale, and the owner has received numerous inquiries about whether
the property may be considered a Commercial Stable or a Riding Academy. Ms. Bensinger stated that it
is a permitted use in that Zoning District, provided it meets the following requirements:
e Plan review requirements, along with Performance standards listed in Section 324 of the Zoning
Ordinance.
¢ To become a Commercial Stable, the applicant would need a building permit, inspection
approvals, and a Certificate of Use & Occupancy.
¢ On-Lot Septic approval to serve the Barn. The current system was approved only for a 4-
bedroom Single-Family Detached Dwelling.
e Ifrelief is not granted for the 150ft setback, the Applicant would still need to seek relief from the
Agricultural setback of 75ft.
