Presentation:    Flock Presentation 
 
Jason Trautman, Station Commander of Pennsylvania State Police Troop M in  
Bethlehem, which provides primary police coverage to the Township, gave a breakdown  
and recap on what they’ve been seeing patrol based and crime trending throughout the  
Township and neighboring communities.  Trooper Trautman mentioned that they have a  
community outreach bringing communities and officers together (i.e. coffee with a cop;  
hoagies for heroes, trick or treat, etc.)  
 
Trevor Chandler, Senior Director of Public Affairs at Flock, addressed the Board  
regarding the Flock system.  He explained that the system is not programmed to identify  
race, color, creed or national origin or license plates.  They are designed to look for  
wanted or suspicious vehicles.  They are motion censored cameras.  It takes a picture of  
every vehicle that goes in front of it.  They are not integrated with any DMV or personal  
identification records (i.e. social security numbers, date of birth, etc.).  It is solely about  
the vehicle and the crime that that vehicle is alleged to be associated with.   The Vice  
Chairman would like to see more transparency so that the communities can understand  
the benefits of a Flock system.    
 
William Kovacs, a resident of the Township, wanted to know who owns the data linked  
to the data base that the Flock system reads.  Mr. Chandler replied that the data is stored  
and the customer (Hanover Township) owns their data and they decide what other  
agencies they choose to share the data with.  Flock does not sell data.  We are not a social  
media company.  Our service is providing public safety technology to communities such  
as Hanover Township, Lehigh County.  Mr. Kovacs mentioned that given the number of  
power outages that this area has had recently, what  happens to the cameras when power  
is lost for a significant period of time if they are powered by a local utility source.   Mr.  
Chandler replied that their cameras are solar powered.  They do offer municipalities the  
availability to connect them to an AC power.  There could be circumstances where the  
cameras are not powered and active depending on the features of the cameras.  Mr.  
Chandler noted that it is important to know that if the cameras were affected by a power  
outage, they have internal tracking systems that alert them to that where they then will  
alert the agencies to the cameras that are potentially either not working or have lost  
power.

The Manager wanted Council to understand that the Township in fact owns the data and 
we do not sell it.   
 
Mr. Chandler noted that the great thing about Flock is that it allows communities to  
customize the system according to their needs.  They don’t violate the 4th Amendment  
and don’t track individuals like a cell phone in a pocket does.  They take a point in time  
image of the rear of a vehicle.  We take every step to ensure that community safety does  
not come at community values.   
 
The Manager will work with Lt. Trautman to put something together for the Township’s  
newsletter introducing the Flock system in our Township to go with what the Vice  
Chairman requested that the community know the value of this system and how it reflects  
on our crime numbers going down.
