B.         PUBLIC HEARING AND VOTING ON LIQUOR LICENSE TRANSFER  
A RESOLUTION APPROVING THE TRANSFER OF PENNSYLVANIA RESTAURANT CASA 
CATRINA LIQUOR LICENSE NO. R-20715 WHITEHALL STORE OF PENNSYLVANIA INC., FOR 
USE AT 4117 MAIN STREET, WHITEHALL TOWNSHIP, LEHIGH COUNTY, PENNSYLVANIA 
(BOC) 
  
    John McCreesh, Esq. and Manish Patel were present.  The applicant is requesting an approval for a 
                restaurant liquor license transfer from Casa Catrina, 1905 Brookside Road, Macungie to the Whitehall  
                Store located at 4117 Main Street, Whitehall.  
                The applicant advised that the current use will continue and he will be adding the sale of hot and cold 
                sandwiches and only intends on selling beer.  The current hours of operation is 6am to 10pm.  The 
                applicant agreed to cease selling at 9pm. 
   
                Commissioner SNYDER had the following concerns:  
- selling alcohol along side a pharmacy 
- that tables and chairs have been added to the location without any approvals,  
- not providing a complete application before the Workshop Meeting with all information needed. 
 
                 Commissioner STAKES verified that although the table and chairs have been added, the establishment 
                 is not operating as a restaurant. 
                
                 President WARREN has concerns with the number of seats required versus the parking that’s available. 
 
                 Applicant agreed he would obtain any type of permits or zoning approvals, if required. 
 
                 Attorney Jack GROSS explained numerous times that the Board is only approving the transfer.  The  
                 Liquor Control Board will then give final approval based on their requirements of what is needed at 
                 the establishment and if the applicant cannot provide that they are doing it at their own risk. 
 
                 Although the restaurant license permits the sale of beer, wine and liquor, the Applicant agreed to 
                 only sell beer and wine, which will be made a condition of approval, along with closing at 9pm and 
                 complying with all Township Ordinances and Regulations. 
                                                    
                        Commissioner STAKES moved to approve, seconded by Commissioner CHRISTMAN 
 
                        SEVEN Commissioners were present and four voting “yes” (STAKES, CHRISTMAN, ATIYEH  
                        & FOX) and three voting “no” (SNYDER, SLONAKER & WARREN).  Resolution No. 3462 was  
                        approved. 
 
   NOTE:  The above represents a brief summation of this Liquor License Transfer hearing.  A 
   complete, verbatim transcript of the proceeding can be viewed upon request at the Township  
   Municipal Building, 3219 MacArthur Road, Whitehall, PA.

COMPRISED OF 2 SEPARATE AND DISTINCT STRUCTURES INTO APARTMENTS, PURSUANT 
TO SEC. 27-78(D)(1) AND SEC. 27-44(D)(1) OF THE WHITEHALL TOWNSHIP ZONING 
ORDINANCE. 
                   William Hutcheson III, Esq and William Snyder of WS Property Holdings were present as well as  
                   Township Solicitor Jack Gross and Zoning Officer Christopher Gittinger. 
 
 The parcel is located within the C-1 Neighborhood Commercial Zoning District which includes 
 apartments as a Conditional Use.  On or about December 19, 2018, the Applicant purchased the 
 property and recorded the deed.  The property contains two distinct residential structures, specifically a   
 pair of units identified as 720 Front and 720 Rear.  There is no common corridor between the two units. 
 Each unit has its own water, sewer and electric meters.  These separate utility connections pre-existed 
 the applicants purchase of the property.  Since acquiring the property, the applicant has received 
 Certificates of Occupancy from the Township and received a business privilege license.  In 2025 
 It was determined through a certificate of occupancy request the property was designated as a 
 Single family dwelling in the Lehigh County Tax Assessment Office an the CO was denied.  The  
 Applicant is pursuing Conditional Use Approval to have the property comply with laws and 
 regulations associated with two units.

D.      PUBLIC HEARING AND VOTING ON ORDINANCES 
 
          1.   BILL NO. 15-2026  (SECOND HEARING) 
                  TITLE:  AN ORDINANCE AMENDING THE OFFICIAL ZONING MAP OF WHITEHALL TOWNSHIP BY  
                                REZONING AN APPROXIMATELY 147.326 ACRE PARCEL OWNED BY LEHIGH VALLEY  
                                HOSPITAL, INC FROM R-2, LOW DENSITY RESIDENTIAL, AND R-4, MEDIUM DENSITY  
                                RESIDENTIAL, TO R-3, LOW/MEDIUM DENSITY RESIDENTIAL; AND AMENDING CHAPTER  
                                27, SECTIONS 27-72, 27-81 AND 27-194 OF THE WHITEHALL TOWNSHIP ZONING  
                                ORDINANCE, AS AMENDED 
 
  President WARREN read Bill No. 21-2026 to the Board. 
            

  Page 4 
 
                          Commissioner SNYDER moved to approve, seconded by Commissioner WARREN 
 
                Dan Quay of Lehigh Valley Health Network, Attorney Graham Simmons, and representatives from  
                Pidcock Company were present. 
 
                Attorney Simmons stated that there would be no presentation as this is a second hearing and nothing 
                has changed from the previous presentations. 
 
                Commissioner STAKES noted his surprise that there were no changes made considering the number of 
                suggestions that were made previously and also the 5-0 denial from the Planning Commission.  It does 
                not appear that the concerns of the residents were addressed.  Attorney Simmons stated that LVPC is in  
                favor of the zoning amendment and most of residents’ concerns were land development related. 
                Commissioner STAKES moved to the size of the LVHN LED digital sign that will be placed at  
                MacArthur Road and the bypass road, which is also adjacent to a residential neighborhood.  It will be  
                the largest sign on MacArthur Road and currently goes against the zoning ordinance. Attorney  
                Simmons advised that the sign was reduced to 40 feet. 
 
                Commissioner CHRISTMAN also stated his displeasure with the sign size. 
 
                Scott Pidcock advised that the sign needs to be size that it is to communicate a message to the 
                public over a distance so that drivers can safely navigate the turn needed to reach the hospital. 
 
                Commissioner SNYDER has no issue with the sign as he is under the assumption that the sign will 
                be angled and could be pointed away from the residential homes and would have less illumination. 
                Commissioner STAKES disagreed due to the curve in the road and also stated that other hospitals 
                Of the same size do not use large LED signs to direct them, especially when adjacent to residential 
                neighborhoods and is very concerned that LVHN will not budge on this issue.  
 
                Commissioner STAKES added that this development will increase traffic on MacArthur Road. 
                Scott Pidcock stated that Mechanicsville Road will get better with the bypass road and this is the 
                only way the bypass road gets built.  They have provided traffic studies that PennDOT has reviewed 
                and noted that they are reliable studies. 
 
                Karen Poshefco verified that a helipad is still part of this proposal with an estimate of 8 flights a year. 
              
                Anthony Kopack questioned the amount of parking spaces and Mr. Pidcock advised 1 to 2,000 will be 
                provided. 
 
                Brent Fenstermaker stated that he does not see that the bypass road will alleviate traffic but will invite 
                more traffic. 
 
                Howard Lieberman expressed the need for a hospital in the township. 
 
                         SEVEN Commissioners were present and six voting “yes” and one voting “no” (STAKES).   
                         Bill 21-2026 was approved.
