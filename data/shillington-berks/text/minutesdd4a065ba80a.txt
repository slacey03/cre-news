The August 13, 2026, meeting of the Shillington Borough Council was 
called to order at 7:30 p.m. by President John W. Hoffert. Following the invocation 
given by Mayor Hivner, and Oath of Allegiance, roll call was taken. Council members 
in attendance were Elizabeth M. Bentz, Nicholas M. Bentz, Mark C. Grounard, 
John W. Hoffert, Michael A. Hoffert, and Jared A. Hopgood. Mr. Ronald R. Klee, Jr., 
was absent. Present in addition to Council members, were Borough Manager 
Scott D. Brossman, Fire Chief David M. Bentz, Mayor Andrew R. Hivner, Solicitor 
Thomas L. Klonis, and Secretary Kristen M. Schlottman. Kenneth Fulmer of Great 
Valley Consultants was also in attendance along with the following residents: 
• Veronica Martin - Mifflin Community Library 
• Rachel Sebastian - Governor Mifflin School District 
• Devon Tarowitz - Governor Mifflin School District 
• Carol A. Leiphart- 325 Wheatland Avenue 
As there were no objections, the minutes of the July 9, 2026, Council 
meeting were approved as forwarded to each member on a motion by Dr. Bentz, 
which was seconded by Mr. Bentz, and unanimously carried. 
Prior to conducting regular Borough business, President Hoffert 
presented Carol A. Leiphart with a plaque in recognition of her years of service to 
the Borough of Shillington as Tax Collector and congratulated her on her retirement 
from that position. Ms. Leiphart expressed her appreciation to the Borough Council 
and staff for the assistance and support they provided throughout her years of 
service. 
PUBLIC COMMENT: Ms. Veronica Martin addressed Council regarding two matters 
involving the Mifflin Community Library. First, she inquired about the Library's 
request for permission to paint the signal control box located adjacent to the 
Library's garden. Ms. Martin stated that the Library has agreed to assume 
responsibility for the routine maintenance and upkeep of the artwork. Based on that 
representation, President Hoffert directed Ms. Martin to contact Mr. Brossman to 
discuss the proposal further and develop a plan for accomplishing the Library's 
request. 
Ms. Martin also advised Council that the Library Director wished to explore 
the possibility of establishing a partnership with the Shillington Borough Pool 
through a day-pass lending program. Under the proposal, pool passes would be 
available for library patrons to "check out" using their library membership, allowing 
admission to the pool for one day at no charge. 

August13,2026 
President Hoffert stated that the proposal would first need to be presented to 
the Recreation Board for consideration and planning. If supported by the Recreation 
Board, the matter would then be forwarded to Borough Council for review and 
possible action. Ms. Martin thanked Council and indicated that she would advise 
the Library Director to contact the Recreation Board. 
Following Ms. Martin's comments, Ms. Terowitz addressed Council regarding 
the Governor Mifflin School District's request to use Shillington Park and several 
park pavilions for a StoryWalk event. The matter was scheduled for consideration 
under the Borough Manager's report. Ms. Terowitz explained that the original 
request included September 26, 2026, September 27, 2026 {rain date), and 
March 18, 2027. However, after further discussion, the School District elected to 
proceed only with the September dates at this time. 
Following a motion by Mr. Michael Hoffert, seconded by Mr. Hopgood, and 
unanimously approved, Council granted permission to Governor Mifflin School 
District to use Shillington Park and several pavilions for their event. 
Committee Reports 
Mayor: Mayor Hivner presented the Police Department's report for the month of July. 
During the month, the Department handled 351 incidents, including 45 arrests and 
officers traveled 2,768 patrol miles. 
Mayor Hivner also reported that the Department participated in the 
Community Days 50th Anniversary Parade held on June 28, 2026. In addition, he 
provided an update on the Civil Service Commission's efforts to establish an 
eligibility list for the hiring of a new Borough Police Officer. He advised that two 
applicants successfully participated in the written and physical agility testing phase 
of the selection process. The next phase will consist of oral examinations. Upon 
completion of the testing process and receipt of the examination scores, the Civil 
Service Commission will determine the appropriate next steps.

Unfinished Business: President Hoffert provided an update regarding the property 
located at 200 West Lancaster Avenue. He reported that, according to information 
received from Building Code Official Terrence Naugle, the anticipated settlement on 
the property did not occur. While the property is expected to remain listed for sale, 
the current owner is reportedly seeking funding to permit the demolition of the 
deteriorated porch due to safety concerns. President Hoffert stated that additional 
information would be provided as it become available. 
President Hoffert also addressed the request submitted by counsel for Jr. 
Lofts, LLC, the owner of 18 Catherine Street, regarding the repaving and resurfacing 
of Maple Alley. He advised that the matter had been discussed by both the Streets 
Committee and Borough Council; however, no decision has been reached at this 
time. The matter remains tabled pending further discussion, and counsel for JR 
Lofts, LLC, will be notified once a determination has been made.
