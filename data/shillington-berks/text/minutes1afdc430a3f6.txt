PUBLIC COMMENT: Ms. Veronica Martin, Board President, and Ms. Natasha
Donaldson, Director, of the Mifflin Community Library presented the Library’s
annual report to Council. They expressed appreciation for the municipal funding
provided by the Borough, noting that it tremendously assists in planning for
maintaining library operations and services. Ms. Martin reported that the Mifflin
Community Library continues to hold its designation as a Gold Star Library and
currently serves 1,261 active cardholders residing within the Borough. Ms. Martin
also noted that a mockup design for the signal control box wrap has been submitted
for review and approval. In closing, Ms. Martin and Ms. Donaldson thanked Council
for their consideration and continued support in 2027.

Mr. Andrew Yetter, representing the American Legion Reber-Moore Post 635,
addressed Council and introduced Ms. Kyla Love. Ms. Love presented information
regarding a proposed Hometown Heroes Banner Program to be sponsored by the
Reber-Moore Post 635 and requested the Borough’s approval for the initiative.

Ms. Love explained that the program would recognize active-duty military personnel
and living veterans, complementing the existing veterans’ monument and clock that
honor military service and those who have passed. Ms. Love would coordinate the

funding, necessary approvals, and installation of all purchased banners. Ms. Love
further advised that no financial contribution would be requested from the Borough,
and therefore no costs would be borne by Borough residents. Council advised

Ms. Love they will take her request under consideration, however, asked that more
information be provided once funding and additional logistics are solidified.

once the requested details have been finalized.

Committee Reports

Mayor: Mayor Hivner presented the Police Department’s report for the month of
September. During the month, the Department handled 348 incidents, including
42 arrests and officers traveled 2,834 patrol miles.

Mayor Hivner also reported that the Civil Service Commission is
recommending that Council postpone initiating the creation of a new eligibility list
due to the status of Sergeant Witkowski’s medical leave. He also reported that the
Department assisted at a residential fire on Kings Boulevard and assisted with
security at Governor Mifflin High School football games during the month.

In closing, Mayor Hivner announced to the public and Council that the
Borough’s annual Trick-or-Treat night will be held on Saturday, October 31, 2026,
from 6:00 p.m. to 9:00 p.m.

Police and Personnel: Mr. Nicholas M. Bentz, chairman, made a motion, which was
seconded by Mr. Hopgood, and unanimously carried, to approve the overtime paid
to the Police Department during the month of September in the amount of
$17,488.42.

Mr. Bentz then made a motion, seconded by Mr. Michael Hoffert, and duly
approved, to renew the agreement with Highway Safety Network for the Borough’s
continued participation in DUI roving patrols for 2027.

an Executive Session to discuss Police Department personnel matters and potential
hiring. The regular meeting reconvened at 8:30 p.m. Upon returning to session,

Mr. Bentz made a motion, which was seconded by Mr. Grounard, and duly carried, to
direct the Civil Service Commission to end its current eligibility list efforts and to

Fire: Mr. Michael A. Hoffert, Chairman, presented the Fire Chief’s report for the
month of September. During the month, the Department responded to fifty (50)
dispatches, including sixteen (16) within the Borough and thirty-four (34) mutual aid
responses to neighboring municipalities. Daytime staff successfully covered all 168
available staffing hours, and Department members participated in six training
sessions.

Mr. Hoffert reported that the Fire Department responded to two working
structure fires within the Borough during the month. The first incident occurred on
September 1 at A&C Auto, located at 10 Museum Road, where three vehicles were
destroyed by fire. Estimated property damage from this incident was approximately
$30,000.00. The second incident occurred on September 19 at 31 Kings Boulevard.
The residence sustained severe fire damage, with estimated losses totaling
approximately $350,000.00. Mr. Hoffert also noted that Chief Bentz is extremely
proud of the firefighters’ response to both incidents and their continued
commitment to training, preparedness, and service to the community.

In closing, Mr. Hoffert made a motion requesting Council’s authorization to
purchase materials for interior improvements at the Fire Station. He advised that
the project cost has been amended to $1,475.00, not to exceed $2,500.00, as labor
costs were removed from the original estimate of $4,195.00, not to exceed
$4,500.00. The reduction was made because the Borough’s Public Works
Department will perform the work in-house. The motion was seconded by Mr.
Hopgood, and unanimously carried, authorizing the purchase of the materials at the
amended cost.

Recreation: Dr. Elizabeth M. Bentz, Chairman, provided Council with a review of the
minutes from the September 14, 2026, and October 5, 2026, Recreation Board

Dr. Bentz advised Council that the pending proposal from the Mifflin
Community Library regarding a day pass for library patrons to “check out” is still

The Recreation Board also discussed pavilion rental fees, playground
program fees and pool membership rates for the 2027 season. The Board voted to
approve the recommended rate increases as presented; however, the final decision
on pool membership rates and playground fees will be reviewed upon receipt of the
report from the Berks County Pool Alliance regarding prevailing wage rates and
compensation for pool and playground personnel.

Unfinished Business: President Hoffert provided an update regarding the property
located at 200 West Lancaster Avenue. He advised Council that Mr. Naugle of Great
Valley Consultants met with Mr. Savory and his contractor, Chris Flood, to discuss
the completed work to date and future proposed improvements. Due to the
extensive deterioration of the structure, the second and third-floor porch decks,
along with the roof, have been removed. The intent is to retain the first-floor porch
deck as a canopy over the building entrances. A temporary support wall will be
installed to allow for the removal and replacement of the existing steel columns
with new wood columns. The first-floor porch deck will then be repaired, and a
sloped metal roof will be installed. It is anticipated that this work will be completed
within the next several weeks to prevent any further deterioration of the building.

There is currently a professional designer preparing renovation plans. Upon
completion of those plans, Mr. Savory will submit applications for the additional
permits required for the project. Mr. Flood is expected to undertake the renovation
work after completing a project currently in progress, with construction anticipated
to begin within approximately three to four months.
