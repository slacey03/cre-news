RULES FOR PUBLIC PARTICIPATION AT COUNCIL MEETINGS 
Public comment at all meetings of the City Council of the City of Reading shall be governed by 
the following: 
• Residents and/or taxpayers of the City who wish to make or submit public comment must 
pre-register utilizing one of three (3) following options: 
1. By calling (610-655-6205) or emailing (council@readingpa.gov) the City 
Clerk's Office by 4 pm on the day of the meeting with your name, address and 
the subject matter or topic to be discussed. If you are not able to attend in-
person, instructions to access the virtual meeting app or dial-in telephone 
number will be provided. 
2. By submitting written public comment to be read into the record at the meeting 
by submitting an email to council@readingpa.gov by 4pm on the day of the 
meeting. The email message must clearly be marked as Public Comment.  
3. By registering in-person at the Council Chambers on the sign-up sheet found 
on the podium in Council Chambers between 5:00 p.m. and 7:00 p.m. on the 
day of the scheduled meeting, with your name, address and the subject matter 
or topic to be discussed.  
• No person shall be permitted to speak who is not a resident or taxpayer of the City, except 
upon permission of the President of Council or a majority of City Council.  
• Public comment will occur only during the Public Comment period listed on the meeting 
agenda at which time the Presiding Officer will recognize speakers and written comment 
that properly registered in the order received.

• All public comment shall be delivered from the podium and speakers may not approach 
the Council tables at any time. 
• Those individuals registered to speak on agenda matters will be allotted no more than 5 
minutes and those individuals registered to speak on non-agenda matters will be allotted 
no more than 3 minutes.  The City Clerk will control a timer to monitor these time periods.  
When the alarm rings, a speaker must immediately end their comment.  If a speaker refuses 
to comply, the microphone at the podium will be turned off and the next speaker will be 
given the floor.  These time periods also apply to written comments that are being read into 
the record such that any written comment may only be read into the record for a 5-minute 
period for agenda items and a 3-minute period for non-agenda items. The President of 
Council or a majority of Council may grant an additional period of time in their discretion.   
• If anyone is present in a group or association, each such group should designate a 
spokesperson to address the City Council on the group’s collective behalf in order to avoid 
repetitive public comments. 
• Comments posted in Zoom Chat and on Facebook are not considered public comment and 
will not be read into the record of the meeting or addressed by City Council.

• All public comment must be directed to Council as a body and not to any individual Council

make a statement only.  It is not designed for questioning and there is no guarantee or 
requirement for a question presented during public comment to be answered or responded 
to.

• Each speaker shall speak clearly and slowly. 
• No speaker shall register or speak more than once at a meeting. 
• A speaker shall not comment in a manner that is disruptive, scandalous, impertinent, 
redundant, rude, unruly, personally offensive, irrelevant to the registered topic, done for 
the purpose of preventing the conduct of the business of the meeting or promotion of 
candidates at an upcoming election, or otherwise in disregard to the rules of order 
or decorum.  Any such comment will not be accepted or included in the record. 
• In the event that a speaker or written comment violates any of these rules, that speaker or 
written comment will be deemed to have forfeited their right to public comment and the 
President of Council or a majority of City Council may direct the City Clerk to turn off the 
microphone or cease reading the comment into the record, have the individual removed 
from Council Chambers and/or cited.

respectful manner that does not disrupt the meeting.  Clapping, calling out, cheering or 
interrupting speakers or the discussions of City Council during or after a public comment 
is not permitted.  Those wishing to have conversations should do so in the hall outside 
Council Chambers in a low speaking voice.

RECOMMENDED ACTION:  Council approval of a Resolution for Sewage Plan Approval 
for the Jairo Ordonez at 324 Barlett St. 
 
RECOMMENDATION: 
The Administration recommends that Council approve this resolution for a sewage planning module 
as the proposed connection does comply with the allocations and other aspects of the provision of 
Chapter 94 of State Statutes. 
 
BACKGROUND: 
The Pennsylvania code requires municipalities to adopt an Official Sewage Facilities Plan providing 
sewage services adequate to prevent contamination of waters of the Commonwealth and/or 
environmental health hazards from sewage wastes.  The code also provides for the revision of the 
plan whenever it is necessary to determine whether a proposed method of sewage disposal for a new 
land development conforms to a comprehensive program of pollution control and water quality

Note: It is proposed that the Riverfront Redevelopment Overlay District, and the

be eliminated. Public access to the riverfront will remain via a mandatory 50-foot access 
easement. (This requirement will need to be codified through a separate “waterfront

Section One: Amending the City Code Chapter 600 Zoning as follows: 
§ 600-820. Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) Overlay District. 
A. Purpose and Intent 
1. Promote and encourage pedestrian-oriented, dense and walkable mixed-
use developments within the TOD Overlay District. 
2. Promote economic development within the TOD Overlay District by 
pursuing the adaptive reuse of existing structures and developing 
underutilized sites for new uses that contribute to the local economy. 
3. Encourage the development of a variety of housing types, including 
workforce housing. 
4. Design buildings, sites and structures using appropriate materials to 
create a walkable environment with highly articulated architecture and 
unified and integrated streetscaping. 
5. Increase access to retail, services, institutions and employment 
opportunities. 
6. Create a welcoming and friendly pedestrian-centered environment through 
the inclusion of public civic spaces, pedestrian and bicycle paths, street 
trees, street furnishings, ornamental lighting and wayfinding. 
B. Application  
1. The TOD Overlay District standards shall apply to any subdivision and/or 
land development application for new construction within the area 
identified in the TOD Overlay District Map. 
2. These standards shall not apply when the subdivision and/or land 
development application is for adaptive reuse of an existing building. The 
provisions of Section 600-1202 of the Zoning Ordinance will govern the 
requirements for adaptive reuse unless the area designated for future 
growth and expansion to the building(s) occupying the adaptive reuse is 
greater than twenty-five percent (25%) gross floor area of the existing 
building. In this instance, the provisions of this section shall apply. 
3. Plans, variances, conditional uses and building approvals that have 
already been approved at the time of the effective date of this section shall 
remain as approved.

Artisanal manufacturing - The on-site production, display, and sale of

§ 515-511. Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) Overlay District. 
A. Purpose and Intent 
1. Support the intent of the Reading TOD Overlay District as contained in the City of 
Reading Zoning Ordinance. 
2. Promote high quality development by utilizing appropriate building materials to 
create highly articulated architecture with a fully integrated streetscape. 

53 
 
53 
 
3. Provide for safe and convenient pedestrian access to buildings and to and from 
public sidewalks, crosswalks, bus stops, train stations, pedestrian or bicycle trails or 
paths and between buildings and sites. 
4. Promote alternate modes of transportation such as bicycle and pedestrian paths, 
bus and train transportation, and provide and maintain high quality public transit 
waiting areas. 
5. Provide a pedestrian-friendly character and environment throughout the TOD 
District with public civic spaces, pedestrian/bicycle paths, street trees, landscaping, 
seating areas, street furnishings, lighting and pedestrian-scale signage and 
wayfinding. 
B. Applicability. All new developments within the TOD Overlay District shall comply with the 
following design standards. If there is a conflict between the design standards of the TOD 
Overlay District and other City SALDO Standards, the TOD Overlay standards shall 
govern. 
C. Compliance 
1. Plan submission shall include the following items related to design standards: 
a. Sketch plan, drawn to scale, showing the plan elements as required by City 
ordinances; 
b. Photographs of existing buildings that illustrate the design intent of the 
proposal; or building elevations drawn to scale; or photo-realistic 
illustrations depicting proposed scale, proportions, design elements, 
materials and color; 
c. Colored rendering of the plan(s); 
d. Landscape plan; and 
e. Current photographs of existing site conditions. 
f. Once the preliminary styles are reviewed by the City Planning Commission, 
and the City Planning Commission provides the applicant with written 
comments, additional and more detailed preliminary plan, architectural 
renderings, prepared by a registered architect, shall be submitted for all 
proposed buildings, together with a list and samples of types of exterior 
building materials that will be visible from any public street. Similarly, a 
rendered landscape plan, prepared by a registered landscape architect 
shall also be submitted for review. An outdoor lighting plan shall also be 
submitted for review. 
g. City Council approval of proposed architectural and streetscape design is 
required for the land development application to be approved.  
2. Waiver of Fees. Fifteen percent (15%) of the filing fees related to Sketch Plan, 
Preliminary Plan and Final Plan submission as enumerated on the City of Reading 
website may be waived when workforce housing units are constructed as part of a 
proposed development. 
D. Building Architectural Design Standards 
1. Building design shall provide contextual references to its surrounding built 
environment, including existing architecture, natural features, and cultural contexts. 
2. Primary and accent building materials shall visually complement each other in terms 
of color, texture, and pattern. 
E. Building Massing and Scale 

54 
 
54 
 
1. The massing and scale of new construction shall consider its surrounding context. 
Where larger scaled buildings are located next to smaller ones, steps shall be taken 
to visually transition in terms of size, massing, and scale, which can be achieved 
using any of the following techniques: 
a. Stepping back upper stories or adjusting the roof pitch of larger massed 
buildings on the side where they are adjacent to smaller massed buildings. 
b. Including additional buffer space between larger massed buildings and 
smaller massed buildings. This buffer space is an appropriate area for 
drives, landscaping, and open space. 
c. Including horizontal architectural details such as cornices and window 
frames on larger massed buildings that align with the roofline of the 
adjacent smaller massed buildings. 
d. Using trees and other greenery to soften hard edges between differently 
massed buildings. 
2. Large-scale commercial and mixed-use buildings shall include architectural or 
material-based articulation to reduce the perceived mass of the building while still 
aligning with the built context of the block, which can be achieved using any of the 
following techniques: 
a. For larger buildings, particularly buildings that fill an entire block, using 
engaging ground-level design and providing multiple pedestrian entry 
points along the frontage. 
b. Avoid large, monolithic buildings by dividing or articulating the facade to 
avoid monolithic appearance. 
c. Incorporating architectural elements such as recessed or protruding 
entrances, balconies, bay windows, brick patterns, etc. 
d. Ensuring the pattern or repetition of any chosen architectural feature, 
recess, projection, or break is uniform and consistent. 
F. Front Facades, Entrances and Building Orientation 
1. Ground-floor facades facing the street shall include at least 50% transparent 
windows and doors. Transparency shall be evenly distributed to avoid long 
stretches of blank wall. 
2. The orientation and placement of buildings shall contribute to a sense of enclosure 
and defined space by maintaining a consistent building frontage along a primary 
street. 
a. Buildings may be set further back to incorporate public spaces, plazas and 
parks in a way that centralizes and activates those spaces, such as by 
having windows and entrances facing into the public spaces. 
b. The front facade of new construction shall orient towards the street by 
having the building's main entrance, primary windows, and facade details 
facing the street. 
c. For corner lots, entrances could be oriented at a 45-degree angle to 
emphasize the corner and encourage pedestrian access from both the 
principal street and secondary street. 
G. Public Transportation 
1. Existing and future public transportation stops and stations shall be considered in 
the layout of new developments to ensure convenient transit access and use. 

55 
 
55 
 
2. The location of entrances, walkways, and pedestrian amenities shall allow for 
convenient, safe, and comfortable pedestrian access from a public transportation 
stop or station to the site. 
H. Pedestrian Amenities 
1. Sidewalk café and seating areas are encouraged and shall maintain at least five 
feet of unobstructed walking space along sidewalks. 
2. Pedestrian amenities such as benches, lighting, and trash and recycling receptacles 
shall be conveniently placed together. 
3. Overhead weather protection—such as awnings, overhangs, and arcades—are 
encouraged and shall be appropriately located to clearly define building entries and 
orient towards pedestrian areas. Where possible, overhead weather protection 
should be located to provide shelter for waiting transit users. 
I. Off-Street Parking 
1. Garages, parking spaces, and driveways shall be designed to be as discreet as 
possible and limit impediments to the walking environment. Garage openings shall 
be placed where it will have the least disruption to pedestrian walkways and limit 
potential conflict points between car traffic and pedestrians. 
2. Buildings shall be arranged on the site such that garages shall be placed behind 
buildings whenever possible, and parking entrances shall be accessible via 
secondary streets. 
3. Whenever garages face a primary street, they shall be lined by buildings that screen 
the parking structure from view and incorporate other uses such as residential, 
retail, and other active uses. 
4. Garages that are visible from a public street shall avoid blank walls. These garages 
shall be mitigated using landscaping or public art or shall incorporate architectural 
design that complements the primary structure through the use of facade 
articulation, architectural elements, color, and elements that break up the mass of 
the garage. Fabric screening of parking garages is discouraged. 
J. Bicycle Parking Standards. Bicycle parking shall conform to the standards of Section 600-
1609.  
K. Public Civic Space Standards 
1. Except for public civic spaces along Penn Street, perpendicular or angled parking 
spaces shall not abut any public civic space; however, parallel on-street parking 
spaces may abut any public civic space. 
2. The public civic space shall be convenient and accessible by sidewalk or internal 
pedestrian path. 
3. The public civic space shall be located next to public streets or front facades of 
buildings or located between buildings. 
4. An operations and maintenance plan, ensuring that all landscaping, natural features 
and constructs are maintained in perpetuity and are replaced if they can no longer 
be feasibly maintained, shall be prepared for the public civic space area and 
submitted to the City for approval. 
5. A minimum of 20%, but no more than 70%, of the public civic space shall be 
landscaped with a combination of trees, shrubs, perennials, grasses, and 
groundcovers to provide year-round visual interest and color. A minimum of one (1) 
shade tree shall be provided per 250 square feet of public civic space. 

56 
 
56 
 
6. The public civic space shall not be used for parking, loading, or vehicular access, 
except emergency and maintenance vehicular access. 
7. The paved surface area shall be composed of high-quality, durable paving 
materials, such as unit pavers, or concrete. 
8. Pedestrian-scale lighting shall be provided within the public civic space and along 
sidewalks or internal pedestrian pathways connecting to the public civic space. 
9. The public civic space shall be entirely open to the air and no portion of a building, 
other than a balcony, shall project over the public gathering space. 
10. Trash and recycling containers shall be distributed throughout the public civic space 
to discourage littering. One (1) trash and one (1) recycling container shall be 
provided for every 600 square feet of public civic space. 
11. Seating spaces shall be provided throughout the public civic space. One (1) seating 
space shall be provided for every 250 square feet of public civic space. 
12. The applicant shall have an agreement with the City for public access to the public 
civic space. 
L. Landscaping and Screening Standards 
1. Landscaping 
a. Landscaping, such as ornamental trees, flowering shrubs, and perennials, 
shall be used to define public entrances and to soften walls and fences. 
b. Landscaping shall be designed to promote sustainable practices of on-site 
water management and infiltration such as through the use of native plants, 
swales, and filter strips. 
1. If any invasive species are found on the site, they shall be handled in 
accordance with the provisions of Chapter 317: Invasive Plants and 
Noxious Weeds. 
2. All landscaping shall conform to plant material listed in Section 515-
509 of SALDO or be approved by the City of Reading Arborist and 
Planning Commission. 
c. Landscaping shall seek to incorporate existing natural features, especially 
large, existing trees, where possible. 
d. Tree preservation and protection strategies, such as fencing and other 
structures, shall be employed to preserve existing trees during 
construction. 
e. Where commercial developments, mixed-use developments, or residential 
structures of more than four units abut properties zoned for single-, two-, or 
three-family dwellings, required buffers (on the property of the commercial, 
mixed-use, or residential structure of more than four units) shall be 
sufficiently landscaped to provide a year-round visual screen along those 
boundary lines according to the standards of Section 600-1401 of Zoning. 
2. Street Trees 
a. Street trees shall be planted within tree lawns along all public rights-of-way. 
A minimum of one large tree shall be planted for every development site, 
and street trees shall be planted at intervals of no more than 30 feet on-
center, on average. Where overhead utilities exist, one small tree must be 
planted every 20 feet on-center, on average.

57 
 
b. The type of street trees selected shall be in accordance with the standards 
of Section 515-509 of SALDO. 
3. Parking Lot Landscaping. All surface lots must be landscaped in accordance with 
Section 600-1602.K.  
4. Screening. Outdoor storage, waste disposal storage and mechanical and electrical 
equipment shall be screened according to the standards of Section 600-1402 of 
Zoning.

Part 16:  Off-Street Parking and Loading 
§600-1601: General Application Criteria 
(A) Application:  Off-street parking and/or loading facilities shall be located, designed, 
constructed and maintained in accordance with the provisions of this Zoning Ordinance.  
These facilities shall be provided whenever the following actions occur: 
(1) A new building has been constructed or a new use has been established in 
accordance with the provisions of this Zoning Ordnance. 
(2) The use of the building or property has been changed, altered or expanded, which 
requires additional off-street parking spaces to accommodate the use. 
(3) An off-street parking facility shall include the driveways, access drives, access isles, 
parking spaces and loading spaces. 
(B) Land Use Compliance:  Parking and loading areas shall be a permitted use as follows: 
(1) Principal Use:  The following off-street parking area and/or off-street loading area 
requirements shall apply if the off-street parking and/or off-street loading area is 
classified as the principal use on the property: 
(a) An off-street parking area containing ten (10) or more parking spaces, which 
are designated as the principal use shall only be permitted provided that such 
use is permitted within the underlying zoning district.  All such off-street 
parking areas shall comply with the lot area, dimensional and coverage 
requirements, as specified by the underlying zoning district. 
(b) An off-street parking area containing less than ten (10) parking spaces, which 
are designated as the principal use shall only be permitted provided that such 
use is permitted within the underlying zoning district.  All such off-street 
parking areas shall comply with: the minimum lot area requirement; the 
dimensional setback requirements may be reduced to three (3) feet from all 
property lines; and the maximum impervious coverage may be increased to 
95 percent of the lot area. 
(c) An off-street loading area designated as the principal use shall only be 
permitted provided that such use is permitted within the underlying zoning 
district.  All such off-street loading areas shall comply with the lot area, 
dimensional and coverage requirements, as specified by the underlying 
zoning district. 
(2) Accessory Use:   An off-street parking area and/or off-street loading area may be 
permitted as an accessory use to a compatible principal use, subject to the provisions 
specified by this Zoning Ordinance. 
(3) Satellite Parking Spaces:  Off-street parking spaces may be established or credited 
at a conforming off-site or off-premises location, subject to the provision specified by 
this Zoning Ordinance. 
(4) On-Street Spaces:  Unless otherwise permitted by the City of Reading, all on-street 
parking spaces and/or on-street loading spaces shall not be counted or credited as a 

60 
 
60 
 
required parking or loading space.  All such spaces shall comply with the provisions 
established by the City of Reading Code.  
(C) Location:  The following provisions shall apply to the location of off-street parking and/or 
loading areas: 
(1) Unless otherwise permitted, off-street parking and loading spaces shall be located on 
the same lot as the principal use.  
(2) All parking and loading facilities shall be arranged and marked to provide safe and 
orderly movement without disrupting traffic or moving other vehicles. 
(3) Unless otherwise permitted by the Public Works Department, the design of parking 
areas for all uses shall be such to prevent the back-up of vehicles onto a public street 
and that no portion of any parked vehicle extends over any property line. 
§600-1602: Design and Construction Standards 
(A) Accessibility:  All off-street parking and loading spaces shall have access to a public street 
by means of an access lane, driveway, alley or road that complies with the provisions 
specified by Chapters 515 and 600 of the Code. 
(B) Size:  Off-street parking and loading spaces shall comply with the following criteria: 
(1) Off-Street Parking Spaces:  The spaces shall have a rectangular shape that complies 
with following dimensional requirements: 
(a) Within an off-street parking lot, each parking space for motor vehicles (except 
those spaces dedicated for use by handicapped persons) shall have a 
minimum rectangular area of 162 square feet, which shall have a minimum 
width of 9 feet and a minimum length of 18 feet have a minimum area of 162 
square feet with a minimum width of 9 feet and a minimum length of 18 feet.  
Angled spaces will need to be longer in order to achieve the rectangular shape 
with the minimum dimensions. 
(b) Where parallel parking is permitted, the parking spaces shall be delineated by 
painted lines and shall have a minimum width of 8 feet, as measured from the 
curb or cartway edge and a minimum length of 20 feet, as measured along 
the curb or cartway edge. 
(2) Off-Street Loading Spaces:  The spaces shall have a rectangular shape that complies 
with following dimensional requirements: 
(a) Off-street loading spaces with a length of sixty (60) feet, width of fourteen (14) 
feet, and clearance height of fifteen (15) feet for commercial and industrial 
uses. 
(b) Off-street loading spaces with a length of forty-five (45) feet, width of twelve 
(12) feet, and clearance height of twelve (12) feet may be permitted for 
commercial, institutional and light industrial uses provided the landowner or 
applicant can demonstrate that the use will not require larger trucks to access 
the site for loading and unloading activities.  
(C) Grade:    The off-street parking and loading areas shall be properly graded to provide 
convenient vehicular access and proper drainage and shall be maintained in usable

(3) Stormwater Management Plan:  If the combination of site improvements exceeds

prepared and submitted with the Land Development Plan. 
(4) Earth Disturbance Activities:  If the combination of site improvements exceeds 5,000 
square feet of earth disturbance activities, an Erosion and Sedimentation Control Plan 
shall be prepared and submitted with the Land Development Plan. 
(5) Other Permits:  If the proposed site improvements encroach or traverse a street right-
of-way for use and occupancy, a Highway Occupancy Permit (HOP), Driveway Permit 
and/or Utility Permit may be required via the Pennsylvania Department of 
Transportation and/or City of Reading.

§600-1605: Emergency Management and Response Standards 
(A) External Access Points:  All means of ingress and/or egress shall be located, designed and 
constructed in order to accommodate traffic in a safe and efficient manner.  The landowner 
or developer shall be responsible for the purchase and installation of any traffic control 
devices as well as the design and construction of additional travel lanes or auxiliary lanes, 
which may be required by the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation or City of Reading. 
(B) Internal Access Drives and Lanes: The width of aisles within the off-street parking facilities 
shall comply with the minimum requirements established under Part 16 of this Zoning 
Ordinance.  All dead-end parking lots shall be designed to provide sufficient back-up area for 
all end spaces. 
(C) Emergency Access:  All off-street parking and loading areas shall be designed to consider 
the provisions established under Section 600-906 of this Zoning Ordinance. 
(D) Fire Lanes:  If required by the Fire and Rescue Department or by the Building and Trades 
Department, a fire lane shall be established within the property containing the off-street 
parking area and/or loading area. 
(E) Emergency Response and Accessibility:  The internal access lanes or aisles serving the off-
street parking area and/or loading area shall be subject to the review and approval of the Fire 
and Rescue Department. 
(F) Sight Distance:  The provisions established under Section 600-1303 of the Zoning Ordinance 
and Section 515-502.F of the Subdivision and Land Development Ordinance shall apply to 
the specifications for sight distance, as required by the Pennsylvania Department of 
Transportation or by the City of Reading Code. 
(G) Clear Sight Triangles:  The provisions established under the provisions established under 
Section 600-1303 of the Zoning Ordinance and Section 515-502.F of the Subdivision and 
Land Development Ordinance shall apply to the specification for clear sight triangles. 
§600-1606: Common or Shared Parking Facilities 
(A) Application:  Common or shared parking facilities or areas may be permitted for two (2) or 
more permitted uses, subject to the following requirements: 
(1) The permitted uses are adjacent or adjoining to one another.

(B) Site Accessibility:  The following documents shall be established and recorded:

§600-1608: Electric Vehicle Charging Stations (EVCS) 
(A) Electric Vehicle Charging Stations (EVCS) shall be permitted as an accessory or subordinate 
use by right within all zoning districts, subject to the following provision: 
(1) The EVCS shall be located within a building (principal or accessory) occupied by the 
principal use or within the parking or loading area for the principal use. 
(2) The EVCS shall be located at least ten (10) feet from the street right-of-way line and 
all other property lines. 
(3) Parking spaces containing an EVCS shall count towards the minimum required 
number of parking spaces for the principal use(s) contained on the property. 
(4) The EVCS may be leased or utilized to other users during non-operational hours 
designated by the principal use. 
(5) The EVCS shall be clearly marked or identified with signage. 
(6) The EVCS shall be outfitted with a standard EVCS connection or plug-in. 
(7) The EVCS spaces shall not exceed 125 percent of the total number of off-street 
parking or loading spaces required for the proposed use. 
(B) A decommissioning plan and agreement shall be established and endorsed as part of the 
zoning permit for the EVCS. 
§600-1609: Non-Motorized Vehicle Stations (NMVS) 
(A) Non-Motorized Vehicle Stations (ENMVS) shall be permitted as an accessory or subordinate 
use by right within all zoning districts, subject to the following provision: 
(1) The NMVS may be located within a building (principal or accessory) occupied by the 
principal use or within the parking area for the principal use.  Alternative locations 
shall be subject to the review of the Zoning Office. 
(2) The NMVS shall be located at least ten (10) feet from the street right-of-way line and 
all other property lines. 
(3) The NMVS may be located on a space dedicated or reserved for an off-street parking 
provided that 90 percent of the remaining parking spaces remain available for the 
principal use(s) contained on the property. 
(4) The NMVS may be leased or utilized to other users during non-operational hours 
designated by the principal use. 
(5) The NMVS shall be clearly marked or identified with signage. 
(B) A decommissioning plan and agreement shall be established and endorsed as part of the 
zoning permit for the NMVS.

(5) Unless otherwise permitted, the maximum height of the garage shall be sixty (60) feet,

the garage is located.

WHEREAS, it is in the best interests of the residents of the City of Reading to 
have a Department of Public Health.

Plain English Explanation:  
This ballot question asks the City’s voters to decide whether Article VI of the 
City of Reading’s Home Rule Charter should be amended to add Section 608, 
which would create a Department of Public Health within the City of Reading. 
Specifically, Section 608 would be titled “Department of Health” and would 
read: “There shall be a Department of Public Health with the authority to

collaborate with community partners and which will be fully operational within 
two years.” A “yes” vote means that you support Section 608 being added to 
the Home Rule Charter. A “no” vote means that you do not support Section 
608 being added to the Home Rule Charter. If a majority of the City’s electors 
voting in this election vote “yes” for this question, then Section 608 will be 
added to the Home Rule Charter. If a majority of the City’s electors voting in 
this election vote “no” for this question, then Section 608 will not be added to 
the Home Rule Charter. 
  
SECTION 2.  This ordinance shall become effective ten (10) days after 
its adoption, in accordance with Section 221 of the City of Reading 
Home Rule Charter.

AN ORDINANCE TO AMEND THE PREAMBLE TO THE HOME RULE 
CHARTER TO ESTABLISH AS THE GOAL OF THE HOME RULE 
CHARTER THE PRESERVATION AND PROTECTION OF NATURAL 
RESOURCES AND THE RIGHTS OF PEOPLE TO CLEAN AIR, PURE 
WATER AND THE PRESERVATION OF NATURAL RESOURCES. 
 
WHEREAS, the Pennsylvania Constitution, in Article I, Section 27 establishes 
the right of people to have clean air, pure water and the preservation of the 
natural, scenic, historic and esthetic values of the environment; 
 
WHEREAS, including a similar statement in the Preamble to the City of 
Reading Home Rule Charter will commit the City of Reading to the 
preservation and protection of the natural resources of the City of Reading 
and the rights of the people of the City of Reading to have clean air, pure 
water and the preservation of natural resources. 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, THE COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF READING HEREBY 
ORDAINS AS FOLLOWS: 
 
SECTION 1.  Directing the Berks County Board of Elections to place the 
following referendum question to voters of the City of Reading on the 2026 
general election ballot: 
 
Shall the preamble to the Home Rule Charter be amended to include a 
statement about the preservation and protection of natural resources and the 
rights of people to clean air, pure water, and the preservation of natural 
resources?  
 
Plain English Explanation:  
This ballot questions asks the City’s voters to decide whether the preamble to 
the City of Reading’s Home Rule Charter should be amended to include a 
statement about the preservation and protection of natural resources and the 
rights of people to clean air, pure water, and the preservation of natural 
resources. Specifically, the addition to the preamble would read: “Whereas, 

103 
 
103 
 
Article 1, Section 27 of the Pennsylvania Constitution provides that the people 
have a right to clean air, pure waters and the preservation of the natural, 
scenic, historic and esthetic values of the environment. Pennsylvania’s public 
natural resources are the common property of all the people, including 
generations yet to come. As a trustee of these resources, the Commonwealth 
shall conserve and maintain them for the benefit of all people. The 
government of the City of Reading shall commit to preserving and protecting 
these natural resources and the rights of people to clean air, pure water and 
preserving its natural resources.” A “yes” vote means that you are in favor of 
this statement being added to the Home Rule Charter’s preamble. A “no” vote 
means that you are not in favor of this statement being added to the Home 
Rule Charter’s preamble. If a majority of the City’s voters voting in this 
election vote “yes” for this question, then this statement regarding the 
preservation and protection of natural resources will be added to the Home 
Rule Charter’s preamble. If a majority of the City’s voters voting in this 
election vote “no” for this question, then this statement regarding the 
preservation and protection of natural resources will not be added to the 
Home Rule Charter’s preamble 
  
SECTION 2.  This ordinance shall become effective ten (10) days after its 
adoption, in accordance with Section 221 of the City of Reading Home Rule 
Charter.
