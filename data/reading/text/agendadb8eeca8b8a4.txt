SUMMARY 
October 19, 2026 
5 pm 
Council Chambers / Hybrid Meeting 
 
COUNCIL MEMBERS PRESENT: 
D. Reed, M. Ventura, C. Miller, R. Baker – in person and J. Baez - virtually 
 
OTHERS PRESENT:  
J. Famiglietti, M. Gombar, L. Kelleher, J. Gombach, E. Moran, E. Bradley, S. Brown, K. Talbot, 
H. Holthaus (Spotlight) – in person and M. Smith, M. Oppenheimer, BCTV, WFMZ - virtually  
 
The meeting was called to order at 5:05 pm by Ms. Reed.  She stated that tonight Council will 
begin their review of the proposed 2027 budget and she asked Finance Chair Ventura to 
conduct the meeting. 
 
Ms. Ventura stated that the proposed draft budget is available for public review on the City’s 
website. She encouraged the public to review this document.   She asked Mr. Famiglietti to 
begin his presentation.   
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that this year’s budget process was conducted in a new way.  This year 
the budget review process started in April with the review of actual expenses between 2022 
and 2026 (year to date), which enabled reductions. He added that the expenses were 
reclassified which matches the type of expense with the proper category, which will create 
data for each line item and allow data driven decisions. 
 
• Mayor’s Office 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that this office is staffed by one elected official and three management 
level staff members.  The staff members are provided with a 3.5% increase, the same afforded 
to all management level employees and both AFSCME units. He stated that the summer intern 
program was separated into its own category to improve clarity and expenses for part-time 
wages, uniforms and social security are included.

Mr. Famiglietti stated that Advertising is for miscellaneous media and outreach services. 
Contrcated Services covers catering, photography, live streaming and the Youth Violence 
website - $9,500 for 2027. Consulting Services was reduced to $60,00 ($100,000 in 2026) for 
McNees advocacy services. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that conferences, training and travel expenses were combined into one 
line item, resulting in a $2,000 budget reduction - $7,000 to $5,000. The $50 assigned to Dues 
and Subscriptions provides the City with a subscription to Sam’s Club. 
 
Ms. Reed asked Mr. Famiglietti for a further breakdown in the actual expenses for conferences 
– expenses for education, lodging and travel. She noted that the Council budget does not cover 
lodging or travel expenses, it only covers the education expense. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that lodging and travel can be reimbursed through an approval process. 
 
The mayor explained that Council is not covered for lodging and travel expenses because they 
are only part-time; however, as the mayor and staff are fulltime those expenses are covered.  
 
In response to a question from Ms. Reed, Mr. Famiglietti stated that the uniform expense 
shown on this form covers only the summer interns. She inquired about shirts and jackets 
worn by various members of administrative staff.  Mr. Famiglietti explained that the expense 
for City clothing is not budgeted but it is paid from the City budget when the order is placed. 
 
Ms. Reed stated that Mr. Nunez has paid for his own shirts and jacket.  
 
In response to a question from Mr. Baker, Mr. Famiglietti stated that the $6,500 for advertising 
in 2027 ($500 in 2026) was caused by the recategorization of expenses. Mr. Baker requested 
additional detail on these expenses. 
 
Mr. Baker also requested additional detail on conferences and travel as that expense jumped 
from $500 to $4,500.  
 
In response to a question from Mr. Baker, Ms. Bradley stated that the uniform expense of 
$4,000 will provide 2-3 work shirts for 77 students so they do not need to wear the same shirt 
every workday. 
 
The mayor stated that the summer intern expense was approved by Council last year.  He also 
stated that the expense for conferences and training has grown due to the need for extended 
engagement and involvement, noting his frequent trips to Harrisburg to advocate for the City 
and to identify new approaches at conferences. 
 
Mr. Miller suggested advising Council on the outcomes from conferences and trips to advocate 
for the City so everyone is on the same page. 

3 
Mr. Miller recalled that when questioned at a prior meeting about the payments to DaBrian for 
the youth violence website the administration stated that no changes or updates occurred.  Mr. 
Famiglietti stated that the payment was made due to contractual requirements. He added that 
all payments may not have been made to DaBrian but DaBrian is under contract with the City. 
 
Mr. Gombach added that the City has taken steps toward building out an improved youth 
violence program. He stated that at the October 12th meeting Council approved a contract for a 
consultant to build a strategic plan for youth violence, which hopefully will be covered by a 
PCCD grant. 
 
Ms. Reed inquired if the travel policy prohibited lodging expenses within a specific distance 
and the policy on the use of a City vehicle. She noted that at times in the past, city vehicles 
were used inappropriately for personal trips. 
 
Mr. Gombach stated that the travel policy was revised and he will share the revised policies. 
He explained that various employees are permitted to use City vehicles for certain trips. He 
added that the mayor is reimbursed for his travel expenses as he is not permitted to use a City 
vehicle.  
 
The mayor stated that the policy permits him to obtain reimbursement for travel expenses 
associated with his personal vehicle, when in the past prior mayors were permitted to use a 
City vehicle.  (Note: that practice was discontinued by the McMahon, Spencer and Scott 
administrations) 
 
Ms. Reed stated that Council members do not file for reimbursements when travel requires 
them to use their personal vehicles due to their commitment to public service, even though 
they receive a small monthly stipend.   
 
The mayor stated that due to his low salary, he needs to seek reimbursement for travel 
expenses.  He added that others in management positions are a better off financially and are 
permitted to use a City vehicle.   
 
Ms. Reed requested a line-by-line breakdown of all approved reimbursements for mileage and 
other expenses. 
 
The mayor stated that he only seeks reimbursements for his travels to Allentown and 
Harrisburg.  He added that he does not currently seek reimbursement for all City-related 
travel in his personal car; however, that may have to change due to due to the pressure his 
wife is exerting. He again stated that his ability to seek reimbursement is because he is a full-
time official.  
 
Ms. Ventura stated that the back-and-forth conversation is unproductive. 
 

4 
As the back and forth continued, Mr. Baker repeatedly called for a point of order. 
 
Mr. Baez stressed the need for conversations between officials to be respectful, especially 
during budget deliberations. He suggested placing more focus on larger allocations, rather 
than small $400 and $500 expenditures. 
 
In response to a question from Ms. Ventura, Mr. Famiglietti stated that he plans to review the 
personnel during the budget review for each department and office. 
 
Ms. Reed stated that previous administrations only had two staff positions in the mayor’s 
office and the mayor’s budget now has two assistants and a chief of staff. She questioned the 
responsibilities of all three. 
 
The mayor explained that the staff members respond to emails and in-office constituents’ 
needs along with assisting with preparation for meetings, gatherings and outreach.  Ms. 
Bradley further explained that one assistant handles scheduling and constituent services via 
triaging based on the need or the sensitivity. 
 
The mayor stated that all mayors for the past 20 years have had three staff positions – from 
McMahon to Scott. Ms. Reed stated that she will verify that information. (Note: the first mayor to 
have three staff members was Spencer and that practice was continued by the Scott and Moran 
administrations) 
 
Mr. Baker questioned how the current staffing of the mayor’s office compares with the staffing 
in the mayor’s office in other 3rd-class cities. Ms. Bradley expressed the belief that the volume 
of constituent traffic justifies the need for three staff members in the mayor’s office, noting that 
they are already stretched thin. 
 
In response to a question from Mr. Baker, Mr. Famiglietti stated that the 3.5% increase is the 
standard COLA which is also being provided to both AFSCME collective bargaining units. 
 
• City Council/City Clerk 
Mr. Famiglietti explained that he will be doing each budget presentation, as he did the ground 
work to reduce expenses and work with each area to reach a compromise.  
 
Mr. Famiglietti explained the wages for the three full-time employees is increased by 3.5% as 
previously explained.  The budget also contains the salaries for the seven part-time councilors.  
The advertising budget will hold at $14,000 with 21st Century Media, which is the required 
newspaper ads. He added that he and Ms. Kelleher discussed a reduction but decided to retain 
the amount budgeted for 2026 due to some fluctuations. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that the Codified Ordinance line item is increasing to $20,000 (from 
$15,000) due to the increase in the number of amendments made to the Code over the past 

5 
year, as that activity is expected to continue.  He added that this week a $5,000 inter-office 
transfer was made to cover Code amendment costs expected for the remainder of 2026. He 
added that the line items for subscriptions and dues were merged in the 2027 budget to 
eliminate redundancy.   
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that due to the lag in the migration to the Tyler records retention 
package (TCM), the DocStar expense will need to remain in place for 2027. The City Clerk’s 
office is the first office moving into the new TCM system. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that all budgets will recategorize the miscellaneous expense line item. 
 
Ms. Reed requested a breakout of the travel expenses in this budget and all others.  
 
The mayor asked to be excused to attend the opening of the Keep PA Beautiful Conference at 
the DoubleTree. He invited others to attend. 
 
• Auditor 
The Auditor’s draft budget was prepared.   Review and potential amendments will occur after 
an interim auditor is appointed.  
 
• Managing Director’s Office 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that the three positions also reflect the across the board 3.5% increase. 
Contracted Services will be retained at $5,000 if that need arises.  Contracted Services covers 
the CELG, GRCA and PML memberships and Crown Castle fiberoptic services.  He explained 
that the Media Supplies line item provides funding required by the Communications 
Coordinator, noting that these expenses have been standard year over year.  
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that the Conferences and Training line item increased by $3,000 to 
$11,000 – additional detail will be provided on the planned conferences during 2027. He 
explained that another reclassified area is office supplies and General Plant which contains 
supplies that do not qualify as standard office supplies but supplies that are needed for safety 
or other reasons.  
 
Mr. Baker questioned why conferences and training will be increased to $11,000 when only 
$600 was used before.  Mr. Famiglietti explained that the conferences and travel line items 
were merged, with $8,000 eliminated from travel which represents an overall savings. He 
added that the budget represents 2026 actual expenses as of mid-September so the PML 
expenses will not be reflected until those expenses are processed.  
 
Mr. Gombach noted that plans to attend conferences are sometimes disrupted by unexpected 
events. 
 

6 
In response to a question from Ms. Reed, Mr. Famiglietti explained that the Media Supplies are 
standard services required by the Communications Coordinator for specific events.  
 
Mr. Famiglietti explained in the Downtown Commercial Corridor  line items, the largest 
expense is for downtown events. He added that the reclassification allowed the reduction of 
expenses for downtown events, contracted services and consulting services.  
 
In response to a question from Mr. Miller, Mr. Famiglietti stated that while the Shade Tree 
Commission budget handles large projects in the downtown, Edwards Landscaping handles 
the plantings for the downtown area. 
 
Mr. Baez arrived in Council Chambers and disconnected from Zoom at this time. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti explained that the majority of the Human Relations Commission Budget 
(HRC) expenses come from a variety of grants. The budget shown reflects the General Fund 
portion of the HRC budget. The grant-related funding is located within the Agency fund 
which will be reviewed at a future meeting. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that the Executive Director, Intake Specialist and Clerk position salaries 
are covered by the General Fund. The Outreach and Education position is covered by a grant 
with a small the required match is included in the General Fund budget. Uniforms (City 
labeled shirts and jackets) are included at $1,000. 
 
• Debt Service – Non Departmental 
Mr. Famiglietti noted the need to add the debt expense of approximately $500,000 for the 2012 
and 2015 RRA Lease Rev note principal and interest payments. The last payment for the 2012 
bond will occur in 2027 and the 2015 bond will be paid off in 2033. He then reviewed the 
expiration dates for the remaining bond payments.  The newest bond is for the sustainability 
project approved in 2024 which expires in 2044.  
 
In response to a question from Mr. Baker, Mr. Famiglietti stated that he is unaware of any 
plans to take on new bond debt. 
 
• Pension 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that the City has four retirement plans – pensions for the Officers & 
Employees, Police and Fire retirees and there is a 401K (defined contribution plan) for new 
hires. He explained that the City’s pension expense is split out over all department, division 
and office budgets.  The 401K started in 2022 and the City’s contribution begins when 
employees reach their third anniversary. The plans currently have the following number of 
retirees: 
 
 Officers & Employees – 264  
Police – 355 

7 
Fire – 169 
 
There are 120 employees participating in the 401K.  He added that the 401K was required by 
the Act 47 Exit Plan with the goal to shift away from the pension obligation as time moves 
forward. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that the four plans together have investment an balance of 
$353,402,954.02 (total for all three) and are funded at the following levels: 
 
Officers & Employees – 102.1% 
Police – 83% 
Fire – 87% 
401K – 80% 
 
Mr. Famiglietti reported that the City received notification in the MMO (Mandatory Municipal 
Obligation) from the State Auditor’s Office that the City has a distress score of one, which is 
the lowest available, which is excellent. The State approved a 5% increase for police and fire 
retirees, which will be initially paid by the City and reimbursed by the State. The was the first 
COLA increase since the early 1980s. 
 
In response to a question from Mr. Baker, Ms. Kelleher stated that the City made milestone 
improvements to the pension plans during Act 47.  Prior to Act 47, the City was unable to 
make the required pension contributions annually.  At one point in time the City’s pension 
plans were a percentage point away from qualifying as distressed which would have allowed 
the City to implement a commuter tax to correct that problem. 
 
There were no further questions. 
 
Mr. Baker moved, seconded by Mr. Miller, to adjourn at 6:22 pm. 
Respectfully Submitted by 
Linda A. Kelleher, CMC, City Clerk
