PUBLIC COMMENT 
Council President Reed stated that there are 12 citizens registered to address Council this 
evening on 3 non-agenda matters and  nine on agenda matters. She read the public 
speaking rules adopted by ordinance in City Code Section 209. She reminded the audience 
of the need to either turn their cell phones off or to silence them. 
 
Alex Darlington, Kutztown Rd, Temple, stated that during 2026 he had seven false 
citations out of ten citations issued by the Parking Authority while using the on-street 
parking meter mobile app and the app in the DoubleTree hotel.   He stated that he has 
appealed each ticket.  The appeals showed that 70% of the tickets were erroneously issued. 

2 
 
For comparison he reviewed the study performed in Chicago where 3.6M tickets were 
issued and 13.2% were erroneously issued. Although Chicago and Reading are vastly 
different cities, the study does provide a rough tally of what a total failure rate should be.  
The 70% rate by the Reading Parking Authority is over five times the failure rate of Chicago 
and points to a deep systemic problem with the local Authority. This problem damages 
residents and visitors to the city. He distributed copies of the tickets and one copy of the 
study. (Scanned and distributed on Tuesday October 27th.) He stressed the need for this problem 
to be resolved. 
 
Nigel Walker, N. 4th St, stated that she is a 33-year resident in Centre Park and the Centre 
Park Historic District is the heart of the neighborhoods identify. The architecture, 
neighborhood clean-ups and regular events make the neighborhood thrive and fosters 
social cohesiveness for all people of different backgrounds.  While the historic district 
anchors the neighborhood, active stewardship is required through the enforcement of the 
Historic District ordinance. The events draw in visitors from inside and outside the City to 
view the breathtaking architecture, intricate stonework and features. These events show all 
that Reading is a great place to live and raise a family. 
 
Mike Lauter, of Centre Ave., expressed the belief that the administration’s plan to eliminate 
the Historic Preservation Specialist is misplaced as this position requires specific 
qualifications and knowledge that Community Development staff members lack. He stated 
that historic districts are a partnership between the City and historic district residents with 
each side bearing responsibility and gaining benefits. The City is responsible for enforcing 
the requirements in the Historic District ordinance, the appointment of qualified HARB 
members, and appointing qualified administrative staff to administer the terms in the 
ordinance.  He added that those living in historic districts are responsible for adhering to 
the terms of the ordinance, performing outreach and engagement and hosting events. The 
returns to both are increased property values, along with a safer, cleaner neighborhood. He 
stated that the fundraising events allow the Centre Park District to contribute 
approximately $90K to 20 public property projects. He implored the City to retain the 
Historic Preservation Specialist and making the required improvements in the Historic 
District Ordinance and ensure its compliance with the State Act. He suggested a meeting 
with Council, the administration and representation from the Centre Park Historic District 
to move this issue forward.  
 
Tania Perez, Madison Ave, stated that she is a 20-year Centre Park resident.  She expressed 
the belief that the conditions in Centre Park are worsening due to drug activities, noise and 
property maintenance problems.  The once beautiful neighborhood, no longer feels like the 
safe space it was in the past.   
 
Carolyn Bazik, no address listed, stated that she has lived in Centre Park for 29 years and 

3 
 
she strongly opposes the proposed elimination of the Historic Preservation Specialist and 
that will severely weaken the protections afforded to historic districts.  She noted that 
historic preservation is not a budgetary decision; it’s a legal responsibility as per City Code 
Chapter 295.  Chapter 295 requires a preservation officer who is charged with the 
responsibility to administer and enforce this chapter.  She stated that historic districts are 
among the City’s greatest assets. She asked the City to correct the Historic District

that dismantling this office is not the answer. 
 
Michael Wright, no address listed, stated that he is a Centre Park resident and serves on 
the Centre Park Board.  Prior to moving to Reading, he lived in an Allentown historic 
district.  When moving to Berks County, he discovered the Centre Park area due to its 
character, history and care about preserving all. He stated that preservation is about 
protecting what makes Reading distinctive.  He noted the money historic district residents 
invest into their homes. He stated that upholding the terms of the Historic District 
ordinance strengthens neighborhoods, creates identity and encourages investment.  He 
agreed with the need to improve the ordinance, then enforce it through the Historic 
Preservation Specialist. He agreed with the application to retain a consultant to assist with 
the correction of the ordinance, as it shows that the City is taking this input seriously. He 
stated that the new ordinance should make preservation clearer, assessable and consistent 
with a Preservation Specialist who will provides guardrails to protect the investment made 
into these neighborhoods. He added that historic preservation turns neighborhoods into 
homes.   
 
Alexa Freyman, no address listed, stated that she is a local historian, preservation advocate 
and the chair of the Pagoda Foundation. She stated that the City’s recent work on the

character of homes. She asked Council and the administration to prioritize the preservation 
system now and build it on solid ground. She suggested building the ordinance around the 
1961 Historic District Act , reinstate the Historic Preservation Specialist position and fill it 
with qualified personal.

Consent Agenda 
The Consent Agenda is designed to provide efficient approval of non-controversial legislation that does not 
require discussion/debate by giving approval via acclimation when the meeting agenda is approved.  The 
President of Council will call Council’s attention to the list of Consent Agenda legislation at the meeting 
before action is taken, which allows Council to remove a piece of legislation for separate consideration.

REPORT FROM DEPT. DIRECTORS, BOARDS, AUTHORITIES, &  
COMMISSIONS  
Anna Miller, Animal Control Board Chair, stated that this is the board’s first update since 2024. 
She stated that currently there is only one permit issued for owning more than six dogs and/or 
cats. These permits exist for a three-year period and can be renewed as long as the animals are 
vaccinated and cared for.  Currently there are no Exotic Pet permits issued; however, there are 
multiple properties that are keeping chickens, ducks, and goats and no permit applications 
have been submitted.  She added that chicken and goat complaints continue to rise. She stated 
that the City updated the Animal Chapter of the Code to address aggressive animals that is 
consistent with the State regulations. She stated that the board created Exotic Pet and Pet 
Maintenance flyers. 
 
Ms. Miller stated that the ARL provides this detail for 2025: 
• 1504 intakes 
• 877 dispatches – 530 were related to animal control 
• 197 cruelty reports 
• 136 stray cats returned to the field after being trapped, neutered, chipped and 
vaccinated (TNR) 
• 14 wellness inspections 
 
Council President Reed thanked Ms. Miller and the Board for the work being performed to 
protect animals.  She asked Mr. Gombach to be sure that the flyers Ms. Miller described are 
available on the website. 
 
In response to a question from Councilor Campos, Ms. Miller stated that she does not have 
comparable data for 2024 or 2026. She added that TNRs have been greatly increased during 
2026.
