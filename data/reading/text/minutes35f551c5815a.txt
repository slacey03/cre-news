Councilor Miller read the following letter submitted by Radarra McLendon: 
 
Dear President and Members of Reading City Council, 
 
I want to start by thanking Melissa, Jamie, Rafael, and Donna. Thank you for sitting 
down with us last week after the emotional outburst at the meeting. In those heavy 
discussions, we talked about what it takes to actually curb violence. We know that it 
takes a multitude of actions to curb violence. Such as safer spaces and mentorship act 
as vital safe havens for the exact population we need to reach- the youth who are most 
at risk and hardest to connect with. 
 
That is why I am writing today about systemic accountability and the tangible choices 
this city makes. Over the last three years, I have submitted at least six formal letters 
that are part of the official record, alongside many other messages that never made it 
into your public minutes. My goal is not to cast blame, but to look honestly at where 
our city dollars go. 
 
For instance, this council allowed $50,000 in ARPA funding to go into a youth violence 
website. A website should have never been a priority. People simply do not turn to 
websites to curb violence or handle a crisis. It has not been used or updated a single 
time since it launched, and it does absolutely nothing to prevent or intervene during 
active moments of violence. Public health experts and criminologists agree that 
websites alone do not stop violence. To pull a young person away from street violence, 
you need human relationships, immediate crisis intervention, and safe physical spaces. 
 
That $50,000 could have funded real, after hours safe spaces for our young people. We 
are talking about spaces that do not look like a traditional rec center. It costs 
only $10,000 to keep the doors open to our safe haven on N. 5th St. for an entire year. 
Yet, I had to close those doors because I can no longer fund it out of my own pocket. 
While $50,000 sat rotting on the internet, a real sanctuary for the population we need 
to reach had to lock its doors for a fraction of that cost. 
 
On April 14, 2025, I asked you to establish a central Office of Violence Prevention 
(OVP). Across Pennsylvania, the state's Office of Gun Violence Prevention (OGVP) is 
proving that real safety requires funding human driven crisis intervention and safe 
environments NOT empty websites. 
 

3 
 
We must move past meetings that do not lead to outcomes. I am thankful for the 
council members who sat with us last week, and I am hopeful we can turn those words 
into action. I am ready to bring my local expertise to the table to ensure our city's 
resources go toward tangible improvements that save lives. 
 
I look forward to your response on how we can fund these safe spaces and move 
forward together.

Consent Agenda 
The Consent Agenda is designed to provide efficient approval of non-controversial legislation that does not 
require discussion/debate by giving approval via acclimation when the meeting agenda is approved.  The 
President of Council will call Council’s attention to the list of Consent Agenda legislation at the meeting before 
action is taken, which allows Council to remove a piece of legislation for separate consideration.

5. ORDINANCES FOR FINAL PASSAGE 
 
A. Bill 31-2026 –amending Chapter 600 Zoning Sections 807.B C-C Commercial Core District 
and 811.B M-C Manufacturing Commercial District allowing by-right use for adaptive reuse for 
residential and taverns/nightclubs, as attached in Exhibit A  
 
Councilor Nunez moved, seconded by Councilor Baker, to enact Bill No. 31-2026. 
 
Councilor Baker stated that he supports this ordinance as it intends to spur redevelopment in the 
downtown and manufacturing commercial areas. 
 
Councilor Miller agreed but noted the need to find balance between residential and 
entertainment uses such as taverns.  He expressed the belief that responsible individuals should 
operate taverns which would reduce the quality of life problems for surrounding properties and 
those who live in the upper floors or in the neighborhood.  He noted the recent problem with 

5 
 
after-hour clubs and taverns that are operating outside of what the PA LCB and zoning permits 
allow. 
 
Bill No.31-2026 was enacted by the following vote:

Nays:  None - 0

• K. Ordinance – Million Youth Chess Club – $2,500 ARPA Grant Funding Allocation
