RECOMMENDED ACTION 
Introduction and enactment of the "Selena Zavala Ordinance," an Ordinance amending Chapter 325 
(Law Enforcement) of the Code of Ordinances of the City of Reading to establish municipal policy, 
administrative benchmarks, confidentiality protections, and annual reporting requirements for federal U 
and T nonimmigrant status certification requests, and amending Chapter 586 (Welcoming City) to 
provide a cross-reference. 
BACKGROUND 
Public safety and effective law enforcement depend upon the cooperation of all residents, regardless of 
immigration status. Crime reporting, investigation, and prosecution are enhanced when victims and 
witnesses can assist law enforcement without fear of adverse civil immigration consequences. Federal 
law under the Immigration and Nationality Act (8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(U) and 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(T)) 
authorizes local law enforcement agencies to certify victim helpfulness on Form I-918, Supplement B (U 
Visa) and Form I-914, Supplement B (T Visa). 
The proposed Selena Zavala Ordinance establishes clear, uniform legislative standards while preserving 
the individual adjudicative discretion of the Chief of Police and the Reading Police Department. 
Specifically, the Ordinance: 
• Establishes a Framework for Department SOPs & Objective Standards: Establishes a framework 
for the Reading Police Department to maintain a Standard Operating Procedure aligned with 
federal statutory and regulatory criteria (8 C.F.R. §§ 214.14 and 214.11), evaluating helpfulness 
under the totality of the circumstances, affirming outcome independence regardless of arrest or 
conviction, and providing written reasons and reconsideration procedures for denials. 
• Sets Predictable Turnaround Timelines & Delay Remedies: Requires standard certification 
determinations within forty-five (45) calendar days of receiving a Complete Packet, and 
expedited determinations within fourteen (14) calendar days for qualifying Emergency 
Certification Requests (including active removal proceedings, immigration detention, 60-day 
statutory age-out or filing deadlines, and acute safety or medical emergencies) and document 
re-issuances, with a seven (7) calendar day resolution window upon overdue notice to the Chief 
of Police. 
• Establishes a Framework for Inter-Agency Coordination with the District Attorney: Establishes a 
framework for the Reading Police Department to coordinate with the Berks County District 
Attorney's Office via a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) or written protocol to streamline 
routing and ensure compliance with Brady v. Maryland and Giglio v. United States discovery 
obligations, while preserving the District Attorney's independent authority. 
• Enacts Data Confidentiality Protections & Annual Reporting: Prohibits disclosure of applicant 
personal identifying information, submission documents, or civil immigration status to federal 
civil immigration enforcement agencies except as required by federal or state statute, binding 
grant conditions, existing intergovernmental agreements, or valid judicial order, warrant, or 
subpoena, and requires an annual aggregate, de-identified report to City Council. 

 
BUDGETARY IMPACT 
None. Administrative review, SOP implementation, and annual aggregate reporting will be absorbed 
within existing Reading Police Department operational resources. 
PREVIOUS ACTION 
None. 
SUBSEQUENT ACTION 
Introduction of the Ordinance at the November 23, 2026 City Council meeting, followed by formal 
enactment at a subsequent regular meeting of City Council. In accordance with Section 221 of the City of 
Reading Home Rule Charter, the Ordinance shall take effect ten (10) days after final passage and 
approval. 
RECOMMENDED BY 
Managing Director Jack R. Gombach and Councilman Jaime Baez Jr. recommend approval.

ORDINANCE NO. ____ 
AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY OF READING, BERKS COUNTY, PENNSYLVANIA, KNOWN AS THE "SELENA 
ZAVALA ORDINANCE," AMENDING CHAPTER 325 (LAW ENFORCEMENT) OF THE CODE OF ORDINANCES 
TO ESTABLISH MUNICIPAL POLICY, ADMINISTRATIVE BENCHMARKS, CONFIDENTIALITY PROTECTIONS, 
AND REPORTING REQUIREMENTS FOR IMMIGRATION CERTIFICATION REQUESTS (U AND T VISAS), 
AND AMENDING CHAPTER 586 (WELCOMING CITY) TO PROVIDE A CROSS-REFERENCE.  
SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE, PURPOSE, AND FINDINGS 
A. Short Title. This Ordinance shall be known and may be cited as the "Selena Zavala Ordinance." 
B. Purpose and Findings. 
City Council recognizes that public safety and effective law enforcement depend upon the cooperation 
of all residents, regardless of immigration status.  

 
Crime reporting, investigation, and prosecution are enhanced when victims and witnesses can assist law 
enforcement without fear of adverse civil immigration consequences.  
Federal law (8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(U) and 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(T)) authorizes local law enforcement 
agencies to certify victim helpfulness.  
It is the intent of City Council to establish baseline legislative standards, governing timelines, objective 
criteria, confidentiality, and transparency, while delegating administrative implementation and 
individual adjudicative discretion to the Chief of Police and the Reading Police Department.  
SECTION 2. CODE AMENDMENT — CHAPTER 325 (LAW ENFORCEMENT)  
Chapter 325 (Law Enforcement) of the Code of Ordinances of the City of Reading is hereby amended by 
adding Article , Section 325- (U and T Status Certifications: Policy and Administrative Mandates), to read 
as follows:  
A. Department Administrative Directive Required  
The Chief of Police shall establish, maintain, and publish an administrative Directive and Standard 
Operating Procedure (SOP) governing the receipt, processing, and determination of Form I-918, 
Supplement B (U Visa) and Form I-914, Supplement B (T Visa) certifications. The administrative directive 
shall remain consistent with the standards set forth in this section.  
B. Definitions  
For purposes of this section, the following terms shall have the meanings indicated:  
• Complete Packet: A certification submission containing: (1) a written request identifying the 
applicant and the qualifying criminal activity or severe form of trafficking in persons; (2) the 
applicable partially completed federal certification form (Form I-918, Supplement B or Form I-
914, Supplement B); (3) any relevant police incident report number, case reference, or 
supporting documentation reasonably available to the applicant establishing victimization and 
helpfulness; and (4) if expedited review is requested, documentation evidencing a qualifying 
emergency circumstance.  
• Emergency Certification Request: A certification request involving an applicant who: (1) is 
currently in active federal removal proceedings, subject to a final order of removal or 
deportation, or facing imminent execution of removal; (2) is detained in federal immigration 
custody; (3) faces an imminent statutory age-out deadline within sixty (60) calendar days for the 
applicant or a qualifying derivative family member; (4) faces a mandatory immigration court or 
USCIS filing deadline within sixty (60) calendar days where absence of the certification would 
result in denial or forfeiture of relief; or (5) faces documented, imminent threats to personal 
safety, witness retaliation, or an acute life-threatening medical emergency requiring urgent 
federal humanitarian protection.  
C. Administrative Goal Posts & Standards  
The Department’s administrative directive shall adhere to the following benchmarks:  

 
1. Predictable Turnaround Times and Remedy for Delay: The policy shall establish clear timelines 
for standard reviews, expedited emergency reviews, and document re-issuances. In no event 
shall the standard determination window exceed forty-five (45) calendar days from receipt of a 
Complete Packet. For any qualifying Emergency Certification Request, the Department shall 
complete its review and issue a written determination within fourteen (14) calendar days of 
receipt of a Complete Packet. Requests for re-issuance of a previously signed certification due to 
clerical error or expiration prior to federal filing shall be processed within fourteen (14) calendar 
days. If the Department fails to issue a determination within the applicable timeframe, the 
applicant or their representative may submit a written notice of overdue review to the Chief of 
Police, who shall ensure a final written determination is issued within seven (7) calendar days of 
receipt of such notice.  
2. Objective Helpfulness Standards: The policy shall define helpfulness consistent with federal 
statutory and regulatory standards (8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(U) and (T); 8 C.F.R. § 214.14 and § 
214.11) using clear, verifiable criteria. In evaluating whether an applicant has refused or failed to 
provide reasonably requested assistance, the Department shall consider the totality of the 
circumstances, including verified language barriers, documented safety concerns, trauma, or 
physical or mental incapacitation.  
3. Outcome Independence: Certification decisions shall turn on qualifying victimization and 
helpfulness in accordance with federal law, independent of whether an arrest, charge, 
indictment, or conviction occurs.  
4. Procedural Fairness: The policy shall mandate written statements of reasons for any denied 
request and provide a distinct administrative avenue for reconsideration within the Department.  
5. Annual Reporting: The Chief of Police shall submit an annual aggregate report to City Council 
setting forth the total number of Form I-918, Supplement B and Form I-914, Supplement B 
certification requests received, approved, denied, and pending during the preceding calendar 
year, containing no personal identifying information. 
D. Inter-Agency Coordination Protocol  
The Chief of Police is authorized and directed to coordinate with the Berks County District Attorney’s 
Office, including through a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) or written inter-agency protocol, to 
establish clear routing procedures for certification requests, eliminate jurisdictional overlap, prevent 
administrative delays, and ensure full compliance with constitutional and statutory prosecution 
discovery obligations (including Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 (1963), and Giglio v. United States, 405 
U.S. 150 (1972)). Nothing in this section shall be construed to supersede or infringe upon the 
independent constitutional or statutory authority of the Berks County District Attorney.  
E. Data Confidentiality & Firewall  
Information submitted by or on behalf of an applicant seeking certification is confidential. Reading 
Police Department personnel shall not disclose an applicant’s personal identifying information, 
submission documents, or civil immigration status to federal civil immigration enforcement agencies, 

 
except when required by federal or state statute, binding federal grant or funding conditions, existing 
intergovernmental agreements, or pursuant to a valid judicial order, warrant, or subpoena issued by a 
court of competent jurisdiction (including an Article III federal court or a court of the Commonwealth of 
Pennsylvania).  
SECTION 3. CODE AMENDMENT — CHAPTER 586 (WELCOMING CITY)  
Chapter 586 (Welcoming City) of the Code of Ordinances of the City of Reading is hereby amended by 
adding Section 586-____ (U and T Visa Certifications) to read as follows:  
The policies, timelines, confidentiality protections, and administrative standards governing requests to 
the Reading Police Department for Form I-918, Supplement B (U Visa) and Form I-914, Supplement B (T 
Visa) certifications shall be governed by Chapter 325 (Law Enforcement), Section 325-____, of the Code 
of Ordinances.  
SECTION 4. SEVERABILITY  
If any provision of this Ordinance or its application is held invalid, such invalidity shall not affect other 
provisions or applications that can be given effect without the invalid provision.  
SECTION 5. EFFECTIVE DATE  
This Ordinance shall take effect ten (10) days after final passage and approval in accordance with 
Section 221 of the City of Reading Home Rule Charter.
