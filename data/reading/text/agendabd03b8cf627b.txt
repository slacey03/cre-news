RULES FOR PUBLIC PARTICIPATION AT COUNCIL MEETINGS 
Public comment at all meetings of the City Council of the City of Reading shall be governed by the following: 
• Residents and/or taxpayers of the City who wish to make or submit public comment must pre-register 
utilizing one of three (3) following options: 
1. By calling (610-655-6205) or emailing (council@readingpa.gov) the City Clerk's Office by 4 
pm on the day of the meeting with your name, address and the subject matter or topic to be 
discussed. If you are not able to attend in-person, instructions to access the virtual meeting app 
or dial-in telephone number will be provided. 
2. By submitting written public comment to be read into the record at the meeting by submitting 
an email to council@readingpa.gov by 4pm on the day of the meeting. The email message must 
clearly be marked as Public Comment.  
3. By registering in-person at the Council Chambers on the sign-up sheet found on the podium in 
Council Chambers between 5:00 p.m. and 7:00 p.m. on the day of the scheduled meeting, with 
your name, address and the subject matter or topic to be discussed.  
• No person shall be permitted to speak who is not a resident or taxpayer of the City, except upon permission 
of the President of Council or a majority of City Council.  
• Public comment will occur only during the Public Comment period listed on the meeting agenda at which 
time the Presiding Officer will recognize speakers and written comment that properly registered in the order 
received.  
• All public comment shall be delivered from the podium and speakers may not approach the Council tables 
at any time. 
• Those individuals registered to speak on agenda matters will be allotted no more than 5 minutes and those 
individuals registered to speak on non-agenda matters will be allotted no more than 3 minutes.  The City 
Clerk will control a timer to monitor these time periods.  When the alarm rings, a speaker must immediately 
end their comment.  If a speaker refuses to comply, the microphone at the podium will be turned off and 
the next speaker will be given the floor.  These time periods also apply to written comments that are being 
read into the record such that any written comment may only be read into the record for a 5-minute period

RECOMMENDED ACTION: Awarding of Contract for Youth Violence Prevention Consultancy 
  
The recommendation is to award the contract for Youth Violence Prevention Consultancy to January 
Advisors, 1500 Hadley Street - Box 728, Houston, TX 77001, in the amount of $75,000.00 utilizing 
account code – 010406-42220.  Submissions were obtained from the following organizations: 
 
 
BACKGROUND 
Building on our ongoing community development initiatives, neighborhood programming, and active 
downtown engagement, the City of Reading has been awarded a Collaborative Community Violence 
Intervention (CCVI) Track 1 Planning Grant. This initiative is proudly funded by the Pennsylvania 
Commission on Crime and Delinquency (PCCD). 
 
Scope of Work & Key Responsibilities 
The selected consultant will serve as the lead and strategic planning partner for the successful 
coordination, neighborhood alignment, and implementation of Reading’s CCVI Planning Grant. The 
scope of work is divided into four primary areas as mentioned in clauses 4.2 through 4.5 below. 
Collaboration & Stakeholder Engagement 
A. Convene Multi-Sector Partners: Regularly schedule, facilitate, and document meetings with key 
local stakeholders, including grassroots community-based organizations, public health professionals, law 
enforcement partners, juvenile justice practitioners, educators, and individuals with lived experience. 
B. Formalize Governance: Define and uphold shared principles of collaboration while clearly 
documenting roles, responsibilities, and accountability metrics across all participating partners. 
C. Establish Infrastructure: Form and maintain tactical subcommittees or working groups to address 
specific city hot-spots or youth focus areas. 
D. Execute Agreements: Draft and finalize Memoranda of Understanding (MOUs) and data-sharing 
agreements among participating local institutions. 
Planning & Needs Assessment 
A. Project Management: Develop a comprehensive project plan detailing a structural timeline, clear 
milestones, a stakeholder management strategy, and a transparent public communications plan. 
B. Lead Needs Assessment: Direct a rigorous, localized assessment process comprising block-level 
data analysis, asset mapping, and service gap identification. 


13 
 
C. Qualitative Gathering: Conduct stakeholder and community engagement activities through 
structured interviews, focus groups, and broad community surveys. 
D. Evaluate Local Services: Analyze existing youth services and supports across Reading, assess 
their quality, and recommend structural, scalable improvements. 
Strategy & Deliverables 
A. Violence Reduction Plan: Coordinate the drafting of a high-quality Local Violence Reduction Plan 
that highlights identified gaps, recommends actionable programs, outlines partner responsibilities, and 
focuses heavily on direct intervention strategies while naturally incorporating prevention, reentry, and 
healing frameworks. 
B. Executive Briefings: Document planning activities and present clear, accessible progress reports to 
City leadership, the Managing Director, and Reading City Council. 
Logistics & Support 
A. Meeting Operations: Handle all operational logistics for convenings, including securing local 
space, scheduling, preparing digital and physical materials, and coordinating refreshments. 
B. Participant Equity: Manage and administer stipend disbursements to value the time of community 
participants and individuals with lived experience involved in focus groups and planning activities. 
C. Peer Learning: Coordinate professional peer learning opportunities, including information 
sessions, trainings, or site visits for local partners. 
Key Deliverables 
The contract will be considered complete upon the successful delivery and administrative approval of: 
1. The Reading Violence Reduction Plan: A final, comprehensive strategy document containing a 
detailed gap analysis, asset inventory, stakeholder-informed recommendations, and a long-term budget 
and resource plan for future funding. 
2. Established Collaborative Infrastructure: Documented meeting schedules, minutes, a finalized 
partner roles/responsibilities matrix, and executed, binding MOUs and data-sharing agreements. 
3. Planning Process & Transition Documentation: A complete analysis report compiling stakeholder 
engagement data, methodology, and explicit transition recommendations for the subsequent 
implementation phase. 
Minimum Qualifications 
Prospective applicants must meet the following criteria: 
A. Proven experience in project coordination and strategic planning within community development, 
public health, municipal government, or social services. 
B. Demonstrated ability to facilitate multi-sector partnerships and manage diverse, complex 
stakeholder dynamics. 
C. Strong technical skills in data synthesis, qualitative research methods, and executive report 
writing. 
D. Specialized knowledge of evidence-based violence prevention, trauma-informed care models, or 
community safety frameworks is highly preferred. 
Reporting Expectations 
A. Monthly written progress reports 
B. Quarterly presentations 
C. Deliverable review schedule

Approval of the sole source purchase chlorine alarm system notification improvements to the SCADA

RECOMMENDATION 
The recommendation is to approve the CoStars purchase for the chlorine system alarm notification improvements 
of the Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) system.  The replacement includes the purchase of the 
strobe and horn equipment for eight (8) locations at the wastewater treatment plant including the wiring, 
installation, configuration updates, setup, and testing.  The work will be authorized for $77,436.50 to be completed 
by BSI Electric from Montgomeryville, PA and includes TRIJAY Systems, Inc., from Line Lexington, PA as the 
subcontractor who is the City’s sole source system integrator.   For security and continuity of operations, TRIJAY 
is the system installation, integration, and configuration contractor allowed access to this operational technology 
of the City’s SCADA system. 
 
BACKGROUND 
The City’s SCADA system and network communication were replaced with the WWTP upgrades 
project.  The SCADA system has alarms with notifications through the SCADA display terminals for 
numerous situations.  Typically, these are operational issues which impact the WWTP Operations team 
and require their action.  However, due to the serious safety hazard with a chlorine system leak, the 
entire WWTP facility and staff should be notified of the issue immediately and evacuate as appropriate.  
The addition of the visual and audible alarm notification throughout WWTP ensures that staff who do 
not use the SCADA system are alerted of the leak and are able to take appropriate action to remain 
safe.  This is designed to reach as close to the Police Firing Range for notifying them to shelter in 
place or the route for evacuation.  To maintain a secure system and network, the City limits access to 
the SCADA system with TRIJAY providing upgrades to SCADA programming, monitoring, and control.  
This aspect is reviewed on an annual basis as required per the City’s Purchasing policy. 
 
BUDGETARY IMPACT 
The Utilities Division of the Public Works Department has determined there are sufficient funds to cover 
the chlorine alarm system notification included in the 2026 budget.

1. SUMMARY 
Following the resignation of the Historic Architecture Review Board the City Administration and Council for 
the City of Reading have agreed to delay the appointment of new members to the HARB to allow careful 
consideration of the resources, capacity, and management of the historic preservation function; whether 
reorganization of that function under the Zoning Office is preferred; and whether assignment of some historic 
preservation staff to address blight is appropriate.  
Following Amy Johnson’s retirement, the Administration has assigned Lisa Charlanza to carry out the 
functions of the Historic Preservation Office, including approving Certificates of Appropriateness for certain 
building improvements, as delegated by the Board.  
The Administration requests temporary authorization of Muhlenberg Greene—to assess proposed 
improvements and issue Certificates of Appropriateness for an unlimited range of construction, demolition, and 
alteration projects, including those that currently require Board review and approval. 
 
 
2. DECISION RATIONALE 
Recently several members of Reading’s Historic Architecture Review Board (HARB) resigned, including the 
Chair and Vice Chair. Their stated reasons focused on deficient staff support. This has prompted internal discussion 
about the level of support that the City can reasonably provide to the HARB given the other demands regarding our 
current staff’s time and budgetary considerations.    
 

40 
 
3. HISTORICAL CONTEXT 
Our first historic district was established in 1978, and has since increased to five districts, some of which are 
quite large.  These historic districts emerged from active neighborhood advocacy groups who petitioned the Mayor 
and City Council to apply for historic district certification.   A nine-member HARB was formed to oversee the 
growing number of districts.   
Over the almost five decades since 1978, the changing demographic, cultural and economic circumstances in 
the City have resulted in a much-diminished level of interest, involvement and commitment by neighborhood groups 
in historic preservation. Only Centre Park has an active and fervent association dedicated to historic preservation. 
About one-third of the city’s 36,000 properties are now rentals, with many out-of-town landlords. Meanwhile, the 
character of the Callowhill and Prince Districts has changed significantly.  Many structures in the Prince District have 
been modified with Permastone and aluminum siding, and the original features modified or removed. A general lack 
of maintenance and misguided improvements have reduced the historic significance of the Prince District.  Likewise, 
some remote blocks of the Callowhill District have experienced the same.  
During this same period the City of Reading has seen significant decline in staffing in the Planning and 
Historic Preservation Office, particularly since Act 47.  This has constrained our ability to monitor and enforce un-
permitted activity in these Districts.  We currently have two staff and one vacant clerk position that is unlikely to be 
filled in the near future due to budget constraints. The volume of included properties, complexity of issues, and 
interactions with owners has grown to a level that we simply cannot support with two people.   
 
4. BACKGROUND / CONTEXT 
The formation of the original historic districts was championed by a dedicated group of local owners who 
procured agreement from two-thirds of the residents. This demonstrated the commitment that then convinced the 
Mayor and Council to support it. Over time, that interest and involvement has mostly disappeared, leaving the HARB 
and City staff to enforce these requirements on a largely uninterested community. The valiant efforts by our 
dedicated HARB and City Historic Preservation staff cannot overcome a lack of commitment by neighborhood 
groups to this cause.  Nor can those living in the economically challenged residents of the Callowhill, Prince, Penn’s 
Common, and Queen Anne Districts afford to replace windows with historically accurate, custom-made wood 
versions, or slate roofs. We need to reduce the boundaries of our historic districts, and moderate our improvement 
guidelines to allow more reasonable, modern, durable, and affordable repairs.  We should strive to maintain the 
overall character of historic neighborhoods without religiously adhering to exacting and expensive standards for 
individual façade elements.

RESOLUTION NO. ____-2026 
 
APPOINTING JESSICA SCHWARTZ AS A FULL MEMBER TO THE  
BOARD OF HEALTH 
 
WHEREAS, City Council appointed Jessica Schwartz as an alternate on the Board of Health in May of 
2022, and 
 
WHEREAS, Dr. Schwartz is a physician and her medical background is beneficial to the Board of 
Health’s work; and 
 
WHEREAS, a vacancy occurred on the Board via resignation in September 2026; and 
 
WHEREAS, at their October 2026 meeting the Board of Health discussed the need to fill this vacancy 
and moved to recommend that Council appoint Dr. Schwartz to full membership. 
 
NOW THEREFORE, the City Council of the City of Reading, Berks County, Pennsylvania hereby 
resolves to appoint Jessica Schwartz as a full member to the Board of Health.
