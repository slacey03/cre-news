I. Code Blue Update for Winter 2026 – 2027    5:00 pm – 6:00 pm 
Ms. McCauley introduced herself as Executive Director of the Berks Coalition to End 
Homelessness (BCEH) and Ms. Bushkie, Executive Director of Opportunity House.  She 
thanked Council for the opportunity to make this presentation and stated that BCEH is 
coordinating a collaborative response to homelessness along with New Journey and Street 
Medicine.  She stated that the collaborative meetings are held two times per month.  A Power 
Point presentation was screen shared. 
 
Mr. Freytiz connected to the meeting virtually at this time. 
 
Ms. Bushkie stated that there were four weather-related deaths in January 2026.   
 
Ms. McCauley expressed the belief that the Point in Time (PIT) counts are understated but that 
there is still an 8% increase in homelessness over last year.  She explained that the Code Blue 
program will run from December 1, 2026 through March 31, 2027 and the Code Red program 
will run from June 1, 2027 through September 30, 2027.  She explained that both cold and heat 
are especially dangerous for the elderly and for children.

Ms. McCauley stated that BCEH has requested $350,000 from the City to continue this work 
and that they have also requested the same amount from the County.  She stated that there is 
already $100,000 committed from the City of Reading to begin this season’s Code Blue 
program. 
 
Mr. Zeiber connected to the meeting virtually at this time. 
 
Ms. Bushkie thanked the City for its past commitments.  She stated that Opportunity House 
cannot address the issue alone and that she looks forward to continuing to work with the City. 
 
Ms. Reed thanked Ms. McCauley and Ms. Bushkie for the presentation.  She questioned the 
amount of funding requested from the Wyomissing Foundation.  Ms. McCauley stated that 
$100,000 has been requested from the Wyomissing Foundation. 
 
In response to a question from Ms. Reed regarding assistance that has been requested from 
Reading’s surrounding municipalities, Ms. McCauley stated that other specific municipalities 
outside of Reading have not been requested to provide funding.  She stated that funding has 
been requested from the County and stated that approximately 50% of the homeless 
population is outside Reading.  She stated that the more funding that is available the more 
families that can be assisted.   
 
Ms. Reed stated that the City has been leading the way on this issue and expressed the belief 
that other municipalities are not meeting their responsibilities.  She noted the increased 
visibility of the homeless population outside of Reading. 
 
Ms. Bushkie stated that BCEH has responded to the homeless in Kutztown and Hamburg and 
noted that the homeless population outside of Reading is growing.  She noted the need for 
additional municipalities to contribute to the issue and to provide locations within their 
municipalities to serve this population. 
 
Mr. Miller thanked Ms. McCauley and Ms. Bushkie for the presentation.  He expressed the 
belief that this is an important annual topic and stated that he is glad that organizations are 
working together to reduce duplicative efforts.  He expressed the belief that New Journey has 
become a trusted organization.  He stated that Council recently had a presentation from the 
Police Department about the homeless population and noted that many people do not enter 
shelters because they don’t want to split up their family.  He questioned the participating 
hotels that will accommodate families.  Ms. McCauley stated that BCEH is working with a 
non-profit that manages the hotel participation.  She stated that there are agreements with the 
Comfort Inn, Days Inn, and Candlewood Suites.  She stated that the hotels will accept pets and 
that no identification is required.  She stated that coordinating with this organization will 
allow BCEH to focus on the clients.  She stated that it is a nationally recognized organization. 
 

3 
Mr. Miller expressed the belief that pets are important and that people prefer not to separate 
from them.  He stated that he is glad to hear that the pets will be accepted at the hotels.  He 
questioned whether transportation from the hotels to social services will be provided.  Ms. 
McCauley stated that bus passes are included in the budget so that people can attend their 
appointments.  She stated that BCEH is also working with the Animal Rescue League and 
Humane PA to rent spaces to hold pets as needed.  She stated that there are many partners. 
 
Mr. Miller noted that the Police presentation also noted that some homeless people are battling 
addiction issues and may not be accepted into shelters.  Ms. Bushkie stated that the goal is to 
save lives.  She stated that organizations that will receive funding must agree to remove 
barriers to entry including admitting those with addiction issues.  She stated that some of 
those assisted during past programs have moved into and through detox programming. 
 
Mr. Miller stated that he is glad to see new partnerships and additional coordination.  He 
questioned if there is a long-term plan.  Ms. McCauley stated that long-term planning is part of 
the updated policy.  She noted the need for programs to be sustainable and stated that BCEH 
will continue to coordinate services.  She noted the need to make it easy for organizations to 
provide services.  Ms. Bushkie stated that BCEH has a plan with strategic elements. 
 
Mr. Miller expressed the belief that homelessness will continue to increase and questioned the 
work that will be done to eliminate the issues rather than use a temporary fix.  He noted the 
need for the homeless population to re-enter society.  He stated that he was impressed with 
this presentation. 
 
Mr. Gombach stated that the program has the City’s full support.  He stated that gaps in care 
will be addressed and that $350,000 has been included in the 2027 budget along with CDBG 
funding for a day shelter.  He suggested that Council and the Administration pass a joint 
resolution supporting the BCEH strategic plan. 
 
Mr. Baez thanked Ms. McCauley and Ms. Bushkie for the presentation.  He noted the need for 
compassion and customer service skills.  He questioned the number of people that can be 
assisted with hotel rooms.  Ms. Bushkie stated that this is difficult to estimate.  She stated that 
it will depend on the number of people in need.  She stated that once funding for this item is 
used, this part of the program will not be available.  She agreed with the need for intense case 
management for people to re-enter society and agreed that hotels are a very costly solution.  
She noted the need for rapid turnover to assist as many people as possible.  Ms. McCauley 
stated that budget reallocations can also occur to make the greatest impact. 
 
Mr. Baez questioned the number of people that would be in the day centers and where they 
would go after the day center closes.  Ms. McCauley stated that there are approximately 35 
people in the day center daily but that they are not always the same people.  She stated that 
there are four staff members at the day center to assist with case management.

Mr. Baez noted his agreement with Ms. Reed that other municipalities should be assisting with 
funding.  He requested the specific funding budget.  Ms. McCauley stated that this is not part

strategic plan. 
 
Mr. Baez noted his agreement with reviewing and updating policy as needed.  He stated that

Mr. Baez noted that when Public Works employees must enter a homeless encampment to 
remove trash, they must sometimes remove tents, blankets, etc.  He questioned whether these 
items are distributed by BCEH.  He noted the need to mitigate this type of situation where 
funding is used to distribute items that will become trash.   
 
Ms. Campos thanked Ms. McCauley and Ms. Bushkie for their presentation.  She stated that 
$350,000 has been requested from the City with a $100,000 commitment and that 50% of the 
homeless population is within the City.  She questioned if there would be follow up with the 
County on the $350,000 request made to them.  Ms. McCauley explained that the Code Blue 
program does not align with the City’s budget process and stated that the $100,000 committed 
to this season’s program is within the 2026 budget.  She stated that the County has requested 
additional information which has been provided.  She stated that she was waiting to hear back 
from them. 
 
Ms. Campos noted her hope that the County would provide funding.   
 
Mr. Gombach stated that the City has historically supported work to assist the homeless 
population.  He stated that beginning in the 2027 budget there is a Code Blue line item. 
 
Mr. Baker noted his agreement with Ms. Reed and Mr. Baez about the need for other 
municipalities to provide support.  He also noted the need to request funding from State 
representatives and senators.  He questioned the percentage of the Reading homeless 
population that has moved into Reading from other municipalities.  Ms. McCauley stated that 
there are State representatives and senators serving on the CoC Board and that they serve on 
several committees.  She stated that they assist when they are able.  She stated that requests 
have also been made to the State. 
 
Mr. Oppenheimer connected to the meeting virtually at this time.

Ms. Kelleher noted that several zip codes in the City also extend out into surrounding

Ms. Reed expressed the belief that this data would be helpful when BCEH approaches other

Ms. Reed thanked Ms. McCauley and Ms. Bushkie for their presentation and stated that

Ms. Vazquez also noted the need to amend the Historic ordinance and the standards and

the current processes to continue operations. 
 
In response to a question from Mr. Baez about how to determine if staff reviews the 
application or if it comes before Council, Ms. Vazquez stated that it is based on the standards 
and guidelines.  She stated that it is the same process but instead of going before HARB, the

the State and many decisions need to be made about the ordinance amendment.  She stated 
that HARB may be recreated or it may be a different board.  She noted the need to establish the 
project approval process.  She stated that this extension follows the current process with some 
applications approved by staff, some by the Managing Director, and some by Council in place 
of HARB. 
 
Ms. Vazquez stated that the City is pursuing a State grant for technical assistance to re-
evaluate and amend the ordinance.

Mr. Miller stated that there are multiple historic districts and many property owners want to 
know what is happening.  He stated that he is asked about this regularly.  He suggested that 
information be posted on the City website and shared on social media.  He noted the need for 
people to understand the review process and stated that it is a complicated process. 
 
Ms. Charlanza stated that the State evaluated the City’s ordinance and the City is now 
considering their recommendations.  She stated that she can also provide the results of the 
evaluation. 
 
In response to questions from Mr. Baker, Ms. Charlanza stated that this extension would allow 
the day to day processes to continue.  She stated that the City has hired Muhlenberg Greene as 
a consultant to help review applications.   
 
Mr. Baker stated that the 2027 Position Ordinance does not include the Historic Preservation 
Specialist or the clerk and questioned how applications would be processed.  Mr. Gombach 
stated that this work would be absorbed by other CD employees. 
 
Mr. Baker noted the increased workload and questioned if the City expects to receive fewer 
applications.  Mr. Gombach stated that the applications are currently being handled by Ms. 
Charlanza. 
 
Mr. Baker noted his concern that these positions were previously filled and there were issues 
with jobs not being performed properly and now that work will be done with fewer people.  
Mr. Gombach suggested that this discussion take place in executive session.  He stated that he 
is not concerned and stated that there will be a larger restructuring of job duties and 
assignments within CD.   
 
Ms. Charlanza expressed the belief that it would be difficult to re-instate HARB before the 
ordinance amendment is completed and staff is restructured.  She stated that there may be a 
different type of advisory board and that the extension is needed in the meantime. 
 
Ms. Reed stated that the HARB is currently vacant and there is no reason why people cannot 
apply to serve.  She expressed the belief that HARB members had a good understanding of 
reviewing applications – from railing and door replacements to much more.  She stated that 
she owned a historic property in the past and the aesthetic needs to remain protected.  She 
stated that there is concern with some replacements not being appropriate even though there 
are many ways to replicate the original.  She stated that she would hate to see decline in the 
Districts because of economics.  She stated that other cities have strong historic districts that 
create tourism and economic development. 
 
Ms. Charlanza stated that she wants to be helpful.  She stated that there are refined products 
and processes.  She stated that the City’s current Historic ordinance is not in compliance with 
the State and the process cannot be done the same way as in the past.  She noted the need to

amend the ordinance before a new board begins.  She stated that there are options besides 
HARB and stated that this must be worked out first. 
 
Mr. Baez noted his agreement with reviewing and updating the ordinance and policies.  He 
noted his concern that things are currently unchecked and that HARB was an independent 
board.  He noted the need to treat all fairly and stated that he is more comfortable with an 
independent board in place.  He stated that the Administration currently has full power over 
what applications are approved and denied.  He noted the need for Council to trust that the 
Administration is making the right decisions on applications that are not reviewed by Council.  
He noted his concern that there is no HARB and that the Administration does not seem 
enthused about restarting it.  He expressed the belief that twelve months is a long time and 
there is no sense of urgency. 
 
Mr. Gombach noted the need to remember that the Historic ordinance is not in compliance 
with State and Federal standards and that this exposes the City to some risk.  He stated that 
the City is working with the State and getting technical assistance to amend the ordinance.  He 
stated that there is progress and stated that the extension is for up to one year to allow people 
to continue to submit applications that will be processed.  He expressed the belief that this is 
progress.  He stated that the extension will allow the City to continue to provide services while 
the CD Department is restructured.  He stated that consultants will be contracted to help 
mitigate costs and to help avoid future legacy costs of employees.   
 
Mr. Baez questioned Mr. Gombar if there is a way for Council to help with the timeline.  Mr. 
Gombar agreed that the State report found the City’s current ordinance deficient and warned 
against rushing to recreate HARB.  He stated that HARB’s role may change when the 
ordinance is amended.  He stated that the Administration is working on the amendment and 
suggested that there be collaboration on the amendment process.  He stated that roadblocks 
are not advisable.  He stated that if the current process expires there will be chaos.  He 
suggested reducing the time limit from twelve months to six months to address Council’s 
concerns.  He stated that this can then be extended again if needed based on forward progress. 
 
Mr. Gombach stated that he supports a six month extension.  He stated that the original 
proposal was for an additional 90 days but after discussions with the Council office it was 
believed that twelve months was warranted due to the transition issues in CD.  He stated that 
the full twelve months may not have been needed.  He stated that requesting twelve months 
would have been more efficient than returning for additional extensions.   
 
Ms. Reed noted the need for an amendment to the resolution to change the extension to six 
months. 
 
In response to a question from Mr. Miller regarding which staff member would visit properties 
and speak with applicants, Ms. Vazquez stated that this would be done by Ms. Charlanza.
