RULES FOR PUBLIC PARTICIPATION AT COUNCIL MEETINGS 
Public comment at all meetings of the City Council of the City of Reading shall be governed by the following: 
• Residents and/or taxpayers of the City who wish to make or submit public comment must pre-register 
utilizing one of three (3) following options: 
1. By calling (610-655-6205) or emailing (council@readingpa.gov) the City Clerk's Office 
by 4 pm on the day of the meeting with your name, address and the subject matter or topic 
to be discussed. If you are not able to attend in-person, instructions to access the virtual 
meeting app or dial-in telephone number will be provided. 
2. By submitting written public comment to be read into the record at the meeting by 
submitting an email to council@readingpa.gov by 4pm on the day of the meeting. The 
email message must clearly be marked as Public Comment.  
3. By registering in-person at the Council Chambers on the sign-up sheet found on the 
podium in Council Chambers between 5:00 p.m. and 7:00 p.m. on the day of the scheduled 
meeting, with your name, address and the subject matter or topic to be discussed.  
• No person shall be permitted to speak who is not a resident or taxpayer of the City, except upon 
permission of the President of Council or a majority of City Council.  
• Public comment will occur only during the Public Comment period listed on the meeting agenda at 
which time the Presiding Officer will recognize speakers and written comment that properly 
registered in the order received.  
• All public comment shall be delivered from the podium and speakers may not approach the Council 
tables at any time. 
• Those individuals registered to speak on agenda matters will be allotted no more than 5 minutes and 
those individuals registered to speak on non-agenda matters will be allotted no more than 3 minutes.  
The City Clerk will control a timer to monitor these time periods.  When the alarm rings, a speaker 
must immediately end their comment.  If a speaker refuses to comply, the microphone at the podium

Weapon (CEW) and related software contract to Axon Enterprise Inc. 
 
RECOMMENDATION 
The recommendation is to approve the 2026 payment of $641,933.52 to Axon relating to the 2025 contract 
award for Body Worn Cameras (BWC’s), Conducted Electrical Weapons (CEW’s), related software, storage 
and hardware to Axon Enterprise Inc. 17800 North 85th Street Scottsdale, AZ 85255 
 
BACKGROUND 
The department currently utilizes Axon Enterprises for Body Worn Cameras (BWC’s), Conducted Electrical 
Weapons (CEW’s) aka Tasers, associated software, hardware and cloud storage.  The City of Reading awarded 
the BWC contract to Axon Enterprises in May 2020.  The City of Reading awarded the CEW contract to Axon 
Enterprises (Formally Taser International) in January 2017, and an equipment upgrade and contract extension 
was awarded in December 2020.  Both BWC and CEW contracts are coming to an end in July 2025 and 
December 2025 respectfully.  The Reading Police Department currently issue all Patrol Division Officers BWC’s 
(Body 3) and related equipment ex BWC mounts, Signal Sidearms etc. to be used while responding to emergency 
and non-emergency police related incidents and citizen interactions.  The Reading Police Department currently 
issues all sworn personnel CEW’s (Taser 7).  The Reading Police Department currently uses Axon Enterprises 
Evidence.com Digital Evidence Management System (DEMS) for storage of all BWC video evidence, non-BWC 
video evidence, digital photographic evidence and other related digital evidence. 
 
The use of Axon BWC’s, Axon Taser CEW’s and Evidence.com DEMS allows a seamless flow of digital evidence 
from point of collection through conclusion of the court proceedings.   
 
Axon’s Officer Safety Plan 10+ (OSP10+) package allows the bundling of hardware, software, accessories, 
training programs, equipment refreshes, and warranties together, allowing the City of Reading to get the most up 
to date BWC and CEW equipment during the term of the contract and allows for discounted cost compared to 
separate individual contracts for the BWC’s, CEW’s and related software. 
 
The equipment is Sole Source, and the justifications have been completed and accepted by the City 
Administration.

RECOMMENDED ACTION:   
Council approval of a Resolution for Sewage Plan Approval for HDC MidAtlantic at 201 South 6th Street also 
known as Cornerstone on 6th. 
 
RECOMMENDATION: 
The Administration recommends that Council approve this resolution for a sewage planning module as the 
proposed connection does comply with the allocations and other aspects of the provision of Chapter 94 of State 
Statutes. 
 
BACKGROUND: 
The Pennsylvania code requires municipalities to adopt an Official Sewage Facilities Plan providing sewage 
services adequate to prevent contamination of waters of the Commonwealth and/or environmental health 
hazards from sewage wastes.  The code also provides for the revision of the plan whenever it is necessary to 
determine whether a proposed method of sewage disposal for a new land development conforms to a 
comprehensive program of pollution control and water quality management. 
 
HDC MidAtlantic at 201 South 6th Street, also known as Cornerstone on 6th intends to selectively demolish and 
renovate the rest of the existing building resulting in eighteen (18) additional equivalent dwelling units (EDUs) 
for a total of eighteen (18) EDUs with a total projected wastewater flow of 4725 gallons per day.  This project 
proposes to selectively demolish parts of the existing building and renovate the rest of the existing building. The 
proposed demolition and renovation plan to create 46 affordable apartments for seniors which is the cause for 
the additional wastewater flows. 
 
The Administration has determined that the land development described in the attached Sewage Facilities 
Planning Module conforms to applicable sewage related zoning and other sewage related municipal ordinances 
and plans, and to a comprehensive program of pollution control and water quality management.  Upon review 
and approval by the City, the planning module will be sent to the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental 
Protection for final review. 
 
BUDGETARY IMPACT: 
The planning module review fee of $750 will be deposited in the sewer fund revenue account to offset the 
expense to review.

RESOLUTION OF THE COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF READING, BERKS COUNTY, 
PENNSYLVANIA (hereinafter “the municipality”). 
 
WHEREAS, Section 5 of the Act of January 24, 1966, P.L.1535, known as the “Pennsylvania Sewage 
Facilities Act”, as amended and the Rules and Regulations of the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental 
Resources adopted thereunder, Chapter 71 of Title 25 of the Pennsylvania code, requires the municipality to 
adopt an Official Sewage Facilities Plan providing for sewage services adequate to prevent contamination of 
waters of the Commonwealth and/or environmental health hazards from sewage wastes, and to revise said 
plan whenever it is necessary to determine whether a proposed method of sewage disposal for a new land 
development conforms to a comprehensive program of pollution control and water quality management, and

known as Cornerstone on 6th. The proposed use for the demolition and renovation is to construct 46 
apartment units for seniors. It is proposed that this land development be served by an existing sewer tap-in. 
 
WHEREAS, the City of Reading finds that the land development described in the attached Sewage Facilities 
Module conforms to applicable sewage related zoning and other sewage related municipal ordinances and 
plans, and to a comprehensive program of pollution control and water quality management. 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that the Council of the City of Reading hereby adopts and submits 
to the Department of Environmental Protection for its approval as a revision to the “Official Sewage Facilities 
Plan” of the municipality the above referenced Sewage Facilities Planning Module which is attached hereto.

WHEREAS, the Planning Commission, Public Works and the Administration are recommending

PennDOT Liquid Fuel Tax revenue, and will help to ensure that the integrity of the streets and

Part 16:  Off-Street Parking and Loading 
§600-1601: General Application Criteria 
(A) Application:  Off-street parking and/or loading facilities shall be located, designed, 
constructed and maintained in accordance with the provisions of this Zoning Ordinance.  
These facilities shall be provided whenever the following actions occur: 
(1) A new building has been constructed or a new use has been established in 
accordance with the provisions of this Zoning Ordnance. 
(2) The use of the building or property has been changed, altered or expanded, which 
requires additional off-street parking spaces to accommodate the use. 
(3) An off-street parking facility shall include the driveways, access drives, access isles, 
parking spaces and loading spaces. 
(B) Land Use Compliance:  Parking and loading areas shall be a permitted use as follows: 
(1) Principal Use:  The following off-street parking area and/or off-street loading area 
requirements shall apply if the off-street parking and/or off-street loading area is 
classified as the principal use on the property: 
(a) An off-street parking area containing ten (10) or more parking spaces, which 
are designated as the principal use shall only be permitted provided that such 
use is permitted within the underlying zoning district.  All such off-street 
parking areas shall comply with the lot area, dimensional and coverage 
requirements, as specified by the underlying zoning district. 
(b) An off-street parking area containing less than ten (10) parking spaces, which 
are designated as the principal use shall only be permitted provided that such 
use is permitted within the underlying zoning district.  All such off-street 
parking areas shall comply with: the minimum lot area requirement; the 
dimensional setback requirements may be reduced to three (3) feet from all 
property lines; and the maximum impervious coverage may be increased to 
95 percent of the lot area. 
(c) An off-street loading area designated as the principal use shall only be 
permitted provided that such use is permitted within the underlying zoning 
district.  All such off-street loading areas shall comply with the lot area, 
dimensional and coverage requirements, as specified by the underlying 
zoning district. 
(2) Accessory Use:   An off-street parking area and/or off-street loading area may be 
permitted as an accessory use to a compatible principal use, subject to the provisions 
specified by this Zoning Ordinance. 
(3) Satellite Parking Spaces:  Off-street parking spaces may be established or credited 
at a conforming off-site or off-premises location, subject to the provision specified by 
this Zoning Ordinance. 
(4) On-Street Spaces:  Unless otherwise permitted by the City of Reading, all on-street 
parking spaces and/or on-street loading spaces shall not be counted or credited as a 
required parking or loading space.  All such spaces shall comply with the provisions 
established by the City of Reading Code.  

27 
 
(C) Location:  The following provisions shall apply to the location of off-street parking and/or 
loading areas: 
(1) Unless otherwise permitted, off-street parking and loading spaces shall be located on 
the same lot as the principal use.  
(2) All parking and loading facilities shall be arranged and marked to provide safe and 
orderly movement without disrupting traffic or moving other vehicles. 
(3) Unless otherwise permitted by the Public Works Department, the design of parking 
areas for all uses shall be such to prevent the back-up of vehicles onto a public street 
and that no portion of any parked vehicle extends over any property line. 
§600-1602: Design and Construction Standards 
(A) Accessibility:  All off-street parking and loading spaces shall have access to a public street 
by means of an access lane, driveway, alley or road that complies with the provisions 
specified by Chapters 515 and 600 of the Code. 
(B) Size:  Off-street parking and loading spaces shall comply with the following criteria: 
(1) Off-Street Parking Spaces:  The spaces shall have a rectangular shape that complies 
with following dimensional requirements: 
(a) Within an off-street parking lot, each parking space for motor vehicles (except 
those spaces dedicated for use by handicapped persons) shall have a 
minimum rectangular area of 162 square feet, which shall have a minimum 
width of 9 feet and a minimum length of 18 feet have a minimum area of 162 
square feet with a minimum width of 9 feet and a minimum length of 18 feet.  
Angled spaces will need to be longer in order to achieve the rectangular shape 
with the minimum dimensions. 
(b) Where parallel parking is permitted, the parking spaces shall be delineated by 
painted lines and shall have a minimum width of 8 feet, as measured from the 
curb or cartway edge and a minimum length of 20 feet, as measured along 
the curb or cartway edge. 
(2) Off-Street Loading Spaces:  The spaces shall have a rectangular shape that complies 
with following dimensional requirements: 
(a) Off-street loading spaces with a length of sixty (60) feet, width of fourteen (14) 
feet, and clearance height of fifteen (15) feet for commercial and industrial 
uses. 
(b) Off-street loading spaces with a length of forty-five (45) feet, width of twelve 
(12) feet, and clearance height of twelve (12) feet may be permitted for 
commercial, institutional and light industrial uses provided the landowner or 
applicant can demonstrate that the use will not require larger trucks to access 
the site for loading and unloading activities.  
(C) Grade:    The off-street parking and loading areas shall be properly graded to provide 
convenient vehicular access and proper drainage and shall be maintained in usable 
condition.  The minimum grade of areas used for parking shall be at least one (1) percent 
and the maximum grade shall not exceed five (5) percent.

extended for an additional twelve (12) month period provided that a land development 
plan is submitted to the Planning Commission for review and consideration.   
(5) All temporary or permanent areas designated for parking and loading shall be 
maintained in a safe, accessible, stabilized and mud-free condition, as approved or 
permitted by the Public Works Department or Zoning Office. 
(F) Curbs, Guiderails and Wheel Stops:  The following provisions shall apply: 
(1) The perimeter of the off-street parking and/or loading area shall be curbed for all 
projects exceeding ten (10) or more parking spaces and/or five (5) or more off-street 
loading spaces. 
(2) The curb radius within the off-street parking area and/or loading area shall be a 
minimum of five (5) feet.

(3) Curbs or wheel stops shall be utilized to define the limits or depth of the off-street 
parking and/or loading area. 
(4) Guiderails and/or retaining walls may be required in order to compensate or protect 
designated areas with topographical relief exceeding 15 percent slope.  The guiderail 
and/or retaining wall shall be subject to the review and approval of the Public Works 
Department. 
(G) Pedestrian Accessibility and Ramps:    The off-street parking area and/or loading area shall 
include sidewalks, pedestrian paths, stairways and/or access ramps in order to 
accommodate internal and external pedestrian movements.  All such improvements shall be 
subject to the review and approval of the Public Works Department. 
(H) Pavement Markings:  All parking facilities shall be adequately marked and maintained for the 
purpose of defining parking spaces and interior drives.  All pavement markings shall be 
consistent with those utilized by the City of Reading and the Pennsylvania Department of 
Transportation. 
(I) Signs:  All traffic control signs within the off-street parking area shall be located, designed 
and installed as per the provisions specified by the City of Reading and the Pennsylvania 
Department of Transportation. 
(J) Plan and Permit Requirements:  The following planning and permitting requirements may 
apply for off-street parking and loading areas: 
(1) Zoning Permit:  A zoning permit issued by the Zoning Office shall be issued for all 
proposed off-street parking areas and off-street loading areas. 
(2) Land Development Plan:  A Land Development Plan shall be prepared and submitted 
for site improvements involving ten (10) or more parking spaces and/or five (5) or 
more off-street loading spaces.  If the combination of site improvements exceed 2,500 
square feet of impervious surface or 800 square feet of building surface, a Land 
Development Plan shall be required. 
(3) Stormwater Management Plan:  If the combination of site improvements exceeds 
2,500 square feet of impervious surface, a Stormwater Management Plan shall be 
prepared and submitted with the Land Development Plan. 
(4) Earth Disturbance Activities:  If the combination of site improvements exceeds 5,000 
square feet of earth disturbance activities, an Erosion and Sedimentation Control Plan 
shall be prepared and submitted with the Land Development Plan. 
(5) Other Permits:  If the proposed site improvements encroach or traverse a street right-
of-way for use and occupancy, a Highway Occupancy Permit (HOP), Driveway Permit 
and/or Utility Permit may be required via the Pennsylvania Department of 
Transportation and/or City of Reading.  
(K) Landscaping and Buffer Yards:  The following provisions shall apply: 
(1) The following buffer yard requirements shall apply to an off-street parking area or off-
street loading area: 
(a)  A six (6) foot wide buffer strip shall be provided along the perimeter of an off-
street parking area containing ten (10) or more parking spaces or off-street

loading area, which is adjacent to an existing residential use or residential

(b) A three (3) foot wide buffer strip shall be provided along the perimeter of an 
off-street parking area containing less than ten (10) parking spaces, which is 
located adjacent to an existing residential use or residential zoning district. 
(c) In lieu of a landscaped buffer strip, a four (4) foot high privacy fence may be 
provided along the perimeter of an off-street parking area or an off-street 
loading area.  The privacy fence shall comply with the provisions established 
under Part 13 of the Zoning Ordinance. 
(d) A combination of a landscaped buffer strip and fencing may be considered in 
order to complement or enhance the design. 
(e) The landscaping and buffer enhancements shall not obstruct the required 
sight distance or within the clear sight triangle. 
(2) A 180 square foot curbed or raised landscaped island shall be incorporated into the 
off-street parking and/or loading areas in order to create a defined green space at 
intervals of 12 consecutive space or 108 foot intervals.  The landscaped island may 
be designed as a rain garden or stormwater management facility. 
(3) The use of existing healthy mature trees (12 inches or more in caliber) is encouraged 
and may be utilized as part of the required buffer yard. 
(4) The landscaping requirements and buffer yard shall be comprised of trees, shrubs 
and other plantings that will establish a complete visual barrier within two (2) years of 
the initial planting. 
(5) The landscaping design of the buffer yard and/or fence shall be subject to the review 
and approval of the Zoning Office. 
(L) Exterior Lighting:  The following provisions shall apply: 
(1) Exterior lighting shall be required for off-street parking areas involving twenty (20) or 
more parking spaces and/or off-street loading areas involving ten (10) or more 
spaces. 
(2) The illumination levels and spillage impacts shall comply with the exterior lighting 
provisions established by Section 600-912 of the Zoning Ordinance. 
(3) The exterior illumination levels shall be reduced by 50 percent between 10:00 pm and 
7:00 am

§600-1605: Emergency Management and Response Standards 
(A) External Access Points:  All means of ingress and/or egress shall be located, designed and 
constructed in order to accommodate traffic in a safe and efficient manner.  The landowner

devices as well as the design and construction of additional travel lanes or auxiliary lanes, 
which may be required by the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation or City of Reading. 
(B) Internal Access Drives and Lanes: The width of aisles within the off-street parking facilities 
shall comply with the minimum requirements established under Part 16 of this Zoning

all end spaces.

the provisions established under Section 600-906 of this Zoning Ordinance. 
(D) Fire Lanes:  If required by the Fire and Rescue Department or by the Building and Trades 
Department, a fire lane shall be established within the property containing the off-street

(E) Emergency Response and Accessibility:  The internal access lanes or aisles serving the off-
street parking area and/or loading area shall be subject to the review and approval of the Fire 
and Rescue Department.

§600-1608: Electric Vehicle Charging Stations (EVCS) 
(A) Electric Vehicle Charging Stations (EVCS) shall be permitted as an accessory or subordinate

(1) The EVCS shall be located within a building (principal or accessory) occupied by the 
principal use or within the parking or loading area for the principal use. 
(2) The EVCS shall be located at least ten (10) feet from the street right-of-way line and 
all other property lines. 
(3) Parking spaces containing an EVCS shall count towards the minimum required 
number of parking spaces for the principal use(s) contained on the property. 
(4) The EVCS may be leased or utilized to other users during non-operational hours 
designated by the principal use. 
(5) The EVCS shall be clearly marked or identified with signage. 
(6) The EVCS shall be outfitted with a standard EVCS connection or plug-in. 
(7) The EVCS spaces shall not exceed 125 percent of the total number of off-street 
parking or loading spaces required for the proposed use. 
(B) A decommissioning plan and agreement shall be established and endorsed as part of the 
zoning permit for the EVCS. 
§600-1609: Non-Motorized Vehicle Stations (NMVS) 
(A) Non-Motorized Vehicle Stations (ENMVS) shall be permitted as an accessory or subordinate 
use by right within all zoning districts, subject to the following provision: 
(1) The NMVS may be located within a building (principal or accessory) occupied by the 
principal use or within the parking area for the principal use.  Alternative locations 
shall be subject to the review of the Zoning Office. 
(2) The NMVS shall be located at least ten (10) feet from the street right-of-way line and 
all other property lines. 
(3) The NMVS may be located on a space dedicated or reserved for an off-street parking 
provided that 90 percent of the remaining parking spaces remain available for the 
principal use(s) contained on the property. 
(4) The NMVS may be leased or utilized to other users during non-operational hours 
designated by the principal use. 
(5) The NMVS shall be clearly marked or identified with signage. 
(B) A decommissioning plan and agreement shall be established and endorsed as part of the 
zoning permit for the NMVS.

(B) Permitted Uses:  The following principal and accessory uses may be contained within the 
public or private parking garage: 
(1) The principal use shall be the public or private parking garage, which shall include the 
driveways, access lanes, parking spaces and all structural components of the garage. 
(2) Additional permitted principal uses may be attached to the public or private parking 
garage provided that such principal uses are permitted by right within the underlying 
zoning district and that the use can be independently operated as a functional 
principal use with access to the street level and parking garage. 
(3) The accessory uses of the public or private parking garage may include the following: 
administrative offices, maintenance facilities; storage facilities; security areas and 
facilities; work stations; toll booths, elevators; stairs; pedestrian crossing areas; 
vending machines, concession stands; snack bars; newsstands; government 
installations; and/or other similar areas.  The total gross floor area for all permitted 
accessory uses shall not exceed twenty (20) percent of the total gross floor area of 
the public or private garage. 
(C) External Dimensional Requirements:  The following provisions shall apply to a public or 
private parking garage: 
(1) The minimum lot area shall be 20,000 square feet. 
(2) The minimum lot width shall be two hundred (200) feet. 
(3) All uses, buildings and/or structures associated with the garage shall be located at 
least ten (10) feet from the street right-of-way line and all property lines. 
(4) No more than ninety (90) percent of a lot shall be covered by any combination of 
buildings, structures and impervious surfaces. 
(5) Unless otherwise permitted, the maximum height of the garage shall be sixty (60) feet, 
as measured from the average ground elevation to the highest point of the building or 
structure. 
(6) All other uses and site improvements shall comply with the bulk and height 
requirements, which are further specified by the appropriate zoning district to which 
the garage is located. 
(D) Internal Dimensional Requirements:  The following provisions shall apply to the aisle width, 
parking space and clearance levels within a public or private parking garage: 
(1) The vehicular aisle or access lanes located within the garage shall comply with the 
following criteria: 
(a) The minimum aisle width shall be at least twenty (20) feet in width in order to 
accommodate two (2) way travel conditions within the garage. 

41 
 
(b) The minimum aisle width shall be at least fourteen (14) feet in width in order 
to accommodate one (1) way travel conditions within the garage. 
(c) The minimum aisle width for entrance and exit lanes shall be a ten (10) feet in 
order to accommodate one (1) way travel, which shall be delineated by curbing 
or markers. 
(2) The individual parking spaces contained within the garage shall comply with the 
following criteria: 
(a) At least 80 percent of the parking spaces shall have a minimum rectangular 
area of 162 square feet, which shall have a minimum width of 9 feet and a 
minimum length of 18 feet. 
(b) A maximum 20 percent of the parking spaces may be reduced in order to 
accommodate compact vehicles with a minimum rectangular area of 128 
square feet, which shall have a minimum width of 8 feet and a minimum length 
of 16 feet. 
(c) Angled spaces will need to be longer in order to achieve the rectangular shape 
with the minimum dimensions. 
(d) Handicapped parking spaces shall comply with the provisions established 
under Section 600-1603.D of this Zoning Ordinance. 
(3) The clearance level within the garage area shall not be less than eight (8) feet, 
as measured from the parking deck floor to the bottom of any support structure 
or ceiling within all deck levels. 
(4) A public or private parking garage may include a section designated for EVCS 
and/or NMVS, as permitted under Part 16 of this Zoning Ordinance. 
(5) A ten (10) percent reduction may be permitted to the internal dimensional 
requirements specified under Section 600-1610(D) of this Zoning Ordinance 
provided that engineering studies can support the reduction without creating 
safety issues within the garage.  All such reductions may be authorized by the 
Zoning Hearing Board as part of a special exception application. 
(E) General Requirements:  The following general provisions shall also apply to a public or 
private parking garage: 
(1) The garage shall be serviced by public sanitary sewer facilities and public water 
supply facilities. 
(2) All exterior speaker, microphone or intercom systems shall be designed in a manner 
so that the messages, music or other sounds shall be reduced by seventy (70) percent 
at any street line or property line. 
(3) Any booths or other structures utilized for the collection of admission and/or parking 
fees shall be setback and arranged to prevent vehicle backups on adjoining roads 
during peak arrival periods.  Any other collection of fees, such as by roaming parking 
lot attendants, shall be conducted in such a manner as to prevent vehicle backups on 
adjoining roads. 

42 
 
(4) The landowner and operator of the garage shall be responsible to resolve, mitigate 
and/or correct any traffic congestion problems that occur on public roads, which are 
related to ingress and egress to the garage. 
(5) The use and related activities shall not emit noise, glare, vibration, electrical 
disturbance, electromagnetic interference, dust, smoke, fumes, toxic gas, radiation, 
heat and/or other perceptible or objectionable nuisances that would impact 
neighboring properties, or be noticeable at or beyond the property line. 
(6) The applicant shall furnish evidence that the proposed use will not be detrimental to 
the use of adjoining properties due to hours of operation, noise, light, dust, smoke, 
litter and pollution. 
(7) All points of vehicular ingress and egress shall comply with the provisions established 
by Chapter 515 and 600 of the City of Reading Code. 
(8) Internal circulation shall allow parking surfaces to be level (without any slope) along 
each parking garage facade adjacent to a public street or public space. All ramps 
between levels shall be located along building facades that are not adjacent to a 
public street or public space, or shall be located internally so that they are not visible 
from adjacent public streets or public spaces. 
(9) A pedestrian accessibility plan shall be developed and implemented to include 
linkages, connections and cross-walks within the neighborhood.  
(10) The location of elevators and stairs shall be highlighted through the use of 
architectural features or changes in façade colors, textures, or materials so that 
visitors can easily identify these entry points. 
(11) The interior parking garage lighting shall not produce glaring sources toward adjacent 
properties while providing safe and adequate lighting levels. The use of sensor 
dimmable LEDs and white stained ceilings are recommended to control light levels 
on-site while improving energy efficiency. 
(12) A streetscape enhancement plan shall be developed and implemented in order to 
consider street trees, landscaping and lighting along all public streets.  
(F) Plans and Permits:  All public and private parking garages shall be subject to the following 
criteria: 
(1) All required site plans, building plans and structural improvements shall be designed 
and sealed by a professional engineer licensed within the Commonwealth of 
Pennsylvania. 
(2) A Land Development Plan shall be submitted to the Planning Commission. 
(3) All plans and permits shall be subject to the review and approval by the Zoning Office, 
Planning Office, Building and Trades Department, Fire and Rescue Department, 
Public Works Department, and Police Department.

RESOLUTION NO. _____ - 2026 
 
A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF READING, 
BERKS COUNTY, PENNSYLVANIA, DECLARING THAT A PORTION OF 
THE CITY OF READING ZONING ORDINANCE IS SUBSTANTIVELY 
INVALID DUE TO A LACK OF ESTABLISHED RULES AND 
REGULATIONS GOVERNING DATA CENTER AND SIMILAR USES AND, 
AS A RESULT THEREOF, INVOKING A 180-DAY MORATORIUM AND 
MUNICIPAL CURE PERIOD PURSUANT TO SECTION 609.2 OF THE 
PENNSYLVANIA MUNICIPALITIES PLANNING CODE IN ORDER TO 
ADDRESS THE INVALIDITY AND PREPARE A CURATIVE 
AMENDMENT TO THE CITY ZONING ORDINANCE. 
 
WHEREAS, the Zoning Ordinance of the City of Reading currently lacks any rules or regulations 
governing data center and similar uses; and 
 
WHEREAS, because of this, the City Council of the City of Reading has determined that a 
portion of the Zoning Ordinance of the City of Reading is substantively invalid because it does not 
define, permit or regulate data center and similar uses; and 
 
WHEREAS, section 609.2 of the Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning Code authorizes a 
municipality to self-declare its zoning ordinance substantively invalid in order to fix regulatory gaps 
such as missing provisions for data centers and similar uses while shielding itself from competing 
landowner permit requests and/or challenges for 180 days; and 
 
WHEREAS, the City Council of the City of Reading believes that it is in the best interests of the 
City of Reading to now invoke the municipal curative amendment process to address data centers and 
similar uses.   
 
NOW, THEREFORE, THE COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF READING HEREBY RESOLVES, that, 
pursuant to and in accordance with section 609.2(1) of the Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning Code, 
the City Council of the City of Reading hereby declares that certain portions of the City of Reading 
Zoning Ordinance, as more specifically set forth below, are substantively invalid due to a lack of any 
rules or regulations governing data center and similar uses and proposes to prepare with the City of 
Reading Planning Commission a curative amendment to overcome such invalidity; and  
 
 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the City Council of the City of Reading hereby declares the 
following portions of the City of Reading Zoning Ordinance as substantively invalid: 
 
1. Section 600-2202 because and solely to the extent that it does not contain any defined 
terms regarding data center(s), data center accessory use(s) and/or similar uses, provided 
that all other portions of Section 600-2022 are substantively valid; 

75 
 
 
2. Part 8 of Section 600 because and solely to the extent that it does not provide for data 
center(s), data center accessory use(s) and/or similar uses, to be allowed by right, special 
exception or conditional use in any of the established zoning districts, provided that all 
other portions of Part 8 of Section 600 are substantively valid; and 
 
3. Part 11 of Section 600 and Section 600-1203 because and solely to the extent that they 
do not contain any defined performance standards, requirements or other conditional use 
standards to govern data center(s), data center accessory use(s) and/or similar uses, 
provided that all other portions of Part 11 of Section 600 are substantively valid. 
 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that, pursuant to and in accordance with section 609.2 of the 
Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning Code, the City Council of the City of Reading, together with the 
City of Reading Planning Commission, shall, within 180 days from the date of this Resolution, initiate 
and enact a municipal curative amendment to the City of Reading Zoning Ordinance in order to cure 
the declared invalidity to the portions of the City of Reading Zoning Ordinance that do not address data 
center and similar uses; and 
 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that, during the aforementioned 180-day period, the City of 
Reading shall not be required to entertain or consider any zoning permit application, zoning approval or 
landowner curative amendment (filed under section 609.1 of the MPC or otherwise) for any use or 
development related to a data center, a data center accessory use or any similar use(s).
