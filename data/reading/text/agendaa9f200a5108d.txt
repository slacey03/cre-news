Per Jaime and Ray's request, I have attached the multi-year fiscal model examining our 2027 
draft budget through 2030, specifically analyzing real estate tax sensitivity and structural 
budget drivers. 
 
Across all models, baseline city expenditures remain fixed, expanding from $129.25M in the 
2027 draft budget to $150.25M by 2030. This represents an organic growth of roughly $7.0M 
annually, driven primarily by contractual salaries and wages (assumed at 4.5% annual growth 
with re-funded vacancies), fringe benefits (compounding at 9.0% annually to $27.39M by 2030), 
pensions (rising 5.0% annually to $22.29M), and overtime (modeled at 5.0% annual growth). 
Debt service also peaks in 2029 at $14.36M per our existing amortization schedule. 
 
Against these escalating fixed commitments, baseline municipal revenues remain relatively flat, 
creating a persistent structural deficit that standard property tax adjustments cannot solve: 
 
- Under a 1.0% annual increase, millage rises from 19.409 to 19.997 mills, but the deficit deepens 
from $12.20M in 2027 to $29.51M by 2030. 
- Under a 2.5% annual increase, millage moves to 21.211 mills by 2030, leaving a $27.93M 
shortfall. 
- Under a 3.0% annual increase, millage reaches 21.630 mills by 2030, leaving a $27.39M deficit. 
- Under a 5.0% annual increase, millage reaches 23.358 mills by 2030, yet the deficit still grows 
from $11.20M in 2027 to $25.15M by 2030. 
 
Because a single-digit property tax hike yields between $275K and $1.4M in new revenue each 
year, it simply cannot absorb an annual $7.0M expenditure escalation. 
 
The model demonstrates the impact if the city attempted to close the budget gap solely through 
property tax increases: 
- 2027 would require an immediate 50.0% tax hike, increasing millage from 19.217 to 28.826 
mills. 
- 2028 would require an additional 12.63% increase to 32.467 mills. 
- 2029 would require an 18.95% increase to 38.619 mills. 
- 2030 would require another 10.78% increase to 42.782 mills. 
 

 
Page4 
In total, relying strictly on property taxes would force millage to more than double over four 
years (a 122.6% cumulative increase), which is economically untenable for Reading residents 
and businesses.  
 
As mentioned in the 2026 budget review and now in the 2027 budget review the takeaway is 
clear: long-term structural balance cannot be achieved through millage adjustments alone. It 
will require targeted vacancy reviews, adjusting headcount, strict overtime management, 
healthcare cost containment, and broader tax-base growth.

PROCLAMATIONS AND COMMENDATIONS.  
Citizens, businesses, and organizations can be honored with (i) a Proclamation or 
Commendation at Council Meetings or by Councilors at events, or (ii) letter(s) of 
congratulations or recognition delivered directly to the recipient. Staff from the City Clerk’s 
office can prepare Proclamations, Commendations, and Certificates of Recognition to assist 
Council to honor people, businesses, events, etc. in accordance with this Policy  
PROCLAMATIONS - Proclamations are formal public statements or announcements and are 
generally issued by the Mayor. (I.E. Proclaiming the month of February to be Black History 
Month). Proclamations can also be issued by Council, as a whole body, if they are adopted by 
resolution at a public meeting.  
COMMENDATIONS - Commendations are official awards given to show high approval or 
expressing favorable opinion and are generally given by individual Council member(s) to 
persons or entities within their respective Council district. Commendations can also be issued 
by Council, as a whole body, if they are adopted by resolution at a public meeting. All 
commendations are signed by the Council President and can include the name of the councilor 
making the request - for very significant events all seven members of Council can be included. 
This category of commendations also includes “certificates of commendation or recognition” 
which are less formal and can be signed by the individual councilor making the request 
provided that it otherwise complies with this policy. A certificate of recognition is a tangible 
acknowledgment of someone’s effort, accomplishment, or positive behavior in a particular 
context vs a commendation or proclamation which is a formal recognition of someone’s lifetime 
or career achievements  
• Council staff keeps a schedule of Commendations and Proclamations that are regularly 
presented by Council or individual Council member(s).  
 
LETTERS OF CONGRATULATIONS OR RECOGNITION –  

 
Page7 
Council staff from the City Clerk’s office will help a Council member or Council, as a whole 
body, to prepare letters that relate to City or Individual Council District issues that require 
intervention by the State, Federal or other governmental entities (I.E. letter to the State Attorney 
General to address potential criminal or civil matters outside the jurisdiction of the City). If a 
district councilor reports or requests a matter to be handled to Council Staff that is outside 
his/her district to address hereunder, he/she must copy the district councilor on the  
email and be subject to item #2 below. As a safety net, Council Staff shall include both 
Councilors on all responses.  
Letters or notes that provide congratulations, express support, express sympathy, recognition, 
etc. shall be written by the Councilor individually on letterhead or on a City notecard. Each 
Councilor shall be provided with a batch of notecards, refills are available, and individual 
letterhead for his/her own individual use in accordance with this policy.  
Requested letters of support for grant funding or other financial support must be approved and 
signed by the Council President and the requesting Council member. If Council President 
objects to approval, the matter shall be submitted to Council, as a body, to decide by majority 
vote at its next regular or special voting meeting.  
REQUESTS FOR PROCLAMATIONS, COMMENDATIONS OR LETTERS HEREUNDER 
SHALL COMPLY WITH THE FOLLOWING:  
1. Submissions or requests from Council member(s) requiring assistance from Council 
Staff must be sent to the City Clerk first, who can then determine who the assignment will be 
directed to.  
 
2. If an individual Council member requests a proclamation, commendation or letter hereunder 
regarding a matter, issue, person or entity that is outside the requesting Councilor’s district, 
he/she must refer the issue to the proper Councilor first in writing who can then join in the 
matter and direct the City Clerk staff, if appropriate, to issue the request in the individual or 
both Councilors’ names. In the event that the resident District Councilor fails to respond within 
48 hours from the date of the written notification, it will be assumed that the District Councilor 
does not want to join in the matter and it can be issued with just the endorsement and signature 
of the original requesting Council member.  
 
1. City postage can be used for individual District-event related correspondence or 
correspondence that provides Council, as a whole body, support. But, City postage may cannot 
be used for letters or notes that provide congratulations, express support, express sympathy, etc. 
provided that the substance of the matter does not include campaign-related, partisan, political 
or other personal purposes of the Council member, and such correspondence– shall be written

by the Councilor individually on letterhead or on a City notecard. Postage used hereunder shall 
be utilized in accordance with any budgetary limits established by Council on an annual basis.  
 
 
1. Requests hereunder must be submitted via email to the City Clerk at least 7 business 
days in advance of the date needed for the proclamation, commendation or letter. The City
