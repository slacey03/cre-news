RULES FOR PUBLIC PARTICIPATION AT COUNCIL MEETINGS 
Public comment at all meetings of the City Council of the City of Reading shall be governed by the following: 
• Residents and/or taxpayers of the City who wish to make or submit public comment must pre-register 
utilizing one of three (3) following options: 
1. By calling (610-655-6205) or emailing (council@readingpa.gov) the City Clerk's Office by 4 
pm on the day of the meeting with your name, address and the subject matter or topic to be 
discussed. If you are not able to attend in-person, instructions to access the virtual meeting app 
or dial-in telephone number will be provided. 
2. By submitting written public comment to be read into the record at the meeting by submitting 
an email to council@readingpa.gov by 4pm on the day of the meeting. The email message must 
clearly be marked as Public Comment.  
3. By registering in-person at the Council Chambers on the sign-up sheet found on the podium in 
Council Chambers between 5:00 p.m. and 7:00 p.m. on the day of the scheduled meeting, with 
your name, address and the subject matter or topic to be discussed.  
• No person shall be permitted to speak who is not a resident or taxpayer of the City, except upon permission 
of the President of Council or a majority of City Council.  
• Public comment will occur only during the Public Comment period listed on the meeting agenda at which 
time the Presiding Officer will recognize speakers and written comment that properly registered in the order 
received.  
• All public comment shall be delivered from the podium and speakers may not approach the Council tables 
at any time. 
• Those individuals registered to speak on agenda matters will be allotted no more than 5 minutes and those 
individuals registered to speak on non-agenda matters will be allotted no more than 3 minutes.  The City 
Clerk will control a timer to monitor these time periods.  When the alarm rings, a speaker must immediately

conduct of the business of the meeting or promotion of candidates at an upcoming election, or otherwise

the record.

will be deemed to have forfeited their right to public comment and the President of Council or a majority 
of City Council may direct the City Clerk to turn off the microphone or cease reading the comment into the 
record, have the individual removed from Council Chambers and/or cited.

BACKGROUND 
Approval is requested for the purchase of the RC Mower R-60 to support vegetation management within the Mount 
Penn Preserve and Neversink Mountain. The mower’s remote-operation capabilities will significantly reduce the time 
required to maintain steep slopes and dense brush areas that are currently labor-intensive to manage.  
This equipment will also improve worker safety by eliminating the need for staff to perform manual cutting on 
hazardous terrain, reducing the risk of injuries associated with slope work and heavy brush clearing. The RC Mower 
R-60 will provide a more efficient, safer, and sustainable method for maintaining these high-priority public lands.

A. INFRARED PAVING RESTORATION AT ADA RAMP IMPROVEMENTS 
A1. DESCRIPTION - This work is the use of infrared technology to eliminate visible seams at pavement 
restoration associated with the installation of ADA curb ramps.  It is the City’s preference to have all ADA 
ramp construction completed before the adjacent roadway is paved, however, infrared repairs are to be used in 
areas where full-width paving at the adjacent roadway is not included in the contract or has been completed as 
part of the contract prior to the construction of the ADA ramp. 
A2. CONSTRUCTION – Use truck mounted or walk-behind infrared equipment to heat asphalt joint and 
then rake and compact to create a thermally bonded seam.  Do not use sealer. 
A3. MEASUREMENT AND PAYMENT - This work is incidental to the construction of concrete curb 
ramps.

Part 16:  Off-Street Parking and Loading 
§600-1601: General Application Criteria 
(A) Application:  Off-street parking and/or loading facilities shall be located, designed, constructed and 
maintained in accordance with the provisions of this Zoning Ordinance.  These facilities shall be 
provided whenever the following actions occur: 
(1) A new building has been constructed or a new use has been established in accordance with 
the provisions of this Zoning Ordnance. 
(2) The use of the building or property has been changed, altered or expanded, which requires 
additional off-street parking spaces to accommodate the use. 
(3) An off-street parking facility shall include the driveways, access drives, access isles, parking 
spaces and loading spaces. 
(B) Land Use Compliance:  Parking and loading areas shall be a permitted use as follows: 
(1) Principal Use:  The following off-street parking area and/or off-street loading area 
requirements shall apply if the off-street parking and/or off-street loading area is classified 
as the principal use on the property: 
(a) An off-street parking area containing ten (10) or more parking spaces, which are 
designated as the principal use shall only be permitted provided that such use is 
permitted within the underlying zoning district.  All such off-street parking areas shall 
comply with the lot area, dimensional and coverage requirements, as specified by 
the underlying zoning district. 
(b) An off-street parking area containing less than ten (10) parking spaces, which are 
designated as the principal use shall only be permitted provided that such use is 
permitted within the underlying zoning district.  All such off-street parking areas shall 
comply with: the minimum lot area requirement; the dimensional setback 
requirements may be reduced to three (3) feet from all property lines; and the 
maximum impervious coverage may be increased to 95 percent of the lot area. 
(c) An off-street loading area designated as the principal use shall only be permitted 
provided that such use is permitted within the underlying zoning district.  All such 
off-street loading areas shall comply with the lot area, dimensional and coverage 
requirements, as specified by the underlying zoning district. 
(2) Accessory Use:   An off-street parking area and/or off-street loading area may be permitted 
as an accessory use to a compatible principal use, subject to the provisions specified by 
this Zoning Ordinance. 
(3) Satellite Parking Spaces:  Off-street parking spaces may be established or credited at a 
conforming off-site or off-premises location, subject to the provision specified by this Zoning 
Ordinance. 
(4) On-Street Spaces:  Unless otherwise permitted by the City of Reading, all on-street parking 
spaces and/or on-street loading spaces shall not be counted or credited as a required 

39 
 
parking or loading space.  All such spaces shall comply with the provisions established by 
the City of Reading Code.  
(C) Location:  The following provisions shall apply to the location of off-street parking and/or loading 
areas: 
(1) Unless otherwise permitted, off-street parking and loading spaces shall be located on the 
same lot as the principal use.  
(2) All parking and loading facilities shall be arranged and marked to provide safe and orderly 
movement without disrupting traffic or moving other vehicles. 
(3) Unless otherwise permitted by the Public Works Department, the design of parking areas 
for all uses shall be such to prevent the back-up of vehicles onto a public street and that no 
portion of any parked vehicle extends over any property line. 
§600-1602: Design and Construction Standards 
(A) Accessibility:  All off-street parking and loading spaces shall have access to a public street by 
means of an access lane, driveway, alley or road that complies with the provisions specified by 
Chapters 515 and 600 of the Code. 
(B) Size:  Off-street parking and loading spaces shall comply with the following criteria: 
(1) Off-Street Parking Spaces:  The spaces shall have a rectangular shape that complies with 
following dimensional requirements: 
(a) Within an off-street parking lot, each parking space for motor vehicles (except those 
spaces dedicated for use by handicapped persons) shall have a minimum 
rectangular area of 162 square feet, which shall have a minimum width of 9 feet and 
a minimum length of 18 feet have a minimum area of 162 square feet with a 
minimum width of 9 feet and a minimum length of 18 feet.  Angled spaces will need 
to be longer in order to achieve the rectangular shape with the minimum dimensions. 
(b) Where parallel parking is permitted, the parking spaces shall be delineated by 
painted lines and shall have a minimum width of 8 feet, as measured from the curb 
or cartway edge and a minimum length of 20 feet, as measured along the curb or 
cartway edge. 
(2) Off-Street Loading Spaces:  The spaces shall have a rectangular shape that complies with 
following dimensional requirements: 
(a) Off-street loading spaces with a length of sixty (60) feet, width of fourteen (14) feet, 
and clearance height of fifteen (15) feet for commercial and industrial uses. 
(b) Off-street loading spaces with a length of forty-five (45) feet, width of twelve (12) 
feet, and clearance height of twelve (12) feet may be permitted for commercial, 
institutional and light industrial uses provided the landowner or applicant can 
demonstrate that the use will not require larger trucks to access the site for loading 
and unloading activities.  
(C) Grade:    The off-street parking and loading areas shall be properly graded to provide convenient 
vehicular access and proper drainage and shall be maintained in usable condition.  The minimum 

40 
 
grade of areas used for parking shall be at least one (1) percent and the maximum grade shall not 
exceed five (5) percent. 
(D) Internal Access Lanes: The width of aisles within the off-street parking and loading areas shall 
comply with the minimum requirements specified in Table 1602-A.

(E) Composition:  All proposed driveways, access lanes, aisles, fire lanes, parking areas and loading

(1) Parking lots with less than ten (10) spaces shall be paved with an all-weather surface within

(2) Parking lots with ten (10) or more spaces shall be paved with an all-weather surface within

Plan, as further specified under Chapter 515 of the Code. 
(3) All loading spaces shall be paved with an all-weather surface within twelve (12) months 
from the date of an approved Zoning Permit. 
(4) Temporary parking or loading areas may be permitted for a period of time that does not

by the Public Works Department and that a temporary permit is issued by the Zoning Office.  
The temporary status of the parking or loading area may be extended for an additional 
twelve (12) month period provided that a land development plan is submitted to the Planning 
Commission for review and consideration.   
(5) All temporary or permanent areas designated for parking and loading shall be maintained 
in a safe, accessible, stabilized and mud-free condition, as approved or permitted by the 
Public Works Department or Zoning Office. 
(F) Curbs, Guiderails and Wheel Stops:  The following provisions shall apply: 
(1) The perimeter of the off-street parking and/or loading area shall be curbed for all projects 
exceeding ten (10) or more parking spaces and/or five (5) or more off-street loading spaces.

(2) The curb radius within the off-street parking area and/or loading area shall be a minimum 
of five (5) feet. 
(3) Curbs or wheel stops shall be utilized to define the limits or depth of the off-street parking 
and/or loading area. 
(4) Guiderails and/or retaining walls may be required in order to compensate or protect 
designated areas with topographical relief exceeding 15 percent slope.  The guiderail and/or 
retaining wall shall be subject to the review and approval of the Public Works Department. 
(G) Pedestrian Accessibility and Ramps:    The off-street parking area and/or loading area shall include 
sidewalks, pedestrian paths, stairways and/or access ramps in order to accommodate internal and 
external pedestrian movements.  All such improvements shall be subject to the review and approval 
of the Public Works Department. 
(H) Pavement Markings:  All parking facilities shall be adequately marked and maintained for the 
purpose of defining parking spaces and interior drives.  All pavement markings shall be consistent 
with those utilized by the City of Reading and the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation. 
(I) Signs:  All traffic control signs within the off-street parking area shall be located, designed and 
installed as per the provisions specified by the City of Reading and the Pennsylvania Department 
of Transportation. 
(J) Plan and Permit Requirements:  The following planning and permitting requirements may apply for 
off-street parking and loading areas: 
(1) Zoning Permit:  A zoning permit issued by the Zoning Office shall be issued for all proposed 
off-street parking areas and off-street loading areas. 
(2) Land Development Plan:  A Land Development Plan shall be prepared and submitted for 
site improvements involving ten (10) or more parking spaces and/or five (5) or more off-
street loading spaces.  If the combination of site improvements exceed 2,500 square feet 
of impervious surface or 800 square feet of building surface, a Land Development Plan shall 
be required. 
(3) Stormwater Management Plan:  If the combination of site improvements exceeds 2,500 
square feet of impervious surface, a Stormwater Management Plan shall be prepared and 
submitted with the Land Development Plan. 
(4) Earth Disturbance Activities:  If the combination of site improvements exceeds 5,000 square 
feet of earth disturbance activities, an Erosion and Sedimentation Control Plan shall be 
prepared and submitted with the Land Development Plan. 
(5) Other Permits:  If the proposed site improvements encroach or traverse a street right-of-
way for use and occupancy, a Highway Occupancy Permit (HOP), Driveway Permit and/or 
Utility Permit may be required via the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation and/or 
City of Reading.  
(K) Landscaping and Buffer Yards:  The following provisions shall apply: 
(1) The following buffer yard requirements shall apply to an off-street parking area or off-street 
loading area:

(a)  A six (6) foot wide buffer strip shall be provided along the perimeter of an off-street 
parking area containing ten (10) or more parking spaces or off-street loading area, 
which is adjacent to an existing residential use or residential zoning district. 
(b) A three (3) foot wide buffer strip shall be provided along the perimeter of an off-street 
parking area containing less than ten (10) parking spaces, which is located adjacent 
to an existing residential use or residential zoning district. 
(c) In lieu of a landscaped buffer strip, a four (4) foot high privacy fence may be provided

(d) A combination of a landscaped buffer strip and fencing may be considered in order

(e) The landscaping and buffer enhancements shall not obstruct the required sight

(2) A 180 square foot curbed or raised landscaped island shall be incorporated into the off-
street parking and/or loading areas in order to create a defined green space at intervals of 
12 consecutive space or 108 foot intervals.  The landscaped island may be designed as a 
rain garden or stormwater management facility. 
(3) The use of existing healthy mature trees (12 inches or more in caliber) is encouraged and 
may be utilized as part of the required buffer yard. 
(4) The landscaping requirements and buffer yard shall be comprised of trees, shrubs and other

(5) The landscaping design of the buffer yard and/or fence shall be subject to the review and

established by Section 600-912 of the Zoning Ordinance.

§600-1605: Emergency Management and Response Standards 
(A) External Access Points:  All means of ingress and/or egress shall be located, designed and 
constructed in order to accommodate traffic in a safe and efficient manner.  The landowner or 
developer shall be responsible for the purchase and installation of any traffic control devices as 
well as the design and construction of additional travel lanes or auxiliary lanes, which may be 
required by the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation or City of Reading. 
(B) Internal Access Drives and Lanes: The width of aisles within the off-street parking facilities shall 
comply with the minimum requirements established under Part 16 of this Zoning Ordinance.  All 
dead-end parking lots shall be designed to provide sufficient back-up area for all end spaces. 
(C) Emergency Access:  All off-street parking and loading areas shall be designed to consider the 
provisions established under Section 600-906 of this Zoning Ordinance. 
(D) Fire Lanes:  If required by the Fire and Rescue Department or by the Building and Trades 
Department, a fire lane shall be established within the property containing the off-street parking 
area and/or loading area. 
(E) Emergency Response and Accessibility:  The internal access lanes or aisles serving the off-street 
parking area and/or loading area shall be subject to the review and approval of the Fire and Rescue 
Department. 
(F) Sight Distance:  The provisions established under Section 600-1303 of the Zoning Ordinance and 
Section 515-502.F of the Subdivision and Land Development Ordinance shall apply to the 
specifications for sight distance, as required by the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation or 
by the City of Reading Code. 
(G) Clear Sight Triangles:  The provisions established under the provisions established under Section 
600-1303 of the Zoning Ordinance and Section 515-502.F of the Subdivision and Land 
Development Ordinance shall apply to the specification for clear sight triangles. 
§600-1606: Common or Shared Parking Facilities 
(A) Application:  Common or shared parking facilities or areas may be permitted for two (2) or more 
permitted uses, subject to the following requirements: 
(1) The permitted uses are adjacent or adjoining to one another. 

50 
 
(2) The common off-street parking spaces are capable of accommodating the combined 
parking requirements that are specified by the permitted uses. 
(3) The use of a common or shared parking area shall be permitted by the Zoning Office as 
part of a Zoning Permit Application and/or the Planning Commission as part of a Subdivision 
Plan or Land Development Plan. 
(B) Site Accessibility:  The following documents shall be established and recorded: 
(1) Access Easement: The record owners of the properties shall prepare and submit written 
documentation of the continued availability of the shared parking arrangement to the Zoning 
Office, Public Works Department and/or Planning Commission.  The limits of the access 
easement shall consider all pedestrian and vehicular traffic movements between the street 
right-of-way, the permitted uses and the internal common or shared off-street parking 
facility. 
(2) Driveway Easement:  If required, a shared or common driveway easement shall be 
established in order to permit access from the public street to the common or shared off-
street parking area.  
(3) Stormwater Easement:  If required, a stormwater management easement shall be 
established for the use, occupancy and maintenance responsibilities that may pertain to the 
common or shared parking area. 
(4) Utility Easement:  If required, a utilities easement shall be established for the use, 
occupancy and maintenance responsibilities that may pertain to the common or shared 
parking area. 
(5) Legal Description:  All easements shall be accurately described by bearings and distances, 
which are referenced within an agreement and depicted on a plan.  If permitted, a blanket 
easement may be considered as an alternative for the description. 
(C) Modifications or Termination:  The common or shared parking agreement may be modified or 
terminated at the direction of the Zoning Office and/or Public Works Department if the common or 
shared parking facilities should create the following conditions: traffic congestion; traffic hazards; 
overflow parking conditions; and/or threats pedestrian safety. 
(D) Car Pool Parking Facility:   All common parking areas classified as “car pool facilities” shall comply 
with the design requirements established under Part 16 of the Zoning Ordinance.  All such facilities 
shall be subject to the review and approval of the City of Reading. 
(E) Valet Parking Service:  All permitted uses may utilize valet service as a parking option at a 
designated on-site parking space on the property or a designated off-site parking space at a 
permitted public or private parking facility.  All such arrangements shall be subject to the review 
and approval of the Zoning Office.

§600-1608: Electric Vehicle Charging Stations (EVCS) 
(A) Electric Vehicle Charging Stations (EVCS) shall be permitted as an accessory or subordinate use 
by right within all zoning districts, subject to the following provision: 
(1) The EVCS shall be located within a building (principal or accessory) occupied by the 
principal use or within the parking or loading area for the principal use. 
(2) The EVCS shall be located at least ten (10) feet from the street right-of-way line and all 
other property lines. 
(3) Parking spaces containing an EVCS shall count towards the minimum required number of 
parking spaces for the principal use(s) contained on the property. 
(4) The EVCS may be leased or utilized to other users during non-operational hours designated 
by the principal use. 
(5) The EVCS shall be clearly marked or identified with signage. 
(6) The EVCS shall be outfitted with a standard EVCS connection or plug-in. 
(7) The EVCS spaces shall not exceed 125 percent of the total number of off-street parking or 
loading spaces required for the proposed use. 
(B) A decommissioning plan and agreement shall be established and endorsed as part of the zoning 
permit for the EVCS. 
§600-1609: Non-Motorized Vehicle Stations (NMVS) 
(A) Non-Motorized Vehicle Stations (ENMVS) shall be permitted as an accessory or subordinate use 
by right within all zoning districts, subject to the following provision: 
(1) The NMVS may be located within a building (principal or accessory) occupied by the 
principal use or within the parking area for the principal use.  Alternative locations shall be 
subject to the review of the Zoning Office. 
(2) The NMVS shall be located at least ten (10) feet from the street right-of-way line and all 
other property lines. 
(3) The NMVS may be located on a space dedicated or reserved for an off-street parking 
provided that 90 percent of the remaining parking spaces remain available for the principal 
use(s) contained on the property. 
(4) The NMVS may be leased or utilized to other users during non-operational hours 
designated by the principal use. 
(5) The NMVS shall be clearly marked or identified with signage. 
(B) A decommissioning plan and agreement shall be established and endorsed as part of the zoning 
permit for the NMVS. 
§600-1610: Public or Private Parking Garages 
(A) Public or private parking garages shall be permitted as follows: 
(1) A public parking garage shall be permitted by right within the Commercial Core (C-C) Zoning 
District. 

52 
 
(2) A public or private parking garage shall be permitted by right within the Manufacturing 
Commercial (M-C), Heavy Manufacturing (H-M) and Municipal (MU) Zoning Districts and 
the Institutional (INS) Overlay District. 
(3) A public or private parking garage shall be permitted by special exception within the 
Commercial Residential (C-R), Commercial Neighborhood (C-N) and Commercial Highway 
(C-H) Zoning Districts. 
(B) Permitted Uses:  The following principal and accessory uses may be contained within the public or 
private parking garage: 
(1) The principal use shall be the public or private parking garage, which shall include the 
driveways, access lanes, parking spaces and all structural components of the garage. 
(2) Additional permitted principal uses may be attached to the public or private parking garage 
provided that such principal uses are permitted by right within the underlying zoning district 
and that the use can be independently operated as a functional principal use with access 
to the street level and parking garage. 
(3) The accessory uses of the public or private parking garage may include the following: 
administrative offices, maintenance facilities; storage facilities; security areas and facilities; 
work stations; toll booths, elevators; stairs; pedestrian crossing areas; vending machines, 
concession stands; snack bars; newsstands; government installations; and/or other similar 
areas.  The total gross floor area for all permitted accessory uses shall not exceed twenty 
(20) percent of the total gross floor area of the public or private garage. 
(C) External Dimensional Requirements:  The following provisions shall apply to a public or private 
parking garage: 
(1) The minimum lot area shall be 20,000 square feet. 
(2) The minimum lot width shall be two hundred (200) feet. 
(3) All uses, buildings and/or structures associated with the garage shall be located at least ten 
(10) feet from the street right-of-way line and all property lines. 
(4) No more than ninety (90) percent of a lot shall be covered by any combination of buildings, 
structures and impervious surfaces. 
(5) Unless otherwise permitted, the maximum height of the garage shall be sixty (60) feet, as 
measured from the average ground elevation to the highest point of the building or structure. 
(6) All other uses and site improvements shall comply with the bulk and height requirements, 
which are further specified by the appropriate zoning district to which the garage is located. 
(D) Internal Dimensional Requirements:  The following provisions shall apply to the aisle width, parking 
space and clearance levels within a public or private parking garage: 
(1) The vehicular aisle or access lanes located within the garage shall comply with the following 
criteria: 
(a) The minimum aisle width shall be at least twenty (20) feet in width in order to 
accommodate two (2) way travel conditions within the garage. 

53 
 
(b) The minimum aisle width shall be at least fourteen (14) feet in width in order to 
accommodate one (1) way travel conditions within the garage. 
(c) The minimum aisle width for entrance and exit lanes shall be a ten (10) feet in order 
to accommodate one (1) way travel, which shall be delineated by curbing or 
markers. 
(2) The individual parking spaces contained within the garage shall comply with the following 
criteria: 
(a) At least 80 percent of the parking spaces shall have a minimum rectangular area of 
162 square feet, which shall have a minimum width of 9 feet and a minimum length 
of 18 feet. 
(b) A maximum 20 percent of the parking spaces may be reduced in order to 
accommodate compact vehicles with a minimum rectangular area of 128 square 
feet, which shall have a minimum width of 8 feet and a minimum length of 16 feet. 
(c) Angled spaces will need to be longer in order to achieve the rectangular shape with 
the minimum dimensions. 
(d) Handicapped parking spaces shall comply with the provisions established under 
Section 600-1603.D of this Zoning Ordinance. 
(3) The clearance level within the garage area shall not be less than eight (8) feet, as 
measured from the parking deck floor to the bottom of any support structure or 
ceiling within all deck levels. 
(4) A public or private parking garage may include a section designated for EVCS 
and/or NMVS, as permitted under Part 16 of this Zoning Ordinance. 
(5) A ten (10) percent reduction may be permitted to the internal dimensional 
requirements specified under Section 600-1610(D) of this Zoning Ordinance 
provided that engineering studies can support the reduction without creating safety 
issues within the garage.  All such reductions may be authorized by the Zoning 
Hearing Board as part of a special exception application. 
(E) General Requirements:  The following general provisions shall also apply to a public or private 
parking garage: 
(1) The garage shall be serviced by public sanitary sewer facilities and public water supply 
facilities. 
(2) All exterior speaker, microphone or intercom systems shall be designed in a manner so that 
the messages, music or other sounds shall be reduced by seventy (70) percent at any street 
line or property line. 
(3) Any booths or other structures utilized for the collection of admission and/or parking fees 
shall be setback and arranged to prevent vehicle backups on adjoining roads during peak 
arrival periods.  Any other collection of fees, such as by roaming parking lot attendants, 
shall be conducted in such a manner as to prevent vehicle backups on adjoining roads. 

54 
 
(4) The landowner and operator of the garage shall be responsible to resolve, mitigate and/or 
correct any traffic congestion problems that occur on public roads, which are related to 
ingress and egress to the garage. 
(5) The use and related activities shall not emit noise, glare, vibration, electrical disturbance, 
electromagnetic interference, dust, smoke, fumes, toxic gas, radiation, heat and/or other 
perceptible or objectionable nuisances that would impact neighboring properties, or be 
noticeable at or beyond the property line. 
(6) The applicant shall furnish evidence that the proposed use will not be detrimental to the use 
of adjoining properties due to hours of operation, noise, light, dust, smoke, litter and 
pollution. 
(7) All points of vehicular ingress and egress shall comply with the provisions established by

Part 10 Accessory Uses 
§ 600-1014. Honeybee Apiaries. 

66 
 
 
It is recognized that honeybees are beneficial to humankind and to Pennsylvania in particular by providing 
both home garden and agricultural pollination services as well as furnishing honey, beeswax, and other useful 
products. 
 
Honeybee apiaries are permitted by right as an accessory use in all Zoning Districts when in compliance with 
the Pennsylvania Bee Law (3 Pa. C.S.A § 2101 et. seq., as amended) and subject to the following regulations: 
 
A. Permits and Certification 
 
(1) No beekeepers may own or maintain an apiary within the City of Reading without first registering and 
maintaining a current permit for all apiaries with the Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture, Bureau 
of Plant Industry as required by the Pennsylvania Bee Law, 3 Pa. C.S.A § 2101 et. seq., as amended. A 
copy of the Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture apiary permit shall be supplied to the City of 
Reading as part of the beekeeping zoning permit application. 
 
(2) Beekeepers who own or maintain apiaries within the City of Reading that are pre-existing prior to the 
enactment of this ordinance shall apply for and receive a zoning permit within six (6) months of 
ordinance enactment. They shall meet all requirements of this ordinance unless stated otherwise within 
this ordinance. 
 
(3) No beekeeper shall own or maintain an apiary within the City of Reading without first obtaining a 
zoning permit. An application for a zoning permit shall be made in writing and upon supplied form or 
in such format as established by the City of Reading. The application shall be accompanied by a site 
plan that includes the lot dimensions, the location and number of hives, the location of the water 
source, the distance of the hives from any property lines, and, if required, the location of any flyway 
barriers. The issuance of a permit shall not obviate the necessity for compliance with all other 
municipal ordinances. 
 
(4) A beekeeper owning or maintaining an apiary in the City of Reading shall promptly notify the Zoning 
Administrator without unnecessary delay, and in no event no longer than 72 hours, if the Pennsylvania 
Department of Agriculture, Bureau of Plant Industry revokes said apiary registration or if said 
registration has lapsed. 
 
(5) Beekeepers who wish to own or maintain an apiary on property that they do not own must include 
written permission from the property owner or landlord that explicitly indicates that the beekeeper has 
permission to own or maintain an apiary on the subject property. Such written permission shall be 
supplied to the City of Reading as part of the beekeeping zoning permit application. 
 
B. Location and Colony Density. 
 
Placement of an apiary on a residential property should conform to the following regulations to minimize and 
eliminate any possible concerns to adjoining neighbors. 
 
(1) Hive Location and Density. Location of hives must comply with the following criteria: 
 
(a) Hives shall not be located within ten (10) feet of any side or rear property line unless a flyway 
barrier is in place or the hive(s) are located at least ten (10) feet above grade. 

67 
 
 
(b) Hives shall not be located within a front yard. 
 
(c) Hives shall not be located within fifty (50) feet of a pre-existing, permitted swimming pool regulated 
by the Zoning Ordinance, or a pre-existing permitted kennel, to include animal shelters for pets. 
 
(d) Apiaries are not permitted within ten (10) feet of any buildings located on adjacent properties. 
 
(2) Maximum Number of Hives.  
 
(a) For a property with a minimum of 2,000 square feet of lot area, a beekeeper is permitted to keep 
two (2) hives. For each additional 2,000 square feet of lot area, the beekeeper is permitted two (2) 
additional hives. 
 
(b) Exceptions to Maximum Number of Hives.  
 
(i) For a property with less than 2,000 square feet of lot area, a beekeeper is permitted to keep 
one (1) hive provided they receive signed consent from all adjacent residents. This is only 
required at the time of the initial permit. 
 
(ii) As part of normal honey bee colony management, a beekeeper may also keep, in addition to 
allowable standard hives, for up to 45 days between April 15 and August 15, two (2) nucleus 
colonies per standard hive, provided they are used for managing colony strength, to 
minimize reproductive swarming, queen rearing, or swam capture. 
 
(iii) For each allowed hive, a single nucleus hive may be kept from August 16 to April 14 to 
allow a beekeeper to mitigate winter bee losses. 
 
(iv) Apiaries that are pre-existing prior to enactment of this ordinance shall not be subject to the 
limitations of Section B.(2) and shall not exceed the number of hives active at the time of the 
ordinance if they apply for and receive a zoning permit within six (6)  months of ordinance 
enactment. The apiaries shall be confirmed by the pre-existing apiary registration of the 
location as reported by the Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture. 
 
C. Signage. 
 
Beekeepers shall post informative signage, provided with the zoning permit, in a place that is visible to the bee-
facing public while bees are on the property. 
 
D. Hive Type, Orientation, and Maintenance 
 
(1) All beekeepers shall comply with rules and regulations set forth by the Pennsylvania Bee Law, 3 Pa. 
C.S.A. § 2101 et seq., as amended. 
 
(2) All beekeepers shall, to the best of their ability, maintain their colonies per the Voluntary Best 
Management Practices for Maintaining European Honeybee Colonies in the Commonwealth of 
Pennsylvania as provided and amended by the Pennsylvania Apiary Advisory Board. 
 

68 
 
(3) To the extent possible, hive entrances shall face away from the closest neighboring property and in 
such a direction that the bees fly across the beekeeper’s property at sufficient distance to gain a height 
of at least the six feet at the property line. The use of barriers may be employed to redirect the bees’ 
flight pathway and establish bee flight pathways above six feet. Should the flight path not be able to be 
obtained as described above, then a flyway barrier shall be placed at least six feet in height, shall be 
placed along side of the hive(s) that contains the entrance to the hive(s), shall be located within five feet 
of the hive(s), and shall extend at least two feet on either side of the hive(s). A flyway barrier shall 
consist of a fence, vegetation, hedge, or a combination thereof. No flyway barrier is required for hive(s) 
that are located greater than ten feet from property lines or on porches, balconies or roof tops that are 
at least ten feet above grade except on adjacent properties where such porch, balcony, or roof is located 
less than ten feet from a property line. Flyway barriers constructed of vegetation shall be reviewed and 
approved by the City Arborist prior to the issuance of a zoning permit. 
 
(a) Exceptions to flyway barrier: a flyway barrier is not required if the property adjoining the apiary lot 
line is: 
 
(i) undeveloped; or 
(ii) zoned agricultural, industrial, or is outside municipal limits; or 
(iii) is a natural park or conservation area and has no pre-existing human or horse trails located 
with twenty-five feet of the property line. 
 
(4) A supply of fresh water shall be maintained in a location readily accessible to all bee colonies on the 
site throughout the day to prevent bees from congregating at neighboring swimming pools or other 
sources of water on nearby properties between April 1 and November 1. 
 
(5) All beekeepers shall ensure that no bee comb or other materials that attract honeybees are left upon the 
ground of the apiary site. Upon removal from the apiary, all such materials shall be properly 
maintained in a sealed container or placed within a building or other bee proof enclosure, so long as 
bees are kept on the property. 
 
E.  Inspection. 
 
If an inspection is required as a result of a nuisance complaint, the Zoning Office will inspect the existence 
and location of the hive only. The Zoning Office may notify the responsible State agency and request that they 
follow up on the nuisance complaint. 
 
F.  Nuisance. 
 
It shall be unlawful for any beekeeper to keep any hive in such a manner as to cause any unhealthy condition 
or purposefully interfere with the normal use of adjoining properties. By way of example and not limited to, 
the following activities are hereby declared a nuisance and therefore unlawful: 
 
(1) The use of receptacles for honeybees that does not comply with the Pennsylvania Bee Law 3 Pa. C.S.A. 
§ 2101 et seq., as amended. 
 
(2) Hive placement and related bee movement such that the bees, without provocation, interfere with the 
reasonable freedom of movement of persons in a public right of way, or the location of bees have 
proven impact to the general safety, health, and welfare of the general public. 

69 
 
 
G.  Other Permits. 
 
If the Apiary operator plans to sell honey, they shall obtain all required City approvals, shall obtain all 
required City approvals, including a Health Permit, a Business Privilege License, and a Zoning Permit if the 
operator plans to sell honey. Any sales of honey from the residential dwelling unit shall comply with the home 
occupation requirements, as applicable, in § 600-1006. 
 
Part 22 Definitions 
§ 600-2207. Beekeeping definitions. 
 
APIARY – any place where one or more colonies of bees are kept at a single location. 
 
BEEKEEPER – A person who owns or has charge of one or more colonies of honey bees. 
 
BEE – any stage of the honeybee (Apis mellifera). 
 
COLONY – an aggregate of honeybees consisting of workers, drones, and a queen. 
 
FLYWAY BARRIER – a wall, fence, vegetation, hedge, or combination thereof that forces bees to fly at a 
higher elevation above ground level over the property lines in the vicinity of the apiary. 
 
HIVE – a receptacle or container, that includes modern moveable frames or combs, in which an active colony 
inhabits and exceeds a volume of 13 gallons (i.e., a single standard Langstroth 9 5/8 inch deep body with ten 
frames plus one additional hive body). 
 
HONEYBEE – all life stages of the common domestic honeybee, Apis mellifera species (European Honey 
bee). 
 
NUCLEUS COLONY – A hive that does not exceed the volume of 13 gallons (i.e., a single ten frame standard 
9 5/8 inch deep body or less).

§ 560-101. Purpose; effect on other ordinances. 
A. This chapter is adopted to: 
(1) Assist the City of Reading in protecting the public health, safety and welfare; 
(2) Monitor the number of defaulted, foreclosed and/or vacant buildings and structures in the City; 
(3) Assess the effects of the conditions of those buildings on nearby businesses, buildings, structures, 
properties and the neighborhoods in which they are located, particularly in light of the fire safety 
hazards and unlawful, temporary occupancy by transients, including illicit drug users and traffickers; 
(4) Require the owners of such defaulted, foreclosed or vacant buildings and structures to register and 
pay related fees; and  
(5) Promote substantial efforts to rehabilitate such vacant buildings and structures. 
B.  The information, including photographs of the registered properties, shall be accessible to the public through 
the City’s website to further encourage property owners to maintain and repair their properties. 
C. The provisions of this chapter are in addition to and not in lieu of any and all other applicable 
provisions of the Codified Ordinances of the City of Reading and all relevant policies, procedures 
and/or regulations adopted pursuant thereto.

§ 560-102. Definitions. 
For the purposes of this chapter, where terms are not defined, such terms shall have ordinarily 
accepted meanings such as the context implies. The following words shall have the meanings 
respectively ascribed to them as follows: 
 
ABANDONED REAL PROPERTY — Any real property located in the City, whether vacant or 
occupied, that is in default on a mortgage, has had a "lis pendens" action filed against it by the lender 
holding a mortgage on the property, is subject to an ongoing foreclosure action by the lender, is subject 
to an application for a tax deed or 
pending tax assessor's lien sale or sheriff's sale of real estate or has been transferred to the lender under 
a deed in lieu of foreclosure. The designation of a property as "abandoned" shall remain in place until 
such time as the property is sold or transferred to a new owner, the foreclosure action has been 
dismissed, and any default on the 
mortgage has been cured. 
 
ACTIVELY MARKETING — Unless considered as "abandoned real property" as defined above, an 
owner who, individually or through an agent, has placed a "for sale" or "for lease" sign on the property 
with accurate contact information which is also provided to the Property Maintenance Division, and 
has done at least one 
of the following.  The property may be placed “for sale” or “for lease” for a maximum of six months, and the 
City can provide no more than two 90-day extension perods. 
A. Engaged the service of a real estate licensee, whose name, address, telephone number and e-
mail will be provided to the Property Maintenance Division. 
B. Listed the property in the Multiple Listing Service (MLS); 
C. Distributed printed advertisements. 
 
BOARDED — A building, structure or dwelling unit subject to the provisions of this chapter if in place 
of one or more exterior doors, other than storm door, or of one or more windows there is a sheet or 
sheets of plywood or similar material covering the space for such door or window. 
 
DEFAULT — A mortgagee has initiated forbearance and/or foreclosure proceedings against the 
mortgagor. 
 
DEFAULT — The mortgagor has not complied with the terms of the mortgage on the property, or the 
promissory note, or other evidence of the debt, referred to in the mortgage. 
 
DWELLING UNIT — A single unit providing complete, independent living facilities for one or more 
persons, including permanent provisions for living, sleeping, eating, cooking and sanitation. 
 

73 
 
FORECLOSURE or FORECLOSURE ACTION — The legal process by which a mortgagee, or other 
lienholder, terminates or attempts to terminate a property owner's equitable right of redemption to 
obtain legal and equitable title to the real property pledged as security for a debt or the real property 
subject to the lien. The legal process is 
not concluded until the property obtained by the mortgagee, lienholder, or their designee, by 
certificate of title, or any other means, is sold to a nonrelated bona fide purchaser in an arm's-length 
transaction to satisfy the debt or lien. 
 
MORTGAGEE — Any bank, mortgage company, lender, partnership, firm or corporation having a 
legal or equitable interest in the property; or is the entity noted in documents recorded in the official 
records of the state, county or municipality as holding title to the property; or otherwise having control 
of the property, including the 
holder of a mortgage, guardian of the estate of any such person, and the executor or administrator of 
the estate of such person if ordered to take possession of real property by a court. 
 
NOTICE — Written notice of a violation or requirement under this chapter mailed, pursuant to first-
class mail, to the last known address of the owner of the property in question. 
 
OCCUPIED — Unless considered as "abandoned real property" as defined above, any building or 
structure where one or more persons actually conducts a lawful business or resides in all or any part of 
the building or structure as the legal business-occupant, or as the legal or equitable owner/occupant or 
tenant on a permanent, non-transient 
basis, or any combination of the same. For purposes of this chapter, evidence offered to prove that a 
building or structure is so occupied may include, but shall not be limited to, the regular receipt of 
delivery of first-class mail through the U.S. Postal Service; proof of continual telephone, electric, gas, 
heating, water, trash/recycling and sewer services; a valid City business license; or the most recent 
federal, state or City income tax statements indicating that the subject property is the official business 
or residence address of the person or business claiming occupancy; or current written leases indicating 
current occupancy of the property. 
 
OPEN — A building or structure where any one or more exterior doors, other than a storm door, is 
broken, open and/or closed, but without a properly functioning lock to secure it, or if one or more 
windows are broken or not capable of being locked and secured from intrusion or any combination of 
the same. 
 
OWNER — Any person, agent, operator, partnership, firm or corporation having a legal or equitable 
interest in the property; or is the person or entity noted in documents recorded in the official records of 
the state, county or municipality as holding title to the property; or otherwise having control of the 
property, including the guardian 
of the estate of any such person, and the executor or administrator of the estate of such person if 
ordered to take possession of real property by a court.

PUBLIC NUISANCE — A public nuisance is: 
A. Any building or structure which because of physical condition, use or occupancy 
is considered an attractive nuisance to children or other unauthorized person. 
B. Any building or structure which because it is dilapidated, unsanitary, unsafe, 
insect or vermin infested or lacking in the facilities and equipment required by the City of Reading 
Codified Ordinances has been designated by the City as a public nuisance or certified as blighted. 
C. Any building or structure which is a fire hazard or is otherwise dangerous to the safety of 
person or property. 
D. Any building or structure from which water, plumbing, heating, sewage or other facilities have 
been disconnected, destroyed, removed or ineffective so that the property creates a hazard to 
neighboring properties. 
E. Any building or structure which by reason of neglect or lack of maintenance has become a place 
for accumulation of trash and debris or a haven for rodents and other vermin. 
F. Any building or structure which lacks an operable fire-protection system meeting the 
requirements of all applicable provisions of the City of Reading Codified Ordinances. 
G. Any building or structure which as a result of its dilapidated, unsanitary, unsafe, insect or 
infested condition creates a danger or a risk of danger to a neighboring property. 
H. Any real property located in the City, whether vacant or occupied, that is encumbered by a 
mortgage in default, is subject to an ongoing foreclosure action by the mortgagee or trustee, has 
been the subject of a foreclosure action by a mortgagee or trustee and a judgment has been entered, 
or has been the subject of a foreclosure sale where the title was transferred to the beneficiary of a 
mortgage involved in the 
foreclosure and any properties transferred under a deed in lieu of foreclosure/sale. The designation 
of a "default/foreclosure" property as "registrable" shall remain in place until such time as the 
property is sold to a nonrelated bona fide purchaser in an arm's-length transaction or the 
foreclosure action has been dismissed and any default on the mortgage has been cured; or 
I. Any property that is vacant for more than 30 days or any cancellation of utility or 
service, whichever occurs first. 
 
REAL PROPERTY—Any residential or commercial land and/or buildings, leasehold improvements 
and anything affixed to the land, or portion thereof identified by a property parcel identification 
number, located in the City limits. 
 
REGISTRABLE PROPERTY—Any building or structure which because of physical condition or use 
has been declared a public nuisance in accordance with the local housing, building, plumbing, fire and 
related codes. 
 
SEMIANNUAL REGISTRATION — Six months from the date of the first action that requires 
registration, as determined by the Property Maintenance Division Manager, or its designee, and every

VACANT — Any building or structure that is not legally occupied and where the owner is not actively 
marketing the building or structure. 
 
§ 560.103. Applicability; registration. 
A. The requirements of this chapter shall be applicable to all abandoned real property and every 
vacant building and structure in the City of Reading where the owner is not actively marketing the 
building or structure and the building or structure is not boarded and each owner of any building, 
whether governmental, commercial, 
residential or institutional or owned by a nonprofit or a for-profit organization. 
B. Registration shall be required for all abandoned real property and any vacant buildings and 
structures, whether vacant and secure, vacant and open, or vacant and boarded where the mortgagee 
or owner is not actively marketing the building or structure. 
 
§ 560.104. Registration for abandoned real property. 
A. Any mortgagee who holds a mortgage on real property located within the City of Reading shall 
perform an inspection of the property to determine vacancy or occupancy, upon default by the 
mortgagor. The mortgagee shall, within 10 days of the inspection, register the property with the 
Property Maintenance Division, on forms or the website application provided by the City, and indicate 
whether the property 
is vacant or occupied. A separate registration is required for each property, whether it is found to be 
vacant or occupied. 
 
B. If the property is occupied but remains in default, it shall be inspected by the mortgagee or his 
designee monthly until a) the mortgagor or other party remedies the default or b) it is found to be 
vacant or shows evidence of vacancy, at which time it is deemed abandoned, and the mortgagee shall, 
within 10 days of that inspection, update the property registration to a vacancy status on forms 
provided by the City. 
 
C. Registration pursuant to this section shall contain the name of the mortgagee and the server, the 
direct mailing address of the mortgagee and the server, a direct contact name and telephone number 
for both parties, e-mail address for both parties, the PIN or tax number, and the name and twenty-
four-hour contact phone number of the property management company responsible for the security 
and maintenance of the property. 
 
D. At the time of initial registration, each registrant shall pay a nonrefundable semiannual registration 
fee of $200 for each defaulted property. Subsequent semiannual registrations of defaulted properties 
and fees in the amount of $200 are due within 10 days of the expiration of the previous registration. 
Said fees shall be used to offset the costs of:  

76 
 
1) registration and registration enforcement,  
2) code enforcement and mitigation related to defaulted properties, and  
3) for any related purposes as may be adopted in the policy set forth in this chapter. Said fees 
shall be deposited to a special account in the City's department dedicated to the cost of 
implementation and enforcement of this chapter, and fulfilling the purpose and intent of this 
chapter.  
 
E. Each individual property on the registry that has been registered for 12 months or more prior to the 
effective date shall have 30 days to renew the registration and pay the $200 semiannual registration 
fee. Properties registered less than 12 months prior to the effective date shall renew the registration 
every six months from the expiration of the original registration renewal date and shall pay the $200.  
 
F. If the defaulted mortgage and/or servicing on a property is sold or transferred, the new mortgagee is 
subject to all the terms of this chapter. Within 10 days of the transfer, the new mortgagee shall register 
the property or update the existing registration. The previous mortgagee(s) will not be released from 
the responsibility of paying all previous unpaid fees, fines, and penalties accrued during that 
mortgagee's involvement with the defaulted property.  
 
G. If the mortgagee sells or transfers the defaulted property in a non-arm's-length transaction to a 
related entity or person, the transferee is subject to all the terms of this chapter. Within 10 days of the 
transfer, the transferee shall register the property or update the existing registration. Any and all 
previous unpaid fees, fines, and 
penalties, regardless of who the mortgagee was at the time registration was required, including but not 
limited to unregistered periods during the foreclosure process, are the responsibility of the transferee 
and are due and payable with the updated registration. The previous mortgagee will not be released 
from the responsibility of paying all previous unpaid fees, fines, and penalties accrued during that 
mortgagee's involvement with the defaulted property.  
 
H. If the defaulted property is not registered, or the registration fee is not paid within 30 days of when 
the registration or renewal is required pursuant to this section, a late fee equivalent to 10% of the 
semiannual registration fee shall be charged for every thirty-day period, or portion thereof, the 
property is not registered shall be due and payable with the registration.  
 
I. All registration fees must be paid directly from the mortgagee, servicer, trustee, or owner. Third-
party registration fees are not allowed without the consent, in writing, of the City and/or its authorized 
designees. 
 
J. This section shall also apply to properties that have been the subject of a foreclosure sale where the 
title was transferred to the beneficiary of a mortgage involved in the foreclosure and any properties 
transferred under a deed in lieu of foreclosure/sale.

§ 560.105. Registration statement for vacant property not subject to default.  
 
A. Any owner of vacant property located within the City shall, within 10 days after the property 
becomes vacant, or within 10 days after assuming ownership of the property, whichever is later, 
register the real property with the Property Maintenance Division. 
 
B. Initial registration pursuant to this section shall contain, at a minimum, the name of the owner, the 
mailing address of the owner (PO Box numbers are prohibited), e-mail address and telephone number 
of the owner, and, if applicable, the name and telephone number of the property manager and said 
person's address (PO Box numbers are prohibited), e-mail address, and telephone number. 
 
C. At the time of initial registration, each registrant shall pay a nonrefundable semiannual registration 
fee of $200 for each vacant property. Subsequent semiannual registrations of vacant properties and fees 
in the amount of $200 are due within 10 days of the expiration of the previous registration. Said fees 
shall be used to offset the costs of:  
1) registration and registration enforcement,   
2) code enforcement and mitigation related to vacant properties, and  
3) for any related purposes as may be adopted in the policy set forth in this chapter. 
 
Said fees shall be deposited to a special account in the City's department dedicated to the cost of 
implementation and enforcement of this chapter, and fulfilling the purpose and intent of this chapter. 
 
D. Each individual property on the registry that has been registered for 12 months or more prior to the 
effective date shall have 30 days to renew the registration and pay the $200 semiannual registration 
fee. Properties registered less than 12 months prior to the effective date shall renew the registration 
every six months from the expiration of the original registration renewal date and shall pay the $200. 
 
E. If the property is sold or transferred, the new owner is subject to all the terms of this chapter. Within 
10 days of the transfer, the new owner shall register the vacant property or update the existing 
registration. The previous owner(s) will not be released from the responsibility of paying all previous 
unpaid fees, fines, and penalties accrued during that owner's involvement with the vacant property. 
 

78 
 
F. If the vacant property is not registered, or either the registration fee or the semiannual registration 
fee is not paid within 30 days of when the registration or semiannual registration is required pursuant 
to this section, a late fee equivalent to 10% of the semiannual registration fee shall be charged for every 
thirty-day period, or 
portion thereof, the property is not registered and shall be due and payable with the registration. This 
section shall apply to the initial registration and registrations required by subsequent owners of the 
vacant property. 
 
G. Properties subject to this section shall remain subject to the semiannual registration requirement, 
and the inspection, security, and maintenance standards of this section, as long as the property is

H. Failure of the owner to properly register or to modify the registration to reflect a change of

I. If any property is in violation of this chapter, the City may take the necessary action to ensure

J. Properties registered as a result of this section are not required to be registered again pursuant to the 
defaulted mortgage property section. 
 
§ 560.106. Local agent. 
If none of the persons listed as an owner, mortgagee, executor, administrator, trustee, or principal on 
the registration statement has an address more than 25 miles from the City of Reading, the registration 
statement also shall provide the name, address, 
telephone number and e-mail address of a person who resides within a 25-mile radius of the City of 
Reading and who shall be designated by the owner as the responsible local agent who is authorized to 
accept service of process, notices, statements, invoices and other communications resulting from 
and/or relating to this chapter on behalf of the owners. The local agent shall be responsible for, 
including, but not limited to, providing City of Reading Property Maintenance personnel with access 
to the building or structure for the purposes of making inspections, receiving notification in 
the event of an emergency affecting the public health, safety or welfare and for purposes of service of 
any and all notices or registration statements as herein authorized and in connection herewith. The 
owner and local agent 
shall be jointly and severally legally responsible for compliance with the City of Reading Codified 
Ordinances and the laws of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania with regard to the registered 
property.

§ 560.111. One-time waiver of registration fee.  
A one-time, one-year waiver of the semiannual registration fee may be granted by the Property 
Maintenance Division Manager upon application of the mortgagee or owner, and upon review and 
advice of the Solicitor's Office, within 10 business days of the date of the bill for the annual registration 
fee, of the mortgagee or owner:

A. Demonstrates with satisfactory proof to the Property Maintenance Division or Department of Fire

§ 560.112. Decision on application for waiver. 
Within 30 days after the waiver application is received by the Property Maintenance Division, and 
upon review by the Division and upon the advice of the Solicitor's Office, the Property Maintenance

owner may seek an appeal by filing an appeal request to the Code and License Appeals Board by filing 
an application and fee as per new section § 212-104 Appeals with the Property Maintenance Division, 
in writing, within 10 business days of receipt of the denial. 
 
§ 560.113. Semiannual inspections.  
A. Semiannual inspections. An inspection of the registered abandoned real estate or vacant building 
may be performed by the Property Maintenance Division semiannually or as necessary. If violations 
are identified, the Property Maintenance Division shall issue a notice of violation as per the 
requirements of the City of Reading's Property Maintenance Code or Fire Code and give notice to the 
mortgagee or owner to comply with the codes with a reasonable time to cure. If the violations are not 
corrected within the time given, the property shall be declared a public nuisance in accordance with 
this 
chapter. 
B. Complaint semiannual inspections. Nothing in this section shall preclude a Property Maintenance 
Inspector from performing a semiannual inspection upon receipt of a complaint of violation on any 
City ordinance. 
C. Right of entry. Code officials are authorized to make semiannual inspections at any reasonable hour 
to determine compliance with the aforementioned codes. Every mortgagee, owner, local agent, or 
operator shall allow inspectors, officers or representatives of the Property Maintenance Division or 
Department of Fire and Rescue Services unrestricted access. 
D. Administrative Search warrant. If any mortgagee, owner, local agent or other person in charge of a 
building or structure subject to the provisions of this chapter refuses, impedes, inhibits, interferes with, 

81 
 
restricts or obstructs entry and access to every part of the structure or premises where semiannual 
inspection authorized by this chapter 
is sought, the appropriate City Property Maintenance Inspector may apply for an administrative 
search warrant for a semiannual inspection to a court of competent jurisdiction and shall supply all 
necessary affidavits and testimony to indicate that there is a reasonable or probable cause to conduct 
such a semiannual inspection. 
E. Notice. All notices scheduling a semiannual inspection shall be mailed via first-class mail to the 
owner of record, with a copy mailed first-class mail to the local agent. 
F. Failure to appear for a semiannual inspection. If the mortgagee, owner or his/her local agent cannot 
be available at the proposed time, said mortgagee, owner or local agent shall provide no less than 24 
hours' written notice to the Property Maintenance Division. Upon failure to give such written notice or 
upon failure to gain entry, an administrative fee of $75 will be assessed against the owner. For each 
rescheduling beyond the second rescheduling, an administrative fee in City Code Chapter 212, Section 
128 B shall be assessed. Failure to pay the administrative fee shall, at the discretion of the Property 
Maintenance Division Manager, constitute a violation of 
this chapter and/or the applicable City code. Failure of a mortgagee, owner or his/her local agent to 
appear for a scheduled inspection shall be considered probable cause for obtaining an administrative 
search warrant to inspect the premises. 
G. Special requested inspections. Whenever an owner, mortgagee, purchaser or other interested party 
requests a special inspection of a property regulated by this chapter, or a certification that a property is 
in compliance with this chapter or other applicable City ordinances pertaining to structural condition, 
if such request is made in connection with the sale, conveyance, transfer, financing or refinancing of 
such property, the requestor 
shall pay a fee in City Code Chapter 212, Section 128 A  to the City of Reading to defray the expenses 
of making such inspection, which fee may be changed from time to time by ordinance of City Council. 
If an inspection or certification is requested under or pursuant to Pennsylvania Act 32 of 20182 or other 
Pennsylvania or federal law, the requestor shall pay the City all of the City's inspection costs and fees, 
including fees for attending depositions and hearings and other court proceedings, and any other fees 
authorized by such actual law, which fees and costs shall be established from time to time by 
ordinance of City Council. Any such inspection required under this section 
is not and shall not take the place of nor be the substitute for a prepurchase property inspection, and 
the City of Reading shall not be liable for any undiscovered problems with the real estate being 
inspected. Inspections held hereunder will determine compliance with the City's Property 
Maintenance Code and not the ICC 
Building Code. Moreover, no such inspection will include an analysis of the age of the property's 
systems and useful life. Should, however, any Property Maintenance Code violations be discovered 
during such inspection, the party requesting the inspection, along with the owners of the property, 
shall be held responsible for rectifying them and will be issued notices of violation in accordance with 
applicable City ordinances.

§ 560.114. Appeal rights. 
A. Registration fees. The owner shall have the right to appeal the imposition of registration fees by 
reason of the property being vacant, but not in default, to the Code and License Appeals Board upon 
filing an appeal in writing to the Property Maintenance Division no later than 10 business days after 
the date of the billing statement. On appeal, the owner shall bear the burden of providing satisfactory 
objective proof of occupancy.  
B. Public nuisance and exterior non-code compliance. The mortgagee or owner shall have the right to 
appeal the determination that a building or structure is a public nuisance or the exterior is non-code 
compliant to the Code and License Appeals Board upon filing an appeal in writing to the Property 
Maintenance Division no later than 10 business days after notification of said determination. On 
appeal, the mortgagee 
or owner shall bear the burden proving that the property is not a public nuisance and the exterior is 
code compliant. 
C. Fees. The fee for filing of an appeal to Code and License Appeal shall be $100 (new section § 212-
104. Appeal), which fee may be changed from time to time by ordinance of City Council. Failure to 
submit the appropriate fee with the request for an appeal shall result in automatic denial of the appeal. 
In addition to said fee, the appellant shall be responsible for all costs incurred to conduct a hearing 
beyond that covered by the fee, including advertising and legal fees. 
 
§ 560.115. Administration and enforcement. 
Administration and enforcement of this chapter shall remain under the control of the Property 
Maintenance Division and the Reading Department of Fire and Rescue Services. The City of Reading 
retains the right to engage the services of a third party to administer any or all of the provisions of this 
chapter. 
 
§ 560.116. Violations and penalties. 
A. Violations. The failure or refusal for any reason of any mortgagee, owner, or local agent of an owner 
acting on behalf of the owner to file a registration statement, to pay any fees required to be paid 
pursuant to the provisions of this chapter, or to appear for an inspection shall constitute a violation. 
The violations shall be deemed a strict liability offense. 
B. Prosecution of violations. The Building Code Official and/or the Building Trades Division or the Fire 
Marshal or their designee(s) shall have the authority to institute the appropriate proceedings at law or 
in equity, to correct or abate such violation of the provisions of this chapter or of the order or direction 
made pursuant thereto. Such authority shall include but not be limited to the institution of summary 
criminal proceedings via the issuance of a nontraffic citation in accord with the Pennsylvania Rules of 
Criminal Procedures in the court of appropriate jurisdiction. 
C. Penalties. Any person who shall violate any provision of this chapter shall, upon conviction thereof, 
be sentenced to pay a fine of not less than $500 nor more than $1,000 for the first two continual and 
uncorrected failure or refusal to register, or for each failure or refusal to pay, and not exceeding $5,000 
for the third and any subsequent continual and uncorrected failure or refusal to register or for each 
subsequent failure or refusal to pay, or imprisonment for any term not exceeding 90 days. Each day 

83 
 
that a violation of this chapter continues, or each section of this chapter which shall be found to have 
been violated, shall constitute a separate offense. 
D. Remediation of public nuisance. In addition to the fines as set forth above, the owner of the 
property can be required to immediately address and eliminate the public nuisance. The property owner 
will define his plan to remediate the public nuisance through the filing of a rehabilitation plan with Property 
Maintenance when the property is registered.  The Rehabilitation Plan shall contain the timeline to cure the 
public nuisance.  The City may approve one 30-day extension. Failure to do so within 15 business days of 
notification, weather permitting, will allow the City to proceed to hire a qualified contractor of its sole 
choosing to eliminate the public nuisance. The City shall bill the owner for said work, and payment 
will be due within 15 business days of the date of the invoice, or do the work itself. Failure of the 
owner to pay the City will constitute a debt due and owing to the City, and the City may either enter a 
lien on the property for the full amount invoiced, plus costs, or turn it over to a collection agency for 
receipt as provided by law. 
 
§ 560.117. Compliance with other codes. 
In no instance shall the registration of abandoned real estate or a vacant building and the payment of 
registration fees be construed to exonerate the owner, local agent or other responsible party from 
responsibility for compliance with any other City of Reading code or ordinance. 
 
§ 560.118. Nonexclusive remedies. 
The penalty, collection and lien provisions of this chapter shall be independent, non-mutually 
exclusive separate remedies, all of which shall be available to the City of Reading as may be deemed 
appropriate for carrying out the proposes of this chapter. The remedies and procedures provided in 
this chapter for violation hereof are not intended to supplant or replace to any degree the remedies and 
procedures available to the City of Reading in the case of a violation of any other City of Reading code 
or ordinance, whether or not such other code or ordinance is referenced in this chapter, and whether or 
not an ongoing violation of such other code or ordinance is cited as the 
underlying ground for a finding of a violation of this chapter. 
 
§ 560.119. Blighted property review. 
Nothing in this chapter shall be meant or utilized to override or circumvent a property under 
consideration, review and in the process of consideration by the City of Reading Blighted Property 
Review Committee. A property under a rehab plan agreement with the Property Maintenance Division 
or under agreement with the Reading Redevelopment Authority to be rehabilitated shall continue to 
be required to register as abandoned real estate or a vacant building or structure pursuant to this 
chapter.

A. Bill 62-2026 – amending the City Code Chapter 212-113 D Fire Fees by increasing the false alarm 
violation fee to $400 per occurrence Introduced at the July 27 regular meeting 
 
Councilor Ventura moved, seconded by Councilor Baez, to enact Bill 62-2026. 
 
Councilor Nunez moved, seconded by Councilor Baker, to amend Bill 62-2026 by increasing the fee 
by $100 to $500 per occurrence. 
 
Councilor Nunez stated that he is proposing this increase due to the severity of the false fire alarm 
problem placing residents and personnel and expensive equipment at risk. He noted that given the 
large number of false fire alarm calls, it is clear that the lower level sliding scale fines clearly have not 
worked to educate the community and he suggested that this larger fine will get the attention of 
individuals who are abusing the current system. 
 

103 
 
In response to a question from Councilor Miller, the Council Solicitor read from the ordinance that the 
sliding scale fines start with no charge for the first false alarm, $80 for the 2nd, $100 for the 3rd to $550 
for the 14th plus. 
 
Councilor Miller recalled seeing a comment on social media questioning how the false alarm fee would 
apply to smaller and larger residential rentals.  He suggested factoring in the responsibility the 
landlord has to the tenants.  He expressed the belief that this larger fine is more about the city’s 
response to false alarms re apparatus and personnel. He noted that a rental property about a block 
away from his home has a false alarm on average of once per month where three pieces of apparatus 
respond. He agreed with the increase of the fine. 
 
Council President Reed invited the Fire Marshal to participate in the discussion.  
 
The Fire Marshal stated that this change is being driven by several issues, one is the need to streamline 
the billing process.  Currently bills are generated quarterly by hand, and it is very difficult to manage 
due to the expansive number of fire calls and the need to track the number of false alarms per 
property. He stated that last week he reported that there were 300 false alarm calls in the past 90 days. 
He added that if a resident burns food and the alarm is triggered, that is not a false or needless alarm, 
as the alarm is doing what it is supposed to do.  
 
Streamlining this process will allow the false fire alarm billing to occur on a monthly by an outside 
billing company. 
 
Councilor Baez questioned going from a sliding scale where the first false alarm is free to a large fine 
per occurrence and how that will impact small businesses. He suggested relaxing the amount of the 
fine to provide people with the ability to adjust to the new model. 
 
Councilor Campos thanked the Fire Marshal for being present and participating in the discussion. She 
agreed with the need for the larger fine and streamlining the process to allow monthly billing.  She 
stated that an alarm triggered by cooking food will not be a false alarm, and she asked for an example 
of what will cause a false alarm. 
 
The Fire Marshal stated that false alarms can be triggered by dust or fumes from interior construction 
multiple times per day, lack of maintenance to the alarm system or an out of inspection system. He 
highlighted the appeal process starting with an email message and phone call which can result in a 
waiver of the fine, if the owner has made repairs to the system. He stated that the goal is to have a fire 
protection system that function properly.  
 
In response to a question from Councilor Baker, the Fire Marshal stated that fines in Scranton, 
Allentown and Bethlehem were reviewed and those fines ranged from $500 to $700. The Fire Marshal 

104 
 
added that overall he is unconcerned with the dollar value of the fine, as long as the fine is flat per 
occurrence which will allow easier tracking and more efficient billing  
 
Councilor Baker reiterated that the main goal is to reduce false fire alarms and encourage property 
owners to maintain their fire protection systems. He noted that freebies do not exist for many activities 
related to fines such as driving without a license, etc.  He asked Council to support the amendment 
proposed by Councilor Nunez.  
 
In response to a question from Councilor Baez, the Fire Marshal stated that most small business 
owners do not have fire protection systems and they are not contributing to the false alarm problem. 
He added that if they do have a system and receive a fine, they can undertake the appeal process. He 
noted that there will be interdepartmental training so officers understand the scope of this issue. 
 
Councilor Baez noted the need to find a way to collaborate to get business owners to cooperate rather 
than punish them with high fines. He noted his hope that this change will be the incentive to have 
maintained fire protection systems. 
 
Council President Reed agreed with the approach to reduce false alarms and encourage maintained 
systems.  
 
In response to a question from Councilor Campos, the Fire Marshal noted the different Code 
requirements for residential uses versus commercial uses – for residential uses, the protection system 
operator calls the owner before the fire department to determine if a response is required and for 
commercial uses the protection system operator calls the department prior to the owner  Therefore, the 
department responds to minimal residential fire alarms. 
 
Council President Reed noted her support for the appeals process. 
 
The Fire Marshal stated that they start with education followed by enforcement.

Nays:  Baez,  Ventura - 2 
 
Bill No. 62-2026, as amended, was enacted by the following vote:
