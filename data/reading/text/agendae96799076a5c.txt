RULES FOR PUBLIC PARTICIPATION AT COUNCIL MEETINGS 
Public comment at all meetings of the City Council of the City of Reading shall be governed by 
the following: 
• Residents and/or taxpayers of the City who wish to make or submit public comment must 
pre-register utilizing one of three (3) following options: 
1. By calling (610-655-6205) or emailing (council@readingpa.gov) the City 
Clerk's Office by 4 pm on the day of the meeting with your name, address and 
the subject matter or topic to be discussed. If you are not able to attend in-
person, instructions to access the virtual meeting app or dial-in telephone 
number will be provided. 
2. By submitting written public comment to be read into the record at the meeting 
by submitting an email to council@readingpa.gov by 4pm on the day of the 
meeting. The email message must clearly be marked as Public Comment.  
3. By registering in-person at the Council Chambers on the sign-up sheet found 
on the podium in Council Chambers between 5:00 p.m. and 7:00 p.m. on the 
day of the scheduled meeting, with your name, address and the subject matter 
or topic to be discussed.  
• No person shall be permitted to speak who is not a resident or taxpayer of the City, except 
upon permission of the President of Council or a majority of City Council.  
• Public comment will occur only during the Public Comment period listed on the meeting 
agenda at which time the Presiding Officer will recognize speakers and written comment 
that properly registered in the order received.

• All public comment shall be delivered from the podium and speakers may not approach 
the Council tables at any time. 
• Those individuals registered to speak on agenda matters will be allotted no more than 5 
minutes and those individuals registered to speak on non-agenda matters will be allotted 
no more than 3 minutes.  The City Clerk will control a timer to monitor these time periods.  
When the alarm rings, a speaker must immediately end their comment.  If a speaker refuses 
to comply, the microphone at the podium will be turned off and the next speaker will be 
given the floor.  These time periods also apply to written comments that are being read into 
the record such that any written comment may only be read into the record for a 5-minute 
period for agenda items and a 3-minute period for non-agenda items. The President of 
Council or a majority of Council may grant an additional period of time in their discretion.   
• If anyone is present in a group or association, each such group should designate a 
spokesperson to address the City Council on the group’s collective behalf in order to avoid 
repetitive public comments. 
• Comments posted in Zoom Chat and on Facebook are not considered public comment and 
will not be read into the record of the meeting or addressed by City Council.

MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING (MOU)  
BETWEEN THE CITY OF READING  
AND THE READING SCHOOL DISTRICT 
 

11 
 
11 
 
This Memorandum of Understanding “MOU” is entered between the CITY OF READING 
(“City”), a municipal corporation in the State of Pennsylvania, and the READING SCHOOL 
DISTRICT, (“School District”), a school district of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, and 
collectively these are known as the “Parties.” 
WHEREAS, the City of Reading has provided recycling collection services to the Reading 
School District for over a decade; and  
 
WHEREAS, the Reading School District desires that the City continue providing collection 
services to said school district; and  
 
WHEREAS, the City of Reading provides recycling through a contracted hauler; and 
 
WHEREAS, the City of Reading and the Reading School District desire to enter into an 
intergovernmental cooperation agreement to memorialize the terms and conditions of the 
recycling collection service,  
 
NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual covenants undertaken herein, and with 
intent to be legally bound, the City and School District hereby agree as follows: 
1) SERVICE PROVIDED BY THE CITY 
A) Provide recycling collection services to the School District, either directly by City 
employees or through the contracted service provided by the City’s contracted hauler. 
 
B) The initial collection schedule shall be according to the specifications identified in 
Exhibit A, Collection Schedule. The schedule and frequency of collection may be 
adjusted from time to time by mutual agreement, based on the volume of recyclable 
material generated by the School District such that material is collected in a timely 
manner. 
 
C) Provide, at no additional charge, containers to be used for storage and collection of 
recyclable materials by the recycling collection vehicle. The City will determine the most 
appropriate collection containers for each location based on the type and amount of 
recyclables generated and the operational needs of the School District. 
 
D) The City may choose to support the district with additional containers for internal 
locations such as offices, classrooms, and cafeterias. 
 
E) The method of collection shall be single stream recycling. Materials collected shall 
include Single Stream Recyclable Materials as identified in the City’s Code of 
Ordinances, Chapter 496, Solid Waste to include at least three materials chosen from: 
clear glass, colored glass, aluminum, steel and bimetallic cans, high-grade office paper, 
newsprint, corrugated paper, and plastics.  

12 
 
12 
 
 
F) Provide the district with recycling guidelines and educational materials indicating the 
type of material that can be recycled as part of this agreement. 
 
G) Provide, with the School District’s assistance, an education program to educate students 
and staff on waste minimization, recycling, proper waste disposal, and related topics. 
 
H) Provide additional advice and assistance to the School District to help the School District 
establish and maintain an effective recycling program. 
2) RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE SCHOOL DISTRICT 
A) Ensure the provision of adequate recycling containers for internal use in classrooms, 
offices, cafeterias, and other locations suitable for recycling. 
 
B) Regularly service all internal recycling containers and prepare recycling for collection by 
the City according to the schedule in Exhibit A. 
 
C) Train staff on proper recycling behavior and ensure a reasonable level of compliance with 
the recycling guidelines provided by the City. 
 
D) Provide City employees, or the employees of the City’s contracted collection service, 
with sufficient access to school buildings and properties as to be able to collect the 
recyclable materials from designated collection points. No City employee or employee of 
the City’s contracted hauler shall be present in a school building without the escort of a 
school district employee. 
 
E) Foster student and staff involvement in the recycling program by directly involving 
students in recycling efforts, establishing recycling ambassadors, integrating recycling 
into the curriculum or otherwise engaging students and staff in waste minimization, 
recycling, and proper disposal.  
3) TERM, RENEWALS, AND TERMINATION 
A) The effective date of this agreement shall be July 01, 2026. 
 
B) The term of this agreement shall be for one year beginning July 1st and ending June 30th. 
 
C) This agreement may be extended for additional one-year renewal terms by mutual 
agreement of both Parties. If the School District intends to renew this agreement it shall 
notify the City at least 90 days prior to the end of the current agreement year. 
 

13 
 
13 
 
D) Either party may terminate this agreement upon 90 days’ prior written notice to the other 
party. In the event of early termination, payments shall be made on a pro-rated basis for 
service received. 
4) COST OF SERVICE 
A) The City shall provide the services specified in this agreement for an annual lump sum 
price. Payment shall be invoiced quarterly and remitted within 45 days of invoice date. 
 
B) The cost for the term of July 01, 2026, through June 30, 2027 shall be $64,031.02 his is a 
2% increase over the past agreement  
 
C) All renewals shall be renewed at the prior year’s rate plus an annual increase of 2%. Rate 
increases beyond 2% shall be agreed to in writing by both parties. 
5) GENERAL TERMS AND CONDITIONS 
A) Modification and Amendment: This agreement may only be amended in writing upon the 
consent of both Parties and as duly approved by official action of the Parties. 
 
B) Indemnification: Nothing in this agreement shall constitute a waiver of sovereign 
immunity by either party. The City and the School District agree to indemnify each other 
for any and all claims which may arise from this agreement which are brought or claimed 
by third party entities. 
 
C) Entire Agreement: This agreement, along with the Exhibit(s) attached herein and 
incorporated by reference herein, comprises the entire agreement between the Parties 
related to the subject matter of this agreement, and supersedes and prior agreement, oral 
or written, between the parties on the subject matter herein. 
 
D) Governing Law: This agreement, and all rights and obligations of the Parties hereto, are 
governed and construed in accordance with the laws of the Commonwealth of 
Pennsylvania.

Section One: Amending the City Code Chapter 600 Zoning as follows: 
§ 600-820. Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) Overlay District. 
A. Purpose and Intent 
1. Promote and encourage pedestrian-oriented, dense and walkable mixed-
use developments within the TOD Overlay District. 
2. Promote economic development within the TOD Overlay District by 
pursuing the adaptive reuse of existing structures and developing 
underutilized sites for new uses that contribute to the local economy. 
3. Encourage the development of a variety of housing types, including 
workforce housing. 
4. Design buildings, sites and structures using appropriate materials to 
create a walkable environment with highly articulated architecture and 
unified and integrated streetscaping. 
5. Increase access to retail, services, institutions and employment 
opportunities. 
6. Create a welcoming and friendly pedestrian-centered environment through 
the inclusion of public civic spaces, pedestrian and bicycle paths, street 
trees, street furnishings, ornamental lighting and wayfinding. 
B. Application  
1. The TOD Overlay District standards shall apply to any subdivision and/or 
land development application for new construction within the area 
identified in the TOD Overlay District Map. 
2. These standards shall not apply when the subdivision and/or land 
development application is for adaptive reuse of an existing building. The 
provisions of Section 600-1202 of the Zoning Ordinance will govern the 
requirements for adaptive reuse unless the area designated for future 
growth and expansion to the building(s) occupying the adaptive reuse is 
greater than twenty-five percent (25%) gross floor area of the existing 
building. In this instance, the provisions of this section shall apply. 
3. Plans, variances, conditional uses and building approvals that have 
already been approved at the time of the effective date of this section shall 
remain as approved.

§ 600-1109. Additional Requirements for the Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) 
Overlay District. 
A. Additional Use Standards 
1. Brewery / Brewpub / Microbrewery: No brewing equipment or product 
storage shall be permitted on the exterior premises.  
2. Hotel: No hotel room shall be rented to any one person for a continuous 
period of more than three months. This is different from a rooming house, 
which is a single dwelling with multiple rooms that are rented out. 
3. Multifamily buildings: Multifamily buildings shall meet the following 
requirements: 
a. The maximum density shall be 30 dwelling units per developable 
acre. 
b. One and two-bedroom units shall comprise at least 75% of total 
dwelling units. 
c. Multifamily buildings with at least  30 units but fewer than 40 
dwelling units shall include at least one or more of the following 
amenities; multifamily buildings with 40 or more dwelling units 
shall include at least two or more of the following amenities:

rooftop) with a minimum size of 1,000 square feet, gym (1,000 
square feet min.), fenced-in dog walking area (with a minimum 
size of 1,500 square feet), business center, lounge or community 
room/game room (500 square feet min.).

§ 600-2202. Definitions. 
Artisanal manufacturing - The on-site production, display, and sale of 
hand-fabricated or hand-manufactured parts and custom or craft 
consumer goods based on the skill and knowledge of the artisan and the 
use of hand tools or small-scale, light mechanical equipment. This 
involves activities such as small bakeries, candy or soap making, coffee 
roasters, breweries, distilleries, or the custom production of artisan 
products such as apparel, cabinetry, glass working, jewelry making, metal 
working, pottery, sculpture, wood working, and leather working. 
 
Bicycle rack - A device consistent with industry standards that is capable 
of supporting a bicycle in a stable position, is made of durable materials, 
and permits the securing of the bicycle frame and at least one wheel. 
 
Bicycle parking, long-term - Bicycle parking that is primarily intended for 
bicyclists who need bicycle parking for more than three hours and is fully 
protected from the weather. This term includes, but is not limited to, 
bicycle lockers, indoor rooms, or areas specifically designated for bicycle 
parking. 
 
Bicycle parking, short-term - Bicycle parking primarily intended for 
bicyclists who need bicycle parking for three hours or less. 
 
Bicycle parking space - A physical space that is a minimum of 2.5 feet in 

31 
 
31 
 
width by 6 feet in length with a vertical clearance of at least 7 feet that 
allows for the parking of one bicycle. 
 
Boutique hotel - A hotel, as defined herein, with a maximum of 40 guest 
rooms. 
 
Brewery - An industrial use that brews ales, beers, meads, and/or similar 
beverages on site. Breweries are classified as a use that manufactures 
more than 15,000 barrels of beverage (all beverages combined) annually. 
In addition, uses that manufacture 15,000 barrels of beverage or less, but 
which do not meet one or more 
of the additional requirements needed to be considered brewpubs, are 
breweries. 
 
Brewpub – A restaurant that manufactures up to 5,000 barrels of 
fermented malt beverages per year on premises for either consumption on 
premises in hand-capped or sealed containers in quantities up to one-half 
barrel or 15.5 gallons sold directly to the consumer. Wholesaling shall be 
permitted only where authorized within the zoning code. 
 
Building stepback - A setback or horizontal offset in the façade of the 
building above the street level or lower floors of the building. 
 
Business incubator - An area designated for the cultivation and 
enhancement of future businesses or business-oriented developments. 
 
Co-working space - A facility that contains workspaces that are made 
available to individuals and businesses for short-term intervals through a 
membership or rental arrangement. 
 
Green roof - An engineered, multilayered roofing system sustaining the 
growth of plants on a rooftop while protecting the integrity of the 
underlying structure. The components of a green roof generally consist of, 
but may not be limited to, a waterproofing membrane, root barrier, 
drainage layer, retention layer, filter fabric, growing medium, and plants. 
 
Hospital - An institution providing health services primarily for human 
inpatient or medical or surgical care for the sick or injured, and including 
the related facilities such laboratories, outpatient departments, training 
facilities, central service facilities, and staff offices which are integral parts 
of the facilities. 
 
Microbrewery – A facility for the production and packaging of malt 
beverages of low alcoholic content for distribution, retail, or wholesale, on 
or off premise, with a capacity of not more than 15,000 barrels per year. 

32 
 
32 
 
The development may include other uses such as a standard restaurant, 
bar or live entertainment as otherwise permitted in the zoning district. 
 
Mixed-use development – a single development that includes both 
residential and non-residential uses that are either horizontal (uses in 
different buildings within a single development site) or vertical (uses within 
the same building).  
 
Pedestrian-oriented - A design principle that emphasizes development 
and infrastructure that improves the access and environment for 
pedestrians, rather than automobile users. 
 
Public civic space - Public or private outdoor space where the public is 
directly or indirectly invited to visit or allowed to congregate. 
 
Shared parking - A calculation method that determines the number of 
parking spaces necessary to meet the parking demand on a site where 
there are multiple uses with different peak periods of parking demand. 
 
Stacked townhouse - A townhome that has two units stacked vertically 
that may or may not share a middle floor. 
 
Streetscape - The area that is between the buildings on either side of a 
street, including the public or private street right-of-way, which defines its 
character. The elements of a streetscape include building frontage/façade, 
landscaping, sidewalks, street paving, street furniture, signage, 
architectural elements such as awnings, and street lighting. 
 
Transit oriented development (TOD) – Dense residential and commercial 
uses concentrated in mixed-use developments located along transit routes 
that emphasize pedestrian-oriented environments and encourage the use 
of public transportation. 
 
Tree lawn – The space between the street curb and sidewalk. If a 
sidewalk does not exist, it is the space to the edge of the public right-of-
way which is dedicated to the City for public purposes. 
 
Underground parking - Below-grade parking facilities which typically 
include ventilation systems where motor vehicles are parked, stored, or 
allowed to remain, 
and where the owner or person storing or parking the vehicle may be 
charged a 
fee. 
 
Workforce housing – Housing that is affordable to those earning between 
60% and 120% of the Area Median Income (AMI) of the Reading MSA

§ 515-511. Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) Overlay District. 
A. Purpose and Intent 
1. Support the intent of the Reading TOD Overlay District as contained in the City of 
Reading Zoning Ordinance. 
2. Promote high quality development by utilizing appropriate building materials to create 
highly articulated architecture with a fully integrated streetscape. 

37 
 
37 
 
3. Provide for safe and convenient pedestrian access to buildings and to and from public 
sidewalks, crosswalks, bus stops, train stations, pedestrian or bicycle trails or paths and 
between buildings and sites. 
4. Promote alternate modes of transportation such as bicycle and pedestrian paths, bus and 
train transportation, and provide and maintain high quality public transit waiting areas. 
5. Provide a pedestrian-friendly character and environment throughout the TOD District with 
public civic spaces, pedestrian/bicycle paths, street trees, landscaping, seating areas, 
street furnishings, lighting and pedestrian-scale signage and wayfinding. 
B. Applicability. All new developments within the TOD Overlay District shall comply with the 
following design standards. If there is a conflict between the design standards of the TOD 
Overlay District and other City SALDO Standards, the TOD Overlay standards shall govern. 
C. Compliance 
1. Plan submission shall include the following items related to design standards: 
a. Sketch plan, drawn to scale, showing the plan elements as required by City 
ordinances; 
b. Photographs of existing buildings that illustrate the design intent of the proposal; 
or building elevations drawn to scale; or photo-realistic illustrations depicting 
proposed scale, proportions, design elements, materials and color; 
c. Colored rendering of the plan(s); 
d. Landscape plan; and 
e. Current photographs of existing site conditions. 
f. Once the preliminary styles are reviewed by the City Planning Commission, and 
the City Planning Commission provides the applicant with written comments, 
additional and more detailed preliminary plan, architectural renderings, prepared 
by a registered architect, shall be submitted for all proposed buildings, together 
with a list and samples of types of exterior building materials that will be visible 
from any public street. Similarly, a rendered landscape plan, prepared by a 
registered landscape architect shall also be submitted for review. An outdoor 
lighting plan shall also be submitted for review. 
g. City Council approval of proposed architectural and streetscape design is 
required for the land development application to be approved.  
2. Waiver of Fees. Fifteen percent (15%) of the filing fees related to Sketch Plan, 
Preliminary Plan and Final Plan submission as enumerated on the City of Reading 
website may be waived when workforce housing units are constructed as part of a 
proposed development. 
D. Building Architectural Design Standards 
1. Building design shall provide contextual references to its surrounding built environment, 
including existing architecture, natural features, and cultural contexts. 
2. Primary and accent building materials shall visually complement each other in terms of 
color, texture, and pattern. 
E. Building Massing and Scale 
1. The massing and scale of new construction shall consider its surrounding context. Where 
larger scaled buildings are located next to smaller ones, steps shall be taken to visually 

38 
 
38 
 
transition in terms of size, massing, and scale, which can be achieved using any of the 
following techniques: 
a. Stepping back upper stories or adjusting the roof pitch of larger massed 
buildings on the side where they are adjacent to smaller massed buildings. 
b. Including additional buffer space between larger massed buildings and smaller 
massed buildings. This buffer space is an appropriate area for drives, 
landscaping, and open space. 
c. Including horizontal architectural details such as cornices and window frames on 
larger massed buildings that align with the roofline of the adjacent smaller 
massed buildings. 
d. Using trees and other greenery to soften hard edges between differently massed 
buildings. 
2. Large-scale commercial and mixed-use buildings shall include architectural or material-
based articulation to reduce the perceived mass of the building while still aligning with the 
built context of the block, which can be achieved using any of the following techniques: 
a. For larger buildings, particularly buildings that fill an entire block, using engaging 
ground-level design and providing multiple pedestrian entry points along the 
frontage. 
b. Avoid large, monolithic buildings by dividing or articulating the facade to avoid 
monolithic appearance. 
c. Incorporating architectural elements such as recessed or protruding entrances, 
balconies, bay windows, brick patterns, etc. 
d. Ensuring the pattern or repetition of any chosen architectural feature, recess, 
projection, or break is uniform and consistent. 
F. Front Facades, Entrances and Building Orientation 
1. Ground-floor facades facing the street shall include at least 50% transparent windows 
and doors. Transparency shall be evenly distributed to avoid long stretches of blank wall. 
2. The orientation and placement of buildings shall contribute to a sense of enclosure and 
defined space by maintaining a consistent building frontage along a primary street. 
a. Buildings may be set further back to incorporate public spaces, plazas and parks 
in a way that centralizes and activates those spaces, such as by having windows 
and entrances facing into the public spaces. 
b. The front facade of new construction shall orient towards the street by having the 
building's main entrance, primary windows, and facade details facing the street. 
c. For corner lots, entrances could be oriented at a 45-degree angle to emphasize 
the corner and encourage pedestrian access from both the principal street and 
secondary street. 
G. Public Transportation 
1. Existing and future public transportation stops and stations shall be considered in the 
layout of new developments to ensure convenient transit access and use. 
2. The location of entrances, walkways, and pedestrian amenities shall allow for convenient, 
safe, and comfortable pedestrian access from a public transportation stop or station to 
the site. 
H. Pedestrian Amenities 

39 
 
39 
 
1. Sidewalk café and seating areas are encouraged and shall maintain at least five feet of 
unobstructed walking space along sidewalks. 
2. Pedestrian amenities such as benches, lighting, and trash and recycling receptacles shall 
be conveniently placed together. 
3. Overhead weather protection—such as awnings, overhangs, and arcades—are 
encouraged and shall be appropriately located to clearly define building entries and orient 
towards pedestrian areas. Where possible, overhead weather protection should be 
located to provide shelter for waiting transit users. 
I. Off-Street Parking 
1. Garages, parking spaces, and driveways shall be designed to be as discreet as possible 
and limit impediments to the walking environment. Garage openings shall be placed 
where it will have the least disruption to pedestrian walkways and limit potential conflict 
points between car traffic and pedestrians. 
2. Buildings shall be arranged on the site such that garages shall be placed behind 
buildings whenever possible, and parking entrances shall be accessible via secondary 
streets. 
3. Whenever garages face a primary street, they shall be lined by buildings that screen the 
parking structure from view and incorporate other uses such as residential, retail, and 
other active uses. 
4. Garages that are visible from a public street shall avoid blank walls. These garages shall 
be mitigated using landscaping or public art or shall incorporate architectural design that 
complements the primary structure through the use of facade articulation, architectural 
elements, color, and elements that break up the mass of the garage. Fabric screening of 
parking garages is discouraged. 
J. Bicycle Parking Standards. Bicycle parking shall conform to the standards of Section 600-1609.  
K. Public Civic Space Standards 
1. Except for public civic spaces along Penn Street, perpendicular or angled parking spaces 
shall not abut any public civic space; however, parallel on-street parking spaces may 
abut any public civic space. 
2. The public civic space shall be convenient and accessible by sidewalk or internal 
pedestrian path. 
3. The public civic space shall be located next to public streets or front facades of buildings 
or located between buildings. 
4. An operations and maintenance plan, ensuring that all landscaping, natural features and 
constructs are maintained in perpetuity and are replaced if they can no longer be feasibly 
maintained, shall be prepared for the public civic space area and submitted to the City for 
approval. 
5. A minimum of 20%, but no more than 70%, of the public civic space shall be landscaped 
with a combination of trees, shrubs, perennials, grasses, and groundcovers to provide 
year-round visual interest and color. A minimum of one (1) shade tree shall be provided 
per 250 square feet of public civic space. 
6. The public civic space shall not be used for parking, loading, or vehicular access, except 
emergency and maintenance vehicular access. 

40 
 
40 
 
7. The paved surface area shall be composed of high-quality, durable paving materials, 
such as unit pavers, or concrete. 
8. Pedestrian-scale lighting shall be provided within the public civic space and along 
sidewalks or internal pedestrian pathways connecting to the public civic space. 
9. The public civic space shall be entirely open to the air and no portion of a building, other 
than a balcony, shall project over the public gathering space. 
10. Trash and recycling containers shall be distributed throughout the public civic space to 
discourage littering. One (1) trash and one (1) recycling container shall be provided for 
every 600 square feet of public civic space. 
11. Seating spaces shall be provided throughout the public civic space. One (1) seating 
space shall be provided for every 250 square feet of public civic space. 
12. The applicant shall have an agreement with the City for public access to the public civic 
space. 
L. Landscaping and Screening Standards 
1. Landscaping 
a. Landscaping, such as ornamental trees, flowering shrubs, and perennials, shall 
be used to define public entrances and to soften walls and fences. 
b. Landscaping shall be designed to promote sustainable practices of on-site water 
management and infiltration such as through the use of native plants, swales, 
and filter strips. 
1. If any invasive species are found on the site, they shall be handled in 
accordance with the provisions of Chapter 317: Invasive Plants and 
Noxious Weeds. 
2. All landscaping shall conform to plant material listed in Section 515-509 of 
SALDO or be approved by the City of Reading Arborist and Planning 
Commission. 
c. Landscaping shall seek to incorporate existing natural features, especially large, 
existing trees, where possible. 
d. Tree preservation and protection strategies, such as fencing and other 
structures, shall be employed to preserve existing trees during construction. 
e. Where commercial developments, mixed-use developments, or residential 
structures of more than four units abut properties zoned for single-, two-, or 
three-family dwellings, required buffers (on the property of the commercial, 
mixed-use, or residential structure of more than four units) shall be sufficiently 
landscaped to provide a year-round visual screen along those boundary lines 
according to the standards of Section 600-1401 of Zoning. 
2. Street Trees 
a. Street trees shall be planted within tree lawns along all public rights-of-way. A 
minimum of one large tree shall be planted for every development site, and 
street trees shall be planted at intervals of no more than 30 feet on-center, on 
average. Where overhead utilities exist, one small tree must be planted every 20 
feet on-center, on average.  
b. The type of street trees selected shall be in accordance with the standards of 
Section 515-509 of SALDO. 

41 
 
41 
 
3. Parking Lot Landscaping. All surface lots must be landscaped in accordance with Section 
600-1602.K.  
4. Screening. Outdoor storage, waste disposal storage and mechanical and electrical 
equipment shall be screened according to the standards of Section 600-1402 of Zoning.

Part 16:  Off-Street Parking and Loading 
§600-1601: General Application Criteria 
(A) Application:  Off-street parking and/or loading facilities shall be located, designed, constructed and 
maintained in accordance with the provisions of this Zoning Ordinance.  These facilities shall be 
provided whenever the following actions occur: 
(1) A new building has been constructed or a new use has been established in accordance with 
the provisions of this Zoning Ordnance. 
(2) The use of the building or property has been changed, altered or expanded, which requires 
additional off-street parking spaces to accommodate the use. 
(3) An off-street parking facility shall include the driveways, access drives, access isles, parking 
spaces and loading spaces. 
(B) Land Use Compliance:  Parking and loading areas shall be a permitted use as follows: 
(1) Principal Use:  The following off-street parking area and/or off-street loading area 
requirements shall apply if the off-street parking and/or off-street loading area is classified 
as the principal use on the property: 
(a) An off-street parking area containing ten (10) or more parking spaces, which are 
designated as the principal use shall only be permitted provided that such use is 
permitted within the underlying zoning district.  All such off-street parking areas shall 
comply with the lot area, dimensional and coverage requirements, as specified by 
the underlying zoning district. 
(b) An off-street parking area containing less than ten (10) parking spaces, which are 
designated as the principal use shall only be permitted provided that such use is 
permitted within the underlying zoning district.  All such off-street parking areas shall 
comply with: the minimum lot area requirement; the dimensional setback 
requirements may be reduced to three (3) feet from all property lines; and the 
maximum impervious coverage may be increased to 95 percent of the lot area. 
(c) An off-street loading area designated as the principal use shall only be permitted 
provided that such use is permitted within the underlying zoning district.  All such 
off-street loading areas shall comply with the lot area, dimensional and coverage 
requirements, as specified by the underlying zoning district. 
(2) Accessory Use:   An off-street parking area and/or off-street loading area may be permitted 
as an accessory use to a compatible principal use, subject to the provisions specified by 
this Zoning Ordinance. 
(3) Satellite Parking Spaces:  Off-street parking spaces may be established or credited at a 
conforming off-site or off-premises location, subject to the provision specified by this Zoning 
Ordinance. 
(4) On-Street Spaces:  Unless otherwise permitted by the City of Reading, all on-street parking 
spaces and/or on-street loading spaces shall not be counted or credited as a required 
parking or loading space.  All such spaces shall comply with the provisions established by 
the City of Reading Code.  

44 
 
44 
 
(C) Location:  The following provisions shall apply to the location of off-street parking and/or loading 
areas: 
(1) Unless otherwise permitted, off-street parking and loading spaces shall be located on the 
same lot as the principal use.  
(2) All parking and loading facilities shall be arranged and marked to provide safe and orderly 
movement without disrupting traffic or moving other vehicles. 
(3) Unless otherwise permitted by the Public Works Department, the design of parking areas 
for all uses shall be such to prevent the back-up of vehicles onto a public street and that no 
portion of any parked vehicle extends over any property line. 
§600-1602: Design and Construction Standards 
(A) Accessibility:  All off-street parking and loading spaces shall have access to a public street by 
means of an access lane, driveway, alley or road that complies with the provisions specified by 
Chapters 515 and 600 of the Code. 
(B) Size:  Off-street parking and loading spaces shall comply with the following criteria: 
(1) Off-Street Parking Spaces:  The spaces shall have a rectangular shape that complies with 
following dimensional requirements: 
(a) Within an off-street parking lot, each parking space for motor vehicles (except those 
spaces dedicated for use by handicapped persons) shall have a minimum 
rectangular area of 162 square feet, which shall have a minimum width of 9 feet and 
a minimum length of 18 feet have a minimum area of 162 square feet with a 
minimum width of 9 feet and a minimum length of 18 feet.  Angled spaces will need 
to be longer in order to achieve the rectangular shape with the minimum dimensions. 
(b) Where parallel parking is permitted, the parking spaces shall be delineated by 
painted lines and shall have a minimum width of 8 feet, as measured from the curb 
or cartway edge and a minimum length of 20 feet, as measured along the curb or 
cartway edge. 
(2) Off-Street Loading Spaces:  The spaces shall have a rectangular shape that complies with 
following dimensional requirements: 
(a) Off-street loading spaces with a length of sixty (60) feet, width of fourteen (14) feet, 
and clearance height of fifteen (15) feet for commercial and industrial uses. 
(b) Off-street loading spaces with a length of forty-five (45) feet, width of twelve (12) 
feet, and clearance height of twelve (12) feet may be permitted for commercial, 
institutional and light industrial uses provided the landowner or applicant can 
demonstrate that the use will not require larger trucks to access the site for loading 
and unloading activities.  
(C) Grade:    The off-street parking and loading areas shall be properly graded to provide convenient 
vehicular access and proper drainage and shall be maintained in usable condition.  The minimum 
grade of areas used for parking shall be at least one (1) percent and the maximum grade shall not 
exceed five (5) percent.

feet of earth disturbance activities, an Erosion and Sedimentation Control Plan shall be 
prepared and submitted with the Land Development Plan. 
(5) Other Permits:  If the proposed site improvements encroach or traverse a street right-of-
way for use and occupancy, a Highway Occupancy Permit (HOP), Driveway Permit and/or 
Utility Permit may be required via the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation and/or 
City of Reading.

along the perimeter of an off-street parking area or an off-street loading area.  The

(d) A combination of a landscaped buffer strip and fencing may be considered in order

(e) The landscaping and buffer enhancements shall not obstruct the required sight

(2) A 180 square foot curbed or raised landscaped island shall be incorporated into the off-

(3) The use of existing healthy mature trees (12 inches or more in caliber) is encouraged and 
may be utilized as part of the required buffer yard. 
(4) The landscaping requirements and buffer yard shall be comprised of trees, shrubs and other 
plantings that will establish a complete visual barrier within two (2) years of the initial

(5) The landscaping design of the buffer yard and/or fence shall be subject to the review and 
approval of the Zoning Office.

(2) The illumination levels and spillage impacts shall comply with the exterior lighting provisions 
established by Section 600-912 of the Zoning Ordinance. 
(3) The exterior illumination levels shall be reduced by 50 percent between 10:00 pm and 7:00

§600-1605: Emergency Management and Response Standards 
(A) External Access Points:  All means of ingress and/or egress shall be located, designed and 
constructed in order to accommodate traffic in a safe and efficient manner.  The landowner or 
developer shall be responsible for the purchase and installation of any traffic control devices as 
well as the design and construction of additional travel lanes or auxiliary lanes, which may be 
required by the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation or City of Reading. 
(B) Internal Access Drives and Lanes: The width of aisles within the off-street parking facilities shall 
comply with the minimum requirements established under Part 16 of this Zoning Ordinance.  All 
dead-end parking lots shall be designed to provide sufficient back-up area for all end spaces. 
(C) Emergency Access:  All off-street parking and loading areas shall be designed to consider the 
provisions established under Section 600-906 of this Zoning Ordinance. 
(D) Fire Lanes:  If required by the Fire and Rescue Department or by the Building and Trades 
Department, a fire lane shall be established within the property containing the off-street parking 
area and/or loading area. 
(E) Emergency Response and Accessibility:  The internal access lanes or aisles serving the off-street 
parking area and/or loading area shall be subject to the review and approval of the Fire and Rescue 
Department. 
(F) Sight Distance:  The provisions established under Section 600-1303 of the Zoning Ordinance and 
Section 515-502.F of the Subdivision and Land Development Ordinance shall apply to the 
specifications for sight distance, as required by the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation or 
by the City of Reading Code. 
(G) Clear Sight Triangles:  The provisions established under the provisions established under Section 
600-1303 of the Zoning Ordinance and Section 515-502.F of the Subdivision and Land 
Development Ordinance shall apply to the specification for clear sight triangles.

55 
 
(A) Application:  Common or shared parking facilities or areas may be permitted for two (2) or more 
permitted uses, subject to the following requirements: 
(1) The permitted uses are adjacent or adjoining to one another. 
(2) The common off-street parking spaces are capable of accommodating the combined 
parking requirements that are specified by the permitted uses. 
(3) The use of a common or shared parking area shall be permitted by the Zoning Office as 
part of a Zoning Permit Application and/or the Planning Commission as part of a Subdivision 
Plan or Land Development Plan. 
(B) Site Accessibility:  The following documents shall be established and recorded: 
(1) Access Easement: The record owners of the properties shall prepare and submit written 
documentation of the continued availability of the shared parking arrangement to the Zoning 
Office, Public Works Department and/or Planning Commission.  The limits of the access 
easement shall consider all pedestrian and vehicular traffic movements between the street 
right-of-way, the permitted uses and the internal common or shared off-street parking 
facility. 
(2) Driveway Easement:  If required, a shared or common driveway easement shall be 
established in order to permit access from the public street to the common or shared off-
street parking area.  
(3) Stormwater Easement:  If required, a stormwater management easement shall be 
established for the use, occupancy and maintenance responsibilities that may pertain to the 
common or shared parking area. 
(4) Utility Easement:  If required, a utilities easement shall be established for the use, 
occupancy and maintenance responsibilities that may pertain to the common or shared 
parking area. 
(5) Legal Description:  All easements shall be accurately described by bearings and distances, 
which are referenced within an agreement and depicted on a plan.  If permitted, a blanket 
easement may be considered as an alternative for the description. 
(C) Modifications or Termination:  The common or shared parking agreement may be modified or 
terminated at the direction of the Zoning Office and/or Public Works Department if the common or 
shared parking facilities should create the following conditions: traffic congestion; traffic hazards; 
overflow parking conditions; and/or threats pedestrian safety. 
(D) Car Pool Parking Facility:   All common parking areas classified as “car pool facilities” shall comply 
with the design requirements established under Part 16 of the Zoning Ordinance.  All such facilities 
shall be subject to the review and approval of the City of Reading. 
(E) Valet Parking Service:  All permitted uses may utilize valet service as a parking option at a 
designated on-site parking space on the property or a designated off-site parking space at a 
permitted public or private parking facility.  All such arrangements shall be subject to the review 
and approval of the Zoning Office.

§600-1608: Electric Vehicle Charging Stations (EVCS) 
(A) Electric Vehicle Charging Stations (EVCS) shall be permitted as an accessory or subordinate use 
by right within all zoning districts, subject to the following provision: 
(1) The EVCS shall be located within a building (principal or accessory) occupied by the 
principal use or within the parking or loading area for the principal use. 
(2) The EVCS shall be located at least ten (10) feet from the street right-of-way line and all 
other property lines. 
(3) Parking spaces containing an EVCS shall count towards the minimum required number of 
parking spaces for the principal use(s) contained on the property. 
(4) The EVCS may be leased or utilized to other users during non-operational hours designated 
by the principal use. 
(5) The EVCS shall be clearly marked or identified with signage. 
(6) The EVCS shall be outfitted with a standard EVCS connection or plug-in. 
(7) The EVCS spaces shall not exceed 125 percent of the total number of off-street parking or 
loading spaces required for the proposed use. 
(B) A decommissioning plan and agreement shall be established and endorsed as part of the zoning 
permit for the EVCS. 
§600-1609: Non-Motorized Vehicle Stations (NMVS) 
(A) Non-Motorized Vehicle Stations (ENMVS) shall be permitted as an accessory or subordinate use 
by right within all zoning districts, subject to the following provision: 
(1) The NMVS may be located within a building (principal or accessory) occupied by the 
principal use or within the parking area for the principal use.  Alternative locations shall be 
subject to the review of the Zoning Office. 
(2) The NMVS shall be located at least ten (10) feet from the street right-of-way line and all 
other property lines. 
(3) The NMVS may be located on a space dedicated or reserved for an off-street parking 
provided that 90 percent of the remaining parking spaces remain available for the principal 
use(s) contained on the property. 
(4) The NMVS may be leased or utilized to other users during non-operational hours 
designated by the principal use. 
(5) The NMVS shall be clearly marked or identified with signage. 
(B) A decommissioning plan and agreement shall be established and endorsed as part of the zoning 
permit for the NMVS.

§600-1610: Public or Private Parking Garages 
(A) Public or private parking garages shall be permitted as follows: 
(1) A public parking garage shall be permitted by right within the Commercial Core (C-C) Zoning 
District. 
(2) A public or private parking garage shall be permitted by right within the Manufacturing 
Commercial (M-C), Heavy Manufacturing (H-M) and Municipal (MU) Zoning Districts and 
the Institutional (INS) Overlay District. 
(3) A public or private parking garage shall be permitted by special exception within the 
Commercial Residential (C-R), Commercial Neighborhood (C-N) and Commercial Highway 
(C-H) Zoning Districts. 
(B) Permitted Uses:  The following principal and accessory uses may be contained within the public or 
private parking garage: 
(1) The principal use shall be the public or private parking garage, which shall include the 
driveways, access lanes, parking spaces and all structural components of the garage. 
(2) Additional permitted principal uses may be attached to the public or private parking garage 
provided that such principal uses are permitted by right within the underlying zoning district 
and that the use can be independently operated as a functional principal use with access 
to the street level and parking garage. 
(3) The accessory uses of the public or private parking garage may include the following: 
administrative offices, maintenance facilities; storage facilities; security areas and facilities; 
work stations; toll booths, elevators; stairs; pedestrian crossing areas; vending machines, 
concession stands; snack bars; newsstands; government installations; and/or other similar 
areas.  The total gross floor area for all permitted accessory uses shall not exceed twenty 
(20) percent of the total gross floor area of the public or private garage. 
(C) External Dimensional Requirements:  The following provisions shall apply to a public or private 
parking garage: 
(1) The minimum lot area shall be 20,000 square feet. 
(2) The minimum lot width shall be two hundred (200) feet. 
(3) All uses, buildings and/or structures associated with the garage shall be located at least ten 
(10) feet from the street right-of-way line and all property lines. 
(4) No more than ninety (90) percent of a lot shall be covered by any combination of buildings, 
structures and impervious surfaces. 
(5) Unless otherwise permitted, the maximum height of the garage shall be sixty (60) feet, as 
measured from the average ground elevation to the highest point of the building or structure. 
(6) All other uses and site improvements shall comply with the bulk and height requirements, 
which are further specified by the appropriate zoning district to which the garage is located. 
(D) Internal Dimensional Requirements:  The following provisions shall apply to the aisle width, parking 
space and clearance levels within a public or private parking garage: 

58 
 
58 
 
(1) The vehicular aisle or access lanes located within the garage shall comply with the following 
criteria: 
(a) The minimum aisle width shall be at least twenty (20) feet in width in order to 
accommodate two (2) way travel conditions within the garage. 
(b) The minimum aisle width shall be at least fourteen (14) feet in width in order to 
accommodate one (1) way travel conditions within the garage. 
(c) The minimum aisle width for entrance and exit lanes shall be a ten (10) feet in order 
to accommodate one (1) way travel, which shall be delineated by curbing or 
markers. 
(2) The individual parking spaces contained within the garage shall comply with the following 
criteria: 
(a) At least 80 percent of the parking spaces shall have a minimum rectangular area of 
162 square feet, which shall have a minimum width of 9 feet and a minimum length 
of 18 feet. 
(b) A maximum 20 percent of the parking spaces may be reduced in order to 
accommodate compact vehicles with a minimum rectangular area of 128 square 
feet, which shall have a minimum width of 8 feet and a minimum length of 16 feet. 
(c) Angled spaces will need to be longer in order to achieve the rectangular shape with 
the minimum dimensions. 
(d) Handicapped parking spaces shall comply with the provisions established under 
Section 600-1603.D of this Zoning Ordinance. 
(3) The clearance level within the garage area shall not be less than eight (8) feet, as 
measured from the parking deck floor to the bottom of any support structure or 
ceiling within all deck levels. 
(4) A public or private parking garage may include a section designated for EVCS 
and/or NMVS, as permitted under Part 16 of this Zoning Ordinance. 
(5) A ten (10) percent reduction may be permitted to the internal dimensional 
requirements specified under Section 600-1610(D) of this Zoning Ordinance 
provided that engineering studies can support the reduction without creating safety 
issues within the garage.  All such reductions may be authorized by the Zoning 
Hearing Board as part of a special exception application. 
(E) General Requirements:  The following general provisions shall also apply to a public or private 
parking garage: 
(1) The garage shall be serviced by public sanitary sewer facilities and public water supply 
facilities. 
(2) All exterior speaker, microphone or intercom systems shall be designed in a manner so that 
the messages, music or other sounds shall be reduced by seventy (70) percent at any street 
line or property line. 

59 
 
59 
 
(3) Any booths or other structures utilized for the collection of admission and/or parking fees 
shall be setback and arranged to prevent vehicle backups on adjoining roads during peak 
arrival periods.  Any other collection of fees, such as by roaming parking lot attendants, 
shall be conducted in such a manner as to prevent vehicle backups on adjoining roads. 
(4) The landowner and operator of the garage shall be responsible to resolve, mitigate and/or 
correct any traffic congestion problems that occur on public roads, which are related to 
ingress and egress to the garage. 
(5) The use and related activities shall not emit noise, glare, vibration, electrical disturbance, 
electromagnetic interference, dust, smoke, fumes, toxic gas, radiation, heat and/or other 
perceptible or objectionable nuisances that would impact neighboring properties, or be 
noticeable at or beyond the property line. 
(6) The applicant shall furnish evidence that the proposed use will not be detrimental to the use 
of adjoining properties due to hours of operation, noise, light, dust, smoke, litter and 
pollution. 
(7) All points of vehicular ingress and egress shall comply with the provisions established by 
Chapter 515 and 600 of the City of Reading Code. 
(8) Internal circulation shall allow parking surfaces to be level (without any slope) along each 
parking garage facade adjacent to a public street or public space. All ramps between levels 
shall be located along building facades that are not adjacent to a public street or public 
space, or shall be located internally so that they are not visible from adjacent public streets 
or public spaces. 
(9) A pedestrian accessibility plan shall be developed and implemented to include linkages, 
connections and cross-walks within the neighborhood.  
(10) The location of elevators and stairs shall be highlighted through the use of architectural 
features or changes in façade colors, textures, or materials so that visitors can easily identify 
these entry points. 
(11) The interior parking garage lighting shall not produce glaring sources toward adjacent 
properties while providing safe and adequate lighting levels. The use of sensor dimmable 
LEDs and white stained ceilings are recommended to control light levels on-site while 
improving energy efficiency. 
(12) A streetscape enhancement plan shall be developed and implemented in order to consider 
street trees, landscaping and lighting along all public streets.  
(F) Plans and Permits:  All public and private parking garages shall be subject to the following criteria: 
(1) All required site plans, building plans and structural improvements shall be designed and 
sealed by a professional engineer licensed within the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. 
(2) A Land Development Plan shall be submitted to the Planning Commission. 
(3) All plans and permits shall be subject to the review and approval by the Zoning Office, 
Planning Office, Building and Trades Department, Fire and Rescue Department, Public 
Works Department, and Police Department.
