Executive Summary 
 
The City of Reading Planning Commission continued to advance strategic Planning, 
redevelopment, land development review, and zoning modernization initiatives throughout the 
2025 calendar year. In accordance with section 207 of the Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning 
Code (MPC), this Annual Report summarizes the Commission’s activities, decisions, and planning 
efforts that supported economic development, neighborhood revitalization, infrastructure 
improvements, housing investment, and long-range planning initiatives across the City of 
Reading.  
 
Throughout 2025, the Planning Commission reviewed and approved a wide range of subdivision 
and land development applications involving residential redevelopment, institutional 
improvements, educational facilities, adaptive reuse projects, parking facilities, industrial 
expansions, and recreational enhancements. 
 
The commission maintains oversight of numerous active development projects requiring 
Municipal Improvements Agreements, stormwater management compliance, escrow 
administration, inspections, and project reaffirmations. Several long-term projects progressed 
towards completion while additional redevelopment proposals remained active and under review, 
entering 2026. 
 
In addition to development review responsibilities, the Planning Commission advanced several 
important Planning and policy initiatives intended to guide the City’s long-term growth and 
revitalization efforts. Significant ordinance and planning initiatives reviewed during 2025 include: 
  
• Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) Overlay District regulations  
• Adaptive reuse and redevelopment zoning amendments 
• Parking and Loading regulation updates (Part 16 of the Zoning Ordinance)  
• Short-term Rental Ordinance  
• Home Occupation ordinance amendment 
• Zoning updates for commercial and mixed-commercial districts  
• Active Transportation and Vision Zero Planning Initiatives 
• Coordination with the Berks County Comprehensive Plan 2040 Process  
• Coordination with Reading Area Transportation Study (RATS) in developing the FFY 2027-
2050 long range Transportation Plan 
 
The Planning Commission continued collaboration with City departments, public agencies, 
educational institutions, developers, and community organizations to support coordinated 
redevelopment efforts and implementation of the Downtown Strategic Plan and City 
Comprehensive Plan initiatives.  
 
Throughout 2025, the commission remained committed to promoting sustainable development 
practices, enhancing public infrastructure, supporting economic growth opportunities, 
encouraging housing investment, improving transportation connectivity, and fostering high-quality 
community development throughout the City of Reading.

Section B: Approved Subdivision and Land Development Plan Applications (2025) 
The following plans have been approved by the Planning Commission in 2025: 
B.1 South 9th Street Parking Facility Plan; Phase 1; SALDO 2502-001 
The development is located on a 32,230 +/- square foot tract of land located along the east 
side of South 9th Street (TPID 02531637061933).  Phase 1 includes the off-street parking 
and storage areas (33 spaces) that will have designated points of ingress and egress along 
South 9th Street.  The required site improvements for Phase 1 shall include a paved parking 
surface, curbs, sidewalks, driveway ramps, handicapped ramps, retaining walls, 
landscaping, lighting, signs, securing fence and gate, erosion and sedimentation control 
measures, and other related improvements.  
B.2 Goggle Works 2 Land Development Plan; Phases 3, 4 and 5; PC 2503-0002 
The development includes building renovations and site revitalization efforts within an area 
that encompasses 27,261 square feet of land located at properties that shall be joined in 
common deed at 229, 243 and 245 Washington Street. The building renovations and site 
revitalization efforts shall be completed with multiple phases, which includes Phase 3 (9 
apartment units), Phase 4 (17 artists' studios/offices); and Phase 5 (Berks County 
Intermediate Unit Education Space).  The Applicant shall be required to revise the City of 
Reading Zoning Map. 
B.3 RSD Thomas Ford Hydroponic Land Development Plan; SALDO 2408-0013 
The development is located on 8.45 +/- acres of land located at 901 Margaret Street (TPID 
18530646155975), which is utilized as the Thomas Ford Elementary School.  The Reading 
School District proposes an Aquaponics Building (2,105 square feet) and other related site 
improvements to enhance educational opportunities to its students. 
B.4 Marella Enterprises Subdivision Plan; SALDO-2504-0005 
The development involves the realignment of an existing property line that separates two 
(2) lots of land, which are located at Eberly Street (Lot 1 TPID 1853065985705) and 301 
Morgantown Road (Lot 2 TPID 18530659845884). The lot line revision was to re-establish 
a lot line that complies with the corresponding uses.  Lots 1 and 2 are located within the 
commercial Highway (C-H) Zoning District. 
B.5 Albright Learning Commons Land Development Plan; SALDO 2505-0007 
The redevelopment project involves the Gingrich Library, which is part of Albright College, 
located at 1621 North 13th Street (TPID 17531723297432). This project is contained within 
the R-3 Zoning District and the institutional (INS) overlay District. This project replaced the 
Albright College Gingrich Library Land Development Plan.  
B.6 Lichty’s Mennonite Church Land Development Plan; PC 2023-101 
The redevelopment project is located on a 2.492-acre parcel of land at 22 Kenhorst 
Boulevard (TPID 18530617210967).  This project involves a change of use (animal 
hospital/veterinary to a place of worship) as well as the expansion of the off-street parking 
area.  The project required a municipal improvements agreement and a stormwater 
management maintenance agreement.

Section K: Possible Recommendations  
K.1 Update the Comprehensive Plan: Initiate the preparation of the comprehensive update to 
the City’s Comprehensive Plan in order to establish a unified long-term vision for land use, 
housing, transportation, economic development, infrastructure, sustainability, public safety 
and neighborhood revitalization.  
K.2 Advance Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) implementation. Continue advancing 
the Transit-Oriented Development Overlay District to encourage mixed-use development, 
higher-density housing, pedestrian-oriented design, and redevelopment opportunities near 
transportation corridors and transit facilities. 
K.3 Promote adaptive Reuse and Redevelopment projects. Encourage the rehabilitation 
and adaptive reuse of vacant, underutilized and historically significant structures to support 
housing growth, commercial revitalization, and neighborhood reinvestment through the city  
K.4 Support Housing Diversity and Affordability. Encourage residential developments that 
provide a mix of housing types, affordability levels, and adaptive reuse opportunities to 
address the growing housing demand within the City of Reading  
K.5 Enhance Development Review Efficiency. Continue evaluating and improving 
subdivision and land development review procedures to streamline application 
processing, improve interdepartmental coordination, and provide greater predictability for 
applicants and stakeholders  
K.6 Continue updating the zoning ordinances. Periodically review and modernize zoning 
regulations to ensure they remain consistent with current development trends, economic 
conditions, housing needs and the goals of the comprehensive plan.  
K.7 Address blighted and Vacant properties. Continue supporting strategies that identify, 
stabilize and redevelop blighted and vacant properties through public private partnership, 
code enforcement coordination, redevelopment incentives, and strategic planning 
initiatives. 
K.8 Support Economic Development and Strategic Investment. Continue coordinating 
planning efforts with CRIZ initiatives, institutional partners, developers and local business 
to attract investment, expand employment opportunities and strengthen neighborhood 
commercial corridors. 
K.9 Expand intergovernmental and institutional partnerships. Maintain coordination with 
regional agencies, educational institutions, neighboring municipalities, Berks County, and 
community organization to support comprehensive planning and shared development 
objectives. 
K.10 Provide ongoing training opportunities for both staff and commissioners. Encourage 
planning Commission members and staff to participate in planning, zoning, land use, and 
community development training programs to remain informed on best practices, legislative 
updates, and emerging planning trends. 
K.11 Prioritize strategic Corridor and Gateway improvement. Focus redevelopment and 
beautification efforts along key gateways, corridors, and neighborhood commercial areas
