In mid-2024, PennDOT and their lead project engineer from Pennoni contacted the Council 
Office and requested assistance to coordinate meetings with certain City offices regarding 
projects for the Centre Ave and N 5th St Bridge. The PA PUC and Norfolk Southern are 
requiring the elevation of both bridges so the bridges can accommodate double-decker railcars. 
It is not possible to lower the railroad tracks due to the topography. The cost of both projects 
will be covered by PennDOT. One contractor will be hired for both projects.  We are unsure 
which bridge project will be handled first. 
The Centre Ave Bridge is a railroad-owned bridge built by the railroad in 1918.  This bridge is in 
very poor condition that will require complete demolition and reconstruction. This bridge is 
currently posted for 12 tons/combination of 22 tons and cannot accommodate certain fire 
apparatus. 
The N 5th St Bridge is a City-owned bridge built by the railroad in 1936. While the bridge is in 
very poor condition it will only require replacement of the superstructure. Note –superstructure 
includes the girders, bearings and deck. Bearings are structural members capable of transferring loads 
from the deck to the substructure. The bearings displace stresses and load to the piers through the girders 
to allow movement between parts of a bridge. The movement can be linear as well as torsional. Bearings 
provide allowance between these parts. 
 
Meetings were held with the affected community in 2025 and additional meetings with the 
public will continue as design continues.  Norfolk Southern refuses to participate. 
 
Two engineering firms are preparing the design:  
• TYlin for the Centre Ave Bridge which will rise to 9 feet on the west and 7 feet on 
the east  
• HNTB for the N 5th St Bridge which will rise 2-3 feet 
PennDOT has asked the City to consider either eliminating the Centre Ave Bridge for vehicular 
traffic and instead having a pedestrian bridge or retaining the bridge with one northbound lane 
between N 5th Street and the bridge and two northbound lanes on the northern side of the 
bridge with one lane for left turns. 
The Police, Fire and Public Works Departments emphatically oppose the elimination of the 
Centre Ave bridge for vehicular traffic and emphatically support the retention of this bridge 
with at least one northbound lane. 
At this point, the City needs to provide PennDOT and the engineering team with a decision 
on keeping the bridge open to vehicular traffic or eliminating it or replacing the structure 
with a pedestrian bridge so they can proceed with the design work.  Public meetings are 
expected to begin in the 1st quarter of 2027.
