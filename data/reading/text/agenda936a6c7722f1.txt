REQUESTED ACTION: Council’s approval for 2026 budget amendments having to do with the annual $4.5 million 
permitted to be transferred from the sewer fund to the general fund. 
 
RECOMMENDATION: 
The Utilities Manager and Public Works Director recommend these budget amendments in the sewer funds; 
regional sewer fund [fund 54] and the retail (stormwater) sewer fund [fund 55].  
 
BACKGROUND: 
In January 2024, the City entered into a Fourth Modification of Consent Decree with the United States of America and 
Pennsylvania DEP which increased the allowed annual transfer from the sewer fund to the general fund to $4.5 million 
from $3.0 million, among other things. 
 
The incremental $1.5 million was budgeted in the regional sewer fund 54 in 2026, but this incremental amount should 
be paid by the retail sewer fund 55. 
 
BUDGETARY IMPACT 
The net budget amendments needed between the sewer funds 54 and 55 is zero.

AN ORDINANCE AMENDING CITY CODE CHAPTER 62, SECTION 62-410 COMPUTATION OF TIME 
OF SERVICE TO INCLUDE PROVISIONS FOR  THE REPAYMENT OF REFUNDED PENSION 
CONTRIBUTIONS FOR A FORMER ACT 120 POLICE OFFICER WHO RETURNS TO THE READING 
POLICE DEPARTMENT 
 
        THE COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF READING HEREBY ORDAINS AS FOLLOWS: 
             
SECTION 1. Section 62-410 shall be amended as follows: 
 
§ 62-410. Computation of time of service. 
   A.   Except as set forth in Subsection B of this section, the period of service used to determine whether a member is 
eligible for pension benefits under the provisions of this part shall be computed from the date on which a person 
becomes a member of the Police Pension Fund. If, after becoming a member, a person leaves the service of the City 
and is subsequently re-employed, for purposes of determining eligibility for benefits, his time of service shall be 
computed from the date on which he last became a member of the fund. 

 
Page12 
   B.   A member who terminates his service in the police force of the City and has been re- employed as a member of 
the police force shall be entitled to credit for prior service in determining his eligibility for pension benefits under the 
following circumstances: 
(1)   Such member shall be entitled to a credit for prior service if he has repaid to the fund any contributions which he 
withdrew from the fund upon leaving the service of the City. The extent of prior service for which such member shall 
be entitled to a credit shall be based on the length of time during which such member originally paid the 
contributions which he withdrew and which he repaid. 
(2) Such member shall repay the fund the amount refunded by the fund with interest compounded at the rate of 
7.25% per annum from the date of refund by the fund to the date of payment which shall be made within one year 
from the date of reappointment. In the event the payment is not made within the specified time limit, the right to 
obtain credit for prior years of service shall be lost for the applicable prior years’ period and any repayments shall 
be refunded without interest. 
 
SECTION 2.  All ordinances or parts of ordinances which are inconsistent herewith are hereby repealed.  
 
SECTION 3.  This Ordinance shall become effective ten (10) days after its adoption in accordance with 
Sections 219 & 221 of the City of Reading Home Rule Charter.

BILL NO. _______ - 2026 
AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY OF READING, PENNSYLVANIA, AMENDING CHAPTER 600, “ZONING,” OF THE CITY 
CODE TO DEFINE AND REGULATE DATA CENTER USES; TO PERMIT DATA CENTERS AND DATA CENTER 
CAMPUSES IN THE M-C MANUFACTURING COMMERCIAL DISTRICT AND THE H-M HEAVY MANUFACTURING 
DISTRICT ONLY BY CONDITIONAL USE; TO ESTABLISH SITING, WATER, ENERGY, NOISE, ENVIRONMENTAL, 
EMERGENCY-RESPONSE, MONITORING, COMMUNITY-IMPACT, COMMUNITY-BENEFIT, AND 
DECOMMISSIONING REQUIREMENTS; AND TO PROTECT FRITZ ISLAND, THE CITY WASTEWATER SYSTEM, 
ADJACENT WATERWAYS, AND POTABLE-WATER CAPACITY. 
THE CITY OF READING CITY COUNCIL HEREBY ORDAINS AS FOLLOWS: 
WHEREAS, the Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning Code, Act of July 31, 1968, P.L. 805, No. 247, as reenacted 
and amended, 53 P.S. §§ 10101 et seq. (the “MPC”), authorizes municipalities to regulate the location, size, intensity, 
design, operation, and impacts of land uses and to provide for conditional uses pursuant to express standards and 
criteria; and 
WHEREAS, MPC Sections 603, 604, 609, and 913.2 authorize zoning regulations designed to protect public 
health, safety, and welfare; secure safety from fire, flood, and other dangers; preserve environmentally sensitive 
areas; assure reliable, safe, and adequate water supplies; and permit City Council to impose reasonable conditions 
and safeguards on conditional uses; and 
WHEREAS, Data Centers are a distinct, infrastructure-intensive land use that may require substantial and 
continuous electrical capacity, cooling, water and wastewater service, emergency generation, battery storage, fuel 
storage, telecommunications infrastructure, and specialized fire and emergency response; and 
WHEREAS, Data Centers may also create continuous noise, low-frequency sound, heat, air emissions, 
stormwater, hazardous-material, visual, cumulative infrastructure, and decommissioning impacts that are not fully 
addressed by the City’s general industrial-use standards; and 
WHEREAS, the City’s potable-water system and drought resilience require that large water users demonstrate 
available capacity, minimize consumptive use, avoid routine groundwater and surface-water withdrawals, use 
efficient closed-loop cooling, and comply with curtailment and reporting requirements established by the Reading 
Area Water Authority and other agencies having jurisdiction; and 
WHEREAS, Data Centers may be permitted by Conditional Use within the M-C Manufacturing Commercial 
District and the H-M Heavy Manufacturing District, subject to the provisions specified under § 600-1110 of the Zoning 
Ordinance as well as the enforceable standards and cumulative-impact review, which shall allow appropriate 
consideration of the use while protecting neighborhoods, public infrastructure, environmental resources, and the 
City’s fiscal and operational interests; and 
WHEREAS, Data Centers shall not be prohibited within the Transit-Oriented Overlay District as well as 
designated areas within the Floodplain Overlay District, the Riparian Buffer Overlay District, the Steep Slope Overlay 
District and other designated areas that are specified within § 600-1110 of the Zoning Ordinance; and  
WHEREAS, the City owns, operates and/or maintains vital community facilities, transportation corridors and 
utility services that have provided opportunities for other compatible land uses that require protective measures 
from intensive industrial uses considering  adjacent land uses, utility services, environmental resources, ecological 
habitats, emergency management services and other quality of life issues; and 
WHEREAS, City Council further finds that project-specific impact mitigation, applicant-funded professional 
review, emergency-response support, operational monitoring, public reporting, successor liability, decommissioning 
financial security, and community-benefit commitments for projects receiving discretionary City assistance are 
necessary components of a responsible regulatory framework; and 
WHEREAS, City Council desires to amend Chapter 600 accordingly. 

 
Page17 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED AND ENACTED by the City Council of the City of Reading, Berks County, 
Pennsylvania, pursuant to the authority conferred by the Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning Code and the City of 
Reading Code, as follows: 
SECTION 1. INCORPORATION OF FINDINGS 
The foregoing recitals are incorporated into this Ordinance as legislative findings and expressions of Council’s 
intent. 
SECTION 2. AMENDMENT OF CHAPTER 600 
Chapter 600, “Zoning,” of the City of Reading Code of Ordinances is amended as set forth in Exhibit “A,” attached 
hereto and incorporated herein by reference. 
SECTION 3. CITY-ASSISTED DATA CENTER PROJECTS; COMMUNITY BENEFITS AGREEMENT 
A data center, major data center, or Data Center Campus that receives or proposes to receive discretionary City 
economic-development assistance, including City-owned land, a grant, loan, loan guarantee, tax abatement or 
similar incentive, utility subsidy, fee waiver, or other financial or in-kind assistance, shall, before such assistance 
is approved or conveyed, enter into a Community Benefits Agreement approved by City Council and in a form 
approved by the Council Solicitor. The Community Benefits Agreement shall be separate from the zoning 
approval and may include enforceable commitments concerning local recruitment and first-source hiring; 
apprenticeship and workforce-development partnerships; collaboration with the Reading School District, Reading 
Area Community College, organized labor, or other training providers; digital-inclusion investments; 
neighborhood energy- or water-affordability assistance; emergency-services support; Schuylkill River and 
Angelica Creek restoration; tree-canopy and green-infrastructure investments; waste-heat reuse; annual public 
reporting; performance milestones; clawbacks; audit rights; and successor-owner obligations. Nothing in this 
Section authorizes a condition or payment prohibited by Federal or Commonwealth law. 
SECTION 4. REPEALER 
All ordinances or parts of ordinances inconsistent with this Ordinance are repealed only to the extent of the 
inconsistency. All other provisions of the City of Reading Code remain in full force and effect. 
SECTION 5. SEVERABILITY 
If any section, subsection, paragraph, sentence, clause, or phrase of this Ordinance is for any reason held to be 
unconstitutional, illegal, or invalid by a court of competent jurisdiction, such decision shall not affect the validity 
of the remaining portions of this Ordinance. City Council declares that it would have enacted this Ordinance and 
each remaining provision notwithstanding the invalidity of any one or more provisions. 
SECTION 6. CODIFICATION AND ADMINISTRATIVE CORRECTIONS 
The City Clerk, in consultation with the Council Solicitor and the codifier, is authorized to correct section 
numbering, cross-references, capitalization, punctuation, typographical errors, and other nonsubstantive matters 
necessary for codification, provided that no substantive meaning is changed. 
SECTION 7. EFFECTIVE DATE 
This Ordinance shall become effective ten (10) days after final adoption and approval, or as otherwise provided 
by Sections 219 and 221 of the City of Reading Home Rule Charter.

EXHIBIT “A” 
AMENDMENTS TO CHAPTER 600, “ZONING” 
A-1. AMENDMENT TO § 600-2202, DEFINITIONS 
Section 600-2202, “Definitions,” is amended by adding the following definitions in alphabetical order: 
ABANDONMENT – The cessation of Data Center operations for a continuous period of twelve (12) months, 
excluding temporary interruptions due to maintenance, repairs, utility outages, or force majeure events. 
ACOUSTICAL PROFESSIONAL – An individual having a bachelor's or graduate degree in acoustics, 
engineering, physics, architecture, environmental science, or a related technical discipline with a minimum 
of five (5) years of documented professional experience in environmental sound measurement, acoustical 
modeling, and noise control, or an individual holding board certification through the Institute of Noise 
Control Engineering (INCE) or a successor credential of comparable rigor.   An acoustical professional 
need not hold a Pennsylvania professional engineer license solely by reason of preparing an acoustical study 
or report unless the particular work performed is required by the laws established by the Commonwealth 
of Pennsylvania to be prepared or sealed by a licensed professional engineer. 
ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE DATA CENTER – A Data Center designed or utilized primarily to support artificial 
intelligence, machine learning, neural network training, inference processing, large language models, or similar 
advanced computational systems. 
BASELINE WATER TESTING – Testing conducted prior to commencement of site disturbance or construction to 
establish existing water quality and water quantity or yield conditions for a public water supply system as well as 
private wells, springs, groundwater, or other water resources potentially affected by a proposed data center. 
BATTERY ENERGY STORAGE SYSTEM (BESS) — A device or group of devices assembled together and capable of 
storing electrical energy for later use, including an uninterruptible power supply using lithium-ion, lead-acid, 
flow, solid-state, or other battery technology. A BESS associated with a data center is data center accessory use 
and/or data center equipment and remains subject to all applicable fire, building, electrical, hazardous-material, 
and emergency-response requirements. 
CLOSED-LOOP COOLING SYSTEM — A cooling system that continuously re-circulates a contained coolant or heat-
transfer fluid within a closed-loop system and does not discharge that fluid to the environment as a routine part 
of operation. The term includes sealed direct-to-chip, rear-door heat exchanger, immersion, plate-and-frame, 
dry-cooler, closed-loop and/or equivalent systems. A system is not considered closed-loop merely because one 
(1) internal circuit is sealed if the overall heat-rejection process routinely consumes and discharges substantial 
quantities of potable water or other liquid, solid or gas discharge. 
COMMUNITY BENEFITS AGREEMENT — A written agreement between the City and a Data Center owner, 
operator, developer, or affiliated entity, approved by City Council and in a form approved by the Council Solicitor, 
that establishes enforceable community-benefit commitments, reporting, remedies, successor obligations, and, 
where applicable, clawbacks associated with discretionary City of Reading assistance. 
DATA CENTER — A building, structure, portion of a building, or group of buildings or structures primarily 
designed or used to house, operate, maintain, process, transmit, transfer, duplicate, enhance, back up, or store 
digital information using computer servers, data-processing equipment, telecommunications or network 
equipment, storage systems, artificial-intelligence or machine-learning computing equipment, or similar digital-
processing hardware. The term includes co-location facilities, enterprise data centers, hyperscale facilities, crypto 
currency mining, block chain transaction processing, server farms, and all data center accessory uses or 
equipment. The term does not include computer, server, telecommunications, or network equipment that is 
secondary and customarily incidental to another lawful principal use, such as equipment serving an office, 
hospital, school, governmental use, financial institution, or manufacturing facility, where the equipment or 
accessory components are not classified as the principal use of the property. 

 
Page19 
DATA CENTER ACCESSORY USE, BUILDING OR EQUIPMENT — Any ancillary use, structure, facility, system, or 
equipment secondary and incidental to a data center, including administrative, office, storage, logistical, fiber-
optic, security, and maintenance facilities; primary, emergency or backup generators; primary and alternative 
energy facilities; fuel storage; BESS; electrical substations, switchgear, transformers, and utility lines; water 
holding, pumping, treatment, and cooling systems; fire-suppression systems; air-conditioning, ventilation, heat-
rejection, and environmental-control equipment; redundant communications connections; screening and 
security features; and related pipes, conduits, structures, and appurtenances. The term does not include a 
combustion-based, nuclear, or other power-generation facility intended to supply electrical power during normal 
data center operations. Any such normal-operation generation facility requires separate zoning approval as an 
independently permitted use.  Primary or alternative energy systems may be accessory only when permitted by 
Chapter 600 of the City of Reading Code. 
DATA CENTER CAMPUS — An integrated or unified development of one or more data centers, whether 
constructed concurrently or in phases, together with any data center accessory uses or data center equipment. A 
data center campus includes: (1) cumulative data center development on one parcel; (2) data centers or 
accessory facilities located on contiguous parcels, parcels separated only by a street, railroad, utility corridor, or 
watercourse, or parcels within 500 feet of one another that are under common or affiliated ownership, 
operation, control, financing, or development; or (3) facilities that share a substation, normal or backup power 
system, cooling system, water or sewer infrastructure, access drive, communications infrastructure, or other 
material infrastructure. All parcels and phases within a data center campus shall be aggregated for purposes of 
this Zoning Ordinance. 
DATA CENTER DECOMMISSIONING – The cessation of data center operations; removal of data 
center buildings, structures, equipment, fuel storage facilities, and other improvements required to be 
removed under an approved decommissioning plan; disconnection, removal, or lawful repurposing of 
associated utility infrastructure; and restoration of the site in accordance with this Zoning  Ordinance and 
the approved decommissioning plan. 
DATA CENTER ELECTRICAL SUBSTATION – A facility used for the transformation, transmission, distribution, or 
switching of electrical power for the primary purpose of serving a data center or data center campus located on 
the same property, adjacent property, or property immediately across a public right-of-way. 
DATA CENTER EQUIPMENT – Equipment, systems, and appurtenances customarily associated with and 
incidental to the operation of a data center, including: electrical switchgear; transformers; uninterruptible 
power supply systems (UPS); battery energy storage systems; cooling equipment; primary, emergency or 
backup generators; fuel storage facilities; pumps; heat rejection systems; and other related equipment and 
infrastructure. 
DATA CENTER OPERATOR - Any person, corporation, partnership, limited liability company, association, 
governmental entity, or other legal entity that owns, leases, manages, operates, or controls a data center or data 
center campus. 
DATA CENTER PRINCIPAL BUILDING – A building containing the primary computing, data storage, 
networking, telecommunications, office, or operational functions of a data center.  
EDGE DATA CENTER – A Data Center designed to provide computing, processing, storage, or networking 
resources closer to end users or devices in order to reduce latency and improve performance.  
EMERGENCY POWER GENERATION FACILITY – Equipment designed to provide electrical power 
during interruptions in utility service, including generators, transfer switches, fuel storage systems, and 
associated infrastructure.  
HYPERSCALE DATA CENTER – A major or large-scale data center or data center campus designed to provide 
cloud computing, artificial intelligence computing, major or large-scale data processing, storage, networking, 
or related services and characterized by extensive computing capacity, redundancy, supporting 
infrastructure, and substantial utility demands. 

 
Page20 
INDEPENDENT QUALIFIED PROFESSIONAL – A qualified professional retained by or on behalf of the City of 
Reading, and not employed by or under contract with the applicant or owner, to independently review a study, 
report, plan, analysis, certification, or other technical submission required by this Zoning Ordinance. 
INFRASOUND – Acoustic energy having a frequency below twenty (20) Hertz (Hz). 
LOW-FREQUENCY SOUND – Acoustic energy concentrated within the lower-frequency portion of the audible 
spectrum, including frequencies from twenty (20) Hertz through two hundred (200) Hertz, or such other range 
recognized by generally accepted acoustical engineering standards. 
MAJOR DATA CENTER — A data center that: (1) contains more than 50,000 square feet of indoor gross floor area 
devoted to the data center use; (2) has a projected or approved maximum coincident electrical demand of 20 
megawatts or more at the point of utility interconnection; or (3) is part of a data center campus. Affiliated 
parcels, buildings, phases, and shared infrastructure shall be aggregated in applying these thresholds.  A “major 
data center” may also be referred to as a “hyperscale data center”. 
MODULAR DATA CENTER – A prefabricated or transportable structure containing servers, networking 
equipment, power systems, cooling systems, or related infrastructure used for Data Center operations. 
ONCE-THROUGH COOLING — A cooling method in which water is withdrawn from a public or private source, 
passes through equipment or a heat exchanger one time or substantially one time, and is then discharged to a 
sanitary sewer, storm sewer, surface water, groundwater, or another location without continuous recirculation 
through the cooling system. 
PROFESSIONAL ENGINEER – A licensed individual who has met specific education and experience requirements, 
passed related examinations and is authorized to offer engineering services in accordance with the laws and 
professional licensing requirements established by the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania.  All such professional 
engineers shall be in good standing with the City of Reading and shall comply with all professional and ethical 
standards. 
QUALIFIED PROFESSIONAL CONSULTANT – An individual who has met specific education, licensing and/or 
experience requirements that may apply to professional services that apply to: legal; engineering; architecture; 
planning; zoning; surveying; environmental; ecological; geology; hydrogeology; air quality; acoustics; utilities; real 
estate; building code compliance; emergency management; landscaping; illumination; transportation; flood 
control; stormwater management; historic and cultural resources; construction; community and economic 
development; housing; or other qualified professionals that may provide services in accordance with Chapter 600 
of the City of Reading Code.  All such professionals may be subject to a pre-qualification assessment or 
evaluation.  
SENSITIVE RECEPTOR — A lot, building, structure, use and/or outdoor activity area occupied or used for a 
residential use; public or private school; preschool, child-care or adult day-care use; hospital; assisted-living, 
personal-care, long-term-care, retirement, or nursing home facility; community center; library; place of worship; 
dormitory; campground; or public park or active-recreation facility, excluding a trail where no other sensitive 
receptor uses are present. 
WATER-INTENSIVE COOLING SYSTEM – A cooling system that relies upon groundwater, surface water, potable 
water, or other water sources and results in substantial consumptive water use through evaporation, blow-down, 
discharge, or other processes, as distinguished from a closed- loop cooling system or other water-efficient cooling 
technology designed to minimize consumptive water use. 
WATER WITHDRAWAL – The removal, diversion, extraction, pumping, or consumption of groundwater, surface 
water, public water, reclaimed water, or any other water source for data center operations.

(1) A data center, major data center, or data center campus is permitted only in the M-C Manufacturing 
Commercial District and the H-M Heavy Manufacturing District and only after Conditional Use approval by 
City Council under §600-606, §600-1110 and §600-1204 and compliance with this Section.

K.   Noise and vibration control standards.  
(1) The purpose of this section is to protect neighboring properties and sensitive receptors from 
excessive operational noise, tonal noise, low-frequency sound, Infrasound, and vibration associated 
with the construction and operation of data centers while permitting reasonable operation of data 
center facilities and associated infrastructure. 
(2) Noise and Vibration Impact Study.  All data center applications shall include a Noise and Vibration 
Impact Study, which shall be prepared by a professional engineer that shall be a qualified 
professional consultant in the field acoustics or acoustical engineering. The study shall be 
sufficiently detailed to permit independent technical review and to demonstrate compliance under 
all reasonably foreseeable operating modes.  The Noise and Vibration Impact Study shall include the 
following: 
(a) existing baseline ambient sound measurements at representative project property lines, 
sensitive receptors, and residentially zoned properties; 
(b) projected operational sound levels under normal operating conditions; 
(c) projected sound levels associated with emergency generators and other backup equipment, 
including the loudest reasonably foreseeable simultaneous operating condition; 
(d) daytime and nighttime sound modeling; 
(e) A-weighted (dBA) and C-weighted (dBC) sound analysis; 
(f) octave-band or other full-spectrum analysis sufficient to evaluate Low-Frequency Sound; 
(g) qualitative identification and discussion of Infrasound where proposed equipment is 
reasonably anticipated to generate such sound; 
(h) evaluation of tonal characteristics; 
(i) evaluation of vibration; 
(j) octave-band sound power levels and locations for all significant modeled equipment; 
(k) source and receptor locations, existing and proposed buildings, terrain and topography, 
ground-absorption assumptions, noise barriers, atmospheric attenuation, modeling software 
and version, and all material model inputs and calculation assumptions; 
(l) modeling of all significant equipment operating simultaneously under the loudest reasonably 
foreseeable condition, including cooling equipment, transformers, ventilation equipment, 
emergency generators, and load banks where used; 
(m) modeling of all sensitive receptors within one thousand  (1,000) feet of the consolidated 
project property boundary and any additional receptor locations reasonably required by the 
City of Reading based upon site-specific conditions; 
(n) noise-contour maps for areas within one thousand (1,000) feet of the consolidated project 
property boundary at a receptor height of approximately five (5) feet above local ground 
elevation; 

 
Page31 
(o) point-receiver calculations at residential dwellings using receiver heights representative of the 
highest occupied floor exposed to the project, including approximately five (5) feet for single-
story dwellings and fifteen (15) feet for two-story dwellings, with greater heights where 
warranted; and 
(p) identification of all proposed mitigation measures. 
As part of the Conditional Use Application, the City of Reading may require additional information in 
order to consider noise and vibration impacts for the study area. 
(3) Measurement Standards.   The sound measurement standards shall include: 
(a) a baseline ambient sound survey conducted for a minimum of forty-eight (48) consecutive 
hours at representative locations; 
(b) utilize ANSI S1.4-2014 / IEC 61672-1 Class 1 instrumentation, or successor standards 
comparable rigor; 
(c) follow applicable ANSI/ASA S12.9 environmental sound measurement procedures, or 
successor standards; 
(d) include meteorological monitoring sufficient to identify invalid or unreliable measurement 
periods; 
(e) utilize fast time-response settings for compliance measurements; 
(f) include field calibration immediately before and after measurements; 
(g) exclude invalid measurement periods resulting from precipitation, excessive wind, 
construction activity unrelated to the facility, equipment malfunction, or other atypical events;

(4) Compliance Demonstration:  The Applicant shall demonstrate that the data center is designed to

(5) Maximum Operational Sound Levels:  The sound generated by a data center, data center accessory 
uses and/or data center equipment, measured at any project property line and where reasonably 
necessary to evaluate compliance, at or within the property line of a sensitive receptor or property 
located within a residential zoning district, shall not exceed the following decibel levels: 
(a) Between 8:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m., Monday through Friday: 50 dBA 
(b) Between 5:00 p.m. and 8:00 a.m., Monday through Friday, and at all times on Saturdays, 
Sundays, and legal holidays: 40 dBA. 
(6) In addition to the fixed operational limits above, project sound shall not exceed the lowest valid 
hourly baseline ambient A-weighted L90 plus five (5) dBA. The more restrictive of the ambient-
based A-weighted criterion and the fixed A-weighted operational limit shall govern. Baseline

ambient levels shall not be used to increase any fixed maximum sound level established by this 
Ordinance. C-weighted and octave-band and low-frequency sound evaluations shall remain subject 
to the fixed and technical criteria otherwise established herein. 
(7) Infrasound and Low-Frequency Sound:  The Noise Impact Study shall identify and evaluate Low-
Frequency Sound using octave- band or other generally accepted acoustical methodologies. 
Infrasound shall be qualitatively identified and discussed where equipment is reasonably anticipated 
to generate such sound. No separate numerical Infrasound compliance limit shall apply unless an 
objective measurement methodology and compliance criterion supported by generally accepted 
professional standards is adopted by the City of Reading or established as a lawful condition of 
approval based upon competent expert evidence. Where Low-Frequency Sound or Infrasound 
analysis identifies the potential for unreasonable impacts upon neighboring properties or sensitive 
receptors, the applicant shall incorporate appropriate mitigation measures that shall be considered. 
(8) Tonal Noise:   The Noise and Vibration Impact Study shall evaluate the prominent tonal noise using 
ISO 1996-2:2017, Annex C, or a successor objective tonal-assessment standard of comparable 
rigor. Where prominent tonality is predicted during acoustical modeling or identified during post-
construction compliance measurements, a five (5) dB tonal adjustment shall be applied for purposes 
of evaluating compliance with the applicable A-weighted limit, and appropriate mitigation 
measures shall be incorporated. 
(9) Alternative Energy and Generator Testing. 
(a) Routine maintenance and testing of emergency generators shall occur only between 9:00 a.m. 
and 3:00 p.m.  The data center operator may conduct periodic or routine generator and 
equipment testing on Monday through Friday between the hours 9:00 a.m. and 3:00 p.m., 
excluding state and federal holidays. Unless otherwise directed by the City of Reading, no 
more than fifty (50) percent of the data center generators may be simultaneously tested at any 
given time, and the testing shall be sequenced and muffled to in order to minimize impacts. 
(b) Routine testing shall be sequenced in order to minimize simultaneous generator operation 
unless simultaneous testing is demonstrated to be operationally necessary and compliance 
with this Section is demonstrated. 
(c) Commissioning or maintenance outside the routine testing hours shall be permitted only where 
demonstrated to be reasonably necessary and specifically authorized as part of the approved 
operational plan, and all such operation shall remain subject to the operational sound limits 
established by this Zoning Ordinance.   The City of Reading may conduct sound and vibration 
control measurements during the testing period. 
(d) The use of alternative energy systems and/or generators testing requirements shall comply with 
the provisions specified under Section 600-1110.H of this Zoning Ordinance.  
(10) Emergency Operations. 
(a) The operational sound limits established herein shall apply during all operating conditions, 
including normal operations, maintenance, commissioning, testing, utility demand-response or 
peak-shaving activity, utility interruptions, and operation of emergency generators. The data 
center facility shall be designed to consider mitigation measures considering foreseeable 
simultaneous or operations for the proposed emergency power systems values that comply 
with the provisions established under Section 600-1110 of this Zoning Ordinance. 
(b) The Noise Impact Study shall model and disclose anticipated sound levels resulting from 
simultaneous operation of emergency generators and other emergency equipment at the 
maximum reasonably foreseeable operating condition and shall identify all mitigation 
necessary to demonstrate compliance. 
(c) The backup energy supply system and/or generator(s) shall only be utilized at the data center

energy supply or noise violations in any given year.  The Applicant shall agree to these terms 
as part of the Conditional Use Application. 
(d) The data center operator shall notify the Police Department, Fire and Rescue Department,

(11) Vibration Control:  The operation of the data center or data center equipment shall not produce

(12) Noise Mitigation Measures.  In order to achieve compliance with Section 600-1110.K, the Applicant 
shall incorporate sound reduction, suppression and/or mitigation measures, including but not

(13) Pre and Post-Construction Compliance. 
(a) A pre-construction sound and vibration study shall be prepared by a qualified acoustical 
professional. The study shall include at least 48 hours of ambient measurements at not fewer 
than three (3) representative property-line and sensitive receptor locations; predictive 
modeling at full operational load; all cooling, electrical, substation, generator, BESS, and 
rooftop sources; tonal and low-frequency analysis; emergency and testing scenarios; 
cumulative impacts; and specific mitigation compliance measures. 
(b) An as-built sound and vibration study shall be completed by a qualified acoustical 
professional at interim and full representative load within three (3) months after issuance of 
the initial certificate of occupancy or  for each phase and before final release of land  
development plan financial security. Noncompliance shall be promptly corrected and retested.  
Failure to comply with these provisions shall result in the retraction of the certificate of 
occupancy.  
(c) The as-built sound and vibration testing shall continue on an annual basis for a five (5) year 
period after the certificate of occupancy has been issued for the final phase of the project.  
(d) Testing shall evaluate the maximum representative operating condition achievable during the 
verification period and shall include dBA, dBC, octave-band/low- frequency sound, tonal 
characteristics, and representative generator testing. Infrasound shall be qualitatively 
evaluated where applicable. 
(e) All data center equipment and emergency or backup generators shall be tested at full capacity 
considering normal and peak demand conditions.   
(f) All pre and post-construction noise verification study shall be prepared by a qualified acoustical 
professional who shall verify compliance with the applicable provisions of this Zoning Ordinance. 
(g) The City of Reading may require additional or alternative testing when operational conditions

L.   Air emissions, generators, fuel, hazardous materials, and BESS. 
(1) The applicant shall submit an Air Emissions and Generator Plan identifying every stationary and mobile 
emissions source; fuel type and storage; generator rating and operating classification; testing, 
commissioning, and emergency operating hours; applicable local, state and federal requirements; required 
permits; and proposed emission controls. 
(2) New emergency generators shall meet the most stringent state and federal emission standards and shall 
incorporate commercially available controls for nitrogen oxides, particulate matter, carbon monoxide, 
volatile organic compounds, and visible emissions where technically feasible and permitted. Generator 
stacks and testing shall be designed to minimize impacts on sensitive receptors. 
(3) Underground storage of generator fuel, coolant, or hazardous chemicals is prohibited. Aboveground tanks 
shall be double-walled or located within approved secondary containment, protected from vehicle impact, 
monitored for leaks, and set back as required by this Section. 
(4) A Spill Prevention, Control, and Countermeasure Plan shall be provided in order to address delivery, 
transfer, storage, inspection, training, reporting, isolation of stormwater systems, and protection of public 
wastewater disposal and treatment facilities and public water supply systems. 
(5) A Hazardous Materials Management Plan shall identify all anticipated fuels, coolants, refrigerants, heat-
transfer fluids, fire-suppression agents, water-treatment chemicals, batteries, cleaners, solvents, and other 
regulated materials; quantities and locations; safety data sheets; storage design; compatibility; inventory 
and reporting; waste handling; and emergency procedures. 
(6) A BESS shall comply with the currently adopted construction and fire codes and applicable NFPA standards, 
including NFPA 855 where applicable, and shall include listed equipment, thermal-runaway detection, 
ventilation, separation, fire suppression, emergency disconnects, fire-department access, and post-incident 
procedures appropriate to the installed technology. 
(7) The data center and data center equipment shall comply with all air quality and/or emission requirements 
that are specified by the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection and the United States 
Environmental Protection Agency.

P.   Decommissioning, abandonment, and financial security. 
(1) The Conditional Use Application shall include a Decommissioning and Site Restoration Plan prepared by 
qualified professionals. The plan shall identify removal, reuse, recycling, demolition, environmental testing 
and remediation, utility disconnection, restoration, stormwater stabilization, and disposition of buildings, 
generators, BESS, substations, tanks, cooling systems, foundations, and other equipment. 
(2) Prior to the issuance of a building permit for the data center, the owner or operator of the data center shall 
provide financial security in a form approved by the City Council Solicitor and Finance Director in an 
amount equal to 125% of the independently reviewed estimated cost of decommissioning and restoration, 
without credit for salvage value. The estimate and security shall be updated at least every five years and 
whenever a material expansion or change increases the cost. 
(3) A data center is abandoned when the principal data center operation has ceased for twelve (12) 
consecutive months, excluding a period of reconstruction after casualty or force majeure under a written 
schedule approved by the Zoning Administrator. Upon abandonment, decommissioning shall begin 
promptly and be completed within twelve (12) months unless City Council approves a written extension 
based on demonstrated progress and public safety. 
(4) The owner and operator are jointly and severally responsible for decommissioning. Transfer of ownership 
or operation does not release any obligation. The City of Reading may draw upon the financial security and 
undertake or complete decommissioning to the extent authorized by law and the security instrument. 
(5) The owner or operator of the data center shall record a covenant, in a form approved by the City Council 
Solicitor, providing notice of the continuing operational, monitoring, decommissioning, and successor 
obligations under this Section  of the Zoning Ordinance. 
(6) The owner or operator of the data center shall be responsible for all required modifications, closures 
and/or abandonment of traffic improvements, building, sanitary sewage facilities, water supply facilies, 
and/or other infrastructure as directed by the City of Reading.
