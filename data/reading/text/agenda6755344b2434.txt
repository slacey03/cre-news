I. Pagoda Skyline Inc (PSI) re Pavilion at the Fire Tower 
Ms. Reed welcomed Mr. Reinert and Mr. Ray from Pagoda Skyline. 
 
Mr. Reinert stated that Pagoda Skyline Inc formed in as a non-profit in 1970 with the mission 
to preserve the Pagoda, the Fire Tower and Mt. Penn. He highlighted the annual events 
organized by and through PSI. He noted that in 2026 PSI held the 113rd Easter dawn service at 
the Fire Tower.   
 
Mr. Reinert stated that PSI is reapplying to install a pavilion on the Fire Tower grounds.  He 
explained that the previous application for a pavilion on the northern side of the Fire Tower 
was denied as PSI expected Public Works to maintain the pavilion. He stated that PSI has 
reapplied for a pavilion on the southern side of the Fire Tower in partnership with Blue 
Mountain Region SCCA (BMR) and the PSI agrees to be responsible for the care and 
maintenance of the structure.   
 
Mr. Reinert described the recent improvement project at the Fire Tower and PSI’s plan to open 
the building the 3rd Sunday of each month from 12-4 pm.

II. Update to Council Policy Book 
Ms. Reed called everyone’s attention to the revision to the policy attached to the agenda.  She 
thanked Ms. Gombar and Ms. Kelleher for their work on this new draft that when ratified will 
streamline operations for the Council office 
 
Mr. Gombar agreed and added that the terms were discussed at the October 19th COW. He 
stated that the policy handbook does have some watered-down policy on commendations and 
proclamations. He stated that based on the discussion last Monday he drafted language and 
worked with the City Clerk’s office on revisions. He described where the draft shows new 
language, revised language and deleted language.  
 
Mr. Gombar noted that this new policy addresses and categorizes proclamations, 
commendations and certificates of recognition along with letters or notes of congratulation or 
recognition – with direction that  
• requests must go to the City Clerk first so she can delegate the task based on the current 
office workload 
• if the request involves a Council district outside the requesting Councilors boundaries, 
the requestor must inform the affected District Councilor providing them with the 
opportunity to join in 

4 
• requests to the City Clerk should be provided at least 7 business days in advance 
• clarifies that letters of congratulations, recognition, sympathy etc. are to be drafted by 
each individual Council Members on their individual letterhead or City notecard 
 
Mr. Gombar stated that that is a summary of the modifications proposed for the policy book 
that describes the interactions and individual work apart from work that is usually assigned to 
staff and is legislative in nature. 
 
When the floor for questions opened, Mr. Baez stated that he is impressed by the scope of this 
draft, due to his belief that the conversation last week was limited to how much preparation 
time was required for commendations.  He stated that the summary provided by Mr. Gombar 
moved a simple calendar to a complicated permission slip – who can recognize whom, inter-
district interaction, approval processes, obtaining permission from another Council prior to 
moving forward, etc. He questioned how something with a simple start grew into something 
complicated. 
 
Mr. Baez reminded the body that Ms. Reed is Council President today, that could change. He 
continued that if he were elected Council President, he might enjoy some of the powers in this 
proposed draft; however, he is not convinced that those powers should be provided to the 
President.  He reminded the body of the national conversation taking place regarding how 
much executive power should be concentrated in one office or seat. He questioned why we 
would make that same mistake here and why seven independent Council members would 
move executive authority to one seat. 
 
Mr. Baez noted the importance of institutions because the person in power changes.  He 
questioned why seven independent Council members would move their authority to one seat. 
He suggested fixing the timing which is a housekeeping matter and not providing one seat 
with the power to decide who gets to approve what or sign what and who gets to tell another 
Council member yes or no. He suggested that prior to handing out more authority, we should 
be reminded of words of advice from his predecessor - when we vote, we do not just vote for 
our district, we vote for our city. 
 
When questioning if the Charter ordains Council which supersedes policy, Mr. Gombar 
clarified that there is nothing in this policy, as ratified or proposed, that provides a Council 
member or the President with the ability to approve or disapprove of anything.  He stated that 
the draft says that if a Councilor asks for a commendation for something or someone located in 
another Council district, that the requesting Councilor needs to provide advance notice to the 
Councilor representing that district so they can choose to join in. The Council President’s only 
role is to be one of the signatories on the commendation. 
In response to a question from Mr. Baez, Mr. Gombar stated that the policy does not require 
the Council President to sign all commendations.  Ms. Kelleher noted that has been past 
practice since the inception of the Council President position 

5 
 
Mr. Baez questioned where in the Charter or Administrative Code is the Council President the 
supervisor of another Council member. Mr. Gombar stated that the Charter and 
Administrative Code does not provide the Council President with supervisory authority; 
however, he added that he is unsure if that concept is in the Policy Handbook.   
Note: under Council’s Structural Evolution, the last line in the 2nd paragraph says “The President 
interacts with the Mayor and other governmental entities and represents the voice of Council.”  
Administrative Code 5-208 B 6 says “Rule No. 6. Duties of the presiding officer. The President of 
Council shall decide all questions subject to appeal by any two members of Council and have general 
supervision of the meeting. He shall determine the appropriate committee to which legislation and other 
matters shall be referred. The President of Council shall be the designated representative of the body and 
shall communicate the intentions of Council, with respect to professional service, contracts, legislative 
action and other matters, as needed.”  
Administrative Code 5-208 B Rule No. 9 c. says “ (c)   President of Council or presiding officer may, at 
his discretion, choose to limit debate time on each agenda item to 10 minutes, as stated in Roberts' Rules 
of Order.” 
 
Ms. Reed explained that this proposed policy is not reassigning power, instead it is attempting 
to provide notice that the District 5 Councilor is presenting a commendation to a business in 
District 6 which will allow the District 6 Councilor to participate if they choose. She added that 
in the past this practice of courtesy was automatic among the body, even during periods when 
a divisive issue occurred. She added that although prior Councils never saw the need to add 
this process to the Policy Handbook, there appears to be some need to take that step now.  She 
stated that in the past Councilors practiced with kindness and common sense and that the 
inclusion of this language now is not a play of power on the Council President’s position. 
 
Mr. Gombar stated that past practice for commendations was to have the signatures of the 
Council President with the Councilor making the request. He added that the draft does not 
provide the Council President with the ability to approve or deny a request. 
 
In response to a question from Mr. Baez, Mr. Gombar noted the need to add a response time 
when the requesting Councilor provides notice to a Councilor from another district. 
 
In response to a question from Mr. Baez, Ms. Reed stated that requests for letters of support for 
non-profits are usually made to the City Clerk or the Council President.  The requesting party 
is usually seeking support from City Council as a body which requires the signature of the 
President.   
Mr. Baez questioned why he individually could not draft a letter showing his individual 
support for a grant or project. 
 
In response to a question from Mr. Gombar, Ms. Reed stated that past practice has been for the 

6 
requests to come to the Council President, as the head of he legislative body, or the City Clerk.  
She noted that the requests are usually made by non-profits that the City supports. 
In response to a question from Mr. Gombar, Ms. Kelleher stated that letters of support from 
non-profits were never rejected. 
 
Mr. Baez questioned the goal of this policy amendment that addresses letters, the Council 
President’s need to sign letters of support, etc. He noted Ms. Reeds prior statements regarding 
the power of the mayor in comparison to the power of Council in this form of government.  He 
expressed the belief that this proposed policy amendment represents a consolidation of the 
Council Presidents power. He stated that if the policy is adopted, he will comply; however, he 
cautioned on the need to consider if the next Council President should be afforded with those 
same abilities in the future. 
 
Ms. Reed stated that the position of Council President is what it is regardless of the person 
elected to hold that position. The draft policy is a process that defines the way Council 
members quietly conducted business in the past.  She added that if this process had been 
defined in the past it would have applied to all who preceded her in this position. She 
expressed the belief that this policy does not consolidate any additional power or authority to 
the Council President. 
 
Mr. Baez asked Mr. Gombar what happene if the policy is not adopted and what specific 
problem is being solved if the Council President signs a letter of support rather than an 
individual Council member.  
Mr. Gombar inquired if there is any objection to having a letter of support signed by the 
Council President and the District Council who made the request, similar to the process for 
commendations. 
Mr. Nunez questioned why this revision to the policy is so controversial.  He stated that he 
fails to see what the problem is. He stated that a Council President was elected and a Vice 
President was appointed and he questioned why adding the process which has been in place 
for multiple years is suddenly a problem. He stated that as the President of Council is elected 
city-wide, that person does have a “supervisory” or leadership role that is a bit different from 
a District Councilor. He again questioned why defining past practice is controversial. He 
suggested letting the Council President fulfill her role. He added there is no need to over 
complicate simple issues.  He suggested focusing on issues that have a greater need to the 
community, rather than wasting an inordinate amount time discussing this proposed policy. 
Mr. Miller agreed that the proposed policy provides a good solution to allowing a District 
Councilor to consider honoring a business or individual from outside of his/her district and 
informing the Councilor where the business or person is located and/or commenting on an 
issue that is occurring in another district. He also agreed with the need to memorialize this in 
the policy book as some may not be aware of what past practice is. He added that today he 
was asked to comment on a District 4 issue and he referred the caller to Mr. Baker or to Ms. 

7 
Reed, if Mr. Baker is unavailable. 
 
Mr. Baker agreed with the need to place unwritten rules and policies in writing so all Council 
members can be made aware of the requirements. He agreed that if a Councilor wants to issue 
a commendation or business opening outside his/her district, he/she should make the District 
Councilor know about this in advance. He stated that he agrees with the need to define these 
processes in the policy book. 
 
Mr. Baez thanked Mr. Nunez for his remarks but questioned the need to add an existing 
unwritten process in the policy book.  He questioned if the Council President supervises 
District Councilors. 
Mr. Gombar stated that the Charter does not provide the Council President with a supervisory 
role.  
Mr. Nunez clarified that it is the role of the Council President, or the Vice President, to provide 
leadership and advice to Councilors if they are unsure of how to handle a situation or if there 
is a process if place that provides insight. He suggested adding language in the policy that 
directs a District Councilor to the President for guidance and/or advice. He added that the 
President can also advise District Councilors about the right way to proceed with situations.  
Mr. Gombar stated that there will be revisions to the draft which can be considered at the 
November 9th meeting.
