DATA CENTER ZONING ORDINANCE 
Review Draft Package 
STATUS OF THIS DOCUMENT 
• Prepared as a working draft for review by Councilor Baez, City Council, the Council Solicitor, the City 
Clerk, Planning and Zoning, Public Works, the Reading Area Water Authority, the Fire Department, 
the Environmental Advisory Council, the Shade Tree Commission, and other affected departments. 
• Not ready for introduction, advertisement, enactment, or reliance until the Council Solicitor and City 
Clerk confirm legal form, codification, procedures, and the official current text of Chapter 600. 
• The recommended numeric standards should be tested through a Planning/GIS parcel-yield analysis 
to confirm that the ordinance leaves realistic, appropriately located opportunities for the use and 
does not create a de facto exclusion. 
 
Prepared by: 
Councilor Baez 
Draft date: 
June 22, 2026 
This draft combines the strongest components of the Allentown and Cumru approaches, while adapting 
the structure to the City of Reading Code and adding Reading-specific protections for Fritz Island, the 
City wastewater system, adjacent waterways, potable-water capacity, community impact mitigation, 
community benefits for City-assisted projects, ongoing monitoring, and decommissioning. 
 

KEY DRAFT ASSUMPTIONS AND REVIEW ITEMS 
1. Permitted location. The draft permits a Data Center or Data Center Campus only in the H-M Heavy 
Manufacturing District and only by Conditional Use before City Council. Planning and GIS should test this 
choice against actual parcel size, sensitive-receptor setbacks, floodplain, river setbacks, and 
infrastructure constraints before advertisement. 
2. Project tiers. The draft uses a five-acre minimum for a Data Center and an eight-acre minimum for a Major 
Data Center or Data Center Campus. A Major Data Center is triggered by more than 50,000 square feet of 
gross floor area, maximum coincident electrical demand of 20 megawatts or more, or campus treatment. 
3. Fritz Island and waterways. The draft prohibits Data Centers on Fritz Island and on wastewater 
infrastructure parcels; establishes additional setbacks from the Fritz Island wastewater treatment plant 
and the Schuylkill River, Mifflin Arm, and Angelica Creek; protects emergency access and sewer 
infrastructure; prohibits direct routine withdrawals and discharges; and restricts high-risk equipment in 
flood-prone areas. 
4. Potable-water protection. The draft requires public water and sewer capacity certifications, closed-loop and 
low-water cooling, drought curtailment, metering, annual reporting, and applicant-funded utility 
improvements. Groundwater and direct surface-water withdrawals for routine operation are prohibited. 
5. Noise and generators. The draft establishes a continuous 55 dBA standard near sensitive receptors, a 65 
dBA standard at other lot lines, tonal and low-frequency controls, staged acoustic studies, restricted 
generator testing, and post-occupancy monitoring. 
6. Community benefits. The zoning provisions require project-specific impact mitigation. Broader workforce, 
educational, digital-inclusion, neighborhood, and environmental benefits are addressed through a 
separate Community Benefits Agreement when a project receives discretionary City assistance. This 
structure is intended to reduce the risk of an unsupported zoning exaction. 
7. Lifecycle obligations. The draft includes annual reporting, operator-change and successor obligations, a 
decommissioning plan, and financial security equal to 125% of estimated decommissioning cost. The 
Council Solicitor should confirm the preferred security instrument and enforcement mechanism. 
8. Procedure. This document is the permanent zoning-amendment draft only. It does not include a 
moratorium or a municipal curative-amendment resolution under MPC Section 609.2. The Solicitor 
should advise separately on whether such a resolution is appropriate. 
MINIMUM REVIEW BEFORE INTRODUCTION 
• Council Solicitor: statutory authority; conditional-use standards; community-benefit structure; 
fee/escrow authority; decommissioning security; enforcement; and due-process language. 
• City Clerk: bill format, section numbering, ordinance history, official Code text, referral, notice, 
advertisement, public-hearing, and enactment schedule. 
• Planning/GIS: parcel-yield and exclusion analysis; H-M mapping; sensitive receptors; floodplain; and 
Fritz Island/waterway setbacks. 
• RAWA and Public Works/WWTP: water, drought, sewer capacity, discharges, Fritz Island access, 
pump stations, interceptors, and spill-response requirements. 
• Fire Department and Emergency Management: BESS, fuel, generator, access, hydrant, training, 
inspection, and emergency-response requirements. 
• Planning Commission, Environmental Advisory Council, Shade Tree Commission, and Berks County 
Planning Commission: review and recommendations under applicable law.

BILL NO. _______-2026 
AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY OF READING, PENNSYLVANIA, AMENDING CHAPTER 600, “ZONING,” OF 
THE CITY CODE TO DEFINE AND REGULATE DATA CENTER USES; TO PERMIT DATA CENTERS AND DATA 
CENTER CAMPUSES IN THE H-M HEAVY MANUFACTURING DISTRICT ONLY BY CONDITIONAL USE; TO 
ESTABLISH SITING, WATER, ENERGY, NOISE, ENVIRONMENTAL, EMERGENCY-RESPONSE, MONITORING, 
COMMUNITY-IMPACT, COMMUNITY-BENEFIT, AND DECOMMISSIONING REQUIREMENTS; AND TO 
PROTECT FRITZ ISLAND, THE CITY WASTEWATER SYSTEM, ADJACENT WATERWAYS, AND POTABLE-
WATER CAPACITY. 
THE CITY OF READING CITY COUNCIL HEREBY ORDAINS AS FOLLOWS: 
WHEREAS, the Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning Code, Act of July 31, 1968, P.L. 805, No. 247, as 
reenacted and amended, 53 P.S. §§ 10101 et seq. (the “MPC”), authorizes municipalities to regulate the 
location, size, intensity, design, operation, and impacts of land uses and to provide for conditional uses 
pursuant to express standards and criteria; and 
WHEREAS, MPC Sections 603, 604, 609, and 913.2 authorize zoning regulations designed to protect 
public health, safety, and welfare; secure safety from fire, flood, and other dangers; preserve environmentally 
sensitive areas; assure reliable, safe, and adequate water supplies; and permit City Council to impose 
reasonable conditions and safeguards on conditional uses; and 
WHEREAS, Data Centers are a distinct, infrastructure-intensive land use that may require substantial and 
continuous electrical capacity, cooling, water and wastewater service, emergency generation, battery storage, 
fuel storage, telecommunications infrastructure, and specialized fire and emergency response; and 
WHEREAS, Data Centers may also create continuous noise, low-frequency sound, heat, air emissions, 
stormwater, hazardous-material, visual, cumulative infrastructure, and decommissioning impacts that are not 
fully addressed by the City’s general industrial-use standards; and 
WHEREAS, the City owns and operates critical wastewater-conveyance and treatment infrastructure on 
Fritz Island, situated between the Mifflin Arm of the Schuylkill River and Angelica Creek, and City Council finds 
that such infrastructure, its emergency access, and the adjacent waterways require heightened protection 
from flood, spill, fire, access, and utility-conflict risks; and 
WHEREAS, the City’s potable-water system and drought resilience require that large water users 
demonstrate available capacity, minimize consumptive use, avoid routine groundwater and surface-water 
withdrawals, use efficient closed-loop cooling, and comply with curtailment and reporting requirements 
established by the Reading Area Water Authority and other agencies having jurisdiction; and 
WHEREAS, City Council finds that limiting Data Centers to the H-M Heavy Manufacturing District as a 
Conditional Use, subject to objective and enforceable standards and cumulative-impact review, will allow 
appropriate consideration of the use while protecting neighborhoods, public infrastructure, environmental 
resources, and the City’s fiscal and operational interests; and 

WHEREAS, City Council further finds that project-specific impact mitigation, applicant-funded 
professional review, emergency-response support, operational monitoring, public reporting, successor liability, 
decommissioning financial security, and community-benefit commitments for projects receiving discretionary 
City assistance are necessary components of a responsible regulatory framework; and 
WHEREAS, City Council desires to amend Chapter 600 accordingly. 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED AND ENACTED by the City Council of the City of Reading, Berks County, 
Pennsylvania, pursuant to the authority conferred by the MPC and the City of Reading Home Rule Charter, as 
follows: 
SECTION 1. INCORPORATION OF FINDINGS 
The foregoing recitals are incorporated into this Ordinance as legislative findings and expressions of 
Council’s intent. 
SECTION 2. AMENDMENT OF CHAPTER 600 
Chapter 600, “Zoning,” of the City of Reading Code of Ordinances is amended as set forth in Exhibit “A,” 
attached hereto and incorporated herein by reference. 
SECTION 3. CITY-ASSISTED DATA CENTER PROJECTS; COMMUNITY BENEFITS AGREEMENT 
A Data Center, Major Data Center, or Data Center Campus that receives or proposes to receive 
discretionary City economic-development assistance, including City-owned land, a grant, loan, loan 
guarantee, tax abatement or similar incentive, utility subsidy, fee waiver, or other financial or in-kind 
assistance, shall, before such assistance is approved or conveyed, enter into a Community Benefits 
Agreement approved by City Council and in a form approved by the Council Solicitor. The Community 
Benefits Agreement shall be separate from the zoning approval and may include enforceable 
commitments concerning local recruitment and first-source hiring; apprenticeship and workforce-
development partnerships; collaboration with the Reading School District, Reading Area Community 
College, organized labor, or other training providers; digital-inclusion investments; neighborhood energy- 
or water-affordability assistance; emergency-services support; Schuylkill River and Angelica Creek 
restoration; tree-canopy and green-infrastructure investments; waste-heat reuse; annual public reporting; 
performance milestones; clawbacks; audit rights; and successor-owner obligations. Nothing in this Section 
authorizes a condition or payment prohibited by Federal or Commonwealth law. 
SECTION 4. REPEALER 
All ordinances or parts of ordinances inconsistent with this Ordinance are repealed only to the extent of 
the inconsistency. All other provisions of the City Code remain in full force and effect.

If any section, subsection, paragraph, sentence, clause, or phrase of this Ordinance is for any reason held 
to be unconstitutional, illegal, or invalid by a court of competent jurisdiction, such decision shall not affect 
the validity of the remaining portions of this Ordinance. City Council declares that it would have enacted 
this Ordinance and each remaining provision notwithstanding the invalidity of any one or more provisions. 
SECTION 6. CODIFICATION AND ADMINISTRATIVE CORRECTIONS 
The City Clerk, in consultation with the Council Solicitor and the codifier, is authorized to correct section 
numbering, cross-references, capitalization, punctuation, typographical errors, and other nonsubstantive 
matters necessary for codification, provided that no substantive meaning is changed. 

SECTION 7. EFFECTIVE DATE 
This Ordinance shall become effective ten (10) days after final adoption and approval, or as otherwise 
provided by Sections 219 and 221 of the City of Reading Home Rule Charter.

AMENDMENTS TO CHAPTER 600, “ZONING” 
A-1. AMENDMENT TO § 600-2202, DEFINITIONS 
Section 600-2202, “Definitions,” is amended by adding the following definitions in alphabetical order: 
BATTERY ENERGY STORAGE SYSTEM (BESS) — A device or group of devices assembled together and capable 
of storing electrical energy for later use, including an uninterruptible power supply using lithium-ion, lead-
acid, flow, solid-state, or other battery technology. A BESS associated with a Data Center is Data Center 
Accessory Use or Equipment and remains subject to all applicable fire, building, electrical, hazardous-
material, and emergency-response requirements. 
CLOSED-LOOP COOLING SYSTEM — A cooling system that continuously recirculates a contained coolant or 
heat-transfer fluid and does not discharge that fluid to the environment as a routine part of operation. The 
term includes sealed direct-to-chip, rear-door heat exchanger, immersion, plate-and-frame, dry-cooler, 
and equivalent systems. A system is not closed-loop merely because one internal circuit is sealed if the 
overall heat-rejection process routinely consumes and discharges substantial quantities of potable water. 
COMMUNITY BENEFITS AGREEMENT — A written agreement between the City and a Data Center owner, 
operator, developer, or affiliated entity, approved by City Council and in a form approved by the Council 
Solicitor, that establishes enforceable community-benefit commitments, reporting, remedies, successor 
obligations, and, where applicable, clawbacks associated with discretionary City assistance. 
DATA CENTER — A building, structure, portion of a building, or group of buildings or structures primarily 
designed or used to house, operate, maintain, process, transmit, transfer, duplicate, enhance, back up, or 
store digital information using computer servers, data-processing equipment, telecommunications or 
network equipment, storage systems, artificial-intelligence or machine-learning computing equipment, or 
similar digital-processing hardware. The term includes colocation facilities, enterprise data centers, 
hyperscale facilities, cryptocurrency mining, blockchain transaction processing, server farms, and all Data 
Center Accessory Uses or Equipment. The term does not include computer, server, telecommunications, or 
network equipment that is secondary and customarily incidental to another lawful principal use, such as 
equipment serving an office, hospital, school, governmental use, financial institution, or manufacturing 
facility, where the equipment is not the principal use of the property. 
DATA CENTER ACCESSORY USE OR EQUIPMENT — Any ancillary use, structure, facility, system, or equipment 
secondary and incidental to a Data Center, including administrative, office, storage, logistical, fiber-optic, 
security, and maintenance facilities; emergency or backup generators; fuel storage; BESS; electrical 
substations, switchgear, transformers, and utility lines; water holding, pumping, treatment, and cooling 
systems; fire-suppression systems; air-conditioning, ventilation, heat-rejection, and environmental-control 
equipment; redundant communications connections; screening and security features; and related pipes, 
conduits, structures, and appurtenances. The term does not include a combustion-based, nuclear, or other 
power-generation facility intended to supply electrical power during normal Data Center operations. Any 
such normal-operation generation facility requires separate zoning approval as an independently 
permitted use. Alternative energy systems may be accessory only when permitted under § 600-1012 and 
all other applicable provisions of the City Code. 

DATA CENTER CAMPUS — An integrated or unified development of one or more Data Centers, whether 
constructed concurrently or in phases, together with any Data Center Accessory Uses or Equipment. A 
Data Center Campus includes: (1) cumulative Data Center development on one parcel; (2) Data Centers or 
accessory facilities located on contiguous parcels, parcels separated only by a street, railroad, utility 
corridor, or watercourse, or parcels within 500 feet of one another that are under common or affiliated 
ownership, operation, control, financing, or development; or (3) facilities that share a substation, normal 
or backup power system, cooling system, water or sewer infrastructure, access drive, communications 
infrastructure, or other material infrastructure. All parcels and phases within a Data Center Campus shall 
be aggregated for purposes of this Chapter. 
DATA CENTER OPERATOR — The person or entity responsible for the operation, maintenance, and post-
construction compliance of a Data Center or Data Center Campus, whether or not the person or entity 
owns the real property. 
MAJOR DATA CENTER — A Data Center that: (1) contains more than 50,000 square feet of indoor gross floor 
area devoted to the Data Center use; (2) has a projected or approved maximum coincident electrical 
demand of 20 megawatts or more at the point of utility interconnection; or (3) is part of a Data Center 
Campus. Affiliated parcels, buildings, phases, and shared infrastructure shall be aggregated in applying 
these thresholds. 
ONCE-THROUGH COOLING — A cooling method in which water is withdrawn from a public or private source, 
passes through equipment or a heat exchanger one time or substantially one time, and is then discharged 
to a sanitary sewer, storm sewer, surface water, groundwater, or another location without continuous 
recirculation through the cooling system. 
SENSITIVE RECEPTOR — A lot, building, structure, or outdoor activity area occupied or used for a residential 
use; school, preschool, child-care or adult day-care use; hospital; assisted-living, personal-care, long-term-
care, retirement, or nursing facility; community center; library; place of worship; dormitory; campground; 
or public park or active-recreation facility, excluding a trail where no other Sensitive Receptor use is 
present.

A-3. NEW § 600-1109, DATA CENTERS 
Part 11, “Additional Requirements for Specific Uses or Zoning Districts,” is amended by adding a new § 
600-1109 as follows: 
§ 600-1109. Data Centers. 
A. Purpose, applicability, and controlling standards. 
(1) A Data Center, Major Data Center, or Data Center Campus is permitted only in the H-M Heavy 
Manufacturing District and only after Conditional Use approval by City Council under §600-606 and 
§600-1204 and compliance with this Section. 
(2) This Section applies to new construction; conversion or adaptive reuse of an existing building; 
expansion, intensification, or material modification of an existing use; establishment or expansion of 
Data Center Accessory Use or Equipment; and each phase of a Data Center Campus. 

(3) All affiliated parcels, buildings, phases, owners, operators, applicants, and shared facilities that meet 
the definition of Data Center Campus shall be reviewed cumulatively and shall not be segmented to 
avoid any threshold, standard, report, approval, or mitigation obligation. 
(4) Where this Section and another provision of the City Code regulate the same matter, the more 
restrictive provision shall control. Compliance with this Section does not relieve an applicant, owner, 
or operator from any other Federal, Commonwealth, regional, authority, utility, or City permit or 
approval. 
(5) All plans, studies, representations, mitigation measures, and commitments submitted by or on behalf 
of the applicant and relied upon in granting approval are incorporated as continuing conditions of the 
Conditional Use approval unless expressly modified in accordance with this Section. 
B. Pre-application conference and public information meeting. 
(1) Before filing a Conditional Use application for a Major Data Center or Data Center Campus, the 
applicant shall participate in a pre-application conference with representatives designated by the City, 
which may include Planning and Zoning, the City Engineer, Public Works and wastewater operations, 
the Reading Area Water Authority, the Fire Department, Emergency Management, the Environmental 
Advisory Council, the Shade Tree Commission, and the Council Solicitor. 
(2) At least 30 days before filing the Conditional Use application, the applicant for a Major Data Center or 
Data Center Campus shall conduct at least one public information meeting in the City. The applicant 
shall provide written notice at least 14 days before the meeting to the City Clerk and to the owners of 
record of property within 1,000 feet of the outer boundary of the proposed Data Center Campus, 
using the most recent available tax records. The notice shall identify the site, meeting date and 
location, anticipated gross floor area, electrical demand, water demand, cooling system, generator 
and BESS capacity, construction phasing, and a contact for questions. The meeting is informational 
and does not replace any hearing required by law. A copy of the minutes from that meeting shall 
accompany the Conditional Use application. 
C. Siting and dimensional standards. 
The following standards supersede less restrictive dimensional standards otherwise applicable in the 
H-M District. Measurements shall be made to the nearest exterior edge of the applicable building, 
structure, equipment, containment area, or use:

(1) A required setback or buffer under this Section may include stormwater or passive open-space 
improvements only if those improvements do not impair screening, emergency access, spill 
containment, or the purpose of the setback. 
(2) No building, equipment, parking, loading, storage, or security facility may encroach into a required 
landscape buffer except a driveway, utility, emergency access, trail, or similar crossing that is 
generally perpendicular and no wider than reasonably necessary. 
(3) Setbacks from a Sensitive Receptor apply whether the Sensitive Receptor is located in the City or an 
adjacent municipality and whether the source and receptor are separated by a street, alley, railroad, 
utility corridor, or municipal boundary. 
(4) The minimum lot area, coverage, open-space, and setback standards shall be calculated over the entire 
Data Center Campus. No subdivision, lease line, condominium boundary, easement, or change in 
ownership shall reduce compliance. 
(5) The minimum setbacks established by this Section may not be reduced by City Council as a condition or 
modification of Conditional Use approval. Any zoning relief shall be obtained only through the 
procedure authorized by the MPC and this Chapter. 
(6) City Council may approve a principal building height greater than 50 feet, but not greater than 70 feet, 
only where the lot or Data Center Campus contains at least 15 acres; the applicant provides one 
additional foot of setback from each public street right-of-way, residential district boundary, and 
Sensitive Receptor lot line for each foot of building height above 50 feet; all roof-mounted equipment 
is screened; and the Fire Chief confirms that emergency access and fire-suppression capabilities are 
adequate for the proposed height. 
D. Data Center Campus master plan; phasing; anti-segmentation. 
(1) An application involving a Data Center Campus shall include a master plan covering all parcels, 
anticipated phases, buildings, power and communications infrastructure, substations, generators, 
BESS, fuel storage, cooling and water systems, stormwater facilities, access, emergency facilities, 
buffers, and open space reasonably contemplated during the next ten years. 

(2) Each phase shall independently comply with all applicable health, safety, environmental, emergency-
access, noise, utility, and screening standards and shall remain consistent with the approved master 
plan. Approval of an initial phase does not authorize an unreviewed later phase. 
(3) The applicant shall disclose all persons and entities with an ownership, operational, development, 
financing, or contractual interest sufficient to establish common or affiliated control and shall identify 
all shared infrastructure. 
E. Site design, landscaping, buffering, architecture, and lighting. 
(1) A landscape and screening plan prepared and sealed by a Pennsylvania-registered landscape architect 
shall be submitted. The plan shall preserve healthy existing vegetation where practicable and use 
diverse native or climate-adapted species appropriate to the site. 
(2) Each required perimeter buffer shall provide a dense, opaque, four-season visual screen. At a 
minimum, each 100 linear feet of buffer shall contain six evergreen trees at least eight feet in height 
at planting, two deciduous canopy trees at least 2½ inches in caliper, two ornamental or understory 
trees, and 20 shrubs, arranged in staggered and naturalistic groupings. The City may approve 
equivalent plant-unit compositions recommended by the Shade Tree Commission or City Arborist. 
(3) Where a Data Center abuts or is across a street from a Sensitive Receptor or residential district, a 
landscaped earthen berm at least six feet high with side slopes no steeper than 3:1 shall be provided 
unless the City Engineer and Shade Tree Commission determine that preserved topography, existing 
vegetation, an acoustical wall, or a combination provides equal or greater year-round visual and 
acoustic protection. 
(4) All plantings shall be perpetually maintained. Dead, diseased, removed, or severely damaged material 
shall be replaced on a one-for-one basis with material meeting the original minimum size during the 
next appropriate planting season. Landscape installation and two full growing seasons of maintenance 
shall be secured by financial guarantee in a form acceptable to the City. 
(5) Ground-mounted and roof-mounted mechanical, electrical, cooling, generator, BESS, substation, and 
similar equipment shall be screened from public streets and Sensitive Receptors by the principal 
building, acoustical barrier, architectural screen, landscape screen, or a combination. Screening shall 
be compatible with the principal building and shall not impair required ventilation, access, or fire 
safety. 
(6) Any building facade facing a public street, residential district, or Sensitive Receptor shall avoid a long, 
monolithic appearance and shall incorporate at least two of the following at intervals not exceeding 
150 horizontal feet: a change in material, pattern, texture, or color; a change in building height or 
roofline; a projection, recess, or step-back at least five feet deep; fenestration or an expressed entry 
feature; or a green-wall or trellis treatment. Multiple public-facing facades shall use consistent 
architectural treatment. 
(7) Security fencing may be not more than eight feet high and shall be dark-colored metal or an 
architecturally compatible solid wall. Barbed wire, razor wire, and similar visibly intrusive deterrents 
are prohibited where visible from a public street or Sensitive Receptor. Fencing shall be located 
behind required landscape buffers except at controlled entrances. 
(8) Outdoor storage is prohibited except approved and fully screened fuel, replacement equipment, 
emergency supplies, and materials required for operation. At least one off-street loading space shall 
be provided. Parking shall be based on the maximum employee shift plus visitor and service demand 
and shall not exceed 110% of the demonstrated need without approval by the Zoning Administrator. 

(9) Exterior lighting shall be fully shielded and directed downward. Light trespass shall not exceed 0.05 
horizontal footcandle at a lot line of a Sensitive Receptor or residential district and 0.20 horizontal 
footcandle at any other lot line, excluding temporary emergency lighting. A photometric plan shall 
include all security lighting. 
F. Environmental and community impact assessment. 
(1) The applicant shall submit an Environmental and Community Impact Assessment prepared by qualified 
professionals. The assessment shall evaluate the proposed use at full buildout and full operational 
load under normal conditions and reasonably foreseeable adverse conditions, including drought, 
extreme heat, extended power outage, fire, flooding, equipment failure, and spill or release. 
(2) The assessment shall address direct and cumulative impacts on air quality; greenhouse-gas emissions; 
soil and groundwater quality; surface-water quality; wetlands and aquatic resources; wildlife and 
habitat; flood risk; stormwater; heat-island conditions; noise and vibration; public water and sewer 
capacity; electric-grid capacity and reliability; emergency services; transportation; solid and electronic 
waste; municipal costs; and nearby residential neighborhoods and Sensitive Receptors. 
(3) Cumulative-impact analysis shall consider existing, approved, and pending Data Centers and other 
major utility users within the same utility service area or within three miles of the site, to the extent 
information is reasonably available. 
(4) The assessment shall identify feasible alternatives and specific mitigation measures. No Conditional Use 
shall be approved where a significant adverse impact identified by the assessment remains 
unmitigated or where the applicant fails to demonstrate that the proposed mitigation is technically 
and financially enforceable. 
(5) The applicant shall provide a nontechnical public summary of the assessment and all proposed 
mitigation measures. 
G. Traffic, access, and construction management. 
(1) A traffic and access assessment shall be prepared at a scope approved by the City Engineer and shall 
evaluate construction traffic, employee and service traffic, emergency access, turning movements, 
intersection operations, pavement and bridge constraints, delivery routes, and conflicts with 
pedestrians, bicycles, transit, and wastewater-facility access. 
(2) A Major Data Center or Data Center Campus shall provide two independent means of ingress and 
egress suitable for emergency apparatus, unless the Fire Chief and City Engineer approve an 
equivalent emergency-access design based on site constraints and public safety. 
(3) A construction-management plan shall identify haul routes, staging, parking, work hours, dust and mud 
control, temporary noise control, neighborhood communication, and protection of public utilities and 
wastewater access. Trucks may not idle except as necessary for safety, traffic, loading, refrigeration, 
or manufacturer-required operation. 
(4) The applicant shall construct and fund all on-site and off-site transportation improvements that are 
lawfully required and reasonably attributable to the project, subject to the limitations of the MPC and 
other applicable law. 
H. Electric power, energy management, and utility lines. 
(1) An Energy Management and Electric Utility Impact Plan prepared and sealed by a professional engineer 
shall state the anticipated average and maximum coincident electrical demand in megawatts; annual 
electricity consumption; source and point of interconnection; proposed utility upgrades; generator, 
BESS, and substation capacity; expected Power Usage Effectiveness; energy-efficiency measures; on-
site alternative energy; and reasonably foreseeable impacts on capacity, reliability, availability, and 
infrastructure serving other customers. 

(2) The applicant shall submit a current letter or agreement from the applicable electric utility confirming 
that the utility is willing and able to serve the proposed use subject to stated conditions and upgrades. 
A Conditional Use approval does not constitute or guarantee utility capacity. 
(3) The applicant shall fund all utility extensions, substations, protection systems, and other improvements 
lawfully required and directly attributable to the project. Utility improvements shall be designed to 
avoid unreasonable interruption or degradation of service to existing customers. 
(4) Emergency or backup generators are accessory only when intended for actual emergencies, outages, 
commissioning, maintenance, and testing. Routine use for peak shaving, participation in demand-
response markets, or normal operation is prohibited unless separately approved as a lawful principal 
or accessory generation use under the City Code and all applicable permits. 
(5) On-site utility, fiber-optic, telecommunications, and electrical distribution lines from the property 
boundary or substation to the Data Center shall be installed underground to the maximum extent 
practicable, except where the utility requires aboveground installation or the City Engineer 
determines that underground installation is infeasible or less protective of public safety or the 
environment. 
I. Water supply, sanitary sewer, cooling, and drought resilience. 
(1) A Water Utilization and Cooling Report prepared and sealed by a professional engineer shall state: 
average-day, maximum-day, and peak-hour water demand; all water sources; intended use by 
category; cooling-system type; expected Water Usage Effectiveness; quantity recirculated, consumed, 
discharged, or lost; wastewater volume and characteristics; discharge destinations; metering; leak 
detection; emergency water needs; and operation under drought, extreme heat, and power-outage 
conditions. 
(2) The applicant shall submit written certifications from the Reading Area Water Authority and the City 
wastewater utility, or their successors, identifying available capacity, pressure and fire-flow 
requirements, sewer-collection and treatment capacity, required upgrades, service conditions, 
drought restrictions, and any limits on cooling or industrial discharges. The applicant shall fund all 
upgrades lawfully required and attributable to the project. 
(3) A Data Center shall be served by public water and public sanitary sewer. Groundwater wells and direct 
withdrawals from the Schuylkill River, Mifflin Arm, Angelica Creek, Tulpehocken Creek and Rose Valley 
Creek or any other surface water are prohibited for routine Data Center operations. A 
nonconsumptive closed-loop geothermal system may be considered only with all required permits 
and a hydrogeologic study demonstrating no adverse impact. 
(4) Once-Through Cooling and open-circuit evaporative cooling towers are prohibited. A Data Center shall 
use a Closed-Loop Cooling System and dry, direct-to-chip, immersion, rear-door heat exchanger, or 
other low-water heat-rejection technology to the maximum extent technically practicable. 
(5) City Council may approve a closed-circuit evaporative or hybrid cooling component only upon written 
findings, supported by the Water Utilization and Cooling Report and independent review, that: (a) a 
non-evaporative alternative capable of meeting the required load is not reasonably feasible; (b) the 
Reading Area Water Authority certifies sufficient capacity under normal and drought conditions; (c) 
reclaimed or nonpotable water has been evaluated and will be used where available and lawful; (d) 
enforceable maximum water-use, metering, leak-detection, discharge, and drought-curtailment 
conditions are established; and (e) no adverse effect on existing customers, water quality, or 
wastewater capacity will result. 
(6) Discharge of cooling water, blowdown, process water, chemicals, or heat to the municipal separate 
storm sewer system, groundwater, or surface water is prohibited. Discharge to the sanitary sewer 
requires written approval from the City wastewater utility and all other agencies having jurisdiction. 

(7) Separate, continuously recording meters shall measure total water supplied, water used for cooling, 
water reused, and wastewater discharged. The operator shall maintain a drought and emergency-
curtailment plan consistent with Reading Area Water Authority requirements and shall comply with 
all mandatory restrictions. 
J. Fritz Island, wastewater infrastructure, flood, and waterway protection. 
(1) No Data Center, Data Center Accessory Use or Equipment, or Data Center Campus shall be located on 
Fritz Island or on a parcel owned or used by the City or a municipal authority for wastewater 
treatment, wastewater conveyance, a pump station, interceptor sewer, flood-control facility, 
drinking-water facility, or related critical public infrastructure. 
(2) A principal Data Center building shall be set back at least 300 feet from the property boundary of the 
Fritz Island wastewater treatment plant. A generator, BESS, electrical substation, bulk fuel or chemical 
storage area, exterior cooling or water-treatment equipment, or hazardous-material containment 
area shall be set back at least 500 feet from that boundary. 
(3) A principal Data Center building shall be set back at least 150 feet from the top of bank or ordinary 
high-water mark, whichever produces the greater setback, of the Schuylkill River, Mifflin Arm, 
Angelica Creek, Tulpehocken Creek and Rose Valley Creek and any other perennial watercourse. A 
generator, BESS, substation, bulk fuel or chemical storage area, exterior cooling or water-treatment 
equipment, or hazardous-material containment area shall be set back at least 300 feet. 
(4) All principal buildings and Data Center Accessory Uses or Equipment shall be located outside the one-
percent-annual-chance floodplain. Generators, BESS, substations, fuel and chemical storage, and 
hazardous-material containment shall also be located outside the 0.2-percent-annual-chance 
floodplain unless a more restrictive Federal, Commonwealth, or City standard applies. 
(5) Where any portion of a proposed Data Center Campus lies within 1,000 feet of the Fritz Island 
wastewater treatment plant, Schuylkill River, Mifflin Arm, Angelica Creek, Tulpehocken Creek and 
Rose Valley Creek, a pump station, or a major interceptor sewer, the applicant shall submit a 
hydrologic, hydraulic, flood, spill-migration, utility-conflict, and emergency-access assessment 
prepared by qualified professionals. 
(6) No Data Center development, utility extension, access drive, grading, stormwater facility, or security 
feature may impair the operation, maintenance, expansion, flood protection, or emergency access of 
the Fritz Island wastewater treatment plant, a pump station, interceptor sewer, or other public water 
or wastewater infrastructure. 
(7) All fuel, coolant, heat-transfer fluid, water-treatment chemical, and other liquid storage or transfer 
areas shall have impervious secondary containment capable of holding at least 110% of the volume of 
the largest container, plus reasonably anticipated precipitation where exposed, and shall include leak 
detection, isolation, and spill-response access. 
K. Noise and vibration. 
(1) The combined sound from the Data Center and all Data Center Accessory Uses or Equipment under full 
normal operational load shall not exceed the following, measured at the point on a receiving property 
where the sound level is highest: 
• At a Sensitive Receptor lot line or residential zoning-district boundary: 55 dBA Leq(1 hour), or the 
documented preconstruction ambient Leq(1 hour) plus 3 dBA, whichever is greater, but in no 
event more than 60 dBA Leq(1 hour). 
• At any other lot line: 65 dBA Leq(1 hour). 
• The A-weighted maximum sound level, Lmax, shall not exceed the applicable Leq limit by more 
than 10 dBA during normal operation. 

(2) If a prominent discrete tone, whine, hum, or cyclic characteristic is present, as determined under an 
applicable ANSI or ISO method, 5 dBA shall be added to the measured or modeled level for 
compliance purposes. At a Sensitive Receptor, Data Center sound in the 31.5, 63, or 125 hertz octave 
bands shall not exceed the documented preconstruction ambient level in the corresponding band by 
more than 5 dB during normal operation. 
(3) No ground-borne or structure-borne vibration generated by the Data Center shall be perceptible 
without instruments beyond the Data Center property line or exceed applicable ISO residential 
vibration criteria. 
(4) A preconstruction sound and vibration study shall be prepared by a licensed professional engineer 
specializing in acoustics or a professional certified by the Institute of Noise Control Engineering. The 
study shall include at least 24 hours of ambient measurements at not fewer than three representative 
property-line and Sensitive Receptor locations; predictive modeling at full operational load; all 
cooling, electrical, substation, generator, BESS, and rooftop sources; tonal and low-frequency analysis; 
emergency and testing scenarios; cumulative impacts; and specific mitigation. 
(5) An interim acoustic certification shall be submitted with the building-permit plans, based on final 
equipment selections and locations. All recommended mitigation shall be incorporated into the 
construction documents. 
(6) An as-built sound and vibration study shall be completed at full representative load within six months 
after issuance of the first certificate of occupancy for each phase and before final release of land-
development financial security. Noncompliance shall be promptly corrected and retested. 
(7) For the first three years of operation, and thereafter not more than once in any 12-month period upon 
written request by the City or upon a documented complaint, the operator shall fund a continuous 
14-day compliance-monitoring study at locations selected by the City’s acoustic consultant. This does 
not limit enforcement based on other competent evidence. 
(8) Routine generator testing is permitted only Monday through Friday between 9:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m., 
excluding Federal holidays. No more than 25% of the facility’s generators may be tested 
simultaneously, and testing shall be sequenced and muffled to minimize impacts. Commissioning 
outside these limits requires advance written approval from the Fire Chief and Zoning Administrator 
based on necessity and a temporary noise-control plan. 
(9) Emergency generator operation during an actual utility outage or emergency is exempt from the 
numerical limits only for the duration reasonably necessary to protect life, safety, equipment, or data. 
The facility shall restore normal-operation compliance as soon as practicable and not later than eight 
hours after utility power is restored, unless the Fire Chief authorizes additional time for safety 
reasons. 
(10) All acoustic studies, monitoring, peer review, and corrective testing shall be performed at the 
applicant’s or operator’s expense by professionals independent of the equipment manufacturer or 
installer. 
L. Air emissions, generators, fuel, hazardous materials, and BESS. 
(1) The applicant shall submit an Air Emissions and Generator Plan identifying every stationary and mobile 
emissions source; fuel type and storage; generator rating and operating classification; testing, 
commissioning, and emergency operating hours; applicable Federal and Commonwealth 
requirements; required permits; and proposed emission controls. 

(2) New emergency generators shall meet the most stringent applicable Federal and Commonwealth 
emission standards and shall incorporate commercially available controls for nitrogen oxides, 
particulate matter, carbon monoxide, volatile organic compounds, and visible emissions where 
technically feasible and permitted. Generator stacks and testing shall be designed to minimize 
impacts on Sensitive Receptors. 
(3) Underground storage of generator fuel, coolant, or hazardous chemicals is prohibited. Aboveground 
tanks shall be double-walled or located within approved secondary containment, protected from 
vehicle impact, monitored for leaks, and set back as required by this Section. 
(4) A Spill Prevention, Control, and Countermeasure Plan shall be provided where required by law and, 
whether or not legally required, a facility-specific spill prevention and response plan shall address 
delivery, transfer, storage, inspection, training, reporting, isolation of stormwater systems, and 
protection of Fritz Island and adjacent waterways. 
(5) A Hazardous Materials Management Plan shall identify all anticipated fuels, coolants, refrigerants, 
heat-transfer fluids, fire-suppression agents, water-treatment chemicals, batteries, cleaners, solvents, 
and other regulated materials; quantities and locations; safety data sheets; storage design; 
compatibility; inventory and reporting; waste handling; and emergency procedures. 
(6) A BESS shall comply with the currently adopted construction and fire codes and applicable NFPA 
standards, including NFPA 855 where applicable, and shall include listed equipment, thermal-runaway 
detection, ventilation, separation, fire suppression, emergency disconnects, fire-department access, 
and post-incident procedures appropriate to the installed technology. 
M. Emergency management and public safety. 
(1) The applicant shall submit an Emergency Response Plan prepared by qualified professionals and 
reviewed by the Fire Department, City Emergency Management, Public Works, and other agencies 
designated by the City. No certificate of occupancy shall be issued until the Fire Chief confirms that 
the final plan, access, water supply, fire-flow, hydrants, fire-protection systems, communications, and 
pre-incident plan are acceptable under applicable codes. 
(2) The Emergency Response Plan shall address fire suppression, containment, ventilation, evacuation, 
shelter-in-place, BESS incidents, electrical hazards, fuel and chemical releases, cooling-system failures, 
flood and severe weather, extended power outage, cybersecurity-related physical emergencies, 
continuity of essential safety systems, and coordination with the Fritz Island wastewater treatment 
plant where relevant. 
(3) The applicant and operator shall fund project-specific initial and recurring training, site familiarization, 
drills, and specialized equipment for City first responders to the extent reasonably attributable to the 
project and identified by the Fire Chief or Emergency Management. 
(4) A 24-hour emergency contact sign shall be posted at each site entrance and shall identify the facility, 
operator, responsible representative, emergency telephone number, and electric utility contact. 
Changes shall be reported to the City within three business days. 
(5) The operator shall permit at least annual fire-safety and hazardous-material inspections and shall 
update the Emergency Response Plan whenever equipment, fuel, BESS technology, cooling, site 
access, or operations materially change. 
N. Heat mitigation, energy and water efficiency, and waste-heat reuse. 
(1) A Thermal Impact Mitigation Plan prepared by a professional engineer shall evaluate heat rejection, 
plume, microclimate, heat-island effects, equipment placement, cool or reflective roofs, green roofs, 
tree canopy, vegetated stormwater systems, building orientation, and other feasible measures. 

(2) A Major Data Center or Data Center Campus shall include a feasibility analysis for beneficial reuse of 
waste heat by nearby municipal, institutional, residential, commercial, greenhouse, district-energy, or 
industrial users. Where feasible and accepted by an end user, the site shall preserve reasonable space 
and connection points for future heat recovery. 
(3) The applicant shall identify projected and actual Power Usage Effectiveness and Water Usage 
Effectiveness and describe measures to improve efficiency over the life of the facility. No specific 
certification program is required unless made a condition of a Community Benefits Agreement or 
other discretionary City assistance. 
O. Electronic waste and solid waste. 
(1) An Electronic Waste and Materials Management Plan shall establish procedures for inventory, secure 
handling, reuse, recycling, and lawful disposal of servers, storage devices, network equipment, 
batteries, cooling equipment, hazardous materials, packaging, and construction and demolition waste 
during upgrades and decommissioning. 
(2) Electronic waste, batteries, and hazardous waste shall be managed only by appropriately licensed or 
qualified vendors and shall not be disposed of as ordinary municipal solid waste where prohibited by 
law. 
P. Decommissioning, abandonment, and financial security. 
(1) The Conditional Use application shall include a Decommissioning and Site Restoration Plan prepared by 
qualified professionals. The plan shall identify removal, reuse, recycling, demolition, environmental 
testing and remediation, utility disconnection, restoration, stormwater stabilization, and disposition 
of buildings, generators, BESS, substations, tanks, cooling systems, foundations, and other equipment. 
(2) Before issuance of the first building permit, the owner shall provide financial security in a form 
approved by the Council Solicitor and Finance Director in an amount equal to 125% of the 
independently reviewed estimated cost of decommissioning and restoration, without credit for 
salvage value. The estimate and security shall be updated at least every five years and whenever a 
material expansion or change increases the cost. 
(3) A Data Center is abandoned when the principal Data Center operation has ceased for 12 consecutive 
months, excluding a period of reconstruction after casualty or force majeure under a written schedule 
approved by the Zoning Administrator. Upon abandonment, decommissioning shall begin promptly 
and be completed within 12 months unless City Council approves a written extension based on 
demonstrated progress and public safety. 
(4) The owner and operator are jointly and severally responsible for decommissioning. Transfer of 
ownership or operation does not release any obligation. The City may draw upon the financial security 
and undertake or complete decommissioning to the extent authorized by law and the security 
instrument. 
(5) The owner shall record a covenant, in a form approved by the Council Solicitor, providing notice of the 
continuing operational, monitoring, decommissioning, and successor obligations under this Section. 

Q. Annual compliance report, monitoring, inspections, and records. 
(1) By March 1 of each year, the Data Center Operator shall submit a certified annual compliance report 
covering the prior calendar year. The report shall include electricity use and peak demand; Power 
Usage Effectiveness; water intake, reuse, consumption, discharge, and Water Usage Effectiveness; 
generator testing and emergency hours; fuel and chemical inventories; spills, fires, alarms, reportable 
releases, and emergency responses; noise monitoring; e-waste and battery disposition; landscape 
maintenance; decommissioning-security status; employment and workforce information committed 
through a Community Benefits Agreement; and the status of all required permits and mitigation 
measures. 
(2) All meters, monitoring systems, inspection records, test results, maintenance records, and reports 
required by this Section shall be retained for at least seven years and made available to the City upon 
reasonable notice. Trade-secret and security-sensitive information may be protected to the extent 
permitted by the Pennsylvania Right-to-Know Law and other applicable law, but aggregate 
compliance information shall not be withheld merely because it concerns facility operations. 
(3) The City and its authorized consultants may inspect the exterior, common, utility, mechanical, 
environmental-control, fire-safety, and monitoring portions of the facility at reasonable times upon 
notice, and at any time reasonably necessary during an emergency or suspected imminent hazard, 
subject to applicable law and safety protocols. 
(4) The owner and operator shall notify the Zoning Administrator, Fire Chief, and City Engineer in writing 
within ten business days of a change in ownership or operator. A successor shall execute an 
assumption agreement accepting all conditions, plans, covenants, security, reporting, mitigation, and 
decommissioning obligations before operating the facility. 
R. Application materials, referrals, independent review, and escrow. 
(1) All plans, studies, reports, evaluations, certifications, and proposed mitigation required by this Section 
shall be submitted with the Conditional Use application. An application is not complete until the 
Zoning Administrator determines that the required materials and professional-review escrow have 
been submitted. 
(2) The application shall be referred for review and comment to the Planning Commission, Environmental 
Advisory Council, Fire Department, City Engineer, Public Works and wastewater operations, Reading 
Area Water Authority, Emergency Management, Shade Tree Commission when landscaping is 
involved, and any other agency designated by the City. 
(3) The City may retain independent engineers, acousticians, hydrogeologists, environmental 
professionals, fire-protection professionals, utility consultants, landscape architects, financial-security 
estimators, or legal counsel to review the application and monitor compliance. The applicant or 
operator shall reimburse the City’s reasonable and necessary professional-review costs and shall 
maintain an escrow deposit in the amount established by the City’s fee schedule or a professional-
services reimbursement agreement, to the extent authorized by law. 
(4) Acceptance of a report for review does not constitute approval of its assumptions, methods, or 
conclusions. The City may require reasonable supplements or corrections necessary to evaluate 
compliance with express standards in this Section. 
S. Community impact mitigation and community-benefit planning. 
(1) The applicant shall submit a Community Impact Mitigation Plan identifying each project impact 
disclosed by the required studies and the proposed mitigation, responsible party, implementation 
date, performance standard, monitoring, funding, and remedy for nonperformance. 

(2) As a condition of approval, the applicant shall enter into an Impact Mitigation Agreement, in a form 
approved by the Council Solicitor, addressing obligations that are lawfully required, reasonably 
related, and proportionate to project impacts, including applicant-funded utility and transportation 
improvements; professional review; environmental, noise, water, and air monitoring; spill prevention 
and remediation; emergency-response training and equipment; landscape security; decommissioning; 
and other mitigation supported by the record. 
(3) The applicant shall also submit a proposed Community Benefit Plan describing voluntary or City-
assistance-related commitments concerning local recruitment; apprenticeships; workforce and 
educational partnerships; digital inclusion; neighborhood energy or water assistance; environmental 
restoration; tree canopy and green infrastructure; waste-heat reuse; and public reporting. Broader 
community benefits that are not required as impact mitigation shall be implemented through a 
separate Community Benefits Agreement or other lawful agreement and shall not be imposed as an 
unsupported zoning exaction. 
T. Additional Conditional Use approval standards. 
(1) In addition to §§ 600-1201 and 600-1204, the applicant bears the burden of demonstrating compliance 
with every applicable objective requirement of this Section by credible evidence. 
(2) City Council shall approve the Conditional Use only upon finding that: 
• The use and all phases, parcels, buildings, and accessory facilities comply with the dimensional, 
siting, water, cooling, noise, environmental, emergency, flood, landscaping, and 
decommissioning standards of this Section. 
• Adequate electric, water, sewer, fire-flow, transportation, emergency-response, and 
communications capacity is available or will be provided through enforceable, applicant-funded 
improvements. 
• The project will not impair Fritz Island wastewater operations, public water or wastewater 
infrastructure, emergency access, flood protection, or the quality of the Schuylkill River, Mifflin 
Arm, Angelica Creek, groundwater, or other waters. 
• Continuous and low-frequency noise, generator testing, air emissions, heat, lighting, vibration, 
hazardous materials, and cumulative impacts will meet the applicable limits and be effectively 
mitigated. 
• The Emergency Response Plan, hazardous-material controls, BESS design, access, hydrants, 
training, and inspection provisions are adequate under applicable codes and the written review 
of the responsible City officials. 
• All required mitigation, monitoring, reporting, professional-review escrow, decommissioning 
security, and successor obligations are enforceable and adequately funded. 
(3) City Council may attach reasonable conditions and safeguards authorized by the MPC and this Chapter, 
provided that each condition is supported by the record, relates to an identified impact or express 
standard, and is stated with sufficient clarity to permit enforcement. 
U. Modifications and expansions. 
(1) An amended Conditional Use approval is required for any of the following after approval: an increase of 
more than 5% in approved gross floor area; an increase of more than 10% in maximum electrical 
demand, average or peak water use, generator count or capacity, fuel storage, BESS capacity, or 
impervious coverage; a change in cooling technology that increases water use or discharge; addition 
of normal-operation energy generation; expansion to another parcel; a reduction in a required 
setback or buffer; or any change that materially increases an impact evaluated in the approval record. 

(2) The Zoning Administrator, after consultation with the City Engineer and other affected departments, 
may approve a minor modification that does not meet a threshold in paragraph (1), does not reduce 
compliance, and does not materially increase an impact. The decision and supporting materials shall 
be placed in the project file. 
V. Continuing compliance and enforcement. 
(1) Compliance with this Section and all conditions of approval is a continuing obligation of the property 
owner and Data Center Operator. Failure to comply constitutes a violation of this Chapter and may be 
enforced through all remedies available under the City Code, the MPC, the conditions of approval, 
recorded covenants, agreements, permits, and financial-security instruments. 
(2) No certificate of occupancy authorizes operation beyond the approved capacity, equipment, cooling 
system, generator use, water use, site plan, or conditions. The City may require corrective action, 
additional testing, cessation of the violating activity, or other lawful relief. 
(3) Remedies under this Section are cumulative and do not limit enforcement by the Reading Area Water 
Authority, Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection, Delaware River Basin Commission, 
electric utility, Fire Department, or any other agency having jurisdiction.

AN ORDINANCE OPENING THE STREETS KNOW AS “KINGS WAY” AND “QUEENS 
COURT” IN THE CITY OF READING,  PENNSYLVANIA, AS PER CITY CODE SECTION 576-
102, AND PA TITLE 11 “CITIES, THIRD CLASS CITY CODE” 
 
WHEREAS, the Home Owner’s Association representing the Castlewood Subdivision have requested, 
by petition, the dedication of the streets within that subdivision which is located in the Second Ward 
in the City of Reading, and 
 
WHEREAS, the two streets within the subdivision known as “Kings Way” and “Queens Court” (the 
Streets) are currently private streets, and are not included in the City’s Official Topographical Survey 
of Streets, and 
 
WHEREAS, Public Works and the Administration have coordinated with the Castlewood Home 
Owner’s Association (the HOA) to accept the request for dedication and to ensure that the Streets, 
together with the associated stormwater management system, are acceptable with respect to the City’s 
engineering standards, and 
 
WHEREAS, the HOA has submitted a “Revision to a Plan of Record” to the Planning Commission, for 
their approval, that revises the original subdivision plan to state that the Streets will be dedicated to 
the City, and  
 
WHEREAS, the HOA and Property Owners have agreed in writing to manage and maintain the 
associated stormwater management system consisting of the street side swales, driveway culverts and 
storm basins, and 
 
WHEREAS, the Planning Commission approval of that plan is contingent upon City Council’s 
approval of the opening of these two streets, and 
 
WHEREAS, the Planning Commission, Public Works and the Administration have agreed to accept 
the Streets with respect to City Code 505-502.G.(2), Cul-de-sac streets, based on the knowledge that 
the Streets have been in use without major problems for over 30 years and that the Streets otherwise 
meet the conditions for cul-de-sac streets, and 

WHEREAS, the Planning Commission, Public Works and the Administration are recommending this 
action since this will benefit the City, including the potential for increased allocation of PennDOT 
Liquid Fuel Tax revenue, and will help to ensure that the integrity of the streets and the associated 
stormwater management system continues to meet the City’s standards and the PA Department of 
Environmental Protection’s standards, and 
 
WHEREAS, the Planning Commission has recommended that the Streets be officially opened and 
placed on the Official Topographic Survey. 
 
NOW THEREFORE, be it ENACTED and ORDAINED by the City Council of the City of Reading, 
Berks County, Pennsylvania as follows: 
 
SECTION ONE:  The streets known as “Kings Way” and “Queens Court” located within the 
Castlewood subdivision, as shown on the attached drawing, “Exhibit A”, shall be opened as city 
streets. 
 
SECTION TWO:  the attached drawing D-4900416 sheets 1 through 4, noted as “Exhibit A”, prepared 
by Ludgate Associates, together with the digital file of the same (AutoCAD file), are all made part of 
this ordinance.    
 
SECTION THREE:  The Director of the Department of Public Works or the City Engineer shall be 
authorized and directed to enter and record the above-described changes in the official Topographical 
Survey of streets in the Department of Public Works. 
 
SECTION FOUR: This Ordinance shall become effective ten (10) days after its adoption in accordance 
with Sections 219 and 221 of the City of Reading Home Rule Charter.

limitation, restriction, sentence, clause, phrase, word, or remaining portion of the within Ordinance. The 
City Council of the City of Reading, Berks County, Pennsylvania, hereby declares that it would have 
adopted the within Ordinance and each article, section, subsection, provision, regulation, limitation, 
restriction, sentence, clause, phrase and word thereof, irrespective of the limitations, restrictions, 
sentences, clauses, phrases, or word that may be declared illegal, unconstitutional or invalid.
