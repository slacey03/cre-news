I. Reading Housing Authority (RHA) re River Oak Project  
Ms. Keppen stated that she submitted a letter in May about the River Oak apartments.  She 
stated that she will provide an overview and Mr. Stock will then describe the need for the 
City’s assistance with the bank notes. 
 
Ms. Keppen stated that this development was built in 1989 as 72 units made up of two- and 
three-bedroom units. In 2018 RHA began managing this development under the RHA voucher 
program. She described the other affordable housing managed by the RHA.  She stated that 
the RHA occupancy rate is 98-99% over the last 18 months.  The average tenure is 57 
households that moved in before 2017. 
 
Ms. Keppen stated that as this development is part of the RHA affordable rentals, there is no 
routine income calculation, other than the review performed during the application process.   
The RHA acts as a private market landlord.

Ms. Keppen stated that in 2024 applied to the Federal Home Loan Bank to address a backlog of 
capital needs at the property, which was explained in the letter. She explained that the goal 
was to use these resources to do a mixed income community – 30%, 50%, 60%, 80% and market 
rate percent of area median income. At that time the average income was $43,118 for a family 
of 2.5 people. Under Federal Policy anyone who pays more than 30% of their income in shelter 
costs (rent plus all related costs) is rent burdened.   
 
Ms. Keppen stated that the cap on rent for the $43,118 family $1,000 or a gross rent of $1067, 
after deducting utility bills, the monthly rent is reduced to $800 for this family. 
 
Ms. Keppen stated that River Oak is filled with working people.  In today’s fair market rents 
HUD looks at average rents and local costs to define a median average rent for a two bedroom 
unit at $1575 and a three bedroom unit at $1937. The RHA rates are $1289 for a two-bedroom 
unit and $1527 for a three-bedroom unit.  These rates are affordable for a family earning 
$42,649, approximately $25.75 per hour.  
 
Mr. Stock stated that going back to 2006-07, the RHA was contacted by a former City 
administration as the administration was concerned about who the next owner of this 
development would be because the original owner decided to walk away. He stated that the 
administration asked the RHA to consider taking ownership and management of this property 
and after consideration RHA agreed to take on the development. 
 
Mr. Stock explained that the project was funded in the early 1980s under a HUD Housing 
Development Program, sometimes referred to as HODAG Housing Opportunity Development 
Action Grants in the amount of $1.475M. The project was not funded by the City. This 
program ended in the early 1990s. The RHA was not the owner or developer at this time. The 
HODAG language contains a promissory note that says if River Oak is sold that the City will 
get half of any proceeds in excess of $2M. He expressed the belief that HUD added this 
language to provide some control over the owner who was a private developer which 
provided some level of risk.  
 
Mr. Stock stated that in 2025, the River Oak Development LLC transferred the property to the 
RHA for $1.6M, based on the appraisal performed by Douglass Haring, an MAI accredited 
appraiser.  Mr. Stock explained that the appraised value also considers the income stream 
based on the rents charged at the property.  He added that the RHA does not charge fair 
market rates, as Ms. Keppen covered earlier. If the RHA walked away the rents would go up 
and so would the appraised value of the property. 
 
Mr. Stock explained that although the RHA has attempted to keep up with maintenance there 
is a considerable amount of deferred maintenance that requires correction. He stated that the 
transaction between River Oak and the RHA was a related party transaction based on the 
accurate appraisal provided by Mr. Haring.  Under the repayment formula in the document, 
the City will not be receiving any income; however, he reminded Council that the funding was

Mr. Gombach stated that he and the mayor support this proposal to forgive the promissory

II. 2nd Quarter Financial Review 
Mr. Famiglietti screenshared spreadsheets showing the General Fund Revenues and Expenses 
for the first six months of 2026 and a snap shot of the same for 2024-25. He stated that overall, 
to date, the revenue collected is $1.2M more than budgeted and the collection rate is 54.04% 
which is similar to the collection rate from 2025. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that the rental revenue is $282K less than 2025 due to the decrease in the 
contribution from the Reading Parking Authority and a reduced lease payment for FirstEnergy 
Stadium. However, Property Tax revenues have increased by $505K, but less was collected 
during the discount period which is unusual. BPT has increased by $904K. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti added that the Act 511 Taxes (including Business Privilege Tax) increased by 
$500K and the Business Privilege Permit fee increased by $380K.  Real Estate Transfer Tax is 
falling under the amount collected in 2025. The LST (formerly Occupational Privilege Tax) has 
increased by $311K. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that while the Admissions Tax is trending $70K higher, the Franchise Fee 
has again fallen short by $140K.  He noted that License and Permit fees are $800K higher due 
to Building/Trades permitting, while Fire permits are $80K less than last year. 
 

4 
Mr. Baez cautioned that the revenue increases do not signal a surplus, as many of the revenues 
are cyclical in nature. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti next reviewed the expenses, noting that spending for the first six months is on 
par with 2025 at 47.4%.  He explained that the largest expense is salaries/wages at 75% of the 
budget.   He noted that personnel costs were much higher in CD due to filling various vacant 
positions and the shift in Fire to add 12 Lieutenants.  He added that the payout of Paid Time 
Off (PTO) for retiring Fire and Police employees is problematic at $191K as it is difficult to 
predict what that amount will be when budgeting. He added that overall, at the end of six 
months, 50% of the Salaries/Wages expenses were paid. 
 
Mr. Gombar left Council Chambers and connected virtually. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that Fire Suppression Overtime (OT) has increased to $441K – or 82% of 
their 2026 budget - compared to the first six months of last year. Police Special Services 
Overtime is at $138K and Public Works at $61K.  He noted that the Public Works OT is mainly 
related to winter weather events. 
 
In response to a question from Ms. Ventura, Mr. Famiglietti stated that winter weather events 
are charged under Highways; however, larger events pool employees from other Public Works 
Divisions, and the charges are then separated to a new line item so they can be captured with 
grants or other non-General Fund dollars. 
 
Ms. VanFossen connected virtually. 
 
Mr. Gombach noted that 70% of the operating budget expenses are personnel costs, the 
majority of which are the four bargaining units – FOP, IAFF, and AFSCME 2763 (rank and file) 
and AFSCME 3799 (1st level supervisors).  All contracts are currently being negotiated. He 
stated that all bargaining units recognize the City’s structural financial issues and the need to 
protect the City’s financial health. 
 
In response to a question from Mr. Baez, Mr. Famiglietti explained that the spike in Police 
Patrol in 2025 and 2026 was caused by the Axiom Body Camera software and equipment – 
paid in August of last year and June of this year.  
 
Mr. Baez noted the potential error - by expense type at $54.8M and by department type at 
$59.8M (from pages 1 and 3) – a $5M discrepancy when the numbers should match. He noted 
his appreciation for having this information distributed a week in advance. 
 
In response to a question from Mr. Baez, Mr. Famiglietti stated that the City is operating on 
budget but noted that this budget has a deficit covered by Fund Balance.
