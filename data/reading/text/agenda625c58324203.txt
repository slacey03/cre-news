I. Budget Review         
 
• CD Department 
o Property Maintenance  
Mr. Famiglietti stated that the No Show revenue fees have grown from $30,000 in 2023 to 
$50,000 for 2026, Housing and Rental Permit fees budgeted at $1,050,000 dropped off to 
$750,000 for 2026, and Housing inspection fees increased $125,000 to $325,000. He added that 
2027 will start an improved approach to collections of delinquencies in all revenue line items. 
Health & Safety Inspections (Pre-settlement inspections) underperformed by $25,000 causing 
the 2027 line item to be reduced to $15,000. In addition, QoL fines was standardly budgeted at 
$50,000 but that line item was reduced to $15,000 based on reliable data. 
 
On the expense side there are 32 full-time positions and one part-time position.  Two of the 
full-time positions are partially grant funded. The $5,000 in advertising expenses was reduced 
to $900 for 2027 after reviewing the data and the abatement expense was reduced by $12,000 to 
$58.000 based on 2025 expenses. Mr. Famiglietti stated that Conferences & Training increased 
slightly and a listing of their training schedule was provided for 2027. 
 
COMMITTEE of the WHOLE 
 
CITY COUNCIL 
 
 


2 
Mr. Famiglietti explained that prior budgets had an expense line item for permit refunds.  
However, moving forward the refunds will come directly out of the revenue line item. 
 
In response to a question from Mr. Baker, Mr. Famiglietti stated that none of the 18 Property 
Maintenance Inspector positions were eliminated; however, one vacant Inspector position is 
not funded.  If the position is filled, a budget amendment will occur. He stated that the head 
count in Property Maintenance was increased several years ago and historically there has 
always been one unfilled Inspector position over several years. 
 
Mr. Miller, as a realtor, has noticed a large uptick in the number of single-family dwellings 
that are converted into single family rental properties.  He questioned if the administration 
plans to increase the Inspector positions in response. 
 
Mr. Gombach stated that there is no need to increase the number of Inspector positions, as this 
increase needs to be addressed as a workflow and organizational issue first. He expressed the 
belief that the number of Inspectors should be able to handle this increase in rentals. 
 
Mr. Miller expressed the belief that only a portion of those who have converted their property 
to a single-family rental are registering their property with Zoning and Property Maintenance 
and obtaining a Business Privilege License.   
 
Mr. Famiglietti agreed with the issue raised by Mr. Miller due to the approximate $250,000 
decrease in Housing Permit revenue warranted by the collection rate over the past three years. 
He added that this problem exists across the organization due to antiquated policies and 
procedures which will be reviewed moving forward.  He noted that positions within the 
Position Ordinance for 2027 without funding can be funded with a budget amendment during 
2027 if the position is filled. 
 
Mr. Gombach stated that most people incorrectly assume that flaws within government 
operations can be solved by adding manpower.  However, that concept proves to be untrue as 
adding staffing in Property Maintenance has not produced tangible results. He noted the need 
to update practices and policies to obtain real results. 
 
Mr. Miller stated that he sees a concentration of single-family rentals selling through LLCs, 
which makes him question if these property owners are registering the properties as rental and 
obtaining a Business Privilege License and submitting the required tax return to account for 
this income. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti and Mr. Gombach agreed that this issue with unregistered rentals and other 
revenue related areas require further internal consideration without adding new positions.

Mr. Gombach suggested that restructuring Property Maintenance to a model used by Police 
Patrol may improve the results with the current staffing levels.  He stated that one should not 
assume that staffing levels is causing poor results when that is not the case. 
 
o Planning & Zoning 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that Land Development fees are consistently under performing and will 
be reduced by $25,000 to $75,000. 
 
In salaries there are five full-time and two part-time positions in Zoning and the Zoning 
Administrator, two Zoning Inspectors, Intern and part-time secretary are not funded. He 
reminded the group that funding could occur during 2027 through a budget amendment. 
 
Contracted Services was reduced by $20,000 to $180,500 with $100,000 for Hailstone and 
$18,400 for Hawk Valley. 
 
o Building/Trades 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that $450,000 was budgeted for emergency demo (note no emergency 
demo funding is included in the CDBG Action Plan) and conferences and training are UCC 
related for the inspectors and the Building Code Official (BCO).  He added that building 
permit revenue increased by $300,000 to $1,500,000. 
 
In response to a question from Ms. Reed, Mr. Famiglietti stated that Building/Trades is making 
the effort to ensure that the Law Department is filing liens with the Prothonotary to recapture 
City funded demolitions.  
 
o CD Administration 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that the CD Director position is 50% funded through the CDBG Action 
Plan and other grants. The Historic Preservation Specialist III and Historic Preservation 
Specialist positions are not funded but that can change through a budget amendment.   He 
added that the Contracted Services line item covers the “match” funding related to various 
grants. 
 
In response to a question from Mr. Baker, Mr. Gombach stated that after the public comment 
at Monday’s meeting, the administration is reevaluating their position for the Historic 
Preservation Specialist III and the HARB. He stated that he now prefers a meeting with some 
Historic District residents, Mr. Miller and the PHMC to determine the future of historic 
preservation in Reading. 
 
In response to question from Ms. Kelleher, Mr. Famiglietti stated that Hailstone is budgeted at 
$100,000 to assist with Zoning and Planning matters and that if expenses exceed the sum 
budgeted, further review will occur. 
 

4 
Mr. Baez requested an independent audit of the City’s financials and financial software by the 
firm who prepares the audit and for that firm to produce independent budget 
recommendations for the 2027 budget. He explained that he is making this request as Council 
will not receive input from the elected City Auditor this year and because the administration is 
having difficulties providing the value of one mil and the amount of funding in the reserve 
along with the amount that is unassigned. He expressed the belief that having that information 
is critical to the adoption of the 2027 budget. He questioned how one can determine how the 
City will use $24M of the reserve over the next two years if the total amount of the fund 
balance cannot be determined using the 2025 audit. 
 
Mr. Gombach stated that the administration reached out to Cherry Bekaert, who is performing 
the 2025 external audit and they advised that it would be a conflict of interest to perform that 
service.  As such, Mr. Famiglietti is obtaining quotes from other firms. 
 
In response to a question from Ms. Campos, Mr. Gombach stated that positions that have been 
vacant for approximately one year were identified as positions that may not be filled during 
2027.  The need for those positions and the position descriptions will be reevaluated. He stated 
that budget amendments can occur if the position is filled. He added that only 72 positions are  
considered non-union, management.    
 
In response to a question from Ms. Campos, Mr. Famiglietti explained that fluctuations in line 
items like office supplies, advertising, conferences and training, contracted services, etc. is due 
to the recategorization or realignment of the expenses. 
 
In response to a question from Ms. Reed, Mr. Famiglietti explained that the Purchasing Policies 
will drive the need for Council approval for the request of an independent evaluation of the 
2025 financials.  He expressed the belief that the cost should fall below $34,999. Mr. Gombach 
agreed. 
 
Mr. Baez noted that he is passionate about the need for transparency and the accuracy of the 
numbers being presented. He stated that Mr. Famiglietti stated that the 2025 reserve balance is 
$48,949,000 and $24M will be used in 2026 and 2027 which leaves a balance of $24M.  He 
continued that as a legislator responsible for the City’s financial health, this will mean higher 
taxes and the potential for another entry into Act 47, or worse. He stressed the need for 
Council to understand the amount of the reserve actually used and remaining. He noted that 
when interviewed, Mr. Famiglietti stressed the need for financial transparency and that is 
what he is seeking, with the understanding that the amount remaining will be an estimate.  
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that he and his department have provided total transparency and he 
highlighted the detail provided to Council and the public. 
 
• CDBG Action Plan 

5 
Ms. Vasquez, Mr. Nemeth and Mr. Carabello came forward to present on the CDBG Action 
Plan.  The spreadsheets for CDBG, ESG and HOME are attached to the agenda. 
 
Mr. Nemeth stated that HUD requires the annual $165,000  payment for the 5th & Penn 108 
loan  from the CDBG entitlement fund.  The value of the CDBG plan is $2.5M. In Admin there 
is $500,000 in salary expenses funded - $200,000 for Property Maintenance and $100,000 for 
Building/Trades.  Admin also covers the Healthy Homes and Lead programs, New Journey 
Day Shelter service and building improvements.  He continued describing all activities 
funded. 
 
Mr. Carabello stated that he oversees the ESG area for homeless organizations such as 
Opportunity House, Mary’s Shelter, etc. along with rapid rehousing and homeless prevention. 
He noted that Mary’s Shelter is expanding by providing services to assist transitional youth, 
homeless and the aging out population, which makes them eligible for funding in 2027. 
 
In response to a question from Ms. Reed, Mr. Carabello stated that ESG funding is focused on 
the homeless population and he explained why Code Blue is eligible under Admin. 
 
Mr. Gombach stated that the administration is most excited by funding the New Journey Day 
Shelter. 
 
Mr. Miller requested detail on the funding the recipient organizations receive from the City, 
County, other organizations and private donations. Mr. Gombach expressed the belief that that 
information can be obtained from the Coalition to End Homelessness.   
 
Mr. Miller noted that while some members of the public may believe that the funding 
provided by the City addresses the homeless situation, that is not the case.  Ms. Vasquez 
agreed. 
 
Ms. Kelleher noted that several years ago the City and County ended the ability of 
organizations from obtaining CDBG funding from the City and the County in the same budget 
year. Ms. Vasquez agreed, noting that policy now comes from the HUD HMIS system. 
 
Ms. Campos questioned the lack of funding for the Sidewalk Improvement program.  Mr. 
Gombach explained that that program was not well received.  Ms. Vasquez and Mr. Baez 
stated that they receive multiple inquiries about the continuation of this program.  
 
Ms. Campos, Mr. Baker and Mr. Baez agreed with the need to continue funding this program. 
Mr. Gombach agreed to provide research and a recommendation. 
 
Ms. Reed stated that the program was initiated in 2019 and she and Ms. Cepeda-Freytiz sat on 
the Committee with the CD Director and one Property Maintenance Supervisor.

Mr. Carabello and Ms. Vasquez noted the long-term vacancy in the appointment of a CHDO

under the HOME Investment Partnerships Program as a nonprofit with the capacity to own, develop, 
or sponsor affordable housing. It must meet federal requirements for legal status, organizational 
structure, and development capacity. At least 15% of the HOME Program must be reserved for CHDO-
led projects). The CHDO has not been appointed since 2018. 
 
Mr. Gombach thanked the CD team for their presentation and their work.
