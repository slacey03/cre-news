Councilor Baez read the written remarks submitted by Ahman Hasani as follows: 
Dear Members of Reading City Council and Council President, 
 
My name is Ahmad Hasani, and I am a taxpayer of Reading, working at 625 Cherry 
Street, and living just outside city limits, in West Reading where many municipal 

2 
 
employees make their home. 
 
I am writing to submit a formal public comment for the upcoming council session, 
urging the administration to prioritize a dedicated paid parental leave policy for city 
municipal workers. Currently, Reading employees must exhaust their own accrued 
sick time, personal days, and vacation hours just to retain an income when 
welcoming a new child. If they lack accumulated time, they face the financial strain 
of completely unpaid leave under FMLA. One must ask themselves, how many years 
would an employee have had to work with using minimum time off to be able to 
afford the first few weeks of a new child? 
 
While cost is frequently cited as a barrier to expanding municipal benefits, a data-
driven projection reveals that the actual fiscal impact on Reading’s budget would be 
minimal and highly manageable: 
 
The Workforce Demographics: The City of Reading employs roughly 560 full-time 
individuals. Because municipal career paths inherently skew older, the vast majority 
of our staff sits entirely outside peak childbearing windows. 
 
The Utilization Numbers: Human resource industry tracking from organizations 
like the Society for Human Resource Management shows that only 1.5% to 3% of a 
standard workforce utilizes parental leave in any given year. For Reading, that 
equates to just 8 to 17 employees per year across all city departments. 
 
The True Cost: Public payroll data shows the average salary for a Reading city 
employee is approximately $72,162, which breaks down to $1,387 per week. If the 
city matched the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania’s benefit of 8 weeks of paid leave, 
which I recommend be the minimum 'up to' allowance, the cost per employee is 
roughly $11,101. A City employee currently will have had to not taken sick or 
vacation time for two years straight to have that time. Then lose that accumulated 
time for future use.  
 
The Annual Budget Impact: Multiplied by our annual utilization projection, a fully 
funded paid parental leave policy would cost the city between $93,255 and $186,511 
per year. 
 
In a city budget, this is a remarkably low price to pay to cure a major hiring 
bottleneck. The administration has already noted that a lack of family-focused 
benefits impairs our ability to recruit for critical 24-hour emergency services and 
administrative departments. 
 

3 
 
Offering paid parental leave is a fiscally responsible investment that protects our 
workforce and makes Reading a competitive employer. I respectfully request that the 
Council collaborate with Human Resources to formally study this low-cost benefit 
integration prior to the next annual budget cycle. If an amendment can be introduced 
even sooner, it would only serve to benefit the city. Thank you for your time, 
leadership, and continued service to our community.

  Dear Members of Reading City Council:  
We are mothers, grieving families, and residents of the City of Reading writing to express 
how deeply hurt and excluded we feel by the memorial brick process at Pendora Park.  
 
For years, we have been waiting and participating in Neighborhood Change Makers Healing 
Garden initiative created to honor victims of gun violence through gardening, memorial 
signage, benches, and other meaningful forms of recognition. We attended meetings, shared 
our stories, trusted the process, and waited patiently while the gardens experienced repeated 
delays.  
Some of us have continued showing up to water, weed, clean, plant, and help care for the 
garden. We understood that building something meaningful and sustainable would take time. 
However, while our memorial benches and signage remained delayed, another group was  
permitted to move forward with permanent memorial bricks at Pendora Park. We were never 
informed that this opportunity existed.  
 
There was no public application, nomination process, announced eligibility criteria, or 
deadline allowing Reading families to submit the names of their loved ones. Instead, certain 
families in our group were personally contacted and selected while other grieving mothers 
including mothers already participating in our Healing Garden were left out.  
 
We want to be clear that we do not object to the four families honoring their loved ones. Their 
grief matters, and their loved ones deserve to be remembered. Our concern is that all grieving 
families deserve a fair opportunity to participate.  
 
The Healing Garden initiative has been tremendously delayed. We have been asked to remain 
patient, follow procedures, attend meetings, help maintain the space, and wait for City 
approval. It was painful to then watch another memorial project move forward quickly 

4 
 
through political relationships and private outreach.  
 
This process made some of us feel that our children’s lives and our grief mattered less 
because we were not personally selected or politically connected.  
 
We are also concerned about the decision to place one participating mother in such a visible 
and politically prominent role. Some families in our community report that they were directly  
affected by serious harm connected to her son. We do not blame a mother for the actions of 
her child, and we do not believe that her grief should be denied. However, repeatedly 
positioning one family as a public representative without considering the experiences of other 
affected families has reopened wounds and caused additional pain.  
 
A healing project should not elevate one family’s story in a way that silences, excludes, or 
retraumatizes others. City officials and organizers working with grieving families have a 
responsibility to understand these community dynamics and use a trauma-informed, fair, and 
transparent process.  
 
This could have been prevented by openly announcing the opportunity, inviting all affected 
families to apply, coordinating with the existing Healing Garden group, and carefully 
considering how public recognition might affect other families.  
 
We respectfully request:  
1. A public explanation of how the four families were selected and who participated in those 
decisions.  
2. An explanation of why the memorial brick opportunity was not made available to all 
Reading residents.  
3. An opportunity for families who were excluded to submit their loved ones’ names for equal 
permanent recognition.  
4. A clear timeline for completing the Healing Garden benches, signage, and other memorial 
features we have been waiting for.  
5. A transparent application and selection process for all future memorials placed on city 
property.  
6. A trauma-informed review process that considers conflicts and the potential impact on 
other victims and families before individuals are placed in highly visible representative roles.  
7. Written assurance that the mothers participating in the Healing Garden will not be expected 
to maintain memorials installed through another group’s project.  
 
We should not have to compete for the acknowledgement of our children. Permanent 
recognition on public property should not depend on who someone knows, who personally 
contacts them, or whether they have political access.  
 
We have already experienced enough loss. A City-supported memorial process should bring 
families together, not create another layer of exclusion, hurt, and division.  
 
We ask the City Council to listen to the mothers and residents who were left out, correct this 
inequity, and ensure that future memorial opportunities are open, transparent, and fair to 
every family.

REPORT FROM DEPT. DIRECTORS, BOARDS, AUTHORITIES, & 
COMMISSIONS  
Mr. Murray, Executive Director of the Reading Area Water Authority (RAWA) stated that he 
has been in this position for 10 years.  He stated that RAWA does not have people filling jobs, 
but people building careers due to the very low employee turnover rate. He thanked City 
Council and the administration for their support. He explained the two-sided handout.  
 
Mr. Murray stated that RAWA went 10 years without having a rate increase by implementing 
efficiencies, which is phenomenal. He stated that in 2004 the RAWA board approved a 3% 
increase followed by 2% increase in 2025 which raised the average homeowners bill by 
approximately $3.83 a month. He noted that the bill contains charges for water, sewer, trash, 
recycling and Clean City – water charges equal approximately 1/3 of the total monthly bill. He 
added that RAWA has very competitive water rates when compared to the rates charged by 
other providers. In addition, the agreement provides the City with 25% of the fees charged to 
new large customers. 
 

14 
 
Mr. Murray explained that RAWA’s largest financial challenges are created by legislation and 
programming created at the Federal and State governments such as the need to replace lead 
water pipes and the need to address PFAs and PFOs.  He noted how RAWA approached each 
challenge. 
 
Mr. Murray stated that RAWA’s largest upcoming challenge will be improvements to the 100-
year-old Ontelaunee dam. He added that the lake is 1000 acres and it sits on a 2000-acre lot and 
can supply 36 MGD but only 16 MGD is currently drawn. The engineers estimated a $20M cost 
for dam upgrades five years ago. He stated that as Route 73 runs over a portion of the dam, a 
PennDOT permit was required.  The application with engineering was submitted five years 
ago and no feedback was obtained until recently when RAWA was told by the PA DEP that the 
engineering was five years old and must be redone. 
 
Council President Reed stated that she is pleased that the cooperation between the City and 
RAWA has continued, recalling a time when the relationship between the two organizations 
was poor. She noted the pristine beauty of the Ontelaunee Lake site and the ecological habitat it 
supports. 
 
Councilor Campos thanked Mr. Murray for his detailed report and for his continued advocacy.
