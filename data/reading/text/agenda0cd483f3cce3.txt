Code and License Appeals Board Facts 
Over time the City enacted ordinances creating separate appeals boards for the Housing 
Ordinance, including the Disruptive Conduct Report (DCR), the Solid Waste and 
Recycling Ordinance, the Vending Machine Ordinance, and the Property Maintenance 
Code Ordinance.  The ordinances required three individual citizens and three alternates 
to serve on each individual appeals board. The City could not find and appoint 15 to 30 
individual citizens to serve on these individual appeals boards so the City in 
consultation with the Solicitor’s Office drafted legislation to merge these five (5) appeals 
boards into one Code and License Appeals Board. This omnibus board is now 
composed of the Finance Director, as Chair, a Council member and three individual 
citizens representing the business community, a community group and a citizen at large 
to hear appeals to the Housing Ordinance, the Solid Waste Ordinance, the Solid Waste 
and Recycling Ordinance, the Vending Machine Ordinance and the Property 
Maintenance Code Ordinance. The representative from the business community shall 
be appointed by the Mayor without Council approval.  The Board will meet with the 
City attorney prior to the start of any appeal hearing to explain the appeal process 
under consideration. There is no set meeting schedule for this Appeals Board. 
Code and License Appeals Board Questions for Appointments 
1. Why are you interested in serving on Code and License Appeals Board? 
2. Have you ever had any violations of the Housing Ordinance, including the 
Disruptive Conduct Report (DCR), the Solid Waste and Recycling Ordinance, the 
Vending Machine Ordinance, and the Property Maintenance Code Ordinance? 
3. What is your opinion on the need for a City appeals board? 
4. Have you had any experience considering testimony and making decisions based 
on the testimony presented? 
 
Code and License Appeals Board Questions for Reappointments 
1. How many appeal hearings has the Board held over your term.  
2. Do you believe that the process used to conduct the hearing is productive and 
efficient?  What changes would you recommend? 
3. What changes to the board composition would you recommend?

Reading Environmental Advisory Council (EAC) Facts 
The EAC was formed by Bill No. 25-2007 to study environmental and sustainability 
issues at the request of City Council and make recommendations to Council on those 
issues. The EAC will work with City Council in an advisory capacity in an effort to help 
Council analyze environmental and sustainability issues and, when appropriate, issue 
recommendations on courses of action necessary to protect the health, safety and 
welfare of the residents of Berks County.  
The EAC may advise and make recommendations to City Council, City Administration, 
and City boards, authorities and commissions on policies and programs that infuse the 
work of City government with an operating philosophy based on environmental 
protection and sustainability, make recommendations as to the possible use of open 
land areas within the City of Reading, and promote community environmental 
awareness programs. 
EAC Questions for New Applicants 
1. What is the purpose of the EAC? 
2. The EAC currently meets on the 4th Tuesday of each month at noon.  Will you be 
able to regularly attend those meetings? 
3. Why are you interested in serving on the EAC? 
4. What environmental issues would you like to have addressed? 
5. Do you have experience with environmental issues or projects? 
 
EAC Questions for Reappointments 
1. Are you able to regularly attend meetings? 
2. What does the EAC do well? 
3. In what areas or ways does the EAC need to improve? 
4. Are there any issues that the City could assist with? 
5. Describe an initiative the EAC has worked on within the past year. 
6. In what ways could the EAC improve the way it operates?
