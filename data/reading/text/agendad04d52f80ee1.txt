Ms. Reed questioned if the reduction in the cost for street lighting is due to more efficient 
lights.  Mr. Famiglietti stated that the reduction is based on current trends. 
 
Mr. Baker stated that not all street lights have been changed to LED.  He questioned if older 
lights would age out and need to be replaced and questioned if the proposed budget would 
cover those costs.  Mr. Zeiber stated that Met Ed is also replacing street lights with LED 
fixtures which should reduce the City’s electricity costs.  Mr. Famiglietti stated that a budget 
amendment would be brought to Council as needed. 
 
Mr. Baker expressed the belief that reducing this division could cause safety issues and 
expressed the belief that street lighting is a fundamental service to residents.  Mr. Zeiber stated 
that poles are replaced when they are knocked down in accidents.  He stated that hundreds of 
light fixtures have been changed to LEDs.  Mr. Famiglietti stated that this division has been 
over budgeted for several years. 
 
In response to a question from Ms. Campos, Mr. Famiglietti explained the difference between 
street lights and traffic signals. 
 
In response to a question from Mr. Miller regarding how poles that are damaged during 
accidents factor into this budget.  Mr. Zeiber stated that Public Works works with the Risk & 
Safety Coordinator to recoup the costs of replacing damaged poles through the driver’s 
insurance company.  He stated that the cost to replace and install a pole is approximately 
$8,000 each. 
 
Mr. Nunez thanked Public Works for their excellent work.  He questioned how damaged poles 
are replaced.  Mr. Zeiber stated that the City’s contractor will provide an estimate to replace 
the pole to the Risk and Safety Coordinator.  He stated that Risk & Safety then pursues the 
costs through the driver’s auto insurance. 
 
o Administration 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that this division has seven full time employees with total wages of 
$631,097.   He stated that the cost of snow removal and overtime costs will be tracked through 
this division.  He stated that this will assist when the City can be reimbursed for these costs 
during declared snow emergencies.  He stated that this line item has increased from $75,000 to 
$90,000.  He stated that the consulting services line item has been removed because it is not 
used.   
 
In response to a question from Ms. Ventura regarding the costs for drug and alcohol testing, 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that this expense is now within the Human Resources budget.   
 
In response to a question from Ms. Campos regarding the overtime line item, Mr. Famiglietti 
stated that if overtime is exceeded, the City will identify other cost savings to provide the 
necessary funding.

o Liquid Fuels 
Mr. Famiglietti explained that there is no breakout for this fund.  He stated that approximately 
$2 million was received from the State in 2026 and that there are specific spending 
requirements.  He stated that it is generally used for snow emergency costs and street paving 
costs.  He stated that $3.5 million is budgeted for 2027.  He stated that State funding is received 
in the fall and the budget will be adjusted as needed at that time. 
 
In response to a question from Mr. Baker regarding the number of streets in need of repaving, 
Mr. Edwards stated that generally, a street should be repaved every 20 years.  He stated that 
with the number of streets that the City has, it should be repaving three miles of streets per 
year.  He stated that the cost of installing handicapped ramps takes approximately 1/3 of the 
budget.  He expressed the belief that the City is catching up on past years when there was little 
or no repaving done.  He reminded Council that CDBG funding has been requested to offset 
the cost of part of the expense for the installation of the handicapped ramps.  He stated that if 
this funding is provided through CDBG, all of the liquid fuels budget can be used for repaving 
and that three miles can be achieved.  He stated that good progress is being made.   
 
Mr. Zeiber explained that when liquid fuels funding is used for repaving, the State requires the 
installation of handicapped ramps at each intersection, each intersection containing four 
ramps.  He agreed that if CDBG funding for the ramps is provided it will allow additional 
repaving to be done.   
 
Mr. Edwards added that once a handicap ramp is installed it does not need to be installed a 
second time. 
 
Mr. Baker questioned if there were other grant funding sources to assist with installing the 
handicapped ramps.  He noted his understanding that the ramps are required for ADA 
accessibility.  Mr. Zeiber stated that grants are pursued.  He stated that the City is applying for 
grants to implement the traffic calming measures that would reduce the need to use liquid 
fuels funding for these projects.  He stated that there is no guarantee that grants are received.  
He stated that grants are regularly pursued. 
 
Mr. Gombach stated that a meeting with PennDOT is scheduled.  He noted his understanding 
that the City must catch up with street paving but also noted the limited funding available for 
these types of projects.  He stated that there are often competing projects and a multi-year plan 
is needed. 
 
In response to a question from Ms. Kelleher, Mr. Edwards stated that the paving costs, not 
including ramp installations, for one mile, is approximately $700,000.

o Solid Waste – Trash and Recycling 
Mr. Famiglietti explained that recycling revenue includes grants and user fees.  He stated that 
there are seven full time and two part time employees with total wages of $365,612.  He stated 
that three of these positions are funded 50% in the recycling budget and 50% in the trash 
budget.  He explained that the contracted services line item is the cost for recycling removal by 
Republic and that the fuel line item has increased from $7,500 to $12,000 due to the increased 
cost of fuel.  He stated that an annual study is conducted to determine indirect costs and that 
expense of $271,587 is reflected in the budget. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated trash revenue includes user fees.  He stated that there are three full time 
and six part time employees in the trash budget with total wages of $335,996.  He stated that 
overtime has been increased from $8,000 to $12,000 and that the $6 million contracted services 
expense is the Republic contract.  He stated that the fuel line item has increased from $20,000 
to $30,000. 
 
Ms. Hoag connected to the meeting virtually at this time. 
 
In response to a request for an update on the value of a 1 mil increase and a 3% tax increase, 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that a 3% property tax increase will provide an additional $740,000 in 
revenue and that the value of 1 mil is $1,295,070. 
 
Mr. Baker request modeling of 2% - 5% annual property tax increases so Council has 
additional options to consider.  He expressed the belief that smaller annual increases would be 
less shocking to property owners rather than no increases for several years and then larger 
increases.  He also requested 3 – 5 year projected cost increases. 
 
Ms. Reed reminded all that Council was presented with a budget for 2026 which included a 
9% tax increase, which Council was able to reduce to 6%.  She agreed with Mr. Baker that 
small, consistent tax increases are less shocking to property owners. 
 
Mr. Famiglietti stated that he will provide these models.   
 
Ms. Campos thanked Mr. Zeiber for his leadership and the responsiveness of the entire Public 
Works Department. 
 
Ms. Reed thanked all those from Public Works in attendance to support Mr. Zeiber.   
 
Ms. Reed thanked Ms. Ventura for leading the budget review discussion.

III. Other Matters 
Mr. Baker reminded all that the polling location for 12-3 has been relocated to 10th & Green 
Elementary School due to the Olivet not being available.  He stated that 10th & Green will 
contain two polls and that Election staff will direct voters to the proper location. 
 
Ms. Reed noted the large fire on N 10th St and stated that 28 adults and four children have been 
displaced.  She stated that there were no civilian or fire fighter injuries and noted the great 
work of the Fire Department.  She stated that those displaced can receive services if needed. 
 
Ms. Ventura stated that District 3 is the smallest but most highly populated District.  She stated 
that many properties are now multi-unit apartments and noted the need for smoke detectors.  
She stated that smoke detectors are required when the properties are inspected.  She expressed 
the belief that not all of these units are permitted and pose a great danger to the community.  
She offered prayers for the families of those affected. 

7 
 
Mr. Miller stated that when a fire occurs in Reading it usually affects more than one property.  
He stated that most row homes share a common roof.  He noted the skills of the Reading Fire 
Department compared to suburban departments that do not have to handle urban fire issues. 
 
Mr. Baker noted the need for neighboring residents to report unpermitted apartments to 
Property Maintenance for follow up.  He noted the differences between an owner occupied 
property and a multi-unit property. 
 
Mr. Gombach agreed with all of these statements.  He stated that he is currently having 
discussions with the Fire Chief about an educational campaign during the colder months 
regarding the risks of using space heaters and the increased risk of kitchen fires during the 
holiday season. 
 
Mr. Miller explained that properties with three or more units must have hard-wired smoke 
detectors but those that are single family rentals or two units must only have battery operated 
smoke detectors.  He stated that it is common for residents to remove the batteries in the 
smoke detectors and not replace them.  He expressed the belief that the property owner and 
the residents share the responsibility for smoke detectors. 
 
Ms. Reed agreed with the need for public education.  She also offered prayers to the families 
affected by the fire.  She reminded all to vote tomorrow. 
 
The meeting adjourned at 6:14 pm on motion of Mr. Baker.
