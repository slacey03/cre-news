Public Comment 
Council President Reed opened the floor for public comment.  She announced that one person 
provided written comment and another will speak at the podium. She read the public 
comment rules adopted by ordinance in the Administrative Code Section 5-209.   
 
Councilor Campos read the comment submitted by Noemi Gonzalez, no address listed, as 
follows: 
 
 My name is Noemi Gonzalez and I am a 40-year resident of Reading. I currently reside in a 
multi-generational household with my elderly mother, who is the registered homeowner. As 
the future inheritor of this home, I am writing to advocate for real solutions to our city's 
severe parking crisis. 
 
 Right now, parking is so scarce that a few residents are being forced to park in their own 
front yards just to avoid an unsafe 20-minute walk home, leaving residents unable to keep an 

2 
 
eye on their vehicles or even hear their car alarms if a break-in or vandalism occurs. My 
neighbors and I are doing what we must to adapt to a severe lack of infrastructure. 
 
 As you deliberate on the allocation of funds in the 2027 Budget, I urge Council to look at 
this from a funding, planning, and street design perspective: 
 
 1. Street Reconfigurations: Is there funding in the 2027 budget for the Public Works or 
Traffic Engineering departments to evaluate widening or converting certain wide streets into 
one-way streets to accommodate back-in angled parking? Angled parking can nearly double 
street capacity, making it a highly cost-effective way to immediately create hundreds of new 
spots around town without buying new land and it's actually considered safer parking.  
 
 2. Zoning & Permit Relief: Can budget funds be allocated to develop an affordable 
"Residential Off-Street Parking Permit" program? This would allow qualified homeowners to 
legally convert small front yard areas into properly surfaced parking spaces without 
navigating a costly, multi-month zoning hurdle. A standard parallel street spot takes up over 
20 feet of curb space, while a yard clearance exit only requires about 10 feet, this program 
would yield a net gain of available parking space for the block.  
 
 3. Alternative Infrastructure: Is the city budgeting for a comprehensive study to create 
municipal or neighborhood parking lots so residents don't have to park blocks away in unsafe 
conditions? 
 
 We need the city to invest in smart, proactive parking solutions that protect and support 
both current and incoming homeowners. With the rising cost of living forcing people into 
multi-family or shared housing situations, our streets are more crowded than ever. If we do 
not address this infrastructure gap, we risk driving tax-paying homeowners out of the city 
entirely. Thank you for your time and consideration. 
 
 Warm regards, 
Noemi Gonzalez

The mayor read the budget message.  He stated that prior to the release of the 2027 General 
Fund and Capital budgets the drafts were closely examined and expenses deemed 
unnecessary were eliminated.  He stated that the 2027 General Fund Budget is $1.29M and the 
Capital Budget is $12.4M.  He thanked the City’s taxpayers who shouldered last year’s tax 
increase along with the poor economy, noting that the 2026 milage will be retained – 19.217 
mils. He stated that 70% of the budget is personnel costs with the Police Department and Fire 
Department carrying the largest costs at $49.7M and $30.6M respectively. He noted that the 
administration remains focused on public safety and infrastructure improvements. 
 
The meeting adjourned on a motion by Councilor Baez and Councilor Miller, respectfully
