Noise, Air Emissions, Heat and Mechanical Equipment 
Even with fewer data center buildings, Design 2 may generate substantial continuous 
noise and heat from cooling equipment, transformers and other mechanical systems. 
The proposed electrical generation and battery storage facilities introduce additional 
operational considerations. 
 
The project narrative anticipates a future acoustical study. The LVPC recommends 
preparing this assessment using the final equipment configuration, including the 
cumulative sound from all six buildings and associated electrical facilities. The study 
should identify existing background conditions and predict sound levels at nearby 
residences, property boundaries, the Community Park and other sensitive locations. 
Evaluation should address continuous operations, low-frequency or tonal noise, 
generator testing and any proposed regular power-generation activities. The applicant 
should identify the equipment enclosures, acoustic barriers and operating practices 
needed to meet applicable standards. Post-construction sound measurements are 
recommended to confirm that operating conditions are consistent with the approved 
analysis. 
 
Information should also be provided on the amount and location of heat discharged from 
the cooling and generation systems, including potential impacts on surrounding 
property, vegetation and equipment. For proposed emission sources, the applicant 
should identify fuel types, capacity, anticipated operating hours and applicable PADEP 
permits. These studies would provide a more complete basis for determining the 
compatibility of the proposed use with nearby land uses and natural resources (of 
Policies 3.2 and 5.3). 
 
Emergency Access and Operational Safety 
Design 2 consolidates data center activity into fewer, larger buildings but continues to 
rely on an extensive internal road network. Emergency access, equipment isolation and 
incident response should be evaluated for the entire campus rather than for individual 
buildings alone. 
 
The plans identify emergency connections to Potomac Street and River Road. The 
LVPC recommends confirming that these routes provide reliable access for responding 
fire, police and emergency medical personnel, with suitable turning areas, roadway 
widths, surface conditions and access controls. 
 
Individual building pads should have adequate access for emergency vehicles from 
more than one direction where feasible. Particular attention should be given to Building 
Pad 9 and other locations served by extended internal access drives. A clear system of 
building numbers, directional signage and emergency access markers should be 
incorporated into the plans. Appropriate emergency-route signage should also be 
coordinated along Route 611 and other approaches to the site.

while also preserving prime agricultural land (§604(3) and (5)). The relevance of surrounding 
industrial uses was also recognized in Carnahan v. Slippery Rock Township Zoning Hearing 
Board, No. 1082 C.D. 2022 (Pa. Cmwlth. Nov. 8, 2023), in which the Commonwealth Court 
upheld a rezoning from Rural Conservation to Light Industrial based in part on surrounding 
industrial operations. The decision supports considering existing land use patterns when 
evaluating zoning changes. 
 
These considerations provide a reasonable planning basis for considering additional Industrial 
zoning in this location by ‘matching development intensity to sustainable infrastructure capacity’ 
(of Policy 1.1) and by considering the specific context of an area when making development 
decisions (of Policy 5.4). 
 
Economic Development 
The proposed expansion could provide opportunities for economic diversification and growth in 
emerging industries (of Policy 4.2), while making use of an established industrial area. However, 
agriculture is also an important component of the regional economy (of Policy 4.4), and the 
conversion of productive farmland should be weighed against these potential benefits. 
 
Additional industrial development could also strengthen the Township's tax base, but may 
introduce long-term costs for infrastructure, public services and emergency response. The 
LVPC encourages the Township to consider both the economic benefits and public costs of the 
potential development when determining an appropriate extent of industrial expansion (of Policy 
4.6). 
 
Natural Resources and Scenic Character 
The proposed rezoning area is near the Delaware River and its associated woodlands and 
natural resources. The properties also front Martins Creek-Belvidere Highway, which is part of 
the Delaware River Valley Scenic Byway. 
 
The LVPC recommends retaining appropriate agricultural or natural buffers along the Scenic 
Byway and Delaware River corridor and evaluating whether the proposed zoning boundaries 
and existing development standards adequately protect woodlands, waterways, floodplains and 
other sensitive resources. These measures would help conserve regional environmental and 
scenic assets and minimize potential impacts from industrial development (of Policies 1.3, 3.1 
and 3.2). 
 
Infrastructure Capacity and Regional Impacts 
The petition identifies proximity to existing power generation facilities as a reason for the 
proposed rezoning. The location presents opportunities to leverage existing electrical 
infrastructure to support emerging industries and technology, supporting FutureLV's emphasis 
on expanding technology, communications and utilities to prepare for future employment 
opportunities (of Policy 2.5). However, proximity to existing infrastructure does not necessarily 
establish that adequate capacity is available to support additional development. 
 
39

[page 40]
 
 
The LVPC recommends establishing a preliminary understanding of the infrastructure capacity 
and potential development intensity that the expanded Industrial District could accommodate 
before adopting the amendment. This should include available water and wastewater service, 
electrical capacity, emergency service needs, and the ability of Martins Creek-Belvidere 
Highway, DePues Ferry Road and connecting roads to accommodate additional industrial 
traffic. 
 
Identifying these considerations at the zoning stage would help the Township determine an 
appropriate extent of industrial expansion, while allowing detailed impact studies and permitting 
to occur during subsequent development reviews (of Policies 1.1, 1.4, 2.5 and 3.2). 
 
Regional Takeaways 
The proposed zoning map amendment presents an opportunity to accommodate industrial 
growth adjacent to established quarry and power generation operations, potentially making 
more efficient use of existing infrastructure and supporting economic development. However, it 
would also expand industrial zoning into an undeveloped area identified for farmland 
preservation in FutureLV, near natural and scenic resources of regional importance. 
 
From a regional planning perspective, the LVPC supports consideration of limited industrial 
expansion in this location, but the extent of expansion should be informed by the agricultural, 
environmental and infrastructure characteristics of the affected properties. The LVPC 
recommends that the Township and applicant further evaluate and refine the proposed zoning 
boundaries to prioritize areas most suitable for industrial development, and more importantly, 
retain existing agricultural and natural lands and protect the Delaware River Valley Scenic 
Byway. Ensuring that the boundaries of Industrial District are expanded intentionally would 
reconcile economic development opportunities with FutureLV's regional preservation and 
growth management priorities. 
 
Municipalities, when considering zoning ordinance amendments, should reasonably attempt to 
be consistent with FutureLV: The Regional Plan, as required by the Pennsylvania Municipalities 
Planning Code (MPC) [Article 1§105, Article III§303, §304 & §306(a), Article VI§603(j)].
