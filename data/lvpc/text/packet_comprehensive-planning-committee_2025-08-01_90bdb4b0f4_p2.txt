Township substantially rewrote its zoning ordinance in 2024, following the update of the 
Northern Lehigh Multi-Municipal Comprehensive Plan (Plan Northern Lehigh). The 
amendment proposed at this time corrects references between the zoning ordinance 
and the Township’s recently updated subdivision and land development ordinance

with the Township SALDO. The amendment clarifies definitions, introduces references 
to scenic resources, refines design standards, expands conditional use procedures, and 
updates lighting and noise requirements. The Township has emphasized that these 
revisions are designed to better align its zoning ordinance with current regulations, 
adopted plans, and the character of its villages and rural landscapes.

REGARDING Electronic Plan Submission Project and Amending County Subdivision 
and Land Development Ordinances 
 
INTRODUCTION 
Under the Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning Code (MPC), Act 247 of 1968, counties 
are authorized to adopt and administer subdivision and land development ordinances 
(SALDOs). Both Counties’ SALDOs were created by the LVPC and first enacted in 
1983, and the most recent amendment to both SALDOs was also made by the LVPC in 
2003. In instances where municipalities have not adopted their own SALDOs, the 
County SALDO applies. In Lehigh and Northampton counties, the Lehigh Valley 
Planning Commission (LVPC) serves as the designated planning agency responsible 
for administering the County SALDOs. This includes: 
• Accepting and reviewing subdivision and land development applications based 
on County SALDO requirements and criteria; 
• Coordinating with applicants and developers on project submissions; 
• Coordinating with municipal officials and engineers; 
• Coordinating with other reviewing agencies; 
• Taking action to approve or deny proposals based on applicable regulations and 
planning standards. 
The LVPC is in the process of launching an Online Submission Portal to modernize and 
streamline the development review process. A key component of this transition is 
enabling electronic plan submission for subdivision and land development reviews. To 
support this, both Lehigh County and Northampton County Subdivision and Land 
Development Ordinances (SALDOs) must be amended to: 
1. Require electronic plan submission through the new portal; and 
2. Remove or revise any existing requirement for submission of paper plans. 
27

[page 28]
    
   
The proposed transition to electronic submission reflects the LVPC’s ongoing efforts to 
provide professional, consistent, and modern planning services on behalf of the 
counties and their municipalities. This updated process aligns with the growing use of 
digital tools in planning, enhance efficiency, reduce costs, and improve access for all 
stakeholders involved in the development review process. This project is currently 
underway, and LVPC staff are working on the County SALDO updates, and working 
with GeoCivix, the project consultant, to develop an online portal where projects for 
review and other requests will be processed and managed.  The new online portal is 
intended to be open by January 1, 2026. 
CASE STUDIES 
Several other counties across Pennsylvania have already completed the process of 
transitioning to an electronic plan submission system including:  
• Montgomery County 
• Chester County 
• Bucks County 
• Lancaster County 
• Tri-County Regional Planning Commission (Dauphin and Perry County) 
Additionally, accepting digital plan submissions is a best practice LVPC encourages 
municipalities to undertake when feasible because it streamlines the submission 
process, reduces paper waste and vehicle trips, saves both applicants and local 
governments time and printing costs, and increases accessibility. 
 
SALDO AMENDMENT PROCESS 
The MPC provides the statutory framework for enacting and amending subdivision and 
land development ordinances at both the municipal and county levels. Based on these 
provisions of the MPC, the process for amending the County SALDOs includes: 
1. Drafting the Amendment Language 
LVPC staff are preparing proposed amendments to both counties' SALDOs in 
accordance with MPC Section 504 that: 
a. Provide procedures for plan submission through the LVPC Online 
Submission Portal; 
b. Eliminate or revise provisions requiring hard-copy submissions; 
c. Clarify submission standards and digital file requirements. 
2. Public Review Period (Publication, Advertisement and Availability of 
Ordinance) 
a. Public notice is given on the proposed enactment in accordance with MPC 
Section 506, specifying information on when and where adoption of the 
28

[page 29]
    
   
amendment will be considered, and where copies of the proposed 
amendment may be examined.  
b. Notice is published in one newspaper of general circulation at least seven 
days (and up to 60 days) before adoption.  
c. Additional online promotion of the amendment will notify municipalities and 
the public of the proposed amendment and provide the opportunity for 
comment. 
3. Public Notice of Hearing 
a. In accordance with MPC Section 505 and Section 107, public notice is 
published in a newspaper of general circulation for two successive weeks.  
b. The first notice must be published at least 14 days (and up to 30 days) 
before the hearing. 
c. Notice will include the subject of the hearing and the time and place the 
hearing will be held. 
4. County Planning Commission Review 
a. In accordance with MPC Section 505.(a), the planning agency (LVPC in 
this case) will review the proposal at their regular meeting at least 30 days 
prior to the public hearings. 
5. Conduct Public Hearing 
a. In accordance with MPC Section 505.(a), each County’s governing body 
will hold a public hearing to receive comments and consider public input. 
6. Ordinance Adoption 
a. Following the hearing, each governing body may vote to adopt the 
amendments. The vote must occur at a public meeting and be duly 
recorded in the minutes. 
7. Notification and Implementation (anticipated in September) 
Once adopted, LVPC will: 
a. In accordance with MPC Section 502.(a), distribute copies of the amended 
SALDO to all municipalities within Lehigh County and Northampton 
County.  
b. Notify development, engineering and planning professionals who have 
been active across the Lehigh Valley according to our records; 
c. Update procedural guides and application materials; 
d. Launch the Online Submission Portal with user support materials. 
This amendment is a necessary and forward-thinking step to improve how land 
development plans are reviewed in Lehigh and Northampton Counties. By aligning the 
SALDOs with modern submission practices, the counties can provide better service to 
constituents, improve internal workflows, and reduce unnecessary burdens on 
applicants.  Enclosed is the draft of the proposed Northampton County and Lehigh 
County amendments for each respective subdivision and land development ordinance 
for review and discussion. Below is the anticipated timeline for adoption to meet the 
portal launch deadline and state law requirements.

SECTION III 
PLAN REQUIREMENTS 
300  Feasibility Review  
301  The feasibility review submission shall include the following maps and materials.  
301.1  A key map, for the purpose of locating the property being subdivided, 
drawn at a scale not smaller than one (1) inch equals two thousand 
(2,000) feet and showing the relation of the property, differentiated by 
tone or pattern, to adjoining property and to all streets, roads and 
municipal boundaries existing within one (1) mile of any part of the 
property. The key map may be based on USGS quad sheet maps.  
301.2  A map illustrating an analysis of natural drainage patterns and water 
resources within the proposed subdivision tract, including delineation of 
streams, natural drainage swales, ponds and lakes, wetlands, flood plains 
subject to a one hundred (100) year flood frequency, and permanent and 
seasonal high water table areas. The map shall be based on USGS quad 
sheets, County Soil Survey Maps, and the flood boundary and floodway 
map from the Municipal Flood Insurance Study, when available.  
301.3  A topographic map of the site based on USGS quad sheets.  
301.4  A map delineating additional significant physical features within the 
proposed subdivision tract, such as woodland areas, large trees, rock

sheets and on-site survey work.  
301.5  Where feasible and legible, the analysis involved in Sections 301.1 
through 301.4 may be illustrated on one or a combination of composite 
maps. The combined impact of the natural characteristics upon the 
development potential of the tract shall be clearly illustrated on the map or 
maps.  
301.6  A letter of intent and a sketch of the proposed subdivision or land 
development tract at a scale of one (1) inch equals one hundred (100) 
feet explaining and illustrating the developer's general development 
concepts for the tract. The type of development, density of development, 
form of ownership, circulation patterns, and means of providing major 
utility service should be explained and illustrated. The sketch may be 
based on deed and tax map information.
