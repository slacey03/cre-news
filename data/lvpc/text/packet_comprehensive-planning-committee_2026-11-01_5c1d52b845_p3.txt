The application proposes a zoning ordinance amendment in the City of Bethlehem to create 
‘heathcare adaptive reuse’ as a permitted use in the Single-Family Residential District (RS) and 
add several provisions under additional requirements for certain uses.  
 
The Single-Family Residential District (RS) under the purposes of each district, intended to 
‘provide medium density neighborhoods that are primarily comprised of single-family detached 
dwellings’ (page 24 of the City of Bethlehem Zoning Ordinance). The intent of the district does 
not align with the proposed use of healthcare buildings, which aligns with the permitted use 
chart, which does not allow for Personal Care Homes or Assisted Living Facilities within the RS 
zone, despite the proposals limitation on only allowing adaptive reuse at existing lawful uses.  
 
The Municipalities Planning Code (MPC) defines such use as a Nonconforming use which ‘does 
not comply with the applicable use provisions in a zoning ordinance… where such use was 
lawful in existence prior to the enactment of such ordinance or amendment’ (Municipalities 
Planning Code Page 5). As Personal Care Homes or Assisted Living Facilities within the RS 
zone that would undergo healthcare adaptive reuse would qualify as nonconforming use, 
healthcare adaptive reuse would seek to change a nonconforming use into a conforming use 
without substantially changing the facility thereby bypassing the City’s regulations for changes in 
impact of nonconforming uses. Section 605(1) of the MPC empowers municipalities to regulate

nonconforming uses and structures (page 47 of the Municipalities Planning Code). The 
implementation of this ordinance would allow an existing nonconforming use to become a 
conforming use without needing to meet the requirements of said use as a part of the adaptive 
reuse.  
 
The proposal also does not define what ‘healthcare adaptive reuse’ means which makes 
measuring its potential impact difficult. The Cities Ordinance defines several different uses that 
all fall within the broader category of healthcare like Medical Office Buildings, Hospitals, and 
Treatment Centers. All these uses have different impacts and any change in use to an existing 
Assisted Living Facility or Personal Care Home within the RS Zone should be compared to the 
impact of the new proposed use. This approach would better align with FutureLV: The Regional 
Plan by ‘guiding the location and intensity of development’ (of Policy 1.1).  
 
FutureLV: The Regional Plan does generally ‘encourage reuse and redevelopment in urban 
areas (of Policy 1.1), and adaptive reuse of Assisted Living Facilities or Personal Care Homes 
into uses more appropriate to the intent of the zone, such as housing or schools, would align 
more with FutureLV. 
 
Municipalities, when considering zoning ordinance amendments, should reasonably attempt to 
be consistent with FutureLV: The Regional Plan, as required by the Pennsylvania Municipalities 
Planning Code (MPC) [Article 1§105, Article III§303, §304 & §306(a), Article VI§603(j)]. Please 
feel free to reach out with any questions about this review.
