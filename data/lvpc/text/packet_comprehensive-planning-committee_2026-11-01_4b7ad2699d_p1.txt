Background 
Slate Belt Holdings, LLC proposes to develop the Slate Belt Technology Campus, also 
known as the River Pointe Data Center Campus, on properties along River Road, 
Marshfield Drive, Demi Road and Potomac Street in Upper Mount Bethel Township. The 
proposed development consists of 29 data center buildings, including 23 west of River 
Road and six east of River Road. The applicant identifies approximately 3.3 million 
square feet of building footprint and an ultimate electrical demand of up to 2,100 
megawatts (MW). 
 
The campus is proposed to include substations, switchyards, backup generators, 
battery storage systems, cooling equipment, parking, loading areas, internal roadways, 
stormwater facilities and water and sewer infrastructure. The plans also identify an area 
for a potential on-site power plant and battery energy storage facility. The applicant 
anticipates approximately 1,200 employees, including full-time contractors, with a 
maximum shift of approximately 600 employees. Construction would occur in phases 
based on the availability of electrical capacity and other infrastructure. 
2

[page 3]
 
   
 
The site was previously proposed for the River Pointe Logistics Center, a planned 
industrial park consisting of approximately 5.9 million square feet of industrial 
development. While the applicant proposes to use portions of the previously approved 
industrial park's grading, roadway and stormwater designs, the proposed data center 
campus introduces different infrastructure requirements and operational impacts that 
warrant further evaluation. 
 
The LVPC also received a separate application for Slate Belt Technology Campus 
(Design 2, West & East), which proposes an alternative development configuration for 
the same site. That application is under review separately. The LVPC recommends that 
the applicant clarify the relationship between the two designs and identify which 
buildings, infrastructure and improvements would be constructed under each 
alternative. Any improvements shared by or necessary to either design should be 
clearly identified.

The site is located in a rural area south of Portland Borough and west of the Delaware 
River. Surrounding land uses include existing industrial facilities along River Road, 
agricultural lands, woodlands, residential properties and the Upper Mount Bethel 
Community Park. The site's landscape and environmental setting are important regional

The area also contains significant scenic and recreational resources. River Road and

The LVPC recognizes the site's industrial zoning and previous approvals. However, its 
location, surrounding communities and sensitive natural resources require careful 
consideration of the proposed development's regional impacts. The LVPC offers the 
following recommendations to improve consistency with FutureLV: The Regional Plan. 
 
Regional Land Use 
  FutureLV General Land Use Plan 
 
Design 1 proposes 29 data center buildings and associated infrastructure on both sides 
of River Road. The campus area includes lands identified for High Priority Farmland 
Preservation, Preservation Buffer and Exurban uses on the FutureLV: The Regional 
Plan General Land Use Plan. Character-Defining Areas are also mapped within the 
surrounding landscape, including along the Delaware River corridor. 
 
FutureLV establishes a conservation-first approach to regional land use planning. 
Farmland Preservation areas are recommended to remain predominantly agricultural, 
while Exurban areas are generally recommended for rural uses and limited 
development. Natural resource conservation and farmland preservation are strongly 
4

[page 5]
 
   
 
preferred within Preservation Buffer areas, where development suitability should be 
evaluated against the Plan’s development criteria. These include infrastructure capacity, 
proximity to existing development and protection of high-priority natural resources. 
 
The proposed conversion of lands identified for farmland preservation to a large data 
center campus does not align with FutureLV’s recommendations to preserve agricultural 
lands and maintain rural character (Policies 1.1, 1.3 and 3.3). The LVPC recognizes that 
the proposal builds upon prior industrial park approvals. However, those approvals do 
not change the regional preservation recommendations. The LVPC recommends 
identifying where buildings, equipment yards, roads, grading and utility improvements 
overlap mapped preservation areas and evaluating opportunities to retain agricultural 
land, woodlands and connected open space. Development near the Delaware River 
corridor should preserve natural and scenic resources and minimize changes to the 
surrounding landscape (Policies 3.1 and 3.2). 
 
Economic Development 
The proposed use would occupy property previously planned for an industrial park 
within the Township's industrial zoning framework. The development has the potential to 
expand the regional technology sector, create employment opportunities and contribute 
to the local tax base. The use of an established industrial development framework and 
coordinated infrastructure planning can support economic development and 
infrastructure efficiency (of Policies 1.1 and 4.2). 
 
Development Intensity and Electrical Infrastructure 
The proposed campus would require up to 2,100 MW of electricity at full build-out. The 
project narrative identifies agreements with FirstEnergy for 1,020 MW, with additional 
capacity anticipated through utility service and potential onsite generation. The final 
configuration and operating characteristics of several major electrical systems have not 
been established. 
 
The Master Site Plan (CS-200) identifies substations and switchyards throughout the 
development, and Building Pad 10B identifies a proposed power plant and battery 
energy storage facility. The narrative describes onsite generation as a potential future 
component. The LVPC recommends clarifying whether these facilities are included in 
the current application or would require separate future land development approvals. 
The applicant should provide a coordinated electrical infrastructure and phasing plan 
identifying the locations, proposed capacities and functions of substations, switchyards, 
generators, battery energy storage facilities and any on-site power plant. The plan 
should distinguish emergency backup equipment from equipment intended to provide 
electricity during normal operations. It should also identify the infrastructure required for 
each development phase and the anticipated responsibilities for construction, operation 
and maintenance. 
 
Given the scale of the proposed demand, the LVPC encourages continued coordination 
with FirstEnergy, the Township and applicable state and federal regulatory agencies. 
The applicant should document the improvements necessary to serve the campus, their 
5

anticipated timing and how associated costs will be addressed. These 
recommendations support matching development intensity with sustainable 
infrastructure capacity and coordinating land use decisions across municipal boundaries 
(of Policies 1.1 and 1.4). 
 
The applicant identifies a target Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) of approximately 
1.25 and proposes to coordinate electrical service with the utility provider as 
development phases advance. The LVPC encourages continued pursuit of energy-
efficient equipment, renewable energy opportunities and other measures that reduce 
resource consumption (of Policy 3.4).  
 
Natural Resources 
The proposed development plans identify streams, wetlands, steep slopes and 
environmentally sensitive habitat areas, including mapped bog turtle habitat buffers. 
Although this project is within the Township’s Industrial Zoning District, given the 
preference of FutureLV to take a conservation-first approach to development, the LVPC 
strongly recommends ensuring that the site design is configured to avoid disturbing 
sensitive natural features. The applicant should provide an updated environmental 
impact assessment identifying the existing condition of each resource, previous and 
proposed disturbance, and measures to avoid, minimize or mitigate impacts. The 
assessment should distinguish improvements authorized under previous approvals from 
changes proposed with the current application. These measures support conserving 
natural resources and minimizing environmental impacts of development (of Policies 3.1 
and 3.2). 
 
Community Compatibility, Scenic Resources and Landscaping 
The plans identify proposed building heights that are 80 feet tall, substantially changing 
the scale and appearance of development in the surrounding rural landscape. Buildings, 
cooling equipment, electrical infrastructure and outdoor equipment yards may be visible 
from River Road, Route 611, the Township Community Park, neighboring properties 
and locations across the Delaware River. 
 
The LVPC recommends providing building elevations and visual simulations from 
representative public viewpoints, including the Community Park, scenic roadways and 
river corridor. These should depict buildings and major equipment at their proposed 
heights, with landscaping shown at installation and maturity. 
 
The project narrative identifies landscaped berms, native plantings, equipment 
screening and full-cutoff lighting. The LVPC supports these measures and recommends 
ensuring that the final plans demonstrate their effectiveness. Particular attention should 
be given to the landscape berm adjacent to the Community Park, the visibility of 
electrical and mechanical equipment, and the protection of scenic views along River 
Road. 
 
The applicant should also incorporate building materials, façade treatments and 
equipment screening that reduce the visual scale of the development. Coordination with 
6

[page 7]
 
   
 
the National Park Service, Lower Delaware Wild and Scenic River Management Council 
and neighboring municipalities is encouraged where scenic impacts extend beyond the 
Township. 
 
These measures support development that complements the surrounding environment 
and community character (of Policies 3.2 and 5.4). 
 
Regional Trail Connection and Multimodal Accessibility 
FutureLV identifies a conceptual regional trail connection along River Road. This 
corridor provides an important connection between existing and proposed trails in 
Portland Borough and Lower Mount Bethel Township. The LVPC previously 
recommended connecting the River Pointe development's internal walking trails to River 
Road and the surrounding trail network. The current proposal provides an opportunity to 
advance this regional connection. 
 
The LVPC recommends that the applicant coordinate with the Township, LVPC and 
surrounding municipalities to identify and preserve a feasible continuous trail corridor 
along the River Road frontage. Where appropriate, the applicant should incorporate a 
trail alignment or easement into the land development plans, with consideration of 
roadway crossings, utility infrastructure, environmental constraints and future 
connections beyond the site. 
 
The development should also provide safe pedestrian connections between building 
entrances, parking areas, internal open spaces and the regional trail corridor. Bicycle 
parking and clearly designated pedestrian routes should be incorporated where 
appropriate. 
 
The LVPC also encourages coordination with the Lehigh and Northampton 
Transportation Authority (LANTA) regarding potential future transit service. A 
designated passenger pick-up and drop-off area, and space for a future transit stop with 
appropriate pedestrian access, would improve transportation options for employees. 
These improvements support multimodal transportation and regional recreational 
connectivity (of Policies 2.2, 5.2 and 5.3). 
 
Transportation Impacts and Roadway Infrastructure 
The applicant submitted a Transportation Impact Study (TIS) prepared by Dynamic 
Traffic, dated October 2026. The study uses Institute of Transportation Engineers (ITE) 
Land Use Code 160, Data Center, to estimate traffic generated by the proposed 
development. 
 
The LVPC recognizes that data centers generally generate fewer routine truck and 
employee trips than similarly sized warehouse and distribution facilities. However, 
several transportation issues remain. 
 
Trip Generation and Construction Traffic 
7

[page 8]
 
   
 
Compared with the previously proposed logistics campus, data center operations are 
expected to involve fewer routine truck deliveries and employee trips. This change has 
the potential to reduce some of the freight-related transportation impacts previously 
identified by the LVPC. The proposed dedicated turn lanes and coordinated site access 
improvements also support transportation safety and efficiency, subject to the 
refinements discussed above (of Policies 2.2 and 2.4). 
 
Note that the proposed square footage exceeds the range of data represented in the 
ITE trip generation dataset for data center land uses. The LVPC recommends that the 
Township evaluate trip estimates using document anticipated staffing, shift schedules, 
deliveries and other operational assumptions. 
 
The project narrative also references a construction traffic management plan that was 
not included in the materials provided for this review. The LVPC recommends providing 
this plan with estimated construction vehicle trips, truck and oversized-load routes, 
delivery schedules, staging areas and measures to minimize impacts on neighboring 
communities and roadways. Construction activity should be evaluated separately from 
routine operations (of Policy 2.2). 
 
Regional Roadway Improvements and the Norfolk Southern Rail Bridge 
The TIS identifies roadway improvements at the Portland-Columbia Bridge approaches, 
including intersection realignments and coordinated traffic signals. Without mitigation, 
the southbound ramp/bridge approach intersection is projected to experience level of 
service F for an eastbound movement during the weekday evening peak hour. The 
LVPC recommends documenting the status of these improvements, required agency 
approvals, implementation responsibilities and the development phases they are 
intended to serve. 
 
The Norfolk Southern railroad bridge over Delaware Avenue/Route 1039, north of the 
development, remains a significant transportation constraint previously identified by the 
LVPC. The existing vertical clearance of approximately 13 feet, 8 inches leaves limited 
clearance for large commercial vehicles. The narrow roadway beneath the bridge also 
presents potential conflicts between opposing trucks. 
 
The LVPC strongly recommends continued coordination among the applicant, Norfolk 
Southern, PennDOT, Portland Borough, Upper Mount Bethel Township and the 
Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission to evaluate feasible improvements. The study 
should address vertical and horizontal clearances, roadway drainage and flooding, utility 
lines beneath the bridge, and the types of vehicles expected to serve the campus. 
Feasible safety measures and improvements should be identified and coordinated with 
development phasing. 
 
The TIS also proposes a 250-foot southbound right-turn lane at River Road and Demi 
Road, although the available length may be constrained by the railroad bridge. The 
LVPC recommends confirming the feasibility of the proposed improvement with the

responsible roadway agencies. These recommendations support a safe and efficient 
transportation network (of Policies 2.2 and 2.4). 
 
Site Access, Loading and Internal Circulation 
The project narrative identifies 27 loading docks, but the locations and configurations of 
the docks are not clearly shown on the site plans. The LVPC recommends depicting 
loading areas and providing sufficient truck staging or parking to prevent delivery 
vehicles from stopping along River Pointe Drive or other access roadways. 
 
A vehicle-turning analysis should be provided to demonstrate that delivery trucks, 
emergency vehicles, refuse trucks and other service vehicles can safely navigate the 
internal road network. The plans should also identify appropriate site access controls, 
wayfinding and circulation routes. 
 
New Marshfield Drive is identified for dedication to Upper Mount Bethel Township. The 
LVPC recommends confirming responsibility for roadway maintenance, snow removal 
and long-term infrastructure upkeep following dedication. The applicant is also 
encouraged to provide electric vehicle charging infrastructure for employees and 
appropriate electrical connections at loading areas to reduce unnecessary vehicle idling. 
 
Water Supply, Wastewater and Cooling Systems 
The applicant proposes to extend public water and sanitary sewer service from Portland 
Borough systems. The project narrative estimates approximately 35,000 gallons per day 
of domestic water use and an equivalent volume of wastewater. The campus would also 
include approximately five million gallons of on-site fire protection water storage. 
The applicant proposes closed-loop cooling without evaporative cooling towers. The 
cooling system would require an initial fill from an approved off-site source, with the 
water then recirculated during operation. The LVPC supports this approach as a means 
of reducing ongoing demand on local water resources (of Policies 3.1 and 3.2). 
 
The applicant should document the volume and source of the initial cooling-system fill, 
anticipated replenishment needs, the timing and rate of filling fire-water storage tanks, 
and any additional water demand associated with power generation or other equipment. 
These demands should be evaluated separately from routine domestic consumption. 
 
The LVPC recommends obtaining documentation from the appropriate water and 
wastewater service providers addressing available capacity, necessary system 
improvements and the anticipated schedule for service extensions. The applicant 
should also coordinate with PADEP, the Delaware River Basin Commission and other 
applicable agencies regarding required approvals. 
 
Act 58 of 2026, effective December 7, 2026, clarifies that the water supply evidence 
required under MPC §503.1 must be provided before final subdivision and land 
development approval. These measures support reliable infrastructure and the 
conservation of water resources (of Policies 1.1, 3.1 and 3.2).

Noise, Vibration, Heat and Air Quality 
The campus would operate continuously, with potential exterior noise sources including 
chillers, heat-rejection equipment, transformers, generators and other electrical 
infrastructure. The project narrative states that a site-specific acoustical study will be 
prepared. 
 
The LVPC recommends providing a cumulative noise study for the full campus that 
evaluates normal operations, generator testing and other reasonably foreseeable 
operating conditions. The study should assess sound levels at property lines and 
nearby sensitive receptors, including residential properties and the Community Park, 
and address low-frequency and tonal noise where applicable. 
 
The final design should identify equipment locations and demonstrate how screening, 
enclosure, orientation and sound attenuation will achieve applicable noise standards. 
Post-construction sound testing is also recommended to verify performance once the 
facilities are operational. 
The applicant should additionally identify the expected locations and impacts of heat 
rejection equipment, and provide information about the number, capacity, fuel types, 
operating schedules and anticipated emissions of generators and any proposed on-site 
power plant. Required PADEP air quality permits and associated operating conditions 
should be coordinated with the Township's land development review. 
 
These measures support minimizing environmental and public health impacts and 
maintaining compatibility with surrounding land uses (of Policies 3.2 and 5.3). 
 
Emergency Access and Public Safety 
The proposed campus includes high-voltage equipment, battery systems, generators, 
cooling fluids and extensive internal circulation infrastructure. The scale and complexity 
of the development require coordinated emergency-response planning. 
 
The plans identify two emergency connections to Potomac Street and one to River 
Road. The LVPC recommends confirming that these connections are suitable for 
emergency vehicles, remain accessible in all weather conditions, and are clearly 
identified through signage and access controls. 
 
The internal road network should also be evaluated for redundant emergency access to 
individual building pads. Particular attention should be given to buildings served by long 
driveways or limited access points, including the southern development areas. Clear 
building identification and wayfinding signage are necessary to help emergency 
responders locate incidents quickly. 
 
The applicant should coordinate with the Township, Northampton County emergency 
management personnel, local fire and emergency medical service providers and 
applicable utilities to prepare a campus-wide emergency response plan. The plan 
should address fire suppression, fire-water storage, emergency shutoffs, battery storage 
hazards, fuel and chemical inventories, spill response, access to electrical facilities and 
10

[page 11]
 
   
 
evacuation routes. Emergency responder training and facility orientation should occur 
before operations begin. 
 
This coordination supports safe community design, effective emergency services and 
protection of critical infrastructure (of Policy 5.1). 
 
Coordinated Plan Review  
The current submission relies on infrastructure and design concepts developed through 
the previous River Pointe Logistics approvals. The LVPC recommends that the 
applicant provide a coordinated summary identifying which previously approved 
improvements remain applicable, which have been completed, and which are being 
revised or replaced. 
 
The applicant also indicates an intent to maintain communication with the Township and 
community as the project advances. Continued engagement and public availability of 
relevant technical information can support informed land use decisions and regional 
coordination (of Policy 1.4). The LVPC also encourages continued communication with 
Portland Borough, Lower Mount Bethel Township and other affected communities 
regarding construction, infrastructure improvements and the development's regional 
impacts (of Policy 1.4). 
 
Conclusion 
The Slate Belt Technology Campus represents a substantial change from the previously 
proposed logistics development. The proposed use has the potential to reduce the 
previously anticipated freight traffic and water consumption while creating new 
economic opportunities. However, the campus's scale, electrical demand, supporting 
infrastructure and proximity to sensitive natural, scenic and recreational resources 
present significant regional planning considerations. 
 
The LVPC encourages the applicant to provide a written response identifying how the 
review recommendations will be addressed. This will help the Township, reviewing 
agencies and surrounding communities understand the status of outstanding issues and 
any proposed mitigation. 
 
Municipalities, when considering land developments, should reasonably attempt to be 
consistent with FutureLV: The Regional Plan, as required by the Pennsylvania 
Municipalities Planning Code (MPC) [Article I §105, Article III §§303, 304 and 306(a), 
and Article VI §603(j)]. The LVPC review does not include an in-depth examination of 
the plans relative to municipal subdivision and land development ordinance 
requirements, which are addressed through the Township's review. 
 
The LVPC has copied appropriate agencies and neighboring municipalities to support 
coordinated land use decisions across municipal boundaries (of Policy 1.4).

consideration and electricity that may be generated on-site.

decisions. The LVPC recommends reconciling these descriptions and identifying which 
improvements are included in the current land development application.

size and purpose of each major electrical facility. This should include substations, 
transformers, standby generators, battery energy storage systems, fuel-related

Transportation Analysis and Regional Roadway Conditions 
Dynamic Traffic submitted a Trip Generation Comparison memorandum dated October 
12, 2026. Rather than preparing a separate Transportation Impact Study (TIS) for 
Design 2, the memorandum compares the western building program with the western 
development analyzed in the Design 1 TIS and concludes that the earlier analysis 
provides a conservative basis for evaluating Design 2. 
 
The LVPC recognizes that fewer data center buildings may generate less routine traffic. 
However, several assumptions require clarification. 
 
Trip Generation and Study Scope 
The trip generation memorandum evaluates five western buildings totaling 1,251,010 
square feet. The Master Site Plan (CS-200) also identifies a 228,186-square-foot data 
center on the eastern property, resulting in approximately 1,479,196 square feet across 
the six data center buildings. This differs from the narrative's approximately 1.4-million-
square-foot total. The LVPC recommends reconciling these figures and updating the 
transportation documentation to clearly account for the complete Design 2 proposal,

including the eastern development and any trips associated with power generation, 
maintenance and supporting facilities. 
 
The LVPC independently evaluated the narrative's 1.4 million square feet using ITE 
Land Use Code 160 and calculated approximately 775 weekday vehicle trips, including 
80 trips during the morning peak hour and 70 during the evening peak hour. However, 
the proposed development exceeds the square-footage range of the underlying ITE 
data. The average-rate methodology produces considerably higher estimates than the 
fitted-curve approach. 
The applicant should compare these methods, explain the selected assumptions and 
demonstrate that anticipated employee shifts, service deliveries and other operating 
characteristics are reflected in the analysis. The Township and PennDOT should also 
confirm whether the Design 1 study's traffic assumptions and recommended 
improvements remain applicable to the full Design 2 configuration. 
 
Roadway Capacity and Rail Bridge Constraints 
The existing transportation network has several limitations identified during previous 
River Pointe reviews, including constrained access to the regional highway system and 
the Norfolk Southern railroad bridge over Delaware Avenue/Route 1039 north of the 
development. 
 
The bridge provides approximately 13 feet, 8 inches of vertical clearance. Its limited 
width and nearby overhead utilities may also constrain movement by large trucks and 
specialized equipment. These conditions are particularly relevant during construction 
and installation of electrical transformers, generators and other oversized components. 
The LVPC strongly encourages the applicant to coordinate with Norfolk Southern, 
PennDOT, the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission, Portland Borough and Upper 
Mount Bethel Township to assess bridge clearances, roadway width, utility conflicts and 
feasible safety improvements. The proposed southbound right-turn lane at River Road 
and Demi Road should also be evaluated for feasibility given its proximity to the railroad 
crossing. 
 
The Design 1 TIS identifies improvements at the Portland-Columbia Bridge approaches, 
including intersection realignments and coordinated traffic signals. The applicant should 
clarify whether these improvements remain necessary under Design 2, the status of 
associated agency approvals and how their construction would be coordinated with the 
campus's development phases. The transportation assessment should consider the 
cumulative effects of surrounding industrial uses, not only traffic generated by the 
subject buildings (of Policies 2.2 and 2.4). 
 
Construction Traffic, Loading and Site Circulation 
The project narrative references a construction traffic management plan, but one was 
not included in the materials provided to LVPC. A construction-specific assessment is 
recommended because temporary truck movements and delivery of major electrical 
equipment may have different impacts than routine data center operations.

Water Supply, Sewer Service and Cooling 
The project proposes connecting to Portland Borough public water and wastewater 
systems. The narrative estimates domestic water use and wastewater generation at 
approximately 17,500 gallons per day. Fire protection would rely in part on on-site 
storage totaling up to three million gallons. 
 
Design 2 proposes a closed-loop water cooling system that recirculates cooling fluid 
rather than relying on routine evaporative cooling. The applicant states that the initial 
cooling system fill would come from an approved off-site source and that cooling fluids 
would be collected for off-site disposal when necessary. The LVPC recognizes the 
potential water conservation benefits of this system. The applicant should nonetheless 
provide a complete water demand assessment distinguishing domestic consumption, 
the initial cooling-system fill, periodic replenishment, fire storage filling and any process 
water requirements associated with electrical generation. 
 
Documentation from Portland's water and sewer service providers should identify 
available capacity, proposed extensions, necessary improvements and the schedule for 
providing service. The review should also consider how infrastructure construction and 
service commitments may affect existing users. 
 
Act 58 of 2026, effective December 7, 2026, requires the water-supply evidence 
specified in MPC §503.1 to be presented before final subdivision and land development 
approval. The Township should coordinate this requirement with the applicant and 
applicable service providers. 
 
The LVPC encourages maintaining the proposed water conservation measures while 
documenting their full operating requirements (of Policies 1.1, 3.1 and 3.2).
