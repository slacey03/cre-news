Transportation 
The applicant submitted a trip generation evaluation prepared by Kimley-Horn using the 
Institute of Transportation Engineers (ITE) Trip Generation Manual, 12th Edition, Land 
Use Code 160, Data Center. 
 
The study estimates 588 weekday daily vehicle trips, including 72 trips during the 
morning site peak hour and 64 during the afternoon site peak hour. The LVPC reviewed 
the trip generation calculations and confirmed the projected totals. 
 
These estimates suggest relatively limited operational traffic compared with other 
industrial uses of similar building size. However, employee shift schedules have not 
been established, and the study provides limited information about construction traffic, 
truck deliveries and other service vehicles. 
 
The project's access to an established industrial roadway network, comparatively low 
projected operational vehicle trips, proposed sidewalks and bicycle parking support 
efficient transportation and improved access options for employees (of Policies 2.2, 2.3, 
5.1 and 5.3). 
 
Freight Operations and Site Access 
Access would be provided by an existing driveway from Commerce Center Boulevard, 
with improvements to accommodate truck turning movements and a proposed 
westbound left-turn lane. 
 
The submitted plans include truck and fire apparatus turning exhibits. The LVPC 
recommends supplementing these exhibits with information on anticipated construction 
and operational truck activity, delivery schedules and the types of vehicles that will 
regularly serve the facility. Garbage trucks and other service vehicles should be 
included in the turning analysis where applicable. 
 
The applicant should also evaluate dedicated truck parking or staging areas to prevent 
vehicles from waiting along access drives when multiple deliveries arrive. The proposed 
security gate should provide sufficient vehicle queuing space without obstructing 
internal circulation or emergency access. 
 
A construction transportation plan should identify truck volumes, anticipated routes, 
construction-related access needs and measures to minimize conflicts with existing 
Commerce Center operations. Final driveway design, sight distances and proposed 
roadway improvements should be coordinated with the City and applicable reviewing 
agencies (of Policies 2.2 and 5.1). 
 
Pedestrian Access and Sustainable Transportation 
Sidewalks are proposed along the site access drive, and pedestrian connections are 
included within the development. These improvements support safer movement 
between parking areas, building entrances and other site facilities (of Policies 5.1 and 
5.3).

LANTA Route 105 operates along Hellertown Road (PA 412), and Route 605 serves 
Commerce Center Boulevard, including a stop near the adjacent McKesson facility. The 
LVPC encourages the applicant to coordinate with LANTA and the City to ensure that

The plans also identify bicycle parking near the building. The LVPC supports this

Community and Cultural Context 
The history, culture and heritage of the Lehigh Valley's communities, including the 
region's Indigenous heritage, contribute to its unique identity and sense of place. The 
LVPC encourages the applicant to consider the historical and cultural context of the 
surrounding community by exploring opportunities to coordinate and consult with 
community members, Indigenous representatives, local commissions and other relevant 
organizations. Where opportunities exist, the applicant is encouraged to incorporate 
community feedback into design elements or other measures that complement and 
respect the area's character and heritage. These considerations support development 
that responds to the unique history, environment, culture and needs of the Lehigh Valley 
(of FutureLV Policy 5.4). 
 
Conclusion 
The Bethlehem Earth Commerce Data Center is proposed on a previously disturbed 
industrial property within an area designated for Development in FutureLV: The 
Regional Plan. This location is generally appropriate for industrial redevelopment and 
offers opportunities to make use of existing infrastructure. 
 
However, the submitted materials do not provide sufficient information for the LVPC to 
determine the project's overall impact and alignment with FutureLV Goals and Policies. 
The proposed generator yards, electrical substation, water infrastructure and cooling 
systems are integral parts of the development. Their operational characteristics and 
associated impacts must be understood to properly evaluate the project. 
 
The LVPC strongly recommends that the City obtain and review the additional 
information identified in this letter before taking final action on the land development 
plan, to the extent authorized by applicable ordinances and state law. Where additional 
studies are not expressly required under the standards governing the application, the 
LVPC encourages the City and applicant to work collaboratively to provide this 
information for public and agency review. 
 
If additional information or subsequent design changes substantially modify the 
submitted plans, the revised materials should be provided to the LVPC for further review 
before plan signing and recordation.

Background 
The application proposes redeveloping a former Dixie cup facility that is currently a brownfield 
into a 465,500 square-foot warehouse, at 605 Kuebler Road (PIN: J9 15 8 & J9 21 3). The site 
still contains most of the foundations of the original building, as well as the parking lots and 
substation. The site also contains a railroad crossing and is directly adjacent to Kesslerville 
Road.

Land Use 
 
FutureLV: The Regional Plan General Land Use Map identifies the site in three different 
overlapping sections. The project is located within the Development Area, where factors 
appropriate for development may exist, but also in the Farmland Preservation Area, which is 
predominantly agriculture and recommended to remain agricultural. The site is also located 
within the Preservation buffer, areas where the factors may be present but conservation may 
also be appropriate. FutureLV lays out specific development criteria for determining whether an 
area of the preservation buffer is appropriate for development: 
 
• If 'the proposal is consistent with the conservation of high-priority natural resources' as 
outlined in the Natural Resources Map in FutureLV.  
The Natural Resource Map in FutureLV highlights several different existing natural 
resources on the site, including the 1% Annual Chance Flood Hazard, Natural Heritage 
Inventory Supporting Landscape, which contains environmentally sensitive species or 
habitats, steep slopes, and several surface depressions from mapping provided by 
USGS. The proposal does take steps to ‘minimize the environmental impact of

impact of flood hazard as a part of their conditional approval, expanding basins to 
contain the extra stormwater created by the steep slopes, and including karst mitigation 
in the erosion and sedimentation plan. The Natural Heritage Inventory Supporting 
Landscaping is unlikely to contain the sensitive species because of the existing 
brownfield, but the LVPC recommends the township consider requesting a Pennsylvania 
Natural Diversity Inventory (PNDI).  
 
• If 'the site is contiguous to existing development'. 
 
Most of the site's surroundings to the north, east, and south are industrial like 
warehousing and manufacturing. To the west of the site are several high-density 
residential neighborhoods with both condos and single-family homes. Given the site was 
previously industrial prior to much of the surrounding industrial expansion and the 
construction of adjacent SR 1009, the site serves as the outer limits of development in 
the industrial area and is contiguous.

The site is identified in the Forks Township 2022 Comprehensive Plan as a site for future

respect to roads, bridges, transit facilities and bicycle/pedestrian facilities, including 
traffic safety or congestion, based on accepted transportation planning and engineering

of the study. According to the Institute of Transportation Engineers (ITE) Trip Generation

land development plans and the traffic impact study, so the LVPC relied on the higher of 
the two numbers for the ITE estimate to create a more conservative estimate. The

• The LVPC supports the inclusion of truck parking on the site. However, the trailer

safely park and allows movement of other vehicles around the parked vehicle. It is not 
clear if the parking spaces are long term or available to address hours of 
compliance. These “overnight” or long-term parking spaces are a critical safety element

number of parking spaces to help the truck parking shortage. It is strongly recommended 
that several parking spaces be provided for tractor-trailers who may require them to rest 
and reset their hours-of-service timeframes.

cause road hazards and unsafe conditions. Snow removal equipment to provide 
compliance with the law for drivers is becoming commonplace for freight-centric 
facilities.

concentrated development build-out.
