Background 
Slate Belt Holdings, LLC proposes the Slate Belt Technology Campus, also referred to 
as the River Pointe Data Center Campus, along River Road, Marshfield Drive, Demi 
Road and Potomac Street in Upper Mount Bethel Township. Design 2 includes six data 
center buildings, with five west of River Road and one east of River Road. The project 
narrative identifies approximately 1.4 million square feet of building footprint, 35-foot 
building heights. The project also includes electrical substations, switchyards, standby 
generators, battery systems, cooling equipment, internal roads, parking and loading 
areas, water and sewer connections, fire protection facilities, and stormwater 
infrastructure. The eastern development area also contains proposed power generation 
and battery energy storage facilities. 
 
The applicant identifies an ultimate electrical demand of up to 2,100 megawatts (MW), 
including 1,020 MW currently under agreement with FirstEnergy and additional capacity 
anticipated through grid service and potential on-site generation. Other proposed

Floodplain Impacts 
The eastern development area is located adjacent to the Delaware River, where 
floodplain conditions and associated environmental hazards warrant particular 
consideration. 
 
Portions of the proposed site improvements are proposed within the mapped 500-year 
floodway, and additional equipment areas are located along the floodplain boundaries. 
The area is prone to severe flooding beyond the mapped line due to a combination of 
proposed development and changing climate patterns (of Policy 3.4). The Federal 
Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) Region 3 office, which includes the Lehigh 
Valley, acknowledges that ‘Over the past 25 years, heavy rain has been on the rise 
across the U.S. The biggest rise has been seen in the Northeast. In the decades to 
come, climate projections show the Northeast will continue to have more frequent and 
severe heavy rains, leading to higher flood risk.’ (FEMA Instructions For Communities 
Mapping Rising Flood Risk)  
 
The LVPC strongly recommends that the applicant configure the site to avoid locating 
critical electrical infrastructure, fuel and chemical storage and other potentially 
hazardous facilities close to flood-prone areas. The applicant should also demonstrate 
that emergency access, stormwater management and hazardous material containment 
measures adequately account for potential flooding.

Regional Land Use and Site Suitability 
The properties are identified within a Development area on the FutureLV General Land 
Use Plan. Development areas generally contain existing development and infrastructure 
and are appropriate locations for continued development and redevelopment. Reusing 
previously disturbed industrial land supports the Regional Plan's recommendations to 
encourage the use of vacant and underutilized properties (of Policy 5.4).

The project would reuse land formerly associated with Bethlehem Steel operations 
within an established industrial development area. This supports the efficient use of 
existing infrastructure and redevelopment of underutilized properties (of Policies 1.1 and 
5.4). The proposed land use is generally compatible with the surrounding industrial 
development pattern. However, the intensity and operational characteristics of the 
facility require further evaluation to demonstrate that the development will use 
infrastructure responsibly and minimize impacts on nearby properties (of Policies 1.4, 
3.1, 3.2 and 5.4). 
 
Electrical Demand and Infrastructure 
The plans identify a proposed privately owned electrical substation occupying 
approximately 123,750 square feet. However, the submission does not identify the data 
center's projected electrical demand, the substation's capacity or the infrastructure 
improvements needed to provide service. 
 
The LVPC recommends that the applicant: 
• Identify the anticipated electrical demand, including the maximum load at full 
operation and any planned expansion. 
• Provide written coordination with PPL Electric Utilities confirming the proposed 
service arrangement, available capacity and any necessary transmission, 
distribution or substation improvements. 
• Identify whether off-site utility improvements are needed, who will be responsible 
for their costs and how service reliability for existing customers will be 
maintained. 
• Provide the anticipated Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) and describe 
measures to reduce energy consumption and incorporate renewable energy 
sources.

These measures would support responsible infrastructure investment, protect the 
reliability of existing services and advance regional energy sustainability goals (of 
Policies 3.4 and 4.6). 
 
Water Supply, Cooling and Wastewater 
The project description identifies water treatment and storage infrastructure, and the 
utility plans depict water and sewer connections. However, the submission does not 
describe the proposed cooling technology or quantify the facility's anticipated water use 
and wastewater generation. 
 
Data centers can have substantially different water demands depending on their cooling 
systems. The LVPC recommends that the applicant identify the type of cooling system 
proposed, including whether it uses air cooling, evaporative cooling, water cooling or a 
hybrid system. 
 
The applicant should also provide: 
• Projected daily, peak and annual water demand, including cooling, equipment 
maintenance and domestic uses. 
• The proposed source and capacity of water treatment and storage facilities, 
along with any water reuse or conservation measures. 
• The anticipated wastewater volume and discharge characteristics, including any 
cooling-system discharge. 
• Written coordination with Bethlehem Water and Sewer Resources confirming 
adequate capacity for the facility's projected demands, including periods of 
drought or peak system demand. 
 
The LVPC also encourages evaluation of water-efficient cooling alternatives and water 
reuse opportunities to reduce long-term demand on regional water resources (of 
Policies 3.2 and 3.4). 
 
Noise, Vibration, Air Quality and Heat 
The plans identify generator yards along both sides of the proposed building. These 
areas, together with the electrical substation and cooling equipment, have the potential 
to generate continuous noise, vibration, heat and air emissions. The application does 
not provide sufficient information about these systems to evaluate their impacts. 
 
The LVPC recommends that the applicant identify the number, location, capacity and 
fuel type of all proposed generators, along with fuel storage, emissions controls and 
anticipated testing and maintenance schedules. The applicant should identify applicable 
state and federal air quality permitting requirements. A professional noise and vibration 
impact study should evaluate the facility under full operational conditions, including 
cooling equipment, transformers, generator testing and other mechanical systems. The 
evaluation should address audible and low-frequency noise, identify anticipated levels 
at property boundaries and nearby sensitive properties, and recommend mitigation 
where necessary.

Natural Resources 
The East Branch Saucon Creek is a significant natural resource along the southern 
edge of the development. The plans identify nearby wetlands, watercourse buffers, 
steep slopes and stormwater management facilities. The LVPC recognizes the 
proposed buffers and landscaping intended to separate development from the creek 
corridor. The applicant should confirm that the proposed grading, drainage facilities, 
utility improvements and equipment areas will not adversely affect the creek, wetlands 
or associated buffers to protect surface water and groundwater, manage stormwater 
runoff and minimize the environmental impacts of development (of Policies 3.1 and 3.2). 
 
The proposed landscape improvements offer opportunities to reduce visual impacts, 
restore vegetation and improve the transition between industrial development and the 
surrounding natural landscape. These measures support context-sensitive development 
and environmental stewardship (of Policies 3.2 and 5.4). 
 
Emergency Services and Public Safety 
Data centers present specialized emergency response considerations because of their 
electrical infrastructure, backup power systems, security controls and potential 
hazardous materials. 
 
The proposed facility includes a long access driveway with staffed security controls and 
a separate emergency access point closer to the building. The LVPC recommends that 
the applicant coordinate with the City of Bethlehem's fire, police and emergency medical 
services to confirm adequate emergency access, equipment clearances, fire 
suppression, water supply and procedures for entering the secured site. 
 
The applicant should prepare an emergency response plan identifying the proposed 
electrical and mechanical systems, fuel and chemical storage, equipment isolation 
procedures, emergency shutdown protocols and any specialized response 
requirements. Emergency access signage should be installed along Commerce Center 
Boulevard and other appropriate approaches to direct responders to the correct 
entrance. 
 
Coordination with surrounding municipalities and nearby industrial facilities, including 
the UGI Bethlehem LNG facility as appropriate, is also encouraged to support regional 
emergency preparedness (of Policies 1.4 and 5.1).
