The proposal amends the Hanover Township Zoning Ordinance Section §185-38 Aircraft 
Flightpath Highway Business Zoning District (AFHBD) by expanding permitted uses to include 
Planned Industrial/Business Park and adding related regulations, design and performance 
standards. The proposal also amends permitted uses in the AFHBD by permitting commercial 
and industrial uses such as indoor agricultural grower/processor (excluding marijuana), food 
service stores, brew pub, microbrewery and distillery, allowing solar energy farms as a 
conditional use, and prohibiting gas stations, parcel hub/package sorting, cold storage, e-
commerce fulfillment, drive-thrus and data centers. 
 
Existing Conditions in the AFHBD 
The AFHBD is an employment district identified in the Township Zoning Ordinance. 
Employment districts, as stated in the Township zoning ordinance, are intended to ‘provide 
sufficient space in appropriate locations for offices, laboratories, light industries, and certain 
other manufacturing and related activities anticipated’ and ‘promote stable manufacturing, office 
uses, and other related development in order to strengthen the Township's tax base and protect 
its tax revenues’ (Section 185-9 D.).  
  
The majority of the AFHBD is undeveloped agricultural land. Adjacent uses include the Lehigh 
Valley International Airport, residential neighborhoods, institutional uses (Hindu Temple Society 
and Christ United Church of Christ), and commercial and industrial developments including 
Lehigh Valley Industrial Park I (LVIP I).

Existing and adjacent infrastructure in the area includes public water and sewer service 
provided by the City of Bethlehem, and the site is adjacent to both Airport Road and 
Schoenersville Road. 
  
The area is identified for Development in the General Land Use Plan of FutureLV: The Regional 
Plan, where the land is appropriate for a variety of uses, including major commercial and 
industrial development. Existing factors needed to support growth like adequate sewer and 
transportation infrastructure capacity and continuity to existing development are present, which 
serves to ‘match land use intensity to sustainable infrastructure capacity’ (of FutureLV Policy 
1.1).   
  
Planned Industrial/Business Park Regulations 
The proposal includes regulations for planned industrial/business park land uses, which 
includes any use permitted in the AFHBD with the addition of warehouses. In the proposal, 
planned Industrial/Business Park uses require a minimum of 200 acres and with buildings up to 
395,000 square feet and a maximum height of 45 feet. The proposal also includes façade 
requirements, a 500-foot buffer from residential zones, screening, open space dedication, 
landscaping including islands in parking areas, and truck driver amenities. 
 
The proposed buffering and berms create separation from residential areas, reducing noise, air 
quality and visual impacts . Truck access limitations protect neighborhood streets from freight 
traffic (of Policy 2.4), and stormwater and landscaping requirements improve environmental 
resilience (of Policy 3.2). 
 
Zoning vs. SALDO Provisions 
The proposed ordinance includes a number of provisions that are more appropriately regulated 
under the Township’s Subdivision and Land Development Ordinance (SALDO), such as detailed 
stormwater design standards, landscaping specifications (e.g., berm dimensions, tree counts 
and calipers), parking ratios, loading dock orientations, lighting design, and truck driver 
amenities. 
 
Best practice under the Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning Code (MPC) is to maintain zoning 
ordinances for the regulation of uses, location, and intensity of development, while reserving the 
SALDO for design, engineering, and construction standards. Mixing the two can create 
duplication, inconsistency, and legal uncertainty, as zoning amendments are not the proper 
vehicle for regulating development details that are intended to be reviewed and enforced at the 
subdivision and land development stage. 
 
The LVPC recommends that Hanover Township relocate the technical and design provisions to 
the SALDO, while retaining in the zoning ordinance the high-level requirements related to 
permitted uses, dimensional standards, and buffering from incompatible zoning districts. Zoning 
language may then cross-reference the SALDO to ensure consistency and enforceability. This 
approach will provide greater clarity for applicants, staff, and decision-makers, while aligning 
with MPC best practices and regional planning standards. 
 
 
Municipalities, when considering zoning ordinance amendments, should reasonably attempt to 
be consistent with FutureLV: The Regional Plan, as required by the Pennsylvania Municipalities 
Planning Code (MPC) [Article 1§105, Article III§303, §304 & §306(a), Article VI§603(j)]. The

The proposal amends the Whitehall Township Subdivision and Land Development Ordinance to 
increase the fair share contribution of Recreation Fees (Article X Impact Regulations Section 
§22-37). A fair share contribution will now be required of all residential subdivision and land 
development projects of 1,398 square feet per dwelling unit (previously 1,107 square feet) or a 
flat fee of $4,200 per dwelling unit (previously $2,000). Contributions are used to fund parks 
and/or recreational facilities, furthering the principles and standards established in the 
Township’s Park, Open Space and Recreation Plan. 
 
Parks and Open Space 
Requiring open space dedication and collecting recreation fees allows a municipality to 
‘minimize environmental impacts of development to protect the health, safety and welfare of the 
public’ (of Policy 3.2) by ensuring parks and recreational spaces are available to residents as 
the Township evolves. Well-maintained parks and recreational spaces improve quality of life for 
residents by ‘creating community spaces that promote physical and mental health’ (of Policy 
5.3) and ‘increasing social and economic access to daily needs of all people’ (of Policy 5.2). 
 
Raising open space impact fees ‘supports evolution and adaptability of government’ (of Policy 
1.1). The Township’s recreation fee was last amended in 2004, and the proposed increase 
reflects the greater costs of maintenance today, aligning fees with inflation and the Township’s

Housing Attainability 
Flat per-unit fees do not account for differences in how households use and impact open space 
resources. Smaller homes and units may generate less demand, while larger households often 
create greater use and impacts but contribute at the same rate. The LVPC recommends 
evaluating opportunities to ensure fee calculations are aligned with actual usage and demand, 
ensuring that contributions are proportionate to the impacts of each project and supporting 
attainable housing (of Policy 4.5).1

SECTION III 
PLAN REQUIREMENTS 
300  Feasibility Review  
301  The feasibility review submission shall include the following maps and materials.  
301.1  A key map, for the purpose of locating the property being subdivided, 
drawn at a scale not smaller than one (1) inch equals two thousand 
(2,000) feet and showing the relation of the property, differentiated by 
tone or pattern, to adjoining property and to all streets, roads and 
municipal boundaries existing within one (1) mile of any part of the 
property. The key map may be based on USGS quad sheet maps.  
301.2  A map illustrating an analysis of natural drainage patterns and water 
resources within the proposed subdivision tract, including delineation of 
streams, natural drainage swales, ponds and lakes, wetlands, flood plains 
subject to a one hundred (100) year flood frequency, and permanent and 
seasonal high water table areas. The map shall be based on USGS quad 
sheets, County Soil Survey Maps, and the flood boundary and floodway 
map from the Municipal Flood Insurance Study, when available.  
301.3  A topographic map of the site based on USGS quad sheets.  
301.4  A map delineating additional significant physical features within the 
proposed subdivision tract, such as woodland areas, large trees, rock 
21

[page 22]
DRAFT DATE: September 9, 2025 
   
 
outcroppings and scenic views. The map may be based on USGS quad 
sheets and on-site survey work.  
301.5  Where feasible and legible, the analysis involved in Sections 301.1 
through 301.4 may be illustrated on one or a combination of composite 
maps. The combined impact of the natural characteristics upon the 
development potential of the tract shall be clearly illustrated on the map or 
maps.  
301.6  A letter of intent and a sketch of the proposed subdivision or land 
development tract at a scale of one (1) inch equals one hundred (100) 
feet explaining and illustrating the developer's general development 
concepts for the tract. The type of development, density of development, 
form of ownership, circulation patterns, and means of providing major 
utility service should be explained and illustrated. The sketch may be 
based on deed and tax map information.
