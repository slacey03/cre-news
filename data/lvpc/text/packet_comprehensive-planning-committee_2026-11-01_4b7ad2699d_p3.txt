The application proposes a zoning ordinance amendment in the City of Bethlehem to create 
“healthcare adaptive reuse” as a permitted use in the Single-Family Residential District (RS) 
and add several provisions under additional requirements for certain uses. 
 
Under the declared purposes of each district, the Single-Family Residential District (RS) is 
intended to “provide medium density neighborhoods that are primarily comprised of single-
family detached dwellings” (page 24 of the City of Bethlehem Zoning Ordinance). The intent of 
this district does not align with the proposed use of healthcare buildings. This mismatch is also 
reflected in the permitted use chart, which does not allow Personal Care Homes or Assisted 
Living Facilities within the RS zone, despite the proposal’s restriction to allow adaptive reuse 
only at existing lawful uses. 
 
The Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning Code (MPC) defines a nonconforming use as one 
that “does not comply with the applicable use provisions in a zoning ordinance… where such 
use was lawfully in existence prior to the enactment of such ordinance or amendment” 
(Municipalities Planning Code, page 5). Because Personal Care Homes or Assisted Living 
Facilities within the RS zone that would undergo healthcare adaptive reuse currently qualify as 
nonconforming uses, creating a "healthcare adaptive reuse" classification would effectively 
change a nonconforming use into a conforming use without substantially changing the facility.

Consequently, this bypasses the City’s regulations governing changes in the impact of 
nonconforming uses. Section 605(1) of the MPC explicitly empowers municipalities to regulate 
nonconforming uses and structures (page 47 of the Municipalities Planning Code). The 
implementation of this ordinance would allow an existing nonconforming use to become 
conforming without needing to meet the standard requirements of the newly permitted use 
under the adaptive reuse framework. 
 
Additionally, the proposal does not define what “healthcare adaptive reuse” actually means, 
making it difficult to evaluate its potential impact. The City's ordinance defines several distinct 
uses that fall under the broader umbrella of healthcare, such as Medical Office Buildings, 
Hospitals, and Treatment Centers. Each of these uses carries different community and traffic 
impacts; therefore, any change in use for an existing Assisted Living Facility or Personal Care 
Home within the RS Zone should be evaluated against the specific impact of the newly 
proposed use. This comparative approach would better align with FutureLV: The Regional Plan 
by “guiding the location and intensity of development” (of Policy 1.1). 
 
While FutureLV: The Regional Plan generally does “encourage reuse and redevelopment in 
urban areas” (of Policy 1.1), the adaptive reuse of Assisted Living Facilities or Personal Care 
Homes into uses more aligned with the district's intent, such as housing or schools, would better 
fulfill the goals of FutureLV. 
 
Municipalities, when considering zoning ordinance amendments, should reasonably attempt to 
be consistent with FutureLV: The Regional Plan, as required by the Pennsylvania Municipalities 
Planning Code (MPC) [Article 1§105, Article III§303, §304 & §306(a), Article VI§603(j)]. Please 
feel free to reach out with any questions about this review.
