Act 97 Solid Waste 
Management Review - Solid 
Waste Transfer Station Permit 
Renewal Application 
Lower Nazareth 
Township 
The purpose of the application is for East Penn Sanitation, Inc. to 
obtain a permit renewal from the PA Department of 
Environmental Protection (DEP) for its existing solid waste 
transfer station located at 4687 Hanoverville Road. The facility is 
permitted to accept up to 750 tons per day of municipal solid 
waste, construction/demolition and residual waste as well as 
recyclable materials for transferring and processing. The renewal 
application involves no changes to the permit. The transfer 
station permit is on a ten-year renewal cycle.  
 
The permit renewal application aligns with the 
FutureLV: The Regional Plan action to: “provide 
environmentally responsible and economical 
solid, electronic and hazardous waste disposal 
and recycling” (under Policy 3.2). The PA DEP 
performs the technical review of the application.  
 
Act 537 Plan Review – Lowhill 
Township Act 537 Plan Update Lowhill Township 
The purpose of this plan update is to document the sewage 
disposal needs within the Township and identify alternatives to 
consider and address short- and long-term sewage needs.  
A small area of the southernmost part of the Township adjacent 
to Upper Macungie Township, approximately 1%, are provided 
with public sewer and water service and wastewater from these 
lots are ultimately conveyed to and treated at the Kline’s Island 
Wastewater Treatment Plant. The majority of the Township is 
served by on-lot sewage disposal.  
The plan update notes that over the next 5-year and 10-year 
planning periods, the Township’s sewage planning needs and 
projected population growth should be satisfied with the 
installation, management and oversight of existing and future on-
lot sewage disposal systems and should not require the 
expansion of public sewer infrastructure. The plan further notes 
that growth is anticipated incrementally rather than as a 
concentrated development build-out.  
Based upon the absence of any suspected or confirmed 
malfunctions during the required field verification surveys and the 
absence of any concentrated sewage needs area, 
implementation of a formal Township-wide Sewage Management 
Program is not proposed at this time. Therefore, the Township 
has selected a No-Action Alternative.  
 
The LVPC recommends that the Township 
reconsider the implementation of a Sewage 
Management Program as these programs 
provide proactive and preventative safeguard 
measures for potential future on-lot sewage 
disposal system malfunctions and would help to 
‘protect the quality and quantity of surface water 
and groundwater” as well as ‘minimize 
environmental impacts to protect the health, 
safety and welfare of the public’ (of FutureLV 
Policy 3.2). The benefits and cost of regular 
pumping and cleanout maintenance versus the 
cost it would take to repair or replace a 
malfunctioning on-lot system should also be 
further evaluated and would align with the 
FutureLV action to ‘promote use of cost-benefit 
analysis in planning, development and 
infrastructure decisions’ (of Policy 4.6).

REGARDING: LVPC Food Drive supporting Second Harvest Food Bank 
 
The Lehigh Valley Planning Commission (LVPC) will facilitate a food drive in support of Second 
Harvest Food Bank this year.  Donations will be collected at the LVPC offices located at 501 
Waterfront Drive, Suite 201, Allentown, PA 18102.  Donations will be accepted from Tuesday, 
November 10th through Thursday, December 17th.  
 
Second Harvest Food Bank’s goal is to provide the most nutritious food to the neighbors they 
serve.  The list of foods that are both high in demand and nutritional value they suggest include: 
 
• Peanut Butter 
• Jelly 
• Pasta 
• Cereal (low sugar) 
• Soup (low sodium) 
• Canned Chicken 
• Canned Tuna (low sodium or water packed) 
• Cooking Oils 
• Rice 
• Soy Milk (shelf stable, unsweetened, unflavored) 
 
Second Harvest asks that only nonperishable, non-expired products that are in boxes, cans or 
plastic be provided.  All items should be recently purchased and in good condition.

Grant Opportunities  
 
PennDOT’s Green Light-Go 
The Green Light-Go Program is a competitive state grant program designed to improve 
the efficiency and operation of existing traffic signals located in the Commonwealth. It is 
a reimbursement grant program that requires a minimum 20% match, and the total 
funding available for 2027 is approximately $40 million. Pre-application scoping forms 
are required and due by January 4, 2027. Applications will be accepted from March 1 to 
March 31, 2027. More information is available at: 
https://docs.penndot.pa.gov/Public/Bureaus/BOO/TSPortal/FUNDGLG.html
