The most recent Plan Lehigh Valley National Public Radio show, which aired at 6:30 pm, 
October 5 on WDIY radio 88.1 FM, gave a summary of the many projects completed and 
underway. Co-hosts Becky Bradley and Matt Assad gave updates on often-discussed projects 
that included the $545 million Transportation Improvement Program and the Lehigh Valley Trail 
Connection Strategy, but also the behind-the-scenes work being done on projects such as the 
regional Arts and Culture Plan and Global Stormwater Management Plan. The podcast is now 
streaming at www.wdiy.org/show/plan-lehigh-valley and www.lvpc.org/newslv. The next Plan 
Lehigh Valley show will air Monday, November 2 at 6:30 pm.  
 
The most recent Morning Call Business Cycle Column was published Sunday, October 11, 
under the headline the headline “Now Comes the Hard Part: Building”. In the column, 
Becky details how the Lehigh Valley fell into its housing crisis and how, over the past two years, 
hundreds of dedicated people worked to build a plan to dig out of it. The column details how the 
Lehigh Valley Housing Supply and Attainability Strategy is designed to chip away at the region’s 
9,000-unit housing shortage. The column is available at www.lvpc.org/newslv and 
mcall.com. The next column in the Morning Call will publish on Sunday, November 29.

Local Technical Assistance Program (LTAP) 
 
The following course will be taught in-person at the LVPC Offices. Registration is free and can

Local Road Safety Plans 
 
What: This course will teach participants how to develop a local road safety plan that provides a 
framework for identifying, analyzing, and prioritizing safety improvements on local roads. The 
content of a local road safety plan is tailored to local issues and needs and will vary accordingly. 
The process of developing a plan results in a prioritized list of issues, safety concerns, actions, 
and improvements, which can then be used to reduce fatalities and serious injuries on local 
roads.

Talking Business with Becky Bradley: Now comes the 
hard part -- building 
By Becky Bradley 
For The Morning Call 
Oct. 9, 2026 at 8:00 AM 
How will the Lehigh Valley meet its housing needs? (Pete Shaheen/The Morning Call) 
Everyone needs a place to live. That’s a pretty simple statement of fact. Yet, few issues 
are as personal, complex or consequential. Where we live affects where our children go 
to school, how much time we spend commuting, what we can afford and whether we 
feel rooted in a community. 
Housing isn’t just another issue. In many ways, it’s the issue above all the others, with a 
cascading effect that washes over every aspect of life. 
29

[page 30]
This is why literally hundreds of really dedicated people from across the region have 
spent the last two years helping us develop the Lehigh Valley Housing Supply and 
Attainability Strategy. 
It was inspiring to see more than 220 of them join us at DeSales University to release 
the strategy and a data dashboard that can help every community analyze its housing 
situation and enable developers to determine where and what to build, while giving 
people a glimpse of where they can — and can’t — afford to live in the Lehigh Valley. 
You can find the Housing Strategy and Data Dashboard at lvpc.org/housing. The 
dashboard is designed track housing production from year to year. Already in 2026, 
more than 4,700 homes have been proposed through September. 
The growing number of people who can no longer afford to live here is what started this 
movement in the first place. Our analysis showed that much of the Lehigh Valley had 
become unattainable for many of the families already living here. It revealed a more 
than 9,000-unit housing shortage, brought on by a decade of suppressed building after 
the housing downturn, even as this region continued to grow by more than 4,000 people 
a year. A national housing shortage was exacerbated here because our high quality of 
life makes us attractive to new people and businesses. In other words, developers 
stopped building, but people just kept coming. 
The ensuing shortage helped cause the median home price to double in just 10 years. 
When our kids can’t afford to live here, the net result is that we jeopardize the 
community and economic success we spent decades building. 
That’s when Lehigh County did an extraordinary thing. Its leaders made housing a 
priority, not just for the county, but the entire region, and invested in a study to find 
solutions. That led to a unique partnership between the LVPC, Urban Land Institute, 
Lehigh County and ultimately Northampton County to develop the 152-page Housing 
Strategy. It includes hundreds of recommendations divided into categories of 
Construction and Development, Finance, Land Use and Regulation, and Collaboration 
and Partnership. 
This is a complex puzzle, so there’s no silver bullet that will solve this issue, but rather a 
slate of achievable actions that range from installing efficiencies to shorten the review 
process, making it easier to provide a greater diversity of home types and encouraging 
innovation — in financing, construction and government regulations — that can bring 
more affordable homes to market more quickly. That means considering modular, tiny 
and 3D printed homes, redeveloping vacant or underused retail centers, developing 
innovative financing to support small builders, and streamlining regulations without 
making the municipality more vulnerable. 
What it means most is partnerships and collaboration between developers, builders, 
banks and municipalities are going to be key in digging out of this shortage. And that’s 
30

[page 31]
where I’m most encouraged because that’s what this region specializes in. No other 
region in Pennsylvania, and probably the nation, has so many municipalities planning 
together in multi-municipal plans, and few other regions have used so many public 
private partnerships to get infrastructure projects done. We have proven we can tackle 
this, but it’s going to take everyone, and even then, it’s going to take time. This problem 
is nearly two decades in the making and it’s going to take years to rebalance this 
housing market. 
Everyone needs a place to live. That was the simple statement we started with. The 
solutions aren’t nearly as simple. But they are possible. 
They will require builders to build, municipalities to innovate, lenders to think differently, 
governments to collaborate and all of us to recognize that housing isn’t somebody else’s 
problem. It is a regional problem because it affects every part of our community. We 
spent two years bringing hundreds of people together to understand the problem and 
build a strategy. Now comes the hard part: turning that strategy into homes. 
It will take time. It will take patience. And it will take all of us. 
This is a contributed opinion column. Becky Bradley is executive director of the Lehigh 
Valley Planning Commission. She can be reached at planning@lvpc.org.
