LVPC’s Review Role

Data Center 
 
Ms. Seitz presented the staff review of the proposed construction of a 146,000-square-foot data 
center, including an area for an electrical substation, at 1024 North Bradford Street. 
 
Commissioner Ghai made a motion to accept the staff review and Commissioner Schmidt 
seconded. 
 
Chair Morgan asked for any comments or questions. Commissioners discussed the 
appropriateness of the site, the difficulty of reviewing proposals without sufficient information, 
the growing amount of on-site energy generation and its impact on air quality, a need for 
general best practices and the Allentown public notification process. Members of the public 
thanked the commission for its letter, asked about non-disclosure agreements related to the 
project. Commissioner Gomez clarified that there were no non-disclosure agreements known by 
the City of Allentown and discussed the power consumption of data centers and their impact.  
 
Chair Morgan called for affirmative votes to accept the staff comments, Commissioners Beitler, 
Beavers, Gomez, Napoli, Pearson, Elliott, and Godshall abstained. The motion passed.

The application proposes improvements to the Moravian Academy Athletic Field at 4313 Green 
Pond Road (PIN: L7 14 1) with the conversion of two natural grass athletic fields to infilled 
synthetic turf athletic fields, and construction of athletic lighting, Americans with Disabilities Act 
(ADA) accessible walkways, storage buildings, and stormwater improvements. The site is 
identified in FutureLV: The Regional Plan General Land Use plan as Development Area and has 
most or all the factors needed to support growth, such as sewer and transportation 
infrastructure capacity and contiguity to existing uses. The redevelopment of the site 
'encourages reuse and redevelopment in urban areas' (of Policy 1.1).  
 
The improvements to the athletic facilities help ‘create community spaces that promote physical 
and mental health’ by ‘diversifying park programing’ (of Policy 5.3).  A bicycle rack is 
recommended to be included near the parking area to support a safe, healthy, inclusive and 
livable community (FutureLV: The Regional Plan Goal 5). The addition of a bicycle rack helps to 
ensure transportation accessibility for all persons, integrates mixed transportation into public 
space design, and it ‘improves connections between bus stops and pedestrian and bicycle 
infrastructure’ (of Policy 5.2). The ADA improvements further these policies.

South Whitehall 
Township 
Application by Parkland High School to approve an increase to 
the permitted capacity of the existing Parkland High School 
wastewater treatment plant (WWTP) and its discharge from 
32,400 gallons per day to 55,000 gallons per day as well as 
approval of proposed upgrades to the WWTP. The proposal to 
increase the permitted capacity is due to the WWTP not being 
able to meet its current National Pollutant Discharge Elimination 
System (NPDES) permit limits for total nitrogen due to an 
increased organic and hydraulic loading to the WWTP that 
exceeds the original design criteria. As for the proposed 
upgrades to the WWTP, the applicant is proposing the 
construction and conversion of treatment train equipment as well 
as the installation of an ultraviolet disinfection system to replace 
the existing disinfection system. 
The applicant’s wastewater treatment plant and 
the proposed increase to the permitted capacity 
will allow the WWTP to continue to serve the 
needs of Parkland High School, aligning with 
the FutureLV: The Regional Plan action to 
‘protect the quality and quantity of surface water 
and groundwater” (of Policy 3.2). The proposed 
upgrades to the treatment train equipment and 
the disinfection system of the wastewater 
treatment plant will ‘improve the utility and 
mobility infrastructure of the region’ as well as 
‘integrate efficiency measures and emerging 
technologies’ (of Policy 1.1), which in turn, will 
also protect the quality and quantity of surface 
water and groundwater.
