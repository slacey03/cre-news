A MOTION APPROVING GRACE INDUSTRIES, INC. APPLICATION 
FOR PAYMENT #1 IN THE AMOUNT OF $7,769.25 FOR THE 
BETHLEHEM TOWNSHIP (SR 2018) FREEMANSBURG AVENUE 
AND 9TH STREET EMERGENCY SIGNAL PROJECT 
 
Upon motion (Deckman-Gallagher), the Board of Commissioners voted 
unanimously by voice approving Grace Industries, Inc. Application for 
Payment #1 in the amount of $7,769.25 for the Bethlehem Township (SR 2018) 
Freemansburg Avenue and 9th Street Emergency Signal Project. 
 
 
 
 
 
Jaclyn Friel, Principal , said this is a very large scale, long term plan expected 
to be completed in 2036, which includes a new stadium, reconstructed 
entrances, increased parking, curb and sidewalk.  She said money will be 
raised to improve the sports field. Chris Brown, Brown Design Corp. added 
that this plan proposes a full build -out including the realignment of the north 
drive way. The p roposed development includes:  
 
• 10,600± square foot potential building addition on the east side of the 
existing high school building;  
• Parking area reconfiguration (additional 227 parking spaces);  
• Athletic field reconfiguration (practice field, new synthetic turf track 
and field/football field);  
• Walking/running path traversing the site;  
• 2 indoor batting cages;  
• Concession stand building;  
• Ticket booth;  
• 6 pickleball court areas;  
• Stormwater management areas (1 proposed and 2 potential). 
 
Mr. Hudak said he toured the facilities and was very impressed.  He said he 
supports this plan.  Mr. Deckman asked if the potential future changes had any 
impact on Church Road.  Ms. Smith said the changes wouldn’t affect the road.  
Mr. Gallagher wondered if Church Road would be able to handle game day 
traffic.  Ms. Friel said she was open to suggestions.  
 
No action was taken as this was a sketch plan.

RESOLUTION R068-26 
 
 
Mr. Merhottein said the money will be used to soften the curb at Easton Avenue 
and Farmersville Road, the culvert on Farmersville Road and repaving roads in 
the township. 
 
Mr. Krier asked if the wall will remain at its current location while completing 
those improvements.  Ms. Smith said the wall remains as it is.  The right-of-
way will shift away from the wall. Sidewalk will be added and the street lanes 
are wide enough to meet standards.  Mr. Hudak added that the road won’t be 
narrower.  Mr. Deckman noted the original plan had the road wider than was 
required.  He said they are still in compliance and the intersection will be 
improved by the township. 
 
Mr. Walker said the Zoning Hearing Board denied their request for variances 
and that this settlement seemed like a bribe.  Mr. Hudak said it was their 
suggestion at the cost of the township giving the right-of-way.  Mr. Merhottein 
said the township is getting a sidewalk.  Mr. Walker said it will be narrow and 
noted they are selling the public right-of-way.   Solicitor Broughal said the 
right-of-way was never accepted by the township.  Mr. Walker said he signed 
the plans with that dedication and felt it was inappropriate.  He also mentioned 
that there is still a vacancy on the Planning Commission. 
 
Mr. Roth said the wall should be moved.  He agreed with Mr. Walker.  They 
could have moved the wall.  The township knew there was a problem and 
looked the other way. He asked what will happen when PennDOT comes in.  
He said this was just not right and asked for a full accounting of the money.  
Mr. Hudak took offence.  Mr. Roth said he was very upset that the ball was 
dropped all the way around.  
 
Mr. Yoder said he was disappointed to see this on the agenda. He said the 
$900,000 is a bribe and shows ordinances only matter to those who can’t cut 
corners.  Developers will come in with deeper pockets and residents will be 
unmatched to get around those ordinances. 
 
Mr. Micklos said it cost $400,000 to replace the wall and didn’t make sense 
why it wasn’t.  Mr. Hudak said that is how the township made out. Mr. 
Deckman said time was a factor as they looked for a solution that complied 
with the ordinances.  The township engineer found a plan that would keep them 
compliant, leave money to pay for the culvert, improve the corner and pave 
roads without any exceptions.  Mr. Gallagher commented that the $500,000 
could be considered a penalty charge.  Mr. Merhottein said he gets complaints 
about the roads and once the settlement is executed they will be able to have 
more roads paved.  Mr. Deckman said he wants to make sure the money goes to 
the improvements promised.   
 
Mr. Keefer agreed with the previous comments; that it was what it was.  Mr. 
Hudak said he didn’t want that word repeated. 
 
Upon motion (Hudak-Gallagher), the Board of Commissioners voted 
unanimously by voice to approve a settlement agreement with TSV-HPIII 
Lehigh Valley for the retaining wall at 3311 Farmersville Road.
