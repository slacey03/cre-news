Chapter 49 - Supervisor’s ordinance restrictions discussion:  
The board discussed the ordinance currently in place which governs elected officials being employed by 
the township during their term and for two years after their term ends. 
There was discussion on whether the intent was this to be for full time positions only though not clearly 
worded to state that.  There was discussion about current supervisors working part time during their 
term.  After much discussion the board directed township staff to draft an ordinance update which 
removes the two year moratorium on holding a paid position with the township but leaves the restrictions 
of a current elected official not holding any paid position, full or part time, with the township during their 
term, in place. 
Tim Dahaney – 535 Paxinosa East asked if the ordinance should be updated to include language 
concerning a supervisor working for a contractor being used by the township.  It was explained that 
under the state ethics requirements any appointed or elected official, so anyone elected as a supervisor 
or appointed to the planning commission or zoning hearing board, must declare a conflict at any time a 
project comes before a board if such a conflict exists.

Property Maintenance Ordinance: 
The board was presented with a draft property maintenance ordinance to consider. 
T. Corallo had the following questions/comments – 
 - Pathways/walkways – there seems to be needed clearer language as it is uncertain when a recreation 
path is actually a sidewalk and should be maintained by the property owner. 
 - Meco – we need to make sure that all of our paths are marked as owned and/or maintained by the 
township and all are posted for the winter as utilize at your own risk.  Discussion followed on what if a 
resident has a recreation path in their back yard, can they mark it that it is not maintained during the 
winter? 
 - Section G – speaks to unwooded, developed properties.  Need a better definition of developed property  
 - discussion took place on if a sidewalk is at the top of a property owner’s driveway can they park their 
car, blocking the sidewalk?  What about cars parking on the grass in the front yard? 
The board discussed the need for this ordinance, providing tools to the zoning officer to have properties 
maintained throughout the township. 
The township manager will work with staff to clarify the issues listed above and circulate a draft to the 
board prior to advertising for a hearing. 
T. Corallo made a motion, seconded by A. Blum, to authorize the solicitor and township manager to 
advertise a hearing concerning the adoption of the Property Maintenance Ordinance for Forks Township, 
once a final draft is approved by the board.  Roll Call Vote: A. Blum, yes; T. Corallo, yes; D. Hughes, yes; 
J. O’Neil, yes; D. Turner, yes. Motion carried. 
 
Multi-municipal Comprehensive Plan: 
D. Turner gave an update on the meeting which was held in Palmer concerning interest with surrounding 
municipalities for a multi-municipal comprehensive plan.  The Lehigh Valley Planning Commission staff 
gave an overview of what this would entail, how the process would work and the time line of about two 
years.  The municipalities that were invited were Forks, Palmer, Wilson, W. Easton, Glendon, Williams, 
and the City of Easton.  Each were asked to speak with their respective boards about whether or not  
there is interest in pursuing this process.  Pennsylvania requires that each municipality provides for each 
use and this would allow us to spread the uses around.  The LVPC would plan to, if made the consultant, 
start with surveys of each municipality to help maintain the identity of each.  The board expressed their 
interest in moving forward to find out more about the process.
