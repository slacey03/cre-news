Consideration of Approval of Minimum Municipal Obligation (MMO) for
Pension Plans:

The Board reviewed the 2027 Minimum Municipal Obligation (MMO) for the
Township pension plans. A correction was noted to the defined contribution plan,
reducing the amount by slightly less than $300,000. It was explained that the
figures presented are estimates and that actual pension payments will be based
upon actual eligible salaries. The estimates were characterized as conservative.

Discussion followed regarding the calculation of eligible salaries and the
significant change in the estimated salary base. It was explained that Act 205 does
not require the Board to formally vote on the MMO; rather, the certified
administrative officer is required to present the MMO to the Board, with the
funding ultimately incorporated into the Township's budget.

ACTION: Motion made by Nolan Kemmerer and seconded by Ken Field to
accept the MMO guidelines as presented. Prior to the vote, Chairman, Glenn
Borger, asked if there were any comments from the governing body or the
public. Motion approved. Vote 4-1, with Jane Mellert opposed.

Discussion of Knitters Hill Trailhead Rehabilitation Options:

Township Engineer Mike Muffley provided an update regarding the proposed
Knitters Hill Trailhead rehabilitation and potential acquisition of the adjacent
Achenbach property. Discussion addressed newly identified wetlands, permitting
and mitigation requirements, driveway access, property boundaries, existing


parking encroachment, subdivision/annexation requirements, and the need for an
updated appraisal reflecting the property's current conditions. The proposed
improvements could formalize the parking area and provide approximately eight
to ten additional parking spaces; however, the identified conditions could increase
project costs and affect the original scope. Solicitor David Backenstoe advised
that an Agreement of Sale should not be prepared until the wetland conditions and
annexation requirements are addressed and an updated appraisal is completed,
after which the Township could determine whether to proceed with a formal offer.

ACTION: Motion was made by Ken Field, and seconded by Nolan
Kemmerer, to proceed with the necessary preliminary work associated with
the potential acquisition of the Achenbach property, including evaluation of
the wetlands and preparation of an updated appraisal. Prior to the vote,
Chairman Glenn Borger asked if there were any comments from the governing
body or the public. Motion approved. Vote 4-1, with Jane Mellert opposed.

During public comments on the matter, resident, Brenda Stoudt, questioned
whether removal of trees between the parking area and trail could provide
additional parking. Township Engineer, Mike Muffley, advised that wetland
restrictions could affect those options. Resident, Christine Houck, questioned the
discussion regarding the Township's existing encroachment and whether
permission or an easement from the property owner could address the issue.
Solicitor, David Backenstoe, explained that a formal easement could be obtained,
but acquisition of the property would more fully resolve the matter. Township
Manager, Paige Stefanelli, confirmed that the property owners had previously
indicated willingness to sell the parcel to the Township and explained that the
proposed additional parking could not be completed without acquiring the land.

2. Discussion of Slate Belt Regional Police Department 2027 Budget:

Chief, Jonathan Hoadley, presented information regarding the proposed 2027 Slate
Belt Regional Police Department Budget, with discussion focusing primarily on
employee health insurance costs and potential changes to the health insurance
program. It was reported that current healthcare costs through Benecon had become
increasingly difficult to sustain and that alternatives had been narrowed to two
options. The discussion included potential transition and run-out costs associated with
leaving Benecon, with an estimated maximum exposure of approximately $181,000
moving into 2027. Potential annual savings under an alternative plan were estimated
at approximately $300,000 to $400,000, although the full savings would not be
realized immediately due to transition costs.

Discussion followed regarding whether prior rental revenue associated with the Police
Commission could be used to help offset those transition costs. Reference was made
to a 2021 review of the lease and rental arrangement and recommendations
concerning the establishment of a separate account for rental revenue. Township
Manager, Paige Stefanelli, explained that revenues received by the Township had
been deposited into the General Fund and used for general operating expenses rather
than maintained in a separate account. The Board discussed whether historical
revenues could be reconstructed and whether a separate account could be established
prospectively to better track related revenues and expenditures. No formal action was
taken.
