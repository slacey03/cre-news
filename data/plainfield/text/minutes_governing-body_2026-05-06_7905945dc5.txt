VI. SOLICITOR’S REPORT - (DAVID BACKENSTOE, ESQ.)

1,

Consideration to Advertise Ordinance 434 which would rezone 18 parcels.
approximately 211 acres from the Solid Waste Processing and Disposal District (SW)
to Farm and Forest Zoning District (FF), effectively repealing Ordinance No. 426:

Solicitor, Dave Backenstoe, presented the draft ordinance which would effectively
repeal Ordinance No. 426 and rezone 18 parcels back to the Farm and Forest Zoning
District. Solicitor, Dave Backenstoe, noted that the draft was sent to the Board of
Supervisors for review and that Exhibit A and Exhibit B were just received from
Hanover Engineering. Exhibit B depicts the area on the zoning map that would
transition from Solid Waste Processing and Disposal District to Farm and Forest.
Sending the Ordinance to the Planning Commission and Lehigh Valley Planning
Commission was the appropriate next step before the ordinance is officially
advertised.

ACTION: Motion was made by Jane Mellert and seconded by Paul Levits to
send Ordinance 434, which would rezone 18 parcels, approximately 211 acres
from the Solid Waste Processing and Disposal District (SW) to Farm and Forest
Zoning District (FF), effectively repealing Ordinance No. 426, to the Lehigh
Valley Planning Commission and Plainfield Township Planning Commission for
review and comment. Prior to the vote, Chairman, Glenn Borger, asked if there
were any comments from the governing body or the public.

Supervisor, Nolan Kemmerer, expressed concern about what would happen when the
land is devalued as a result of the rezoning, noting that the Township could face
litigation. Supervisor, Jane Mellert, noted that the original request came in from Slate
Spring Farms. Supervisor, Nolan Kemmerer, noted that the landowner is not making
a request to rezone back. Supervisor, Jane Mellert, stated that it is very apparent that
the landowner no longer has an interest in the rezoning and is no longer interested in
pursuing the request. Supervisor, Nolan Kemmerer, maintained that the landowner is
not making that request.


Solicitor, Dave Backenstoe, noted that anytime there is a change in a property's
zoning, there will likely be a corresponding change in value. He stated that if there is
evidence and an objection at that time, the Township will need to address it
accordingly. Supervisor, Nolan Kemmerer, asked whether the Township would then
be required to pay the owner the difference in value. Solicitor, Dave Backenstoe,
disagreed and indicated that he does not believe that to be the applicable standard in
this matter.

Motion approved. Vote 3-2 with Ken Field and Nolan Kemmerer in opposition.

At 4:10 P.M., Solicitor, Dave Backenstoe, requested that the Board recess into
Executive Session to discuss a personnel matter and potential litigation. The
Executive Session concluded at 4:26 P.M.

The meeting was called back to order.

Solicitor, Dave Backenstoe, reported that the Executive Session addressed matters of
personnel and possible litigation.

ACTION: Motion was made by Ken Field and seconded by Nolan Kemmerer to
appoint Attorney, Abigail Gross, to conduct a personnel investigation pursuant
to the recommendation of Labor Attorney, John Harrison. Prior to the vote,
Chairman, Glenn Borger, asked if there were any comments from the governing
body or the public. Motion approved. Vote 4-0-1 with Paul Levits abstaining.

5. Jane Mellert- Supervisor, Jane Mellert, provided information from an ACRE 101
Seminar conducted by Robert Willig, the Senior Deputy Attorney General at the PA
Office of the Attorney General. Ms. Mellert stated that Mr. Willig reported that in the
past biosolids were not regulated by local law but now there are PFA’s. He noted that
one municipality passed an Ordinance which is now being challenged in court by a
municipal authority and he is waiting to view the outcome. He then mentioned the
municipality is Plainfield Township in Northampton County and the Township took a
different approach with including PFA’s in the Ordinance. He noted the Ordinance
was very thorough. It was mentioned that Earth Justice was involved with assisting
the Township Solicitor with the document.

In addition to the topic of Biosolids, several municipalities had concerns on the
application of Food Processing Residual Waste (FPR). The residual waste is put in
storage tanks and spread on fields. There were concerns over odor issues, etc.
