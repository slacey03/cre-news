July 28, 2026 Business Meeting 
Meeting minutes 
The Mount Penn Borough Council met for the Monthly Business Meeting on July 28, 2026, 
at Mount Penn Borough Hall, 200 N. 25th Street, Reading, PA 19606. Council President Rick 
Lombardo called the meeting to order at 7:00 PM and adjourned at 8:05 PM. 
 
Council President Rick Lombardo, Council Vice President Christine Dise, Council Member 
Troy Goodman, Council Member Michael Kindlick, and Council Member David Korinchock 
were present. Mayor Ryan Maurer, Council Member Tom Baer, Council Member Kelly 
Dudash were absent. Staff and professionals present included Solicitor Zachary Morey, Esq., 
Borough Engineer Ty Leinneweber, P.E., Chief of Police Justin Johnson, Codes Administrator 
Arthur Kelly, Jr., Streets Laborer Ricky Lombardo, and Borough Manager Hunter L. Ahrens. 
Several members of the public were present including Meriam Tiderman, Ron Tiderman, 
Curt Hill, Patti Rose, and Brian Hartman. 
Meeting Opening 
The meeting was called to order at 7:00 PM. All present were asked to rise for the Pledge of 
Allegiance, which was led by Mr. Lombardo. 
Public Comment 
Meriam Tiderman, 121 S. 22nd Street – Ms. Tiderman addressed the Council regarding a 
skateboarding incident in which she was struck and injured in the parking lot of the 
Perkiomen Avenue mini-mall near Dollar Tree. She requested that the borough contact the 
property owner to install more prominent signage prohibiting skateboards, e-bikes, and 
scooters on the premises. She noted that an existing ordinance from 2004 addresses 
motorized vehicles on public property but does not cover private property or skateboards 
specifically. 
 
Chief Johnson confirmed that the department can enforce such prohibitions on private 
property provided the owner grants authorization and appropriate signage is posted, and 
stated he would follow up with the property owner. Mr. Morey noted that the borough's 
regulatory authority does not extend to private parking lots, but suggested Ms. Tiderman 
consider consulting a private attorney. 
 
Ron Tiderman, 121 S. 22nd Street - Mr. Tiderman expressed concern about his wife's injuries 
and a separate issue of potholes on South 22nd Street above Fairview. Mr. Ahrens and Mr. 
Lombardo1 acknowledged the complaint and explained that ongoing rain had delayed

Discuss and Consider Street Project Change Order 
Motion by Troy Goodman, seconded by Christine Dise, to approve Pay Application No. 5 in 
the amount of $12,395.80 to Allen Myers, Contract A. Motion carried unanimously. 
 
Mr. Leinneweber reported that following a Teams meeting with Allen Myers LP, the 
contractor's original additional cost claim of $146,455.90 for Contract B was reduced to 
$85,656.70 after unjustified items were identified and removed. The change order pertains 
to additional labor, equipment, and hauling costs associated with 12-inch milling performed 
in two cuts. The Engineer requested conditional board approval, contingent upon receipt of 
signed documentation from Allen Myers LP. 
 
Motion by David Korinchock, seconded by Troy Goodman, to conditionally approve Change 
Order No. 8 to Allen Myers LP for Contract B in the amount of $85,656.70, subject to receipt 
of signed documentation from the contractor. Motion carried unanimously. 
 
The Engineer also noted that approximately $256,507 remains in the grant balance, less 
engineering costs already submitted for reimbursement, leaving roughly $200,000 on the 
table. It was discussed that any amounts the Water Authority had committed to paying—
approximately $40,000—would be submitted to the state for grant coverage in the hope 
that the State would also approve those expenses rather than require the Authority to pay 
for those changes. 
Discussion and Consider Revised Speed Cushion Quote 
The Council and Borough Manager discussed a revised quote for speed cushions to be 
installed on a borough street near the high school. It was explained that the originally

Motion by Michael Kindlick, seconded by Christine Dise, to advertise a speed cushion

Motion carried unanimously. 
Discuss and Consider Procurement of Public Works Truck 
Mr. Lombardo3 and Mr. Ahrens presented a proposal for the procurement of a new public 
works truck to replace the borough's 2006 pickup. The total purchase price is $146,206, 
consisting of a truck body from Reading Body at $69,572 and a chassis from Chapman Ford 
and Ram at $76,634. The chassis price represents a maximum figure, as final 2027 model 
year pricing has not been released and could be lower. A 25% deposit is required to place 
the body order; no deposit is required for the chassis, with payment due within 30 days of 
delivery. Build times are approximately 24–26 weeks for the body and 5–6 months for the 
chassis. 
 
The available grant balance applicable to this purchase is $64,521, with the remainder to be 
funded through a combination of the general fund and liquid fuels funds, consistent with 
prior practice and with confirmation from the regional financial consultant. Mr. Goodman 
asked when the grant expires June 30, 2028. Key benefits of the new unit include an

Discuss and Consider Resignation of AVMA Board Member 
The Council acknowledged the resignation of Frank Kelly from the AVMA board. 
Motion by Christine Dise, seconded by Michael Kindlick, to accept the resignation of the 
Francis Kelly effective July 7, 2026. Motion carried unanimously. 
Discuss and Consider Appointment of AVMA Board Member 
The Council discussed the process of filling the vacancy on the Antietam Valley Municipal 
Authority. It was agreed to advertise the position via the borough website and social media. 
Public interviews are planned for the August 18th meeting, beginning at 6:30 PM, with the 
possibility of moving to 6:00 PM if additional applicants are received. 
Motion by Troy Goodman, seconded by David Korinchock, to advertise the vacant AVMA 
Board Member position via social media and the borough website. Motion carried 
unanimously.

Reports 
Chief of Police 
Chief Johnson noted that his written report was included in the meeting packet. He 
reiterated the department's commitment to addressing skateboard, scooter, and e-bike 
complaints in the borough's commercial areas, and confirmed he would follow up with the 
owner of the Perkiomen Mini-Mall regarding signage and enforcement authorization. 
Codes Administrator 
Mr. Kelly reported the following activity for the month of July: 116 cases and notices of 
violation opened, 74 closed, and 42 remaining open. Seven zoning permits were issued and 
one inspection was conducted. Property maintenance enforcement—including high grass 
and weeds, overgrown vegetation, trash accumulation, and general maintenance issues—
remained the department's highest priority. 
 
Mr. Kelly raised a significant ongoing concern regarding 1 Weimoor Court, a property with 
multiple outstanding citations, an admitted unsafe structure, and tenants from whom rent is 
being collected despite the conditions. The property has had a condemned double garage 
for over two years, and a required structural engineer's report for its demolition has not 
been submitted. Mr. Kelly indicated he would be requesting guidance on potential next 
steps, including possible blight designation or condemnation. 
 
Mr. Kelly noted that the borough currently has no ordinances governing complaints related 
to feral cats or wildlife feeding (including squirrels), and that without such an ordinance, 
enforcement is limited to sanitary or health-based violations. Mr. Ahrens confirmed that

these matters fall outside the current scope of enforceable code provisions absent a specific 
ordinance. 
 
Mr. Kelly noted that the second quarter report included in the packet reflects that code 
activity is running ahead of last year's projections. 
Merger Committee 
No report was presented; Mr. Ahrens noted that a presentation on merger-related matters 
is being planned for the August 18th workshop meeting. 
Borough Manager 
The Borough Manager reported the following: 
1. A one-year contract extension with J.P. Mascaro was executed at a 2.24% increase. 
2. A request was received from a Cub Scout pack leader for a borough council member 
to serve as a judge at a trunk-or-treat event on Wednesday, October 28, 2026, at the 
Boscov's outlet parking lot near Panera. Mr. Kindlick indicated availability and Mr. 
Ahrens agreed to make the connection. 
3. A review of the borough's employee insurance plan revealed that the borough 
carries a no-opt-out plan, requiring all an employee’s dependents to be enrolled 
regardless of other coverage. An enrollment discrepancy was identified and 
corrected, resulting in a credit of over $14,000 to be applied across the next two 
billing cycles. 
4. Mr. Ahrens shared an article in the Wall Street Journal regarding email-based 
financial fraud schemes in which hackers intercept project-related email chains and 
insert fraudulent banking information to redirect EFT payments. Council members 
were encouraged to remain vigilant. 
5. The borough is working on an updated contract with Environ, an energy 
management company, to seek cost savings on street lights, traffic lights, and 
borough building energy costs. 
Other Business 
Mr. Goodman commented that only Mount Penn Borough residents should be appointed to 
the three Mount Penn-designated seats on the AVMA board, distinguishing those from the 
two seats appointed by Lower Alsace Township. It was also noted that a vacancy exists on 
the Planning Commission and that advertisement for that position could be beneficial. 
Adjournment 
Motion by Michael Kindlick, seconded by David Korinchock, to adjourn that meeting at 8:05 
PM. Motion carried unanimously. 
Mr. Lombardo announced the Council would adjourn to an Executive Session after the 
meeting at 8:05 PM to discuss personnel, potential litigation, and active litigation matters.
