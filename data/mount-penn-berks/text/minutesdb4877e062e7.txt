May 26, 2026 Business Meeting 
Meeting minutes 
The Mount Penn Borough Council met for the Monthly Business Meeting on May 26, 2026, 
at Mount Penn Borough Hall, 200 N. 25th Street, Reading, PA 19606. Council President Rick 
Lombardo called the meeting to order at 7:00 PM and adjourned at 9:09 PM. 
 
Council President Rick Lombardo, Council Vice President Christine Dise, Council Member 
Thomas Baer, Council Member Troy Goodman, Council Member Michael Kindlick, Council 
Member David Korinchock, and Mayor Ryan Maurer were present. Council Member Kelly 
Dudash was absent. Staff and professionals present included Solicitor Zachary Morey, Esq., 
Borough Engineer Ty Leinneweber, P.E., Chief of Police Justin Johnson, Codes Administrator 
Arthur Kelly, Jr., Borough Intern Alexander Darlington, and Borough Manager Hunter L. 
Ahrens. 
 
Several members of the public were present including Tuan Duong.  
Meeting Opening 
The meeting was called to order at 7:00 PM. All present were asked to rise for the Pledge of 
Allegiance, which was led by Mr. Lombardo. 
Presentation on Economic Development Progrees 
Alexander Darlington delivered a presentation on an economic development plan for Mt. 
Penn Borough, which was moved on the agenda to accommodate his attendance. Mr. 
Darlington outlined five strategic priorities: leveraging the Neversink Mountain, Mt. Penn 
Mountain, and Carsonia Avenue corridor to develop an adventure economy; allowing 
flexible zoning for mixed-use development; growing small business development to increase 
business privilege tax revenue; utilizing state programs such as LERTA, Keystone 
Opportunity Zones, and Tax Increment Financing; and strengthening code enforcement 
through a "fix it up, pay it up, give it up" approach.  
Mr. Darlington also noted that Mt. Penn contains four distinct community identities — 
Lower Pennside, East Reading, Mt. Neversink, and an urban core along Perkiomen Avenue 
— which must be considered in building a shared borough identity. Council discussed 
potential qualifying areas for Keystone Opportunity Zones and a previously identified large 
parcel stretching from Herk Yeoman to Fairview Avenue as a possible seed project. 
Public Comments 
There were no public comments. 
Discussion of Berks County Radio Upgrades 

Page 2 
Mt. Penn Fire Chief provided an update on the Berks County-wide radio replacement 
program. Berks County is financing a bond to purchase replacement radios and offering 
participating municipalities a projected discount of 35–55% off the unit price, with 
repayment structured over seven annual installments. The current radios will no longer be 
supported after December 31, 2027, meaning future replacements outside the county 
program would be purchased at full price. The Chief estimated per-unit costs in the range of 
$5,000–$7,000 before discount. The Borough, as the host municipality for the fire company, 
would bear the majority of the cost, though contracted municipalities — Alsace Township 
and St. Lawrence Borough — may be approached to contribute. The possibility of applying 
for a county-level LSA gaming grant to offset the first year's cost was raised. Council was 
advised the final radio count and pricing remain pending the county's bulk procurement 
process. 
 
The Chief also introduced a proposed volunteer firefighter tax incentive program for 
consideration in the 2027 budget year. The proposal would provide a tiered rebate of up to 
$3,500 per year on property and earned income taxes for qualifying volunteer firefighters 
meeting a minimum 75% activity threshold. The program would be reciprocal with Lower 
Alsace Township. The Chief emphasized this was a proactive retention measure, noting that 
the borough currently has only a small number of qualifying firefighters, and that the cost of 
a paid fire service would far exceed the cost of the incentive program. 
Engineers Report 
Borough Engineer Mr. Leinneweber reported that water main installation on Laurel and 
Summit Avenues had been completed and temporary paving is in place. Final paving is 
recommended for August to allow adequate trench settlement time. Scheduling 
coordination with Allan Myers remains ongoing. 
 
Regarding liquidated damages related to Allan Myers' delayed performance, Mr. 
Leinneweber identified approximately 57 days of non-performance, of which 45 days 
involved the contractor removing equipment entirely from the site. Council directed the 
Borough Manager and Engineer to attempt to negotiate with Allan Myers to extend work at 
current contract pricing to include resurfacing the affected section of Ochre Street, in 
exchange for a reduction or release of liquidated damages. If the contractor declines, the 
matter would proceed to arbitration as provided under the contract. Council noted that any 
waiver of the full ordinance restoration requirement on the 2400 block of Endlich Avenue, 
where the Authority has agreed to perform half-road repairs, required a formal vote. 
 
Motion by Thomas Baer, seconded by Troy Goodman, to waive the full-width restoration 
requirement on the 2400 block of Endlich Avenue, permitting Mount Penn Borough 
Municipal Authority to repair only half of the roadway while maintaining a two-year 
warranty obligation. Motion carried unanimously.

Discuss and Consider Traffic Calming Options 
Mr. Leinneweber presented a traffic analysis conducted on Endlich Avenue during school 
hours. The data showed average speeds of 21–23 mph, with maximum speeds during school 
hours ranging from 37 to 45 mph. Based on this analysis, Mr. Leinneweber recommended 
the installation of speed cushions to address excessive speeds without significantly 
impacting drivers traveling at or near the posted limit. Council agreed with the 
recommendation. It was noted that proper pavement markings and signage would 
accompany the installation, and that a formal ordinance would be required prior to 
installation. Total project cost, inclusive of all materials and labor, was estimated to be 
below the bid threshold. Council approved moving forward. 
 
Motion by Christine Dise, seconded by Michael Kindlick, to authorize the installation of speed 
cushions on Aetna Avenue, not to exceed the three-quotes bid threshold in total expenditure. 
Motion carried unanimously. 
Discussion & Updates on Conveyance of MPBMA 
Mr. Ahrens provided an update on the conveyance of the Mt. Penn Borough Municipal 
Authority (MPBMA). The next steps outlined include directing the Authority to pass a 
certificate of dissolution at their June 10th meeting and to approve a personnel 
reimbursement agreement, which would allow authority employees to be transitioned to 
borough employment while still being compensated through collected water rates during 
the interim period. Mr. Ahrens noted that the State’s manual on Authorities affirms the 
borough's right to act without the authority board's approval on this matter. 
 
Mr. Ahrens also reported that a bargaining session with the union to negotiate a 
memorandum of understanding for transitioning water operators is scheduled for the 
following Thursday. The primary outstanding issue is satisfaction of the Authority's existing 
PENNVEST loan tied to the Spook Lane tank construction project, though it is anticipated the 
project may be completed within the current year, rendering this a non-issue, as any 
conveyance would occur after the project’s completion. 
 
Council was also briefed on the outcome of the most recent Authority board meeting, at 
which Authority board member Mr. Boyle made a motion to hire special counsel and read a 
statement characterizing the borough's actions as unlawful. Following an executive session, 
the board clarified the vote was only to retain an attorney for legal guidance, not to file an 
injunction, though Mr. Boyle indicated a preference for the latter. Mr. Ahrens noted that 
the borough's special counsel advised that the strongest posture is to press forward and 
pursue court action if the Authority fails to comply with the ordinance. Council expressed 
frustration with the conduct of the authority board members and noted the matter would 
be discussed further in executive session following adjournment.

Discussion of Fleet Tracking Devices 
Mr. Ahrens presented quotes for vehicle tracking devices for borough, code enforcement, 
and authority vehicles. A comparison of Zuby and FirstNet systems was reviewed; Zuby was 
identified as the more cost-effective option upfront, with the total first-year contract value 
estimated at $2,931 for approximately 11 units. Council discussed implementation timing 
and agreed that a July 1st start date was appropriate, providing time to install units, notify 
employees, and bargain appropriate contract language with union-represented staff. The 
tracking policy will be incorporated into employee acknowledgment forms. 
 
Motion by Troy Goodman, seconded by Thomas Baer, to authorize the purchase and 
installation of fleet tracking devices through Zuby, not to exceed $2,931 for the initial 
contract period, with an implementation date of July 1st. Motion carried unanimously. 
Discussion of Fleet Tracking Devices 
Mr. Ahrens presented a proposed external bank transfer policy to streamline payment to 
Central Berks Regional Police Department. The policy would allow the borough to transfer 
funds electronically via an external bank transfer rather than by check, reducing processing 
time. Transfers would be manually initiated and would include validation procedures and 
documentation on both sides for audit purposes. The transaction fee was estimated to be 
$0.50 per transfer with First National Bank. The policy would apply solely to payments to 
Central Berks PD. 
 
Motion by Troy Goodman, seconded by Michael Kindlick, to approve the External Bank 
Transfer Policy for Central Berks PD as presented. Motion carried unanimously.

Reports 
Codes Administrator 
The Code Administrator reported 36 open cases, 4 inspections conducted, 1 citation issued, 
and 1 citation withdrawn for compliance. Twelve zoning improvement notices were issued. 
A building permit was issued for deck work following identification of unpermitted 
construction. Four additional properties with unpermitted work were identified.  
 
A hearing was held regarding a light intrusion complaint on the 2600 block of Glen Terrace, 
with a 30-day compliance period ordered. An ongoing concern on the 1900 block of Fairview 
involving dogs running loose at a rental property is being addressed jointly with the police 
department. The property owner has been advised to seek legal counsel regarding lease 
enforcement. 
Chief of Police 
Chief Johnson reported on a robbery that occurred on the 2100 block of Fairview Avenue in 
which a group of juveniles from Reading attacked and robbed a resident outside his vehicle 
at approximately 10:30 PM. All juveniles were identified and charged. One juvenile is to be 
detained at Abraxas Academy, which the Chief noted is an uncommon outcome in the 
current juvenile justice environment. 
Borough Manager 
Mr. Ahrens noted that his paternity leave could begin at any time and is expected to last 
approximately seven to ten calendar days. He expressed gratitude for council's flexibility 
and confirmed internal preparations are in place. The 2025 audit is scheduled to begin the 
following week.
