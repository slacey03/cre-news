August 25, 2026 Business Meeting 
Meeting minutes 
The Mount Penn Borough Council met for the Monthly Business Meeting on August 25, 
2026, at Mount Penn Borough Hall, 200 N. 25th Street, Reading, PA 19606. Council President 
Rick Lombardo called the meeting to order at 7:00 PM and adjourned at 9:37 PM. 
 
Council President Rick Lombardo, Council Vice President Christine Dise, Council Member 
Kelly Dudash, Council Member Troy Goodman, Council Member Michael Kindlick, Council 
Member David Korinchock, and Mayor Ryan Maurer were present. Council Member Tom 
Baer was absent. Staff and professionals present included Solicitor Tom Klonis, Esq., 
Borough Engineer Ty Leinneweber, P.E., Chief of Police Justin Johnson, Codes Administrator

were present including Patti Rose, Michelle Boyle, Cindy Heizmann, Debbie Moore, John

Troy Jarvis, Chris Gardecki, Dave Weikel, Linda Orth, Tom Orth, Michael Fritz, Connie Bright, 
Mike Trexler, Kelly Koch, Tom Koch, Amanda Muso, Gwen Williams, Jalee Maurer, and 
several other members of the public.

Public Comment on Conveyance-Leaseback Discussions 
Council President Mr. Lombardo opened the floor for public comment and questions 
specific to the proposed Conveyance-Leaseback of the Mt. Penn Municipal Water Authority. 
He requested that all participants conduct themselves respectfully. A substantial number of 
residents and officials from Mt. Penn, Lower Alsace Township, St. Lawrence Borough, and 
Exeter Township attended and addressed Council. 
 
Patti Rose [Lower Alsace Township] – Ms. Rose submitted a photocopy of a petition bearing 
approximately 390 signatures from ratepayers across Mt. Penn, Lower Alsace, St. Lawrence, 

Page 3 
and Exeter. The petition requests that Borough Council reconvene negotiations with the 
Water Authority to finalize a fair and equitable conveyance leaseback agreement and a 
guaranteed no-sale covenant. Council President Mr. Lombardo directed that the minutes 
reflect receipt of the petition. 
 
Michelle Boyle [Exeter Township] – Ms. Boyle presented a detailed chronological timeline of 
events beginning in November 2025, raising concerns about the process by which the 
Borough engaged legal counsel and financial advisors before formally notifying the Water 
Authority of the proposed conveyance leaseback. She noted that the Water Authority chair 
learned of the proposal via social media prior to any official communication, a 
characterization Mr. Ahrens acknowledged in writing was not the intended approach. Mrs. 
Boyle expressed the view that negotiations were ultimately cut short and that the Borough's 
decision on July 14, 2026, to sue the Authority came shortly after the Water Authority 
declined to vote on a draft lease provided with only two business days' notice. She further 
noted that the Water Authority subsequently approved a 10-year lease on August 12, 2026, 
and that the Borough nonetheless sent a letter on August 18, 2026, signed by all Council 
members, stating it would not deviate from its current course. 
 
Deborah Moore [Lower Alsace Township] – Ms. Moore provided an extensive review of the 
minutes from multiple Borough Council and Water Authority meetings. She raised concerns 
about a meeting between Mr. Ahrens and Pennsylvania American Water in the summer of 
2025. Mr. Ahrens clarified that he, along with Water Authority board member Mr. Worrall 
and staff member Mr. Hauck, attended a single meeting with Pennsylvania American Water, 
that he had notified Water Authority Vice Chair Mr. Boyle in advance, and that he stated in 
no uncertain terms at that meeting that the Borough had no interest in selling the water 
system. Mr. Ahrens noted that the Water Authority minutes' characterization of this 
meeting as one he arranged was inaccurate, and invited any resident to submit a Right-to-
Know request for all related communications. Ms. Moore questioned why, if the Borough's 
financial pressures are a primary driver of the transaction, ratepayers—67% of whom live 
outside Mt. Penn—should bear the cost of solving the Borough's budget challenges. 
 
Tom Watcke [Lower Alsace Township] – Mr. Watcke commented that he understood that 
Mount Penn was facing challenging financials, but commented that he wanted the Water 
Authority to be preserved and preferred to not contribute to the Borough’s budget. 
 
Dale Mengel [Exeter Township] – Mr. Mengel commented that the entire community should 
have a voice in the decisions and the Water Authority should be preserved. 
 
Troy Jarvis [Exeter Township] – Mr. Jarvis commented that the entire community should 
have a voice in the decisions and the Water Authority should be preserved and he did not 

Page 4 
support subsidizing Mount Penn Borough. He asked for clarification on discussions that 
occurred with PA American Water. 
 
Michael Fritz [St. Lawrence Borough] – Mr. Fritz a 35-year resident and longtime official of 
St. Lawrence Borough, stated that the funding figures presented by Mr. Ahrens for a 
potential tri-authority buy-in were beyond St. Lawrence's financial capacity, but expressed 
strong interest in further multilateral discussions. He suggested that a collaborative 
approach involving all affected municipalities could produce a resolution acceptable to all 
parties and urged Council to pursue that path before finalizing any dissolution. 
 
Tom Koch [Lower Alsace Township] – Mr. Koch agreed with Mr. Fritz’s comments and ask 
that parties enter discussions to resolve to issues amicably. 
 
Amanda Muso [Lower Alsace Township] – Ms. Muso suggested that Mount Penn Borough 
residents could consider supporting a sale in a public referendum and effectively decide for 
all the neighboring communities. She believed that maintaining the existing authority was 
the best solution for moving forward and believed that forming a joint authority was the 
best solution and that neighboring communities should not be charged a discounted cost to 
form that Authority. 
 
Multiple Members of the public raised concerns about the financial implications for 
ratepayers across all served municipalities, the planned dissolution of the existing Water 
Authority and creation of a successor authority, the adequacy of the no-sale covenant as a 
long-term protection mechanism, and the overall transparency and good faith of the 
negotiation process. Several speakers expressed support for the Borough's stated goal of 
protecting the water system from future privatization, some agreed that the Borough could 
benefit financially for that work while questioning whether dissolution was necessary to 
accomplish that goal. 
 
Council President Mr. Lombardo reminded those in attendance that Council takes public 
comment into consideration and that a public hearing is scheduled for September 2, 2026, 
at Antietam High School Auditorium, at which residents will have the opportunity to 
formally submit comments for the record before any final vote is taken. 
Discuss of September 2, 2026 Public Hearing 
Council noted that the September 2, 2026 public hearing will be conducted as a formal 
proceeding. Each speaker will be required to state their name and address. The format will 
not include back-and-forth questioning; it will be a one-way public comment session for the 
record. Mr. Klonis was identified as the hearing officer. Council President Mr. Lombardo 
indicated that special counsel will be present to address legal questions that Council cannot 
answer directly.

Reports 
Chief of Police 
Chief Johnson reported that following a prior meeting discussion regarding an individual 
who fell in the Dollar Tree parking lot due to a skateboarder or scooter rider, he followed up 
with the property owner the next day. The owner was cooperative and agreed to order and 
post signs prohibiting skateboards and scooters, which will enable enforcement once 
posted. 
 
Chief Johnson also confirmed his department's availability to provide a presence at the 
September 2 public hearing, given the potential for a large and engaged crowd. 
Codes Administrator 
The Code Administrator reported on activity from July through August 21, 2026. A total of 
170 cases and notices of violation were closed, with 30 remaining open. Four zoning permits 
were issued. Ongoing enforcement actions include high grass and weeds, overgrown trees 
and bushes, accumulation of trash, general property maintenance, and work performed 
without valid permits. The department has handled nearly 900 cases and notices of violation 
to date in Mt. Penn. The Code Administrator noted that the property at 1 Weimoor Court 
remains in violation of multiple requirements; an on-site inspection with SDE is scheduled 
for Thursday, and the Borough Solicitor is engaged to determine appropriate legal action, up 
to and including condemnation or vacation of the property. Work on the combined Mt. 
Penn and Lower Alsace zoning ordinance book is ongoing and expected to be completed 
shortly. 
 
A brief discussion arose regarding roof replacement permits. Mr. Ahrens and Mr. Kelly 
raised the question of whether the Borough should begin requiring permits for roof 
replacements, as surrounding municipalities do. Concerns were raised about the practicality 
of inspection if permits are issued without physical roof inspections. The matter was tabled 
for further discussion at the next meeting, with Mr. Ahrens directed to obtain Lower Alsace 
Township's roof permit ordinance for reference.

Projects Assistant, whose last day was the prior week, and shared a letter of appreciation 
from with Council. Mr. Ahrens also reported that he had presented at the Exeter Township
