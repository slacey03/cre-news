August 18, 2026 Business Meeting 
Meeting minutes 
The Mount Penn Borough Council met for the Monthly Business Meeting on August 18, 
2026, at Mount Penn Borough Hall, 200 N. 25th Street, Reading, PA 19606. Council President 
Rick Lombardo called the meeting to order at 6:00 PM and adjourned at 8:10 PM. 
 
Council President Rick Lombardo, Council Vice President Christine Dise, Council Member 
Thomas Baer, Council Member Kelly Dudash, Council Member Troy Goodman, Council 
Member Michael Kindlick, and Council Member David Korinchock were present. Mayor Ryan 
Maurer was absent. Staff and professionals present included Borough Manager Hunter L. 
Ahrens. Several members of the public were present including William McGuire, Cheryl Ann 
McGuire, Tom Crosier, Kim Crosier, Curt Hill, and several other members of the public. 
Meeting Opening 
The meeting was called to order at 7:00 PM. All present were asked to rise for the Pledge of 
Allegiance, which was led by Mr. Lombardo. 
Antietam Valley Municipal Authority (AVMA) Board Member Interviews 
Council conducted interviews for four candidates seeking appointment to the Antietam 
Valley Municipal Authority (AVMA) Board. Each candidate was asked to confirm eligibility 
requirements and respond to a standard set of eight questions covering their background, 
opposition to the sale of the sewer system, organizational change management, staff 
morale, board decision-making in the absence of a traditional daily manager, and handling 
of unauthorized board member directives to staff. 
 
Candidate 1 – Mr. Brian Sands: Mr. Sands confirmed eligibility, noting 52 years of residency 
in the area. He described extensive involvement with AVMA dating to 2000, including 
infiltration inspections and grease trap inspections for local restaurants. He holds a master 
plumber license and has been in the trade since 1959. Mr. Sands stated his opposition to 
any sale of the system and committed to voting against such a proposal. He emphasized 
communication and majority-based decision-making as his approach to organizational 
matters. 
 
Candidate 2 – Mr. Brian Horner: Mr. Horner1 confirmed eligibility and described nearly 38 
years in law enforcement, including service as a detective, police chief for Northern Berks 
Regional and Laureldale Borough, and current employment as a police officer with the 
Boyertown Area School District. He stated that, without hearing more information, he

Candidate 3 – Mr. Thomas Canonico: Mr. Canonico confirmed eligibility and described 25

Public Comment 
Tom Crosier, 231 Seidel Street – Mr. Crosier commented that he saw discussion about the 
Borough selling the Water system on Facebook. He asked if the Borough was planning to sell 
the Authority. Mr. Lombardo responded that the Borough was not considering selling and 
was working on the Conveyance-Leaseback to create prohibitions on a sale so that it could 
not be sold. Mr. Lombardo shared details about the upcoming expenses, especially the 
emergency radios, and use the lease revenue to support these costs. 
 
Mr. Crosier asked how to better share this information with the public. The Council 
responded that the information was shared at several public meetings and was featured on 
the water bill. 
 
Unidentified Resident – The resident asked how this would support the Borough with 
financial assistance. Mr. Lombardo emphasized that the proposed leaseback arrangement is 
not a sale and cited similar arrangements in other Berks County municipalities, including 
Hamburg, Kutztown, Leesport, Shillington, and Reading. Mr. Lombardo and Mr. Ahrens 
responded to questions from members of the public, providing a detailed explanation of the 
three-part plan under consideration: (1) the Borough exercising its statutory right to take 
back the water system from the authority and lease it back, generating a revenue stream 
estimated at $295,300 annually to offset emergency services costs, particularly police 
services, Mr. Ahrens acknowledged that rates would increase, estimating an average 
increase of approximately $7.00 per quarter for ratepayers.; (2) a 50-year no-sale covenant 

Page 3 
embedded in the lease agreement, supplemented by a Borough Code provision requiring a 
public referendum before any sale could occur; and (3) integration of authority staff into a 
Borough public works department to create long-term operational synergies. 
 
Unidentified Resident – The resident asked whether the Borough was in active litigation. Mr. 
Ahrens responded about active litigation with the Borough of St. Lawrence over a 4-acre 
parcel hosting a water tower, and a separate action seeking court direction for the authority 
to transfer the system to the Borough. 
 
Unidentified Resident – The resident asked whether the changes the Borough was seeking 
would jeopardize any active loans or grants that the Authority currently holds. Mr. Ahrens 
responded that it was possible these activities jeopardized the grants or loans. Mr. Ahrens 
commented on the status of Penn Vest loans, noting that the Borough is actively working to 
have those loans transferred rather than called, and that the subsidized interest rate of 
1.75% versus an estimated open-market rate of approximately 4% represents a significant 
cost difference the Borough is working to protect. He shared that the Borough was actively 
working to have the loans and existing grant transferred to the Borough. 
 
Cheryl Ann McGuire, 617 Byram Street – Ms. McGuire asked several questions, including the 
how the term of the lease would affect these issues. Mr. Ahrens responded that there were 
several factors at play with the no-sale covenant. He shared some of the challenges the 
Authority has shared about Act 47 and its ability to change contracts; he commented that 
the entire effort was meant to avoid that trajectory all together by securing additional 
revenue and creating the prohibitions on sale through the lease and Borough Code. Mr. 
Lombardo shared that financial struggle is also why the Borough is considering a merger 
with Lower Alsace and these protections against a sale would help prevent concerns about a 
potential sale, which he commented contributed to the defeat of the last merger vote. 
 
Cheryl Ann McGuire, 617 Byram Street – Ms. McGuire asked about how the rates would be 
set so that rates would not be dramatically increased after the first installment of the new 
costs under the lease. Mr. Ahrens explained the methodological standard that PFM 
recommends and current state regulations require to limit the increases to be sustainable 
for the Authority and supports an appropriate return on equity to the Borough. Mr. Ahrens 
commented that after considering the total defensible figure of $478K, the Borough 
believed that was not necessary and deferred to a beginning fee of $295.3K.

Discuss Shingle Replacement Permit Practice 
Mr. Ahrens reported that the historical practice has been to not require a permit for shingle-
only roof replacements, while any work involving sheeting or structural elements does 
require a permit. Staff research revealed an existing ordinance requiring permits for re-
shingling projects exceeding 25% of the roof area. The Code Administrator and Borough 
Plumbing Inspector were noted to favor enforcing the permit requirement at that threshold. 
 
Council discussed the practical and financial implications for residents, noting that a full UCC 
building permit would impose significant costs. The consensus direction was for the Borough 
Manager to develop a separate, simplified roof permit outside of the UCC framework, 
administered by codes staff, requiring contractors to provide proof of insurance. This 
approach was viewed as a means of protecting residents by ensuring licensed and insured 
contractors perform the work, without creating a burdensome fee structure. The Borough 
Manager was directed to prepare a draft ordinance accordingly. 
Discuss Budget Process Outline 
Mr. Ahrens reported that the budget process for the upcoming fiscal year would follow the 
same general timeline as the prior year. A budget projection through the summer would be 
distributed to Council by the first Friday in September. A Finance Committee meeting is 
being scheduled prior to the workshop meeting.  
 
Mr. Ahrens noted that the current budget being prepared covers Borough operations only 
and does not integrate the water authority financials, given the complexity and the pending 
status of the reorganization. He further noted that if a new authority is constituted, a 
separate budget consistent with the Municipal Authorities Act would likely need to be 
adopted by December 31st.
