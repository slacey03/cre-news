motorists may not yet be accustomed to its presence, potentially causing them to slow down or stop rather

F. 2027 Budget: Ms. Eckhart mentioned that the draft budget document was circulated to the Board and 
explained that she typically provides a memorandum regarding the Township’s fiscal standpoint, 
which continues to reflect a very positive and stable financial position. She noted the Township’s 
significant growth, reassessments, and high resale values throughout the community. Ms. Eckhart 
outlined several priorities for discussion, beginning with the reorganization of the Public Works crew 
and ensuring adequate staffing. While working with Mr. Jason Hertz and following the addition of the 
approved Maintenance Workers, she explained that crew development has remained the main focus. 
She noted that equipment requests were included within the departmental request and that Mr. Hertz 
was given time to assess the capital equipment fleet and make recommendations based on efficiency 
and future needs. The replacement of a loader was identified as a capital expenditure, while newer 
mowing equipment could improve the Township’s efficiency. Ms. Eckhart also discussed larger 
upcoming needs involving sewer maintenance and the sewer and stormwater systems, including 
potential grant opportunities for a vac-truck. At the request of the administrative office, she mentioned 
promoting at least one position in-house for the Zoning Officer role. She also suggested implementing 
a better public-facing recording system and redesigning the Township website to make it easier for 
residents to navigate and reduce staff time by making the site more intuitive. Regarding additional 
equipment, Ms. Eckhart noted that document archiving has been progressively completed and that 
acquiring a large-scale scanner would make it easier to process smaller batches in-house.  
 
Ms. Eckhart noted that the Fire Department submitted its own budget separately and requested that the 
Township continue setting aside funds for capital replacement schedules and supporting recruitment 
and retention programs. She indicated that although the Township does not provide cash contributions, 
it has typically provided a fuel stipend for the Northampton Regional EMS. Regarding Parks and 
Recreation, Ms. Eckhart referenced previous discussions concerning Howertown Park and the 
automated gates at the Savage Road entrance. She confirmed that the lighting project was completed,

while discussions regarding a batting cage, participant recreation and the possibility of a part-time 
seasonal park coordinator remain ongoing.  
 
For Planning, Zoning, and Code Enforcement, Ms. Eckhart identified property resale programs for 
reinspection, the landlord/tenant program, and the development of a program utilizing a third-party for 
periodic commercial life-safety inspections as items for further discussion. She also expressed interest 
in reviewing SALDO, sanitary sewer ordinance, and zoning ordinance performing standards to 
improve the existing language. Regarding sewer, water, and utilities, Ms. Eckhart identified 
redesigning the Willow Green Pump Station to create a gravity system as a main priority. She 
explained that the system was implemented with the stub constructed across West Bullshead Road and 
that the next step would involve obtaining the Township engineer’s recommendation on the proper 
proceedings. Further information will also be sought regarding the Township’s R537 Plan, including 
whether it needs to be amended and any requirements from a DEP permit standpoint, as well as 
intermunicipal agreements with the Borough of Northampton and Catasauqua due to flow 
considerations. Ms. Eckhart noted that the previously mentioned vac-truck would also fall under this 
category. As Mr. Hertz continues becoming familiar with the Township’s overall system, developing a 
routine maintenance program remains a priority, with the goal of making the Township self-sufficient 
in line flushing for the sanitary sewer and stormwater systems.  
 
Ms. Eckhart noted that while the municipal office is well up to date with information technology, she 
suggested replacing Public Works’ workbooks with laptops. She also suggested considering a backup 
internet connection through Starlink at several locations to provide flexibility if the Township’s 
primary system, such as Astound, experiences issues. She emphasized that internet connectivity should 
be as reliable as electricity due to the Township’s dependence on it. Ms. Eckhart further noted that 
although the Township maintains a cyber insurance policy, carriers recommend developing and 
implementing cybersecurity training for all staff members to help prevent a breach. She stated that 
additional properties will continue to be identified for potential long-term facility upgrade planning, 
particularly for Public Works and Parks.  
 
Under additional catch-all items, Ms. Eckhart identified litigation and contingency expenses. She 
noted the ongoing litigation involving 1438 Willowbrook Road and the Township’s continued efforts 
to obtain cooperation from DEP, explaining that the litigation remains an ongoing expense due to the 
experts hired. She also highlighted the annual contingency discussion, noting that the Township is 
located in a sinkhole-prone area and that the area south of Route 329 contains a significant amount of 
infrastructure. While there is nothing that can be done to prevent the situation, she emphasized that the 
Township must remain aware of the risk because repairs may need to be completed immediately and 
can result in significant costs.  
 
Ms. Eckhart sought recommendations from the Board regarding the overall budget and requested that 
any potentially missed items be discussed. Mr. Behler stated that he reviewed approximately $60,000 
in projected expenditure on revenue and considered the amount to be relatively low. He highlighted 
the Township’s positive fiscal position and noted that the Township does not project tax increases. Mr. 
Behler indicated that available funds should continually be used toward infrastructure, equipment, and 
personnel upgrades. Ms. Eckhart explained that no action was required at the present meeting and that 
additional comments, along with a motion to publish the budgetary draft, could be discussed at the 
following meeting. Mr. Behler inquired about holding an executive session regarding personnel due to 
the mention of a promotion. Ms. Eckhart noted that a spreadsheet had been circulated showing

Public Comment Policy and Procedures- 
 
 
1. A period for public comment will be held at the beginning of the meeting and at the conclusion of all agenda 
business items. Any public comments or questions shall be reserved until time on the agenda. Public comments 
and questions will not be permitted during the course of the Board’s/Commission’s business items. 
 
2. Individuals who speak must give their name, address and municipality prior to speaking. 
 
3. Time limit on length of public comment or presentation will be three (3) minutes per person, per meeting. 
 
4. A speaker will only be granted one three (3) minute extension, if given, for a maximum speaking time of six (6) 
minutes, at the discretion of the Chairman. 
 
5. Comments/questions shall be directed to the Board/Commission members only. 
 
6. Procedures will be in place to maintain proper decorum for the hearing. Public participation will be allowed 
but the Board will ensure respect for all citizens and maintain order. Personal attacks and outbursts will be ruled 
out of order. The Chairman of the Board or Commission may, within this discretion, rule out of order 
scandalous, impertinent, and redundant comment or any comment the discernible purpose of which is to disrupt 
or prevent the conduct of the business of the meeting. 
 
7. Individuals with lengthy written statements may submit them for the record and provide a verbal summary of 
three (3) minutes or less. Interested persons may email public comments via email, sent to 
manager@allentownship.org until 3:00pm local time the day of each meeting or by contacting the Township 
office. Public comment received via email will be read at the outset of the meeting. If you require an auxiliary 
aid, service or other accommodation, please contact the Allen Township offices in advance. In addition, an audio 
recording of the meeting will be posted to the Township website: www.allentownship.org within 48 hours of the 
meeting.
