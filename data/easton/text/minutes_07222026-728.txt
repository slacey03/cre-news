PRESENTATION TO COUNCIL: 
 
Brandon Sullivan and Nell Panella – Using Community & Bikes to Become Citizen Scientists 
(PowerPoint attached): The primary presentation focused on a new environmental monitoring 
project designed to identify "hidden heat and pollution islands" throughout Easton. 
Key points included: 
• A partnership between the City of Easton, City of Allentown, Penn State, and Community 
Bike Works.  
• Grant-funded air quality sensors are being carried on bicycles to collect hyper -local 
environmental data.  
• The project measures pollutants including particulate matter (PM1, PM2.5, PM10), carbon 
dioxide, nitrogen dioxide, VOCs, and ozone.  
• Officials explained that Easton currently lacks sufficient localized weather and air-quality 
monitoring data.  
• The information collected will help guide future decisions on:  
o Tree planting  
o Green infrastructure  
o Urban planning  
o Public health initiatives  
• Community Bike Works students shared personal experiences describing how poor air 
quality affects residents with asthma and emphasized the importance of the project.

PUBLIC COMMENT – On any matter 
 
Art Ravitz, Church St.: Mr. Ravitz raised concerns about the condition of a Peruvian restaurant 
and the City’s enforcement process for property code violations. He said the restaurant has 
experienced ongoing issues with its landlords and identified several outstanding repairs, 
including cracked glass, a large opening covered with plywood, and the need for a fire-resistant 
metal door. He stated that the repairs would be relatively inexpensive but have remained 
unresolved despite previous code violations and fines. 
 
He also expressed concern that landlords can receive extended periods to complete repairs, 
allowing unsafe conditions to continue. He suggested that the City consider requiring landlords 
to begin repairs within one month of a complaint and complete them within three months, while 
recognizing that any changes would need to comply with legal requirements. 
In response, City officials acknowledged the concern and explained that enforcement can be 
difficult when cases go before a magistrate, particularly when fines are minimal or violations are 
dismissed. They noted that the City’s code team is responsive but that its authority is sometimes 
limited by the legal process. 
 
The discussion emphasized the broader concern that unresolved property conditions can 
negatively affect small businesses, particularly in the West Ward, which the resident noted has 

lost approximately 10 businesses this year. Council discussed whether the ordinance could be 
changed to provide stronger or faster enforcement for commercial properties. 
 
David Brotzman 828 Philadelphia Rd.: Mr. Brotzman raised several concerns regarding the 
DNL Trail and nearby infrastructure. He reported encountering a vehicle driving on the trail near 
the Hope Road entrance on July 10 and noted that there are still no signs at that entrance 
identifying it as a bike trail. He also stated that concrete barriers that were moved during work on 
May 4 have not been replaced, creating a safety concern. 
 
He described significant deterioration of the trail between Hope Road and the public boat ramp, 
including a washout caused by a tree that occurred more than four years ago. He stated that a 
section intended to be approximately eight feet wide has been reduced to about three feet and 
that other areas near the boat ramps have significant damage. He offered to meet with city 
officials to show them the problem areas. The City Administrator indicated that he had 
previously inspected part of the trail and would review his prior report and follow up with Public 
Works regarding the outstanding concerns. 
 
Mr. Brotzman also raised concerns about the Abbott Street Bridge, which has a three-ton weight 
limit. He noted that a weight-limit sign is posted in only one direction and expressed concern that 
emergency vehicles could approach the bridge from the opposite direction without seeing the 
restriction. He recommended that signage be posted for traffic approaching from both directions. 
Finally, he reported that numerous streetlights in his neighborhood are malfunctioning, with 
some lights turning on briefly before going out and others remaining out entirely. City officials 
advised him to report the outages through the appropriate utility's online outage-reporting system 
and offered assistance if needed.
