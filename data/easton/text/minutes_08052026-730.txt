5. 329 Folk Street – Special Exception for A7 Multifamily Use 
Staff Presentation and Recommendation: The applicant, Atin Verma, proposes to convert an 
existing A12 Mixed Residential/Business into an A7 Multifamily use with four units. The 
proposed A7 Multifamily use is permitted by special exception in the South Side (SS) district. 
Staff presented the proposal and analysis as detailed in the staff report in the August 5, 
2026 Meeting Packet.  Staff recommended that the Planning Commission recommend that 
the Zoning Hearing Board grant the special exception with conditions. 
 
Applicant Presentation: A representative of the applicant initially addressed the Commission 
and distributed a project summary sheet on behalf of the property owner, who was delayed 
in traffic. After arriving, the applicant responded to questions from the Commission regarding 
the proposed building layout and use. The applicant explained that the existing property 
consists of a residential dwelling and a vacant commercial space that has remained difficult 
to lease for approximately five years. He stated that various attempts to attract commercial 
tenants had been unsuccessful due to zoning limitations and neighborhood concerns related 
to previous commercial uses.  
 
Commission Discussion: Commissioners discussed the requirement for an impact 
assessment and mitigation statement and whether this was a standard component of future 
land development review. Staff confirmed that such a report is routinely required during land 
development review and was specifically identified as a condition because it is noted in the 
special exception criteria outlined in the zoning ordinance.

Street Trees: Questions were raised regarding the lack of proposed street trees and the 
associated variance request. Staff explained that street trees are required but were not 
included on the submitted plans, necessitating a variance request. Staff also clarified that 
existing street tree wells were present on the Wilkes-Barre Street frontage and that the 
variance would be heard by the Zoning Hearing Board. The applicant indicated a willingness 
to plant street trees. Commissioners discussed how that commitment might affect the 
pending variance request and agreed that the information should be identified for 
consideration by the Zoning Hearing Board. The consideration of this new information by the 
Zoning Hearing Board was added as a condition to the Commission’s action. 
 
Apartment Layouts: Commissioners expressed concerns about the apartment layouts, 
specifically bedrooms shown on plans without clearly identified windows or secondary 
means of egress. Staff explained that the current review was limited to the requested use 
and that issues related to building code compliance, lighting, ventilation, and egress would 
be fully reviewed during land development and building permit review. Commissioners 
encouraged the applicant to address these concerns. In response to concerns regarding 
bedroom windows and egress, the applicant stated that the preliminary drawings did not 
include all proposed windows and that revisions could be made to address those concerns. 
He explained that the garage door would likely be removed and replaced with windows and 
residential entrances and that additional windows could be incorporated into the design 
where feasible. The applicant acknowledged the Commission’s concerns and indicated a 
willingness to modify the plans during future project review stages. 
 
Additional discussion focused on the absence of green space on the property, potential 
opportunities to replace portions of existing concrete areas with landscaping, the possibility 
of incorporating solar panels, and the applicant’s request for a variance related to dwelling 
unit size. The applicant explained that the existing building and property constraints limited 
alternatives and that the proposed unit sizes were only slightly below the ordinance 
requirement. The applicant also discussed the existing paved rear yard area, explaining that 
while he would be willing to consider adding green space, maintenance concerns and 
potential costs to future tenants would need to be considered. He stated that many 
prospective tenants are primarily seeking safe and affordable housing rather than outdoor 
recreational space. 
 
Public Comment: Robert McDonald, 60 North 3rd Street, addressed the Commission and 
encouraged consideration of solar readiness and renewable energy opportunities as part of 
future development projects. He also raised questions regarding the comparative cost of 
maintaining paved areas versus green space. 
 
Commission Action: Recommend to the Zoning Hearing Board that the special 
exception be granted for the A7 Multifamily use at 329 Folk Street based on the findings 
that the proposed use is generally consistent with § 595-07(C), considerations for 
special exception requests, § 595-40(C), criteria for special exceptions, and the City’s 
Comprehensive Plan, with the following conditions:

6. 260 West Milton Street – Special Exception for 6-Foot-High Fence in Side Yard 
Staff Presentation and Recommendation: The applicant, Raymond Gonzalez, on behalf of 
the property owner, Katherine Pfeifer, proposes to install a six-foot fence in the side yard of 
the property. Per § 595-14(F)(13)(f)[1] of the zoning ordinance, fences shall not exceed four 
feet in the side yard except by special exception. Staff presented the proposal and analysis 
as detailed in the staff report in the August 5, 2026 Meeting Packet.  Staff recommended 
that the Planning Commission recommend that the Zoning Hearing Board grant the special 
exception with conditions. 
 
Applicant Presentation: The property owner, Katherine Pfeiffer, stated that the proposed 
fence was being requested to improve privacy and safety along her property, which is 
located adjacent to the busy intersection of West Milton Street and Old Philadelphia Road. 
She explained that the area experiences significant pedestrian traffic and that individuals 
frequently cross through her property rather than using designated walkways.  
 
Commission Discussion: Commissioners discussed the challenging traffic conditions at the 
intersection of West Milton Street and Old Philadelphia Road and acknowledged the 
applicant’s concerns about traffic and pedestrian activity. Discussion also included the 
placement of the fence in relation to existing trees and the applicant’s desire to preserve 
mature landscaping on the property. Commissioners confirmed that the proposed fence 
would be constructed of wood rather than chain link fencing and noted that the design 
appeared appropriate given the property’s unusual shape and location. The Chair also noted 
that, if passed, proposed zoning ordinance amendments under consideration by the City 
would permit six-foot fences in similar circumstances without requiring a special exception. 
 
Public Comment: There was no public comment on this item.
