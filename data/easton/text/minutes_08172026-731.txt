1. 28 South Locust Street  
Description: The applicant, Michael Brett, on behalf of the Redevelopment Authority of 
Easton, requested variances from § 595-15(D) to permit a rear yard setback of 12 feet 10.5 
inches where 25 feet is required and from § 595-33(J)(3) to permit off-street parking spaces 
within the required four-foot side yard setback. The proposal is to construct a two-unit 
stacked duplex on an existing West Ward, Block Class C lot. Fifty adjacent property owners 
were notified, the hearing was advertised, and the property was posted.  
 
Applicant Presentation: Will Oetinger, solicitor for the Redevelopment Authority, described 
the property as a formerly blighted site acquired through eminent domain. The 
Redevelopment Authority previously consolidated and adjusted the irregular lots with 
adjoining Dutchtown Park property to create a more buildable parcel.  
 
Michael Brett, Deputy Director of the Redevelopment Authority, presented the approved lot 
configuration and proposed zoning plan. The lower dwelling will be an ADA-compliant, 
wheelchair-accessible aging-in-place unit, with an approximately 1,300-square-foot dwelling 
above.

The applicant explained that the site is constrained by steep slopes, Dutchtown Park to the 
rear, and limited on-street parking. The building was designed with a relatively narrow 
footprint to preserve frontage for three required off-street parking spaces. The parking 
spaces meet dimensional requirements but are located closer than four feet to the side lot 
lines. The rear setback also adjoins City property.  
 
The Board received the planning materials, memorandum, and August 3, 2022 Planning 
Commission resolution as Exhibit A-1.  
 
Public Comment: There was no public comment on this item. 
 
Board Action: Approved the requested variances. (Motion passed 4-0) 
 
 Motion: Ms. Panto 
 Second: Mr. Loebsack 
 Ayes:  Mr. Civitella, Mr. Loebsack, Ms. Panto, and Ms. Thomas 
 Nays:    None 
 
2. 260 West Milton Street 
Description: The applicant, Raymond Gonzalez, on behalf of property owner Katherine 
Pfeiffer, requested a special exception pursuant to § 595-14(F)(13)(f)[1] to install an 
approximately 50-foot-long, six-foot-high pressure-treated wood privacy fence with one gate 
in the side yard, where fences over four feet require special exception approval. Twenty-nine 
adjacent property owners were notified, the hearing was advertised, and the property was 
posted. The August 5, 2026 Planning Commission resolution recommending approval with 
conditions was read into the record. 
 
Applicant Presentation: Katherine Pfeiffer stated that the six-foot fence was requested 
because of traffic and safety conditions near the property and because her cattle dog could 
jump a four-foot fence. She explained that the fence was pushed back to preserve visibility 
around the corner and to respect the adjoining property line. The Board discussed the traffic 
conditions near West Milton Street and Philadelphia Avenue and the need for a six-foot 
fence rather than a four-foot fence.  
 
Public Comment: There was no public comment on this item.  
 
Board Action: Approved the requested special exception. (Motion passed 4-0) 
 
 Motion: Ms. Thomas 
 Second: Ms. Panto 
 Ayes:  Mr. Civitella, Mr. Loebsack, Ms. Panto, and Ms. Thomas 
 Nays:    None

3. 34 South 13th Street 
Description: The applicant, Gladys Rodriguez, requested variances from § 595-15(F)(6) to 
permit a 19.5-foot-wide driveway where 10 feet is permitted and from § 595-33(J)(3) for a 
side yard parking setback. The proposal is to remove deteriorated brick/paver surfaces and 
install a paved rear parking area for vehicles serving the property. Forty-seven adjacent 
property owners were notified, the hearing was advertised, and the property was posted.  
 
Applicant Presentation: Brian Rodriguez and Gladys Rodriguez explained that the rear yard 
is already substantially improved with concrete and pavers and that the proposed work 
would provide two usable off-street parking spaces, particularly when parking is restricted on 
13th Street during snow emergencies. Seven photographs depicting the alley, rear yard, 
existing paving, landscaping area, and neighboring rear yards were provided.  
 
The Board discussed the existing impervious area, the proposed garden area and the use of 
the spaces by the household. To address maintenance and drainage concerns, the applicant 
agreed on the record to revise the requested zero-foot side yard setback to a one-foot 
setback on each side. The Board also discussed limiting use of the spaces to residents of 
the property. 
 
Public Comment: David Kubich expressed concern that the property might become a multi-
unit residence and that additional vehicles could affect alley access, particularly during 
winter conditions. The applicant confirmed that the property would remain a single-family 
dwelling. Another speaker asked whether the work would affect surrounding properties and 
uses; the applicant stated that it would not.  
 
Board Action: Approved the requested variance with the condition that the parking 
spaces may only be used by residents of the property and may not be rented to others. 
(Motion passed 4-0) 
 
 Motion: Ms. Thomas 
 Second: Mr. Loebsack 
 Ayes:  Mr. Civitella, Mr. Loebsack, Ms. Panto, and Ms. Thomas 
 Nays:    None

5. 1100 Bushkill Drive  
Description: The applicant, Brandon Walker, on behalf of the development team for the City-
owned property, requested a special exception for an A12 Mixed Residential/Business use in 
the River Corridor and variances from § 595-17(D)(1) for building height, § 595-17(F)(3) for 
street trees, and § 595-17(F)(6) for 24-foot-wide driveways. The proposal is a mixed-use 
residential/commercial building as part of redevelopment of the former industrial property. 
Twenty-five adjacent property owners were notified, the hearing was advertised, and the 
property was posted. The July 1, 2026 Planning Commission conditional preliminary land 
development approval was read into the record. 
 
Applicant Presentation: Mark Mulligan and Gretchen Rice of VM Development Group and 
Brandon Walker of HDR Engineering presented the application. The development team 
stated that exterior facade revisions were still being considered but would not change the 
zoning relief requested. The proposed principal building is approximately 50 feet high, with 
an elevator and stair tower extending to approximately 60 feet to provide access to a limited 
roof deck.  
 
The applicant stated that the additional height allows approximately 10-foot residential 
ceiling heights and a roof amenity while maintaining a massing comparable to larger 
buildings in the Bushkill corridor. For street trees, the applicant cited overhead high-voltage 
lines, proposed on-street parking, nearby landscaping, and extensive additional tree planting 
required through environmental permitting. Two 24-foot driveways were requested to 
accommodate two-way traffic, emergency access, and a planned crosswalk connection 
between the two development parcels.

Staff and the applicant acknowledged that a small portion of the parcel extends into the 
FEMA-designated floodplain. The applicant agreed to relocate the stormwater outlet and 
associated facilities outside the 100-year floodplain. Mr. Walker stated that post-construction 
stormwater plans and calculations had been submitted through the Northampton County 
Conservation District as part of the NPDES permitting process and were also available 
through the Planning Department. He stated that the design is intended to reduce peak 
discharge and runoff volume and improve water quality compared with existing conditions. 
The applicant also explained that infiltration is constrained by the Act 2 remediation status of 
the site and that other measures, including planting and soil amendments, are proposed. 
 
Public Comment: Jennifer Tallarico asked whether the parking and impervious coverage 
shown on the current plan had changed from previously distributed plans; the applicant 
stated that the parking plan had not changed and that the project complies with impervious 
coverage requirements.  
 
Jim Toya, Executive Director of the Karl Stirner Arts Trail, expressed support for the 
development team and asked that future landscaping preserve adequate space for public art 
along the trail.  
 
Bruce McCutcheon, President of the Forks of the Delaware Chapter of Trout Unlimited, 
asked about the stormwater plan, permitting status, and protection of Bushkill Creek.  
 
Curt Ehly submitted excerpts of the zoning and floodplain regulations and argued that the 
requested variances did not satisfy the legal hardship standard.  
 
Cody Harding raised concerns regarding floodplain applicability, stormwater permitting, 
impervious coverage, and the necessity of the requested variances.  
 
Richard Sparshatt questioned the need for additional height to provide 10-foot ceilings and 
suggested a three-story building could comply more closely with the ordinance.  
 
Toni Mittman urged the Board to consider the character of the Bushkill corridor, open space, 
and alternative approaches to redevelopment.  
 
Caron Anderson raised concerns regarding building height, apartments adjacent to 
established neighborhoods, and potential traffic impacts on Bushkill Drive and surrounding 
streets. 
 
Board Action: Approved the special exception and requested variances. (Motion 
passed 4-0) 
 Motion: Mr. Civitella 
 Second: Ms. Panto 
 Ayes:  Mr. Civitella, Mr. Loebsack, Ms. Panto, and Ms. Thomas 
 Nays:    None

6. 1111-1113 Bushkill Drive  
Description: The applicant, Brandon Walker, on behalf of the development team for the City-
owned property, requested variances from § 595-22(D) to permit a built-to line of 
approximately 17 feet where five feet is required and from § 595-17(F)(3) regarding street 
tree requirements. The property is in the Adaptive Reuse zoning district, Block Class B. 
Twenty-five adjacent property owners were notified, the hearing was advertised, and the 
property was posted.  
 
Applicant Presentation: Mark Mulligan and the project team explained that the proposed 
building setback varies because Bushkill Drive curves, with approximately 15 to 20 feet 
between the curb and building. The applicant stated that strict compliance with a five-foot 
built-to line would create an uncomfortably narrow pedestrian zone next to on-street parking 
for a multi-story building. Sidewalks and landscaping are proposed within the wider setback. 
Regarding street trees, the applicant cited the same overhead utility, on-street parking, and 
landscaping considerations presented for the south parcel. Three street trees are proposed, 
with additional trees located near the street and existing mature vegetation along the rear 
escarpment preserved where feasible. 
 
Public Comment: There was no public comment on this item. 
 
Board Action: Approved the requested variances. (Motion passed 4-0) 
 
 Motion: Ms. Thomas 
 Second: Mr. Loebsack 
 Ayes:  Mr. Civitella, Mr. Loebsack, Ms. Panto, and Ms. Thomas 
 Nays:    None 
 
7. 36 South 12th Street  
Description: The applicant, Jose Portes, requested variances related to the existing 16-foot 
lot width where 18 feet is required, street trees, zero off-street parking spaces where two 
spaces are required, and existing impervious coverage of 68 percent where 50 percent is 
permitted. The proposal was to convert the existing single-family dwelling to two units. Sixty-
nine adjacent property owners were notified, the hearing was advertised, and the property 
was posted. 
 
The Chair disclosed that he lives two doors from the subject property and recused himself 
from the hearing and vote. Vice Chair Loebsack presided over the item. 
 
Applicant Presentation: Jose Portes appeared with Alexis Nunez, who assisted with 
translation. The applicant stated that he sought to create a second dwelling unit to help with 
mortgage expenses following a change in his personal financial circumstances. The property 
has no feasible area for off-street parking. The applicant indicated that he wanted to 
undertake any changes with the required permits.

The Board focused primarily on the parking variance and the requirement that a dimensional 
variance be supported by a hardship related to the property rather than a personal financial 
hardship. Members discussed whether a roommate arrangement could provide financial 
assistance without converting the property to two units and adding another dwelling unit 
without required off-street parking. 
 
Board members also acknowledged broader housing affordability and supply concerns while 
distinguishing those policy issues from the variance criteria before the Board. 
 
Public Comment: Julie Zando-Dennis described significant parking constraints in the West 
Ward, particularly south of Northampton Street, and discussed the cumulative effect of 
residential conversions on parking demand and neighborhood conditions.  
 
Curt Ehly discussed the history of residential conversions in the West Ward and urged the 
Board to consider their cumulative impacts. 
 
Board Action: Denied the requested variances. (Motion passed 3-0) 
 
 Motion: Mr. Loebsack 
 Second: Ms. Panto 
 Ayes:  Mr. Loebsack, Ms. Panto, and Ms. Thomas 
 Nays:    None 
 
The meeting was adjourned at 8:44 p.m.
