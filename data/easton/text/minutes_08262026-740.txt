PRESENTATION TO COUNCIL: 
 
Swearing in of Police Officers: Brandon Homoki and Biagio Colandrea: new officers 
Before the swearing in, Chief Scalzo welcomed Brandon Homoki and Bagio Colandrio as 
the newest members of the Easton Police Department. The Chief recognized their commitment to 
a career of courage, integrity, responsibility, and service to the community. He expressed 
gratitude to their families for their support and sacrifices and pledged to provide the officers with 
the training, equipment, and support needed for successful careers. He concluded by 
congratulating the officers and welcoming them to the department. 
Mr. Pintabone stated Brandon Homoki is the third Homoki to join the department. 
Mayor Panto then administered the oath of office to Officers Homoki and Colandrea.  
Before moving on to the promotion, Chief Scalzo recognized Brandon Homoki as a third-
generation Easton Police Officer, following in the footsteps of his father, Lieutenant Steve 
Homoki, and grandfather, Steve Homoki Sr., who retired in 2005 after 32 years of service. The 
mayor expressed appreciation to the Homoki family for their decades of dedication, sacrifice, 
and service to the Easton Police Department and welcomed Brandon into the department with 
pride. 
 
Swearing in of Scott Ednie promotion to Sergeant 
 Before his swearing in as Sergeant, Chief Scalzo recognized Officer Ednie’s 
accomplishments. Scott Ednie is being promoted to the rank of Sergeant, where he will serve as 
Officer in Charge of Squad D in the Patrol Division. He joined the Easton Police Department in 
2018 and has established himself as a dedicated, dependable, and professional officer. In 2021, 
he became a Special Response Team operator, demonstrating advanced tactical skills, sound 
judgment under pressure, and a commitment to specialized training. Throughout his career, 
Sergeant Ednie has earned the respect of his peers, supervisors, and the community through his 
reliability, strong work ethic, leadership, and steady presence during critical incidents. His 
dedication to law enforcement and the City of Easton has prepared him to take on greater 
responsibility as a sergeant overseeing Squad D. 
 Mayor Panto administered the oath of office to Officer Ednie as Sergeant. Council 
members took the opportunity to congratulate the new officers and Sergeant Ednie. 
 
 
 

Presentation by David Hopkins:TrailConnection and Pedestrian Improvements. 
 
Council received an update on two Bushkill Drive projects designed to provide pedestrian and 
recreational infrastructure supporting the Iron and Metal property development. 
• Pedestrian Bridge: Construction is underway and grant-funded. Crews encountered 
some foundation issues but are working through them. The fabricated bridge is currently 
in Minnesota, with installation anticipated in October.  
• Trail Connection: A portion of the proposed trail will likely need to wait until future 
development occurs on the property. The remaining section along Bushkill Drive is 
included in the construction contract on the council agenda for approval and could begin 
soon if approved.  
• Ideally, the bridge and trail projects would have been constructed together, but 
development delays and grant funding timelines required them to proceed separately. 
This may temporarily result in the bridge and trail not being fully connected.  
• Bridge foundation work is currently progressing, with piles installed on the north side of 
Bushkill Drive and additional foundation work planned on the opposite side of the creek.  
• The new bridge is expected to complement the existing pedestrian bridge leading to the 
Silk Mill.  
• The trail will extend along Bushkill Drive from the Deer Brothers property toward the 
dealership, with a proposed crosswalk equipped with rectangular rapid flashing beacons.  
• Because there is no public right-of-way along part of the dealership area, the current 
design requires pedestrians to cross Bushkill Drive, Dietrich Road, and then Bushkill 
Drive again. 
 
Ms. Zando-Dennis had a question/comment. It was described to her, the complicated process of 
putting the bridge in where the utilities have to be taken out, like utility poles. Mr. Hopkins 
replied.The bridge project has involved significant utility relocation and site preparation. Brush 
clearing has been completed, and the approximately 165-foot bridge will arrive in three sections. 
The sections will be assembled along Bushkill Drive before being lifted and placed into position. 
PPL relocated utility poles and wires from the bridge side to the north side to accommodate 
construction; once the bridge is installed, the poles and wires will be moved back to their original 
location. A possible gathering or ceremony may be held to mark the bridge installation.

Mr. Pintabone asked if the College Hill Fire Station roof replacement could be completed this 
year if approved. Mr. Hopkins said that was the goal.

Glenn Pap, 235 Park Drive W, Kintnersville, Pa.: Glenn Pap, a landlord in Easton, addressed 
Council regarding the Business Privilege Tax return and its treatment of utility charges. He 
questioned why the current tax-return instructions do not allow landlords to deduct utilities from 
taxable gross income. 
Pap cited Pennsylvania Act 511 of 1965, which prohibits local taxing authorities from taxing 
gross receipts from utility services provided by companies whose rates are regulated by the 
Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission (PUC). He noted that Easton adopted similar language in 
its City Code in 1971 to comply with state law. 
He then cited Pennsylvania Title 66, Section 1313, concerning the resale of public utility services. 
Based on that provision, Pap argued that when landlords purchase utility services and pass those 
costs on to residential tenants, the rates they charge are subject to PUC regulation. 
Pap said he had previously raised this issue when filing his Business Privilege Tax return and was 
repeatedly told that the exemption applies only to utility companies. He asked Council to clarify 
why the language referring to “any person” or “any company” in both state law and Easton’s 
code is being interpreted as applying only to utility companies, rather than to landlords who 
resell regulated utility services. 
Mr. Lysynecky stated our interpretation was that it did not apply to landlords, Mr. Pap stated “it 
clearly does”. Mr. Lysynecky asked City Solicitor Vargo to speak on the topic. There was a lengthy 
discussion on the topic. 
 

Colleen O’Neal, 138 S. 11th St.: asked for clarification about the city’s involvement in mediation 
concerning Wilson. It was clarified that the solicitor’s office, not the city itself, agreed to 
participate in mediation. The city had not discussed or voted on mediation. The solicitor 
explained that he was caught off guard by Wilson’s solicitor announcing the mediation because it 
occurred before the scheduled executive session and he had not had an opportunity to discuss it 
with Council. 
Ms. O’Neal expressed concern about negotiating with the developer, arguing that the developer 
had failed to perform adequate due diligence and had omitted important information, including 
existing wetlands and Spring Brook. They questioned why the city should negotiate with a 
developer they believe had previously withheld or disregarded such information. They also 
expressed concern that negotiations could undermine the authority of the Planning and Zoning 
process and set a precedent allowing developers to sue the city whenever they disagree with 
municipal decisions. 
The speaker then asked about Solicitor Nitchkey’s role. It was explained that Nitchkey represents 
the Easton Zoning Hearing Board regarding the appeal of the zoning decision, while outside 
counsel represents the Zoning Hearing Board in the mandamus action. Because multiple attorneys 
are counsel of record, they would all need to participate in related discussions. 
The speaker raised a potential conflict-of-interest concern, noting that Nitchkey also represents 
Wilson’s zoning interests while representing Easton’s Zoning Hearing Board. They requested that 
he remove himself from the matter and argued that it was inappropriate for him to provide counsel 
to Easton while also communicating with Wilson, which is suing the city. The solicitor responded 
that conflict-of-interest decisions are ultimately matters for the individual attorneys, but said he 
would pass the concerns along. 
Ms. O’Neal concluded by emphasizing that the public should be aware of these circumstances, 
stating that even if the arrangement is legally permissible, they believed it raised serious ethical 
concerns given the ongoing litigation involving Wilson.

Amy Boccadoro, 108 Ferry St.: former Carl Sterner Arts Trail board member and longtime 
volunteer spoke to Council about the proposed development of the Easton Iron & Metal site. 
Having served on the board for approximately 10 years, she explained that the Arts Trail had 

originally worked with the property owners to facilitate the purchase of the site with the intention 
of expanding the trail. 
Ms. Boccadoro said the Arts Trail viewed the property as an important opportunity to create 
additional green space, accommodate sculptures and programming, and connect the trail with the 
stream, cemetery, West Ward, Lafayette College, College Hill, and downtown. The Arts Trail also 
envisioned environmental and educational programming there, working with Lafayette and the 
Nurture Nature Center as part of broader environmental remediation efforts. 
Over time, the plans changed. An apartment building was introduced while some Arts Trail space 
was still expected to remain, but the Nurture Nature Center eventually left the project. She said the 
current proposal now contains no meaningful space for the Arts Trail, which they found confusing 
and disappointing given the property's original vision. 
Ms. Boccadoro expressed feeling “duped” and used as a former board member, particularly 
because previous plans and promises had suggested the Arts Trail would have a role in the site's 
redevelopment. She emphasized that office space should not be presented as an alternative because 
office space had also previously been promised to the Arts Trail at the Silk Mill. 
She urged Council to recognize the site's unique potential and reconsider the proposal—
particularly the south side of the property—to restore elements of the original vision for an 
expanded Arts Trail and connected green corridor. 
There was lengthy discussion with Ms. Boccadoro and council members.
