PRESENTATIONS
Mayor Panto presented Valerie Wheeler Davis:
Valerie Wheeler Davis, an Easton resident, was recognized for her accomplishments as a star 
athlete at Easton High School, her coaching career with the Lehigh Valley Legends, and her 
work at Lafayette College. She shared the story behind her artwork, Full Circle, created as a 
tribute to President Barack Obama upon leaving office.

Davis explained that she initially presented the artwork to Mayor Panto, who later arranged for 
her to present it to Senator Bob Casey. Senator Casey was deeply moved by the piece and 
believed President Obama would appreciate it. Eight months later, Davis was invited to the 
White House to present the artwork to President Obama. The piece was subsequently taken by 
President Obama and is now housed in the Barack Obama Presidential Library archives.
Ms. Wheeler Davis presented the Mayor with a drawing of her artwork “Full Circle”. Mayor 
Panto presented Ms. Wheeler Davis with a plaque “City of Easton Recognition Award presented 
to Valerie Davis for her exceptional artistic talent and the induction of her artwork, Full Circle 
into the Barack Obama Presidential Library”

by Mrs. Hartranft-Bittinger by the following vote:
Before the vote was taken, Mrs. Rose has two pieces. First to abstain for the Center Square 
proposal due to work conflicts, they are funding the project. Second for the 185 S 3rd St would 
like to pull this and have developer come back. Council members discussed the South Third 
Street request and expressed concerns regarding the unfinished appearance of the pedestrian 
bridge connecting the Confluence project to the parking garage. Members noted that the 
completed bridge does not match the originally approved design and appears inconsistent with 

the overall quality of the development. Several members felt the developer should be required to
complete the work according to the approved plans.
The discussion included whether the matter should be remanded to the Historic District 
Commission (HDC) for reconsideration. While some members supported sending it back to 
HDC, others cautioned against Council overruling or challenging decisions made by HDC, 
Planning, and Zoning, emphasizing the importance of respecting those boards’ authority and 
avoiding setting a precedent.
Members also expressed concern that the changes occurred after residents had moved into the 
Confluence, suggesting that the developer sought approval after the work was already completed.
The discussion emphasized the need for developers to follow approved designs and maintain 
accountability for future projects.
Yeas: Mr. Brown, Ms. Zando-Dennis, Mr. Pintabone, Mrs. Rose, Mr. Graziano, Mrs. Hartranft-
Bittinger, Mayor Panto (7)

PUBLIC COMMENT – On any matter
1-Several residents addressed City Council regarding the proposed recognition of Claren 
Boyer and Francis Kitchen. Valerie Boyer expressed disappointment over opposition to 
the project, stating that her late husband Marvin had devoted significant time and effort to
it. She alleged that the opposition was motivated by family disputes and urged Council to 
move forward with recognizing the women. Lillian June Robinson spoke about Claren 
Boyer’s community involvement and emphasized the importance of accurately 
preserving local history. Marie Boyer  also urged Council to establish a date for the 
recognition, suggesting November 7.
2-Artie Ravitz raised concerns about residents struggling to afford rent and utility bills, 
citing an electric bill he found among discarded belongings. He encouraged Council to 
plan for increasing housing and utility affordability challenges. Council offered to 
provide information about available assistance programs.

3-Craig Robanowitz requested that motorcycles be permitted in the Third Street parking 
garage, noting their fuel efficiency. Council explained that current gate technology and 
license plate recognition systems create safety and operational issues but agreed to have 
the Public Works Director review possible accommodations and report back.
Robanowitz also expressed concerns about traffic congestion following the removal of 
the northbound right-turn lane from Larry Holmes Drive onto the Free Bridge. He 
reported increased delays and requested reinstatement of the turn lane, citing the City’s 
Safe Streets for All Study. Council explained that the changes were intended to improve 
pedestrian safety and accommodate proposed bump-outs. Members noted that traffic 
signal timing is being monitored and adjusted, and that pedestrian safety remains a 
priority. Council indicated it would continue evaluating the intersection as the changes 
are assessed.
Before Council adjourned, Council woman Zando-Dennis wanted to discuss a few concerns that 
the public may be unaware of the in-depth discussions held during Tuesday committee meetings,
resulting in confusion when Council votes on Wednesday. Members explained that committee 
meetings provide time to review proposals, ask questions, and gather information before voting. 
They agreed that the public should be better informed about committee discussions and that 
Wednesday meetings should include a brief overview of items previously discussed. Members 
also suggested that informational presentations, such as the Friends of Easton lighting project, be
scheduled during regular Council meetings to increase public awareness and participation.
Council also discussed involving the Environmental Advisory Council (EAC) in projects with 
potential environmental impacts. Members expressed support for seeking EAC input on issues 
such as lighting, light pollution, migratory birds, sustainability, and tree preservation. The EAC 
was recognized for its valuable advisory role and previous contributions to city projects, 
including LED lighting improvements and Lafayette Steps planning.
