7. Stewart Street Residential – Subdivision and Land Development  
Staff Presentation and Recommendation: Staff presented a report and recommendation on 
a proposed preapplication/final subdivision and land development plan in the South Side 
(SS) zoning district on Pastor Fred Davis Street (known as “Stewart Street Residential”).  
The applicant, Manshi Patel, on behalf of the property owner, Scott Kindred, proposes to 
subdivide the existing lot into eight lots. Townhomes (A3 Single-family attached) are 
proposed on seven of the lots. Each residential lot will have a driveway with an off-street 
parking space in a one-car garage. Staff recommended approval of the plans with 
conditions.  
 
Commission Discussion: Commissioners expressed displeasure with granting of variances 
from the Zoning Hearing Board in advance of Planning Commission review of land 
development applications. Commissioners reasoned that review of land development 
applications is the purview of the Planning Commission and that the Zoning Hearing Board 
granting a variance in advance of Planning Commission review limits the ability to discuss 
the full scope of an application, is out of order, violates the spirit of the Municipal Planning 
Code, and does not allow proper due process for said applications. Staff agreed to require 
land development applications that are seeking variances to seek pre-application approval 
from the Planning Commission first. This will allow Planning Commission the full opportunity 
to review the land development application as well as comment on any and all prospective 
variance applications so that the Zoning Hearing Boad can view the application in its 
entirety. 
 
Commissioners also discussed building width and bedroom sizes, building massing and 
grade stepping, rear yard space, retaining walls, drainage swales, stormwater maintenance, 
emergency access, fire protection, water main extension, street trees, and sidewalk 
connectivity. 
 
Site Design, Grading, and Building Placement: Commissioners asked the applicant to 
explain how the proposed townhomes would respond to the site's steep topography. The 
applicant explained that the buildings would be stepped with the grade of Pastor Fred Davis 
Street, with the individual finished-floor elevations changing as the development moves up 
the slope. The applicant stated that this approach was intended to minimize disturbance of 
the steep slopes while accommodating the proposed units. Commissioners questioned the 
resulting relationship between the buildings, rear yards, and hillside, including whether the 
areas shown as rear yards would provide meaningful usable outdoor space. 
 
The applicant explained that the lots extend farther behind the buildings than may initially 
appear on the plan and that open space would remain behind the units. The rear grading 
includes relatively shallow drainage swales intended to intercept runoff coming down the 
hillside and direct it around the buildings. Commissioners questioned whether those swales 
would substantially reduce the usable rear-yard area and whether homeowners would have 
practical access to the rear portions of their lots for ordinary maintenance or other purposes.

Retaining Walls and Steep-Slope Disturbance: Commissioners discussed the retaining walls 
proposed for Lots 6 and 7, with the applicant clarifying that the walls would be approximately 
10–12 feet high and intended to minimize disturbance to the steep slopes. Commissioners 
asked about fencing and fall-protection requirements; staff explained that the zoning fence 
provisions did not apply in the same manner because the walls function as part of the site-
grading and stormwater system, while the applicant characterized the rear wall as a “cut 
wall” holding the hillside rather than creating a drop-off along an accessible route. 
 
Rear Yards and Maintenance Access: Commissioners questioned how homeowners would 
reach and maintain the areas behind the units, particularly given the swales, steep slopes, 
retaining walls, and the fact that the lots would be individually owned.  
 
The discussion included the practical issue of future homeowners needing access to shared 
or common infrastructure for maintenance, including stormwater facilities and potentially 
fallen trees or other obstructions. The applicant explained that blanket or shared easements 
are commonly used in this type of development to provide necessary maintenance access 
across individual lots. Commissioners emphasized the importance of ensuring that the final 
development documents clearly establish who is responsible for maintaining the shared 
features and how future property owners will have access to them. 
 
Stormwater Management: Commissioners discussed runoff and the proposed drainage 
swales, which the applicant explained would intercept hillside water and direct it around the 
buildings, with erosion-control matting and seeding. Staff noted that the swales would be 
privately owned and maintained, with requirements for regular maintenance and City 
Engineer approval before any alteration. Commissioners expressed concern that future 
homeowners might not be aware of these obligations through a plan note alone, and the 
applicant's attorney agreed to record a stormwater covenant. The Commission included the 
recorded covenant as an additional condition of approval. 
 
Fire and Emergency Access: Commissioners expressed significant concern about 
emergency access to the rear of the attached townhomes. The Commission therefore 
requested that the Fire Department specifically review the proposed rear access. Staff 
agreed to obtain that review. Commissioners clarified that the condition should require not 
merely that the Fire Department review the plan, but that all comments resulting from that 
review be addressed. The applicant additionally confirmed that the townhomes would be 
equipped with fire sprinklers. Questions concerning fire separation between attached units 
were identified as matters to be addressed through building-code and permitting review.

Utilities and Required Agreements: Commissioners questioned staff's statement that the 
project would utilize existing public infrastructure because the Water Authority had identified 
the need for a water-main extension. The applicant explained that the water main would be 
extended from the Cooper Street area, rather than from the Canal Street side of the 
property, and estimated the extension at approximately 500 feet. The applicant stated that 
he had already discussed the proposed extension with the Water Authority. 
 
Street Trees and Landscape Plan: Commissioners asked whether street trees were actually 
included in the proposed development and whether the selected species would be 
appropriate for the site. The applicant explained that the City Forester had recommended 
replacing the proposed red maple with serviceberry because of concerns about tree roots 
eventually affecting or upheaving the adjacent sidewalk. The applicant agreed to make the 
change. The Commission's discussion also recognized that the landscape plan remained 
subject to final City Forester review. Staff's conditions required a final landscape plan 
compliant with the City's subdivision and land development requirements, review by the City 
Forester, resolution of all Forester comments, and a corresponding landscape cost estimate 
and escrow. 
Sidewalks and Lot 8: Commissioners had an extended discussion regarding LVPC's 
recommendation that sidewalks be installed along the entire frontage of the development. 
Staff explained that the submitted plans provided for frontage sidewalks but that the 
applicant had requested a deferral of the sidewalk along Lot 8 because that lot would remain 
vacant. The applicant explained that Lot 8 was substantially different from the developed lots 
because it would have no residence or regular occupant responsible for maintaining and 
shoveling the sidewalk. The applicant also pointed out that the nearest existing sidewalk 
connection to the north was approximately 500 feet away and that there was currently no 
sidewalk connection beyond Lot 8. The applicant therefore argued that installing a sidewalk 
along a vacant lot would create a facility with no immediate connection or clear maintenance 
responsibility. 
 
Commissioners acknowledged the maintenance concern but considered the issue from a 
longer-term connectivity perspective. Discussion focused on the fact that Lot 8 could remain 
undeveloped for many years, while the sidewalk would provide a continuous improvement 
along the property's frontage and could become important if the adjacent property or Lot 8 
were developed in the future. The Commission also considered LVPC's conclusion that a 
continuous sidewalk would promote safe and secure community design and facilitate a 
future connection to the broader sidewalk network. 
 
After further discussion, the applicant agreed that the sidewalk could be installed through 
Lot 8. The Commission therefore declined to grant the requested deferral and made 
construction of the full frontage sidewalk, including through Lot 8, an additional condition of 
approval.

8. Proposed Zoning and Subdivision and Land Development (SALDO) Amendments  
Staff Presentation: Staff presented a draft package of proposed zoning and SALDO 
amendments that had been under development since 2024. Staff explained that the 
amendments were organized around five general categories: administrative and cleanup 
amendments; amendments addressing contemporary land use and development issues; 
new or supplemental use standards; regulatory gaps identified through development review; 
and codification of existing development patterns. 
 
Staff emphasized that zoning and land development ordinances are intended to be living 
documents and that future amendments could address additional issues or fine-tune the 
proposed changes. Staff requested Commission feedback before transmitting the draft 
amendments to the Lehigh Valley Planning Commission (LVPC) for review. 
 
Commission Discussion: The Planning Commission discussed the proposed amendments.  
 
Data Centers: Commissioners suggested that the data center provisions consider an 
energy-offset requirement and/or a narrative evaluation of alternative energy and mitigation 
measures. Commissioners also discussed noise evaluation and whether the proposed 
standards should account for ambient conditions and adjacent neighborhoods. 
 
Warehouse Uses: Commissioners discussed proposed warehouse regulations, including 
truck parking and idling, truck access, electrical infrastructure for truck parking, impervious 
coverage, alternative energy considerations, emergency access and fire-lane review, and 
noise impacts on adjacent neighborhoods. Staff indicated that the draft provisions could be 
refined based on the Commission's comments and that a different or more precise definition 
of warehouse uses could be considered. 
 
Transportation Impact Study: Staff explained that the proposed transportation impact study 
provisions originated with the Engineering Department to provide a more specific framework 
for evaluating transportation impacts. Commissioners suggested that traffic studies account 
for seasonality and avoid selective use of traffic data. 
 
Downtown Building Heights: Commissioners discussed proposed downtown building-height 
changes and the relationship between the downtown base district and the street corridor 
overlay. Staff clarified that the street corridor overlay provides additional height allowances 
and that those allowances are part of the existing zoning framework. Commissioners noted 
that community input may be useful in considering appropriate downtown building heights, 
particularly in the context of the Comprehensive Plan update. 
 
Next Steps: Staff proposed incorporating the Commission's comments regarding data center 
and warehouse regulations into the draft materials for LVPC review and returning to the 
Commission with an update on how the comments were incorporated. The Commission 
indicated support for that process. Staff also noted that amendments that do not
