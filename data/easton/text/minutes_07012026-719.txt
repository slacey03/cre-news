5. Bushkill Drive – Subdivision/Lot Consolidation  
Staff Presentation and Recommendation: The applicant, City of Easton, has proposed 
subdividing one parcel (L9NE 23 2) into two parcels located along Bushkill Dr. The 
applicant is then consolidating the new 48,167sf parcel with parcel L9 23 3, which is also 
owned by the City. The lot size for the consolidated parcel will go from 174,027sf to 
222,194sf. The address for this newly consolidated parcel will be 1100 Bushkill Drive. 
The purpose of this application is to accommodate the proposed Foundry project. Staff 
recommended conditional final approval of the lot consolidation plan. 
 
Commission Discussion: Planning Commission members asked questions to clarify 
parcel labels, old railroad easements, and future trail development along Bushkill Drive. 
Future trail development will be discussed at a later date and is not part of this 
subdivision/lot consolidation.  
 
Public Comment: Paula Keyak, 1165 Bushkill Drive, addressed the Commission with 
questions relating to future construction impacts. She was advised those concerns would 
be more appropriately addressed during review of the land development applications.  
 
Commission Action: Grant conditional final approval of the submitted plan titled, “KSAT 
Lot Consolidation” provided the applicant adheres to the following conditions: 
1) The plan shall comply with the Bureau of Planning staff analysis memorandum dated 
June 15, 2026.  
2) All additional comments and concerns from the City Engineer, Zoning Administrator, 
and Codes Department shall be met.  
3) The plan shall not be recorded with the County of Northampton, Pennsylvania until 
all plan titles and certificates are endorsed by authorized signatories. (Motion passed

6. 1100 Bushkill Drive – Land Development for a Mixed-Use Building 
Staff Presentation and Recommendation: The applicant, Foundry Development Group, 
LLC, has submitted land development plans proposing the construction of a four-story 
mixed-use building on a vacant parcel located at 1100 Bushkill Drive. There will be 100 
dwelling units, which will consist of 21 studios, 66 one-bedrooms and 13 two-bedrooms. 
The first floor will have a 1,165sf retail/office area. There will also be 117 off-street 
parking spaces onsite. This site was formerly the Easton Iron Metal Recycling 
Facility/Scrapyard and is currently vacant. The property is located in the River Corridor 
Zoning District Block Class A, where the proposed A12 – Mixed-Use is permitted by 
Special Exception per Article §595-17(C).

The City of Easton owns this parcel and the Foundry Development Group, LLC, will 
purchase this property once they receive their approvals. Staff recommended: 
conditional preliminary plan approval with conditions and Planning Commission’s 
recommendation to the Zoning Hearing Board for granting the Special Exception 
request.  
 
Applicant Presentation: The applicant presented the project. Key points included: 
• The site has been under development planning since 2021. 
• Environmental remediation is underway utilizing EPA grant funding. 
• Development will occur in two phases. 
• The south parcel will be developed first. 
• A portion of the property will later be conveyed back to the City for construction of an 
extension of the Karl Stirner Arts Trail. 
• Building materials will include stone and fiber-cement products rather than vinyl 
siding. 
• Roadway improvements along Bushkill Drive include widening, street parking, 
landscaping, and traffic-calming measures. 
• The retail space is being discussed as a potential future headquarters/community 
space for the Karl Stirner Arts Trail. 
 
Commission Discussion: Commission members engaged in an extensive discussion of the 
proposed development, focusing on site design, transportation, environmental conditions, 
zoning compliance, and project phasing. 
 
Roadway Improvements and Traffic: The Commission discussed proposed improvements to 
Bushkill Drive, including roadway widening and the addition of on-street parking along the 
project frontage. Applicants explained that on-street parking has been incorporated both to 
provide additional parking and to serve as a traffic-calming measure. Commissioners noted 
ongoing concerns regarding vehicle speeds along Bushkill Drive and asked whether turn 
lanes or other traffic-control features were proposed. The applicant stated that a traffic study 
completed for the project did not identify the need for dedicated turning lanes and that the 
City's review concluded the projected traffic increase could be accommodated by the 
existing roadway network. 
 
Commissioners also sought clarification regarding the extent of roadway widening and 
whether improvements would continue beyond the project frontage. The applicant explained 
that widening would occur only along the project parcels and would accommodate parking 
on both sides of the roadway. 
 
Environmental Remediation: Considerable discussion centered on the environmental 
remediation of the former Easton Iron & Metal site. Commissioners requested clarification 
regarding the status of the City's ongoing EPA-funded cleanup efforts and how those 
activities would coordinate with construction.

The applicant explained that environmental remediation is being performed under EPA grant 
funding and oversight, with HDR managing remediation activities. While certain 
contaminated "hot spots" are being excavated and removed by the City, the final 
remediation strategy relies on the Act 2 process, in which portions of the site will be capped 
through construction of buildings, paved surfaces, and landscaped areas. The applicant 
stated that site improvements associated with development will constitute the final phase of 
environmental remediation and that development cannot proceed until the required 
environmental approvals are obtained. 
 
Stormwater Management: The Commission reviewed proposed stormwater management 
facilities and asked how stormwater infrastructure would function given the phased nature of 
development. The project engineer explained that runoff from both development phases will 
ultimately be directed through a shared stormwater treatment system, including 
underground infrastructure and a hydrodynamic separator designed to remove oil, sediment, 
and debris before discharge. 
Commissioners expressed interest in understanding how the south parcel could function 
independently during construction of Phase I. The engineer stated that the necessary water-
quality infrastructure would be installed as part of the initial phase and designed to 
accommodate future development of the north parcel. 
 
Parking and Site Design: Questions were raised regarding the project's parking calculations. 
Commissioners noted a discrepancy between parking counts discussed during the 
presentation and counts shown on plans. The applicant clarified that the development would 
provide approximately 139 parking spaces when on-street parking spaces are included, 
exceeding ordinance requirements. The applicant noted that the additional parking was 
intended to accommodate both residents and anticipated users of the Karl Stirner Arts Trail. 
 
Commissioners also reviewed comments previously provided by the Lehigh Valley Planning 
Commission (LVPC) concerning parking-lot landscaping and parking-area design. Questions 
were raised regarding tree islands, landscape buffers, and the breakup of long rows of 
parking spaces. The applicant advised that revised plans had been submitted incorporating 
many of the recommended landscape improvements and that some comments had not yet 
been reflected in staff reports because the revisions had not been fully reviewed. 
 
Zoning Variances and Plan Revisions: Members expressed concern that the staff report 
identified several requested variances that appeared to have been addressed on revised 
plans. Mr. Tillman confirmed that revisions submitted after the initial review added required 
lighting, buffering, and landscaping features. As a result, several variances originally 
identified may no longer be necessary and would be withdrawn or addressed during the 
Zoning Hearing Board process. 
 
The Commission discussed whether conditions should be added to ensure that unresolved 
variance issues and outstanding review comments were fully addressed before final 
approval. Staff agreed that such conditions could be incorporated into the approval.

Building Height, Design, and Neighborhood Context: The Commission discussed the 
proposed building height and its relationship to surrounding development. Members noted 
that the building would exceed the River Corridor District's 40-foot height limitation and 
would require zoning relief for the proposed approximately 54-foot height. Discussion 
focused on the building's relationship to nearby townhomes, the Silk Mill development, and 
the surrounding landscape. 
 
The applicant explained that the building's positioning and massing were influenced by site 
conditions and zoning requirements, including the ordinance requirement that buildings be 
placed closer to the roadway. The applicant further stated that architectural materials would 
consist primarily of stone and fiber-cement products in muted earth-tone colors intended to 
complement the surrounding cliff face and natural environment. 
 
Several commissioners also asked whether solar energy systems were being considered. 
The applicant stated that rooftop solar installation was not currently planned but that the 
buildings would be designed with the necessary infrastructure and conduit systems to allow 
solar panels to be installed in the future. 
 
LVPC and Staff Review Comments: The Commission discussed comments from both City 
staff and the Lehigh Valley Planning Commission. Members noted that several 
recommendations contained in the LVPC review letter appeared not to be reflected in the 
materials provided to the Planning Commission. The applicant responded that a point-by-
point response letter had been prepared and submitted describing how the plans were 
revised to address comments. 
 
Commission members requested that these responses be provided clearly with future 
submissions so that commissioners could more easily verify which comments had been 
addressed and which remained outstanding prior to final land development review. 
 
Public Comment:  
Gary and Paula Keyak, 1165 Bushkill Drive, expressed concerns and questions regarding 
construction traffic, privacy impacts, lighting, noise, traffic safety, and relationship of 
proposed structures to their property. Applicant representatives explained buffering, 
landscaping, parking layout, and traffic-calming measures included in the design. 
 
Yasmeen Haddad, 1133 Spring Garden Street, asked about pedestrian improvements and 
expressed concerns regarding modern architectural design compared to Easton's historic 
character. 
 
Quentin Mayfield, area school bus driver and former Easton resident, asked about traffic-
calming measures and roadway improvements.

7. 1111-13 Bushkill Drive – Land Development for a Mixed-Use Building  
Staff Presentation and Recommendation: The applicant, Foundry Development Group, 
LLC, has submitted land development plans proposing the construction of a seven-story 
mixed-use building on a vacant parcel located at 1111-13 Bushkill Drive. There will be 
159 dwelling units, which will consist of 32 studios, 99 one-bedrooms and 28 two-
bedrooms. The first floor will have a 1,380sf retail/office area. There will also be 198 off-
street parking spaces onsite, and 77 of these spaces will be located underneath the 
building on the first level. This site was formerly the Easton Iron Metal Recycling Facility 
and is currently vacant. The property is located in the Adaptive Reuse Zoning District 
Block Class A, where the proposed A12 – Mixed-Use is permitted per Article §595-22(B). 
The City of Easton owns this parcel and the Foundry Development Group, LLC, will 
purchase this property once they receive their approvals. Staff recommends conditional 
preliminary plan approval.  
 
Applicant Presentation: A parking podium design beneath the residential structure: 
• Revised plans addressing prior staff comments. 
• Similar architecture and materials as the south parcel. 
• Additional landscaping, buffering, lighting, and streetscape improvements.

Commission Discussion: 
Building Height and Massing: Commissioners discussed the scale of the proposed 
seven-story building, noting the contrast between the north building and the shorter 
south building. Questions were raised about whether alternative designs had been 
considered to reduce the height difference between the two structures. The applicant 
explained that multiple layouts were evaluated, including concepts with additional 
buildings and podiums on both parcels, but the proposed design was determined to be 
the most financially feasible while achieving the desired unit count. Staff confirmed that 
the building complies with height requirements in the Adaptive Reuse Zoning District and 
does not require a height variance. 
 
Relationship to Surrounding Development and the Silk Mill Complex: The Commission 
discussed how the building relates to surrounding development, including nearby 
residential properties and the Silk Mill complex. Members questioned whether the 
appropriate benchmark for neighborhood compatibility was the smaller residential 
buildings along Bushkill Drive or the larger former industrial buildings in the area. The 
applicant stated that the design reflects the site's industrial history and natural setting, 
utilizing earth-tone materials intended to complement the surrounding environment 
rather than replicate historic architecture. 
 
Proximity to the Existing Rock Face/Cliff: Commissioners sought clarification regarding 
the rock face and wooded hillside behind the proposed building. The applicant explained 
that the building was intentionally located closer to the hillside to maximize parking and 
developable area elsewhere on the site. Questions were raised regarding the distance 
between the building and the cliff face, and the applicant noted that the separation varies 
across the site and that the hillside remains a prominent natural feature that influenced 
the building's placement and design. 
 
Solar-Ready Infrastructure: Following discussion during review of the south parcel, 
commissioners asked whether the north building would also accommodate future solar 
installations. The applicant confirmed that the building would include the necessary 
conduit and infrastructure to support solar panels in the future, although solar panels are 
not proposed at this time. Any rooftop amenity areas would occupy only a portion of the 
roof and would not preclude future solar opportunities. 
 
Stormwater Management: The Commission discussed stormwater management and 
drainage, particularly in response to concerns raised by neighboring property owners 
regarding existing runoff conditions along Bushkill Drive. The applicant explained that 
runoff from the north parcel will be captured through an integrated stormwater system 
serving the entire redevelopment. Site grading, curbing, drainage facilities, and 
underground infrastructure will direct runoff to treatment facilities designed to improve 
water quality before discharge. The engineer confirmed that stormwater from the north 
parcel will ultimately connect to management facilities constructed as part of the south 
parcel development.

Landscape Preservation and Replacement: Commissioners discussed the project's 
impact on existing trees and vegetation. The applicant acknowledged that some mature 
trees will be removed during construction but stated that replacement landscaping will be 
provided in accordance with City requirements. The proposed landscape plan includes 
street trees, parking lot landscaping, buffers, and other planted areas intended to soften 
the appearance of the building and parking areas. The applicant noted that revised plans 
addressed many of the landscaping concerns previously identified by staff and the 
Lehigh Valley Planning Commission.
