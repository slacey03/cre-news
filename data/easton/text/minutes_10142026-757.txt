1.) Artie Rabbit (144 Church Street, Easton):
  * Inquired about the timeline for new parking meters along Larry Holmes Drive. Mayor
     PANTO clarified that physical meters are not being installed; the 12 new spots near
     The Free Bridge will utilize a pay-by-app system only.
  * Requested clarification on the upcoming Zoning Hearing Board meeting date. Council
     confirmed it is scheduled for Monday, October 19th.
  * Noted a dead tree on Second Street needing replacement. Councilmember
      ZANDO-DENNIS confirmed that funds are actively available for
      replacement trees.
2.) Bret Webber (120 Morrison Avenue, Easton):
  * Requested clarification/details about what improvements are in the contract for
     Hooper House
     (referencing: Resolution (180-2026) Authorizing Agreement with Michael Richline for
       Improvements to the Hooper House)
  *Mayor PANTO clarified that the scope of work includes repointing roof work, and
    window improvements.
3.) John Pitts (Post Commander, Bethlehem Buffalo Soldiers):
  * Clarified for the record that the Bethlehem Buffalo Soldiers organizational entity had
     no involvement with the decision to change the name of the park on Nesquehoning

     Street, noting that responsibility remained with Councilmember BROWN's
     committee.
   * John was there in support of Ashley Wooley-Pinkowski & Tarea Wooley-
                   Wadlington.
4.) Ashley Woolley-Pinkowski (1522 Eaton Avenue, Bethlehem) Tarea Woolley-Wadlington
     (136 W. Milton St, Easton):
  * Ashley Spoke as a direct descendant of Aaron Hop, the founder of the First Colored
      Lutheran Church of Easton and the historical burial ground on Nesquehoning Street.
  * Expressed strong opposition to the park's renaming to "Ketchen-Boyer Memorial
     Park," noting that neither individual had ancestral ties to the sacred cemetery.
  * It was requested that Council pause the upcoming October 17th dedication ceremony
      until all relevant parties reach an agreement.
  * Proposed honoring historical community figures like Clarine Boyer and Frances
     Ketchen elsewhere in the city, and suggested renaming the burial park site after First
     Sergeant George Hoff of the United States Colored Troops.
  *Tarea spoke on behalf of the granddaughter of Clarine Boyer who was unable to attend
    the meeting. Clarine Boyers granddaughter was also requesting a pause of the
    upcoming October 17th dedication ceremony until all relevant parties reach an
    agreement.
