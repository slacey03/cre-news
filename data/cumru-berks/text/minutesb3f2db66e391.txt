CUMRU TOWNSHIP BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS
SPECIAL MEETING JUNE 1, 2026

The Special Meeting of the Cumru Township Board of Commissioners was called to order at
7:18 p.m. by President Andy Donnell. Other Commissioners in attendance were Vice-President
Tonya L. Campbell, Mark Ferrero, Greg Miller, and Lisha L. Rowe . Also attending were
Solicitor Kelsey Frankowski, and Special Projects/Asst. Secretary Patricia A. Wylezik-Pfeiffer.

NOTICE:

Public comment will be accepted in person. Please include your first name, last name and
address for all comments. Any comments without a name or address will not be considered. To
accommodate as many people as possible, we request that each person’s comments be limited to
3 minutes (resolution no. 301). The meeting comment period is limited to a total of 60 minutes.
This time period may be extended at the discretion of the Commissioners. While public
comments will be given due consideration, immediate responses will be made at the discretion of
the Commissioners.

ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT

A.) PROPOSED TRADITIONAL NEIGHBORHOOD DEVELOPMENT (TND)
OVERLAY DISTRICT TEXT AND MAP AMENDMENTS TO ZONING ORDINANCE
OF 2009, AS AMENDED -— consider and vote on enactment

Mr. Ferrero asked if the overlay supersedes the previous zoning. Atty. Frankowski said that it
would be owner’s choice.

Mr. Ferrero asked if the ordinance was fixed or if it could be changed. Atty. Frankowski said
that it could be changed in the future by following the amendments procedure.

Mr. Ferrero said that Cumru Township is a dead zone for development and needs to move
forward. What do we gain or lose with a delay?

Mr. Miller stated that the feedback is that Cumru Township is not an easy place to do
business. Lancaster Pike is not appealing. We need a better customer experience.

The Planning Commission can dig into this and create an amendment.

Mr. Donnell said this was not a rushed process. The consultant was hired in 2025. It was a
thoughtful process and they tried to get feedback.

Mr. Ferrero stated that he has lived here for 25-30 years and he likes Nolde Forest and the
natural beauty of the area. He wants to preserve the beauty. It is a balancing act of Nature vs
Development.

Mr. Miller said that it needs to be smart development to make sense.

Ms. Campbell said that is a difficult decision. She attended the community events. People said
that they missed the traditional neighborhood. She gave Lewes, Delaware as an example.

Mr. Miller said that they were working with the school district.

Ms. Rowe said that once the land is gone, it is gone.

Mr. Donnell replied that the Commissioners as Cumru residents themselves don’t take this
lightly. We must face reality and address the issue head on.

Mr. Miller stated that it was not a conflict of interest that his wife is on the school board.

Wayne Hetrick, a resident of Exeter, talked about development in the Mt. Penn area. The
Bowl-O-Rama used to be there. Giant was there and moved down the road. Stores come and go.
They are just leases in the strip malls. Pie in the Sky idea with income for short periods of time.

Georgette Krick, a resident of Winged Foot, asked about the commercial area where the Rite-
Aid used to be located.

Nick Szeredai, a resident of Rim View Lane, agreed that it is a hard task. He stated that he
would prefer commercial to residential. He talked about the disconnect between the Planning
Commission and the Board. They need to do their homework. He asked if reviews were done by
the Fire Department. Police, School District, etc. He asked about eliminating apartments and
considering a 55+ Community. He stated that residential was supposed to come later after
commercial development.

Richard Keffer, a resident of Mt: Penn Rd., said that he had previously reviewed plans and
zoning ordinances as a member of the Berks County Planning Commission. The overlay ides is
not a bad idea. He had made a motion to delay the TND at the last Planning Commission
meeting.

He said that items need to be cleaned up. The same definitions used in the current Zoning
Ordinance should be incorporated. Codes should be separate from zoning.

Mr. Keffer talked about how parts of Flying Hills had Planned Residential Development
(PRD) and Planned Unit Development (PUD). West Reading has mixed-use development. An
overlay is a compliment to the underlying zoning district. He recommended including day care,
real estate offices, etc. with. commercial on the 1* floor and residential on the upper levels.

Mr. Donnell asked about the raising taxes question. Mr. Keffer said that money came from
commercial and retail services.

Mr. Donnell asked for a motion to approve enacting the TND Overlay ordinance.

ON MOTION OF MR. MILLER, SECOND OF MR. FERRERO, A MAJORITY VOTE
TO ENACT THE TRADITIONAL NEIGHBORHOOD DEVELOPMENT (TND)
OVERLAY DISTRICT TEXT AND MAP AMENDMENTS TO THE ZONING
ORDINANCE OF 2009, AS AMENDED. MS. ROWE VOTED NO. MR. MILLER, MR.
DONNELL, MR. FERRERO AND MS. CAMPBELL VOTED YES.
