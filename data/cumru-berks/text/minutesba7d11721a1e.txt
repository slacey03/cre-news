Atty. Folino explained that the PLCB requires approval and a resolution before they will
consider the application for an Intermunicipal Transfer of a Liquor License. The Public Hearing
is the first step in the process. The applicant presents testimony and any evidence to gain
approval. Once the approval is granted, the PLCB will investigate and conduct background
checks to determine whether the applicant receives the R license, which is a restaurant license.
The process usually takes at least 3 to 6 months to be completed.

Tonight’s Public Hearing is to transfer the liquor license from Ruby Tuesday 1665 State Hill
Rd. in Wyomissing to Crooked Hen in Cumru Township.

Mr. Hambartsoumian questions. There is a lease with the property owner.

Tuesday through Sunday. She also asked if they ever expect to operate for an amusement permit

amend and modernize an existing ordinance for public events, not private events.

He proposed 3 tiers based on the number of attendees. Small-Scale would be 50 to 150
people; Medium-Scale would be 151 to 500 people; and Large-Scale would be more than 500.
The requirements increase with each level.

There will be an application and timelines to be followed to allow for review by the various
departments depending on the event being proposed.

There was discussion with the public that was not captured on the video. Mr. Miller offered to
send the draft version to members of the public. One permit could cover a recurring event. There
was concern about micromanaging Township businesses that hold events. Chief Winchester
supports this effort because even small events can require Police Department involvement.

The Special Events Ordinance is not ready to advertise. It will be finalized and then
advertised for adoption.
