Atty. Frankowski introduced Megan Zepiora, a representative from the Delta Development
Group, who gave a verbal explanation of the proposed Traditional Neighborhood Development
(TND) Overlay District after she was sworn in due to technical difficulties with the PowerPoint.

The proposed amendment was created in response to some of the public outreach that was part
of the economic development strategy. There was a desire for a variety of new housing as well as
new retail and dining options.

Ms. Zepiora explained that the TND j is designed to be walkable and increase community. It
has a mix of different types of housing with commercial and retail space. It is meant to
encourage a blend of recreation and medium-density housing. There will be safe pedestrian,
bicycle and vehicle circulation. The ultimate goal is to encourage people to work and live in the
same community space.

Ms. Zepiora explained the design elements of the TND. There would be mixed housing
including apartments, townhouses and single-family homes. There would be centrally located
common open spaces and a variety of retail and dining options. There is a 30% open space
requirement. The maximum housing density is 5 and % dwelling units per acre. This could be
increased to 6 and 1/2 dwelling units per acre if the developer increases the open space to 40%.
She clarified that each apartment is considered 1 dwelling unit.

She explained that the overlay district creates 2 options for the developers. They can follow
the existing zoning or they can follow the TND zoning.

Mr. Donnell gave a little background on what happened to lead up to the hearing today. It
began with the Board hiring Delta to figure out solutions to address the short falls in the
projected revenues over the long-term for the commercial and residential tax base. Delta worked
with the Township through a year’s worth of investigation, fact-finding and research.

The Board wanted to do this in a data-driven way with their eyes open, not guessing, and
trying to make decisions that were good for all the residents. He explained that there were public
engagement sessions to gather data. An Economic Development Steering Committee was formed
with representation from a diverse spectrum of personalities and backgrounds.

Once Ms. Zepiora was able to access the PowerPoint, she showed Legacy Park in
Mechanicsburg as an example of a TND in an area with characteristics similar to Cumru
Township.

The zoning ordinance would not dictate architectural style but the developers must create
architectural guidelines so the development will look good.

Mr. Donnell wanted to clarify that the Township was not developing anything. The property
owners would be developing their property as they see fit. We are facilitating what is allowed
and how development is allowed within the Township and perhaps create incentives for people
to do or not do things.

Atty. Frankowski opened the hearing for the Board to make public comments on the record.

Mr. Miller wanted to add that this is an overlay so the existing zoning that’s in place for these
parcels will remain. No one has to do anything. This just presents an additional opportunity.

No other Board members or Township employees had any additional comments.

Atty. Frankowski opened the hearing for comments from the public. She explained that this is
not a Zoning Hearing so the public does not have the right to become a party or present evidence
or call witnesses. This is a Public Hearing designed to inform and obtain public comment prior to
the Board taking any action on the ordinance. The Board is not required to answer questions or
make comments but the members can if they wish. A transcription would be available.

A.) Nick Szeredai, a resident of Rim View Lane, addressed the Board.

Mr. Szeredai stated that he has a municipal engineering background and works for
municipalities. He was a member of the Cumru Township Park & Recreation Board when it was
active. He had attended all the community events held by Delta and believes that the study
results were biased.

Mr. Szeredai voiced opposition to the proposed TND Overlay. He does not believe that it is
compatible or compliant with the economic study. The focus of the study was the 222 corridor
and developing a pathway to creating tax revenue. The school district is near maximum capacity.
The plan called for true economic development through retail, commercial, and industrial but we
are going straight to residential. Usually the tax revenue on dwellings is pennies on the dollar
compared to true retail and commercial space.

The study stated that we needed affordable housing of all different types. Mr. Szeredai does
not believe that 50-60 ft. apartment buildings are the answer. He mentioned the Sencit
Apartments and all the ongoing problems with that building. The cost of resources and ancillary
activity with emergency services is going to outweigh any tax revenue that you think you are
going to make.

In reference to the proposed ordinance, he stated that he was very frustrated with watching the
meetings and how this played out. The Cumru Planning Commission should have been able to
fully vet this ordinance. The Cumru Planning Commission had very little say and that is not how
this is supposed to work. There should be a better relationship between the Planning Commission
and the Board. The process should be followed.

The TND creates a lot of conflicts with the current ordinance. He cited roads and definitions
as 2 examples. Mr. Szeredai said that he received community outreach after he had reviewed the
proposed ordinance and provided feedback to the Planning Commission. People didn’t know
what to do because they know that this will be pushed through.

Mr. Szeredai stated that he watches all the meetings and that the Board is doing a great job
90% of the time. This is not it.

He highly disagreed with the section that states that the ordinance will need flexibility. The
Zoning Ordinance is restrictive and in order to get a variance, you need to prove a hardship.

He said that the ordinance should have included a sample layout. The example provided in the
presentation tonight was the first sketch shown to him and it was very unclear which region was
which.

He ended by recommending that this ordinance should be sent back to the Planning
Commission and be revetted to put a higher focus on retail and commercial space not just
housing.
