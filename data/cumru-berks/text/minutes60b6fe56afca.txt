POLICE DEPARTMENT

A.) Monthly Key Performance Indicator (KPI) Report — July 2026

The Board reviewed the July Police Department KPI report and discussed the use of performance
measures for higher-level oversight. Discussion included self-directed activity outside of 911 calls,
mandatory training, public education, complaints and citations, theft trends, and repeat calls that may
identify locations or issues requiring additional attention. The Board also discussed tracking training and
public education efforts as measurable activities and suggested displaying KPI graphs in a larger format
for easier review. The discussion emphasized maintaining useful data while retaining the ability to
extract information as needed.

B.) National Night Out — August 4, 2026 @ Shillington Park from 6 ~ 8 p.m.

Chief Winchester discussed National Night Out and the success of the community event. Discussion
included continuing the event at this location and building on the event in future years. Trick-or-Treat
activities for October were also discussed, including possible use of the Township campus or a park.

C.) General Police Information Report

The Board discussed public education regarding scams and fraud, including warnings that residents
should not be directed to pay fines through Bitcoin or similar methods.

The Board also discussed license-plate and vehicle-recognition camera technology. The Township
does not currently have a flock camera system or an agreement to use one. A separate intersection
camera system that captures vehicle and license-plate information with a time stamp was discussed as a
potential investigative resource. The discussion noted that certain State Police information must be
requested and justified and is not automatically provided. The Board also discussed access controls and
proprietary software limitations. The Viper System can not be converted to the Flock System.

automated collection. Mr. Marshall stated that the containers for mechanical collection are a | time cost
which means that mechanical collection costs should be less for the next contract because the residents
already would have the containers.

The Board discussed whether to reject the refuse bids and rebid the work with revised specifications,
including a manual collection option for the whole Township. The possibility of accepting the recycling
bids separately from the refuse portion was also discussed. Contractors bid the whole package, so bids
could change if recycling is divided out. Staff was asked to further evaluate container costs and other
components of the bids before proceeding.

The Board also discussed the timing of a potential rebid in relation to the current contract, which
expires at the end of the year. The possibility of seeking County assistance for grant funding for
recycling containers was discussed.
