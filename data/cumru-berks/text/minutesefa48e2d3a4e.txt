A.) What’ SUP Berks — presentation on plastics and the impact of single-use plastic bags

Deanna Witzel from What’SUP Berks gave a presentation about single-use plastic.

Only a small % of plastics, less than 10 %, are recycled. That number is even smaller for
single-use plastic bags with approximately 2.8% being recycled.

Plastics never go away. They just get smaller and smaller with micro plastics becoming nano
plastics. The micro plastics and the nano plastics are stored in body membranes and cause many
health issues.

She showed a slide depicting 5 large areas of “plastic film” known collectively as the Great
Pacific Garbage Patches.

Ms. Witzel also shared information about the cost incurred for plastic cleanup for the past
almost 10 years. The City of Reading spent over $2 million and PennDOT spent approximately
$14 million.

Ms. Witzel asked the Board to consider a plastic bag ban for Cumru Township.

B.) SMP Overdue Inspection and Malfunction letters - 450 compliance letters were mailed for
overdue on-lot septic inspections and noted malfunctions

Mrs. Wylezik-Pfeiffer explained that 450 non-compliance letters were sent to residents with
on-lot septic systems. The SMP Ordinance requires that on-lot septic systems are pumped out
and inspected every 3 years. The inspection is done by the Township Sewage Enforcement
Officer (SEO) or his designee so that everyone gets the same inspection and report.

The SEO report contains recommendations to help maintain your system and avoid costly
repairs. The SMP system was implemented due to DEP cracking down on on-lot systems due to
many of them failing.

There was discussion about Cumru Township having 1000-1200 on-lot septic systems and the
large number of letters that were sent out. The letters were sent out based on the inspection
information on record. Many properties have different owners from when they were last
inspected and many owners have mailing addresses that are different from the property address.
Some people have been able to produce inspection reports either from their haulers or from when
the property was purchased. An inspection is much less expensive than paying the non-
compliance fines. The letters are a gentler approach for getting the inspections completed.
