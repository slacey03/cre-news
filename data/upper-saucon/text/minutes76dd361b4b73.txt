Carolyn Boscia, 6407 Winding Road, stated she and her husband are very happy residents of
the Township but wished to express concerns regarding the Brookhill Estates Subdivision.
She stated that numerous trees along their rear property line were removed during
construction of the subdivision and, in her opinion, the trees were not authorized to be

removed.

Ms. Boscia stated she contacted the developer regarding the matter, but the developer has
not, to date, presented a plan to remediate the situation. She also stated the Lehigh County
Conservation District confirmed the developer violated its NPDES permit for the
development. She expressed concern that the remediation of the violation may not require the
replacement of the trees that were removed.

Ms. Boscia noted the trees were located on a steep embankment and expressed concern about
the potential for erosion resulting from their removal. She requested the Board review the
permits and approved plans for the subdivision and determine whether the Township can
assist in enforcing the applicable requirements and the intent of the approved development,
particularly with respect to the trees that were removed.

Ms. Boscia emphasized she has no objection to the development of the property but is
concerned about the removal of the trees and the potential impacts on adjoining properties.

John Boscia, 6407 Winding Road, reiterated many of his wife's concerns regarding the
removal of trees along their rear property line. He asked the Board to consider the matter as
an opportunity to ensure that applicable rules and permit requirements are enforced. He
stated that, in his view, allowing developers to disregard applicable requirements without
regard to the impact on existing residents sends the wrong message. He asked the Board to
look into the matter in light of the NPDES violation reported by his wife and stated he would
be happy to provide the Board with photographs documenting the trees that were removed.

Gretchen Fisher of 3575 Preston Lane questioned whether the tract was large enough to
accommodate the proposed medical office building and associated parking. She stated that
both properties could support viable residential uses and suggested consideration be given to
renting the existing homes on the properties to students attending DeSales University. She
expressed concerns about traffic, distracted drivers, and speeding on Preston Lane, all of
which she stated would be exacerbated by the proposed medical office building. She also
expressed concerns about the safety of students crossing Preston Lane and stated that the

concern about the gradual transition of the neighborhood from residential to commercial

USES.

Tony Mirra of 3629 Preston Lane expressed concerns regarding increased traffic on Preston
Lane and suggested that making Preston Lane one-way should be considered as a possible
solution to the traffic issues. He questioned the need for another medical office building in
the community and stated he preferred keeping the tract residential or using the existing
homes for student housing. He stated that he saw no need for a medical office building at the
proposed location. Mr. Mirra also expressed concern that access into and out of the proposed
medical office building parking lot would be difficult.

Carolyn Boscia of 6407 Winding Road said she believed the proposed development
represented a missed opportunity given the properties’ proximity to the schools. She
suggested that a community center or after-school recreation center might be a better use of
these properties. She also expressed concern that the proposed medical office building would
make it more dangerous for students to cross Preston Lane. Ms. Boscia questioned the need
for another medical office building in the area, stating that several physician offices in the
vicinity appear to have ample space and do not appear to be heavily utilized. She reiterated
her request that consideration be given to creating usable community space on the properties.

Supervisor Dolan asked about possible alternative sites for the proposed medical office
building. Ms. Peters said an alternative site has not been identified.

An unidentified woman suggested consideration be given to an alternative site behind the
Copperhead Grille. It was noted the property behind the Copperhead Grille is owned by the
Township and is not for sale.

COURTESY OF THE FLOOR

Saralyn Foley of 7510 Pheasant Drive asked what residents can do to protect their property
from irresponsible developers. She noted the situation involving the Brookhill Estates
Subdivision was not the first instance she had heard of developers overstepping their bounds.

Solicitor Dinkelacker explained that neighbors who suffer harm as a result of a developer’s
actions may have legal remedies available to them, including a private cause of action. It was
noted that, in the Boscias’ case, the violation involved a federal permit rather than a

Township permit.

Ms. Foley asked what the Township could do to protect residents. Solicitor Dinkelacker
explained that the Township’s authority depends on the nature of the violation. He noted the
Township regulates development and enforces requirements within its jurisdiction, but its
authority is subject to certain limitations.

Supervisor Dolan asked whether there would be value in bringing the concerns raised by the
Boscias to the attention of the Conservation District. Solicitor Dinkelacker indicated that

doing so would be worthwhile.

Ms. Boscia then asked questions regarding the conditions attached to the approval of the
Brookhill Estates Subdivision Plan, which Solicitor Dinkelacker addressed.
