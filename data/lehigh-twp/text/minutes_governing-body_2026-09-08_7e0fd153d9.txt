Centers and Data Center Accessory Uses.  Attorney Backenstoe commented as 
required by the Municipalities Planning Code, this ordinance has been advertised two 
times, on August 24 and August 31 and the proof of publication has been received.  A 
copy of the ordinance was sent to the Northampton County Law Library and he has a 
stamped receipt dated July 17th.  A draft of the ordinance was sent to the Lehigh 
Township Planning Commission on April 23rd and favorable comments were 
received.  A draft was also sent to the Lehigh Valley Planning Commission on July 22 
and they also provided a favorable review.  Some of the items they liked were the

definitions and it is a conditional use in the Industrial District.  Some specific items 
they applauded were the buffering requirements, the emergency management 
planning, environmental impact studies, the aesthetic requirements, parking 
requirements, traffic impact, decommissioning, and groundwater monitoring pre-
development, post development, and after development.  They did request we 
consider some additional items such as how it compares to other industrial 
developments in the ordinance, additional decommissioning for electrified waste, heat 
mitigation, and potential increase in setback requirements.  These are items that can 
be looked into after the ordinance is adopted.  It’s best to have an ordinance in place 
sooner than later.  If data centers are not permitted in any district, an applicant can file 
for exclusionary zoning which can be a disaster for a municipality because it means 
they can place the data center in any location they chose.  
 
Cindy Miller made a motion to adopt Ordinance 2026-2.  Janet Sheats seconded the 
motion.  Jerry Pritchard commented with the data centers being allowed by 
conditional use, they need to come before the Board and prove they meet the 
ordinance.  Attorney Backenstoe commented the requirements for obtaining a 
conditional use is basically the same requirements or law as an special exception use 
before the Zoning Hearing Board.  The applicant must prove that they meet or are 
going to meet all of the requirements in the ordinance.  They must be able to 
demonstrate that.  If there are objectors who provide evidence or testimony contrary 
to what they are submitting or are able to show that this particular application is more 
detrimental to the Township than another data center in other areas would be, the 
application could be denied.  It is not an automatic approval.  If there is an issue and 
residents and entities are opposed to it, they have the right to appear and present 
testimony that must be considered.  If the Board finds the applicant does not meet the 
criteria or the objectors establish that the use is more detrimental to the community 
that over uses like it in the State, the Board can deny it and it will be upheld in court.  
Janet Sheats commented the Board took this very seriously and looking at everything 
going on around use, they felt it was urgent to get something in place.  The Board did 
their due diligence to get ahead of this before we received any submissions.  Cindy 
Miller commented the State just passed a law regarding the decommissioning, but she  
thinks it still allows Townships to regulate them.  Attorney Backenstoe commented 
the law is still being published, but what he saw was that it would be more like an 
umbrella policy.  He believes the applicants will have to comply with the Governor’s 
requirements and the local requirements.  If a Township didn’t have an ordinance and 
exclusionary zoning was applied for, they would still have to comply with the State 
requirements.  He does not believe the law will preempt Townships, but provide for 
additional requirements.  Cindy Miller commented she read that they will now have 
to go to DEP to obtain authorization.  Merion Miller commented the data centers do
