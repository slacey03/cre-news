IV . PLANNING RELATED ITEMS 
A. Lehigh Valley Resort & Spa Update.  Fred Ebert and Nicole Galio were present to 
provide an update on the project.  Fred Ebert commented they broke ground for the 
new sewer plant at the beginning of May.  The tanks are expected to be set in June or 
early July.  The majority of the work will be completed by the end of summer, and 
they will continue working through the remaining punch list items, most likely 
through November.  The installation of the mechanical equipment will be delayed 
until they are closer to getting the flows from the resort.  By delaying the installation 
of mechanical items, the warrantees will be pushed out further.  It is also not good to 
start up the equipment and then let it sit.  
 
Nichole Galio commented they have been addressing some internal issues with the 
resort so they can get to the budget they are looking for.  They will be coming back to  
the Board most likely in July for an extension of time for the recording of the plan so 
that it is more in line with the completion of the plant expansion.

Linda Roman questioned how they will be handling the flood water.  Mr. Ebert 
commented DEP requires them to have the plant at least one foot above the 100 year 
flood plain.  The plant is actually six feet above the 100 year flood plain.

B. Manager’s Report.  Alice Rehrig reported the Township was successful in obtaining 
$89,500 from the Northampton County Gaming Grant and $60,987 from the Monroe 
County Gaming Grant for the digital radio upgrades.

the final draft of the data center ordinance and it should be ready for the next meeting 
so the formal process of adopting the ordinance can begin, which includes sending the 
ordinance to the LVPC for review and comment.  He is also expecting to have 
completed his review of the noise ordinance for the next meeting; however, he will 
not be in attendance at that meeting. 
 
Jerry Pritchard commented he would prefer holding off on the discussion regarding 
the data center ordinance until Attorney Backenstoe was present at the meeting.  He 
has the confidence in Attorney Backenstoe that he will draft an ordinance that is in  
the best interest of the Township and to protect the Township.  The information that is 
being distributed on Facebook is a matter of scare tactics and it is not right to do that 
to the public.

VII. PUBLIC COMMENT.  Zach Szoke provided the Board with information to substantiate 
 the statement that has been made regarding the costs of residential development in that 
 for every $1 that is collected in taxes, the Township is technically paying out $1.16 in 
 services to the residential property.  He also reminded the Board that land preservation is 
 important to many residents and the Board should make a plan to get this on the 
 November ballot.  If the Board wants them to stop talking about land preservation, put it 
 on the ballot, and if it is not approved, then it is done and they will stop talking about it. 
 
 Katherine Mack commented she comes to a majority of the Supervisors meetings and 
 usually at each meeting there is a discussion about land preservation and open space.  
 There are different opinions presented and the discussions could go on and on, some of 
 them can become polarized.  There are people who are supporters of land preservation at 
 the Township level and are willing to pay the tax increase, others will give their voice 
 about land preservation, but are unwilling to fund it at a Township level, and there 
 probably are some people who don’t believe in land preservation at all; but she feels the 
 main issue that the Township Supervisors need to take into account is the people’s right to 
 voice their opinions through the ballot box.  The only way to know how the constituents 
 really feel is at the ballot box.  The right to vote is essential in a well-functioning 
 democracy.  It is a fundamental right of all Americans and you are taking that away.  Five 
 people have made the decision rather than the number of voting people in the Township.  
 The ballot box is the most effective reflection of public support or dissent.  Allowing the 
 referendum should not be feared.  It should be a welcome method of addressing this 
 issue.  V otes are the only decisive and objective way of determining the will of the 
 people.  The voters voices can and should be heard, both those who agree or are opposed 
 to the land preservation.  The Board represents all the citizens of Lehigh Township.  The 
 only way to represent them the best is to allow them to voice their vote at the ballot box.  
 There is no personal responsibility on the Board from the public if the referendum passes 
 or it doesn’t.  Allow them to vote and the voters have to accept that vote.  The 
 responsibility falls on that of the voters.  She would ask the Board to reconsider the 
 referendum and give them their right to vote. 
 
 Jerry Pritchard commented he has always been a believer in the ballot box.  That is the 
 way this nation has been set up.  Janet Sheats commented she took it very seriously.  She 
 stood out at the polls talking about land preservation and data centers to clear the air and 
 let them know what it meant and what it entailed.  She discovered the majority of the 
 people did not understand what she was talking about.  That is where her issue is with 
 this.  It was shocking to her that people, especially the elderly, didn’t know what she was   
 talking about.  The voters need to know and understand what this is all about.  She cannot 
 put this on the ballot box until all the correct information is out there.  An informed vote 
 is crucial and important.  She also believes taxes will be going up in the long run because 
 of the lost revenue.
