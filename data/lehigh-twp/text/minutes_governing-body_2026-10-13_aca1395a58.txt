Chief Henry provided a brief explanation of how the department uses Flock Cameras.  
There are two cameras in the Township that are owned by Northampton County.  One 
camera is on Route 145 in Treichlers and the other on Route 248 in the Gap.  The 
cameras are fixed position cameras and cannot be remotely manipulated.  They take 
still images of license plates.  They are used for investigative purposes and cannot be 
used for traffic enforcement.  The cameras only collect what is visible from public

roadways. They do not collect names, addresses, phone numbers, driver identities, 
distinctive traits or other confidential information.  There are safeguards in place to 
prevent unauthorized access.  Every search of the cameras is recorded and requires a 
justification so an agency can see who is asking for the information, why they are 
asking, and when they asked.  In addition, the Department is required to do quarterly 
audits to verify the legitimacy of any search that is done.  The Department has used 
Flock information several times.  Two specific instances where they use the Flock 
information was to develop a suspect vehicle when there was a robbery at the vape 
shop and to locate a missing juvenile.

Sergeant Roth provided information regarding e-bikes and the concerns with them.  
He recently attended a task force meeting regarding these because they are becoming 
a concern especially in the boroughs and towns and they are looking at how to 
address the issue because it is relatively new.  There have been seven fatalities 
involving e-bikes and scooters.  The task force is working on developing an 
educational video for parents.  The parents can actually be cited for permitting their 
child to ride these bikes on the roads.  The peddle assist bikes are not as much of a 
problem as the electric dirt bikes.  There are some people with DUIs who think that 
are able to drive these on the roadway, which is not the case.  In addition, someone 
can be arrested for DUI if they are operating an e-bike on the roadway.  The laws in 
Pennsylvania have not caught up to the different types of e-bikes that are out there.  
There is also a lot of education that is needed for parents and law enforcement.  Right 
now, the laws in effect require you to be 16 years old and wearing a helmet. 
 
Linda Roman commented over the last few days, she has seen e-bikes doing jumps 
from the roadway into the parks. 
 
Sherri Szoke questioned if it is illegal for any age under 16 to be riding a bike on the 
road.  Sergeant Roth commented the 16 year old requirement only pertains to e-bikes.
