There were two resolutions that were created outlining guidelines for public

Attorney Backenstoe commented there are two differ concepts involved with this.  
One concept is motions.  When a motion is made, it must be seconded, then there is 
discussion.  The Board of Supervisors discusses it first, then it is opened to the public 
to comment and ask questions or comment on the motion.  There is not a three minute 
limit, but there should not be any debates amongst the public.  They should be 
addressing the Board.  When all the discussion is done, a vote is called for.  The 
second concept is public comment which is required under the Sunshine Law.  Public 
comment is the right for the public to comment on anything, although it should 
pertain to Township business, or matters and affairs that may be state or federal and 
pertain to the Township.  The Board is discussing implementing rules, such as that 
you would like them to come up to the microphone, give their name and address, 
which is common practice.  There are two proposed resolutions before the Board.   
One sets forth they need to be recognized by the chair, identify themselves, and 
conduct themselves in an orderly fashion.  The other resolution contains the exact 
same language, except that the comments are restricted to three minutes.  The Board

1. Consideration of interim change to Zoning Ordinance.  Attorney Backenstoe 
commented the Board had received a letter from a resident, Michael 
Druckenmiller, inquiring about changing the zoning requirements regarding solar 
panels.  Mr. Druckenmiller lives at 4020 Schaeffer Drive, and his neighbor 
installed ground mounted solar panels.  Under the ordinance, there were no 
requirements regarding screening.  This upset Mr. Druckenmiller because he can 
see the panels when in his back yard.    There are several things that Mr. 
Druckenmiller wants to see done.  He would like the Township to require the 
neighbor to plant screening and also have the Township redraft a new ordinance 
which would require buffering.  He is also not happy with the pace in which the 
new ordinance is being completed.  The Township’s current ordinance regulates 
principal solar system, which is a large scale system that sells power back to the 
grid, and accessory solar systems which supply power to individual homes or 
businesses.  The ordinance allows panels to be placed on the home or on the 
ground.  The ordinance does not require screening for Accessory Solar Systems, 
only for Principal Solar Energy Systems.  In 2022, the Township amended their 
ordinance  and increased the allowable kilowatts to 30 kilowatts for an accessory 
system; however, they still did not require screening for accessory solar systems.  
In 2023, the Township again amended their ordinance and made Principal Solar 
Energy Systems a conditional use only permitted in the Industrial Zoning District.  
These are the ordinances in which Mr. Druckenmiller’s neighbor installed their 
solar panels.  Their accessory system was under 30 kilowatts and did not require 
screening.  The Township is now in the process of revising their zoning ordinance 
which is a large undertaking.  One of the aspects if this revision is to include 
screening for accessory solar systems.  There will also be glare requirements.  
Once the new ordinance is adopted, the screening and anti-glare requirements will 
be in effect; however, it will not affect Mr. Druckenmiller.  The solar panels that 
are of a concern to Mr. Druckenmiller will become a legal non-conformity.  When
