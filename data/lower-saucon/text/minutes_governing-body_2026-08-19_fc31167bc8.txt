A. BAT BOX EAGLE SCOUT PROJECT/SEAN JEBITSCH (YT: 9:18) 
 
Ms. Opthof-Cordaro said next is presentations.    We welcome the Boy Scout to come forward and 
explain what is going on with his proposal on bat boxes. 
 
Sean Jebitsch presented his Eagle Scout project to install four bat houses on two metal poles at 
Southeastern Park and explained the bats habitat . Sean Jebitsch said he’s in the 11 th grade at Saucon 
Valley High School and is in Boy Scout Troop 191.  He’s currently working on his Eagle Scout project 
and came here today to present his proposal on a bat box.  Within the past 20 years, white nose syndrome 
has devastated the local bat population.  Experts estimate that 99% of the population has been wiped 
out.   Bat boxes provide a safe home and community for  them to breed and rebuild a population. He is 
proposing to put two posts at Southeastern Park.  Each of them will have two bat boxes on either side 
because bat boxes are warmed by the sun, and the bats need a warm environment, especially in the cold 
fall months or the cold spring months.  Bats are very important to our community for a few reasons.   A 
singular bat is estimated to eat between 1,000 and 1,200 insects per hour, and these insects are insects 
that are harmful to the environment which are dephosphorylating butterflies and wood -borne beetles.  
They are very dangerous to local tree populations, and bats are a non -chemical way and an 
environmentally friendly way to keep these populations low. He has a map with five locations in which 
he thinks it would be best for the bats to go.  They need to be within 200 yards of a water source and 20 
feet away from any tree line to protect them from predators such as owls or low staking predators such 
as foxes. They will be two feet wide and three feet tall.  Each one can hold 250 bats. It is a seven-layer 
bat box, so there's seven different slots for them to stay in.  They will be posted on two metal poles that 
are 16 feet tall that  will be cemented into the ground.  This will give them the proper distance to fly.   
Months ago, his dad, an advisor and a carpenter , built an example bat box and presented it to Jay 
Finnigan, and they learned how long it took to build it, the cost, and gave them a good idea of what it 
would be like to build one.  He is going to accept volunteer requests from inside his troop and his friends 
and family. He proposes building it in September and then posting it in October for fundraising.  He will 
go to local businesses for donations.  He would ask for help from Council to put the bat boxes into the 
ground. 
 
Ms. Opthof-Cordaro thanked him for putting this together and said it’s wonderful he put so much effort 
into this and his family must be very proud of him.   She thought Southeastern Park would be an ideal 
place to put the bat houses and asked if he has gone out to see what the bat population is and whether it 
would attract bats at that park. 
 
Sean Jebitsch said he lives five minutes away from the park.  When he was little, he could see 20 to 30 
bats flying around outside and now when he goes out, he sees maybe one or two.  He believes putting 
the bat boxes there will give them a place to rebuild the population. 
  
Ms. Ray said it was a fantastic project and loved the idea.  She thinks Southeastern Park is a perfect 
place.  If he needs help putting the boxes up, she’s sure Public Works would have equipment that might 
be helpful. 
 
Jay Finnigan said Public Works will be more than willing to assist in getting the stakes post put in. 
 
Sean Jebitsch said from research he has done; it would be digging the hole and then on the ground you 
lay down the post, you attach both bat boxes and then you put it up all together as one piece. 
 
Ms. Ray said it’s pretty tall then.
