is the architect on the project with Village Architecture and Preservation, and also John Price, who is 
the structural engineer from Green Cottage Industries.  She said she will present a slide presentation and 
explain each slide as follows: 
 
• This slide, the master plan was developed over the past year. It began with interviews and a site 
survey to understand existing conditions, and then they started a feedback loop, submitting 
drafts of the master plan, and getting feedback from the committee, from the Council, and then 
leading to this final presentation of the master plan tonight. 
• The next slide shows the park and the existing conditions that they found within the last year.   
Heller Homestead Park is located adjacent to Hellertown Borough and the City of Bethlehem.  
• The next slide shows the park is a stretch of 13.9 acres that runs along the west side of Saucon 
Creek, and is adjacent green spaces, historic sites, and community spaces.  The park can provide 
an important connection between existing residential, heritage, natural, and recreational areas. 
• The next slide shows the park which itself includes the historic Heller Homestead to the north 
that's leased to the Saucon Valley Conservancy.  In the center section, there's nature trails and a 
picnic area, and to the south , that sort of long skinny section next to the creek, there are 
unimproved wetlands, then it has a little bit of a toe hold on the other end of Skibo Road, and 
also adjacent is some unimproved land that belongs to the residential community up the hill.  
• The next  slide, they mapped the majority of the park.  It has either a natural or cultural 
designation.  The Heller Homestead is designated on the National Register of Historic Places.  
Blue areas are on the National Wetlands Register and also shows the regulatory flood plain. 
• The next slide shows that they met with the Township committee, and started out identifying 
the needs they met here, and this is the National Historic Plan for the Homestead.  They need 
larger gathering spaces protected from the weather, a need to expand the parking lot, and the 
need to connect the historic section to the southern end of the park.  In order to expand public 
access to those maybe additional seven acres there was a need to make the existing 1790 stone 
barn ruin safe and accessible to the publ ic and integrate it into the experience of the historic 
homestead. 
• The next slide is what the 1790 stone barn looked like. It was once a prominent feature at the 
Homestead, and then at one point later on, a timber frame barn was added to the south .  These 
photos were taken in 1941. 
• The next slide shows the stone barn roof which collapsed in 1998 and the remaining walls were 
taken down for safety reasons. 
• The next slide is what's left of the barn today.  The walls are not stable.  Much of the barn is set 
behind a fence to keep the public out of this area.  They would like to take the fence down and 
return the barn as part of the homestead.  
• The next  slide is a master plan. Each one of the interventions that they recommend can be 
undertaken separately but they grouped them together based on priority.  The first proposed 
recommendation will make the barn saf e and integrate that barn feature into the historic 
homestead experience.  It will provide easier access to that unimproved wetland area to the 
south.  They propose stabilizing and rebuilding portions of the 1790 stone barn, so it'll be safe 
and provide an outdoor gathering area.  They are inspired by Lock Ridge, if anyone's ever been 
there, it’s beautiful.  They propose building a wooden stair down that steep grade change and 
then adding some stabilized soil trails to allow improved access to that lower wetland area. 
• The next slide is a site plan of the northern end of the park, just to show that where they create 
that barn ruin of the 1790 stone barn , which could be used for maybe tent staging, and then 
expanding the existing parking lot with a new eight spaces.
