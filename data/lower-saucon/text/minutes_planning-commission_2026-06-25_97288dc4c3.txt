Shorav Kaushik asked what's going to happen to the existing system? Are you going to remove it? 
 
Joseph Valentine stated the common practice for in Pennsylvania for sewage systems is that tankage 
would be pumped out and collapsed in place.  The actual drain field, in this case system, would just be 
abandoned in place. If you can see this plan from your standpoint, here this is the building with the 
apartments, and the existing drain field is located behind the building. These are going to be the new 
systems, they're elevated sand mounds .  One is going to be built and one would be the replacement 
area.  A nd there would be new tankage down in this area, tankage meaning new septic tanks and a 
dosing tank to the elevated sand mound. 
 
Thomas Bartek asked will the septic tank will have an alarm system in just in case it gets filled then 
you know when to get a pumped? 
 
Joseph Valentine stated well it doesn't have an alarm system that septic tank per  se, that would be a 
holding tank that would have an alarm to tell you when you need to pump a holding tank. Septic tanks 
are generally maintained in the three to five year period where you call sewage hauler to come in and 
empty the contents.  These are septic tanks , holding tanks are a vessel that don't have an outlet, so a 
holding tank, once it's filled, then you have to call the honey dipper, and that's why they have alarms 
to say, hey, that's 75% of capacity for pumping .  A  septic tank, once it's at operating level, is if 100 
gallons goes in, 100 gallons goes out into the drain the field. 
 
Chris Nagy asked what if there’s an overflow? 
 
Joseph Valentine stated the septic tank works where the solids settle to the bottom, the grease and scum 
flows to the top, and the water that carries everything goes over to either directly to the absorption area 
or, in this case, goes to what we call a dosing tank that has a pump in it, and then when that fills up to 
the appropriate level, it pumps that up into the sand mound. 
 
Shorav Kaushik asked will that have an alarm? 
 
Joseph Valentine stated that will have an alarm. 
 
Shorav Kaushik asked can you tell us about the topography of the area?  Is from the dosing tank sending 
the affluent up the hill or is it all sort of downhill? 
 
Joseph Valentine stated this is River Drive and on my side is the Lehigh River, right here.   Where the 
building is, is the highest point in the property, and it slopes from the building down towards Jefferson. 
Once you get off the building, what I would call a t ier at the higher area. It's fairly flat. I mean, it 
probably is about a 2% slope. It's kind of interesting. These soils here, are what we call glacial outwash, 
they were put down when the Lehigh River was really flowing, say 10,000 years ago, and it has sands 
and gravels in this area here over top of the residual bedrock, which is the carbonate, the limestone, 
which is at some depth .  It’s gravity fed from the apartments to treatment tanks, the septic tank, and 
from the pump tank it pumps it up to the sand mound.
