2. PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE 
Ms. Opthof-Cordaro 
Ms. Opthof-Cordaro called the Lower Saucon Township Special Workshop Meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. and 
welcomed everyone. She explained that the purpose of the workshop was to review reports from the Robert B. 
and Helen S. Meyner Center at Lafayette College and the Lehigh Valley Planning Commission (LVPC), both 
of which were authorized by Council in January to run simultaneously. The goal of commissioning both 
studies, she said, was to provide Council with a comprehensive and robust understanding of where the 
township stands historically and currently, as well as its future opportunities, particularly in terms of revenue 
generation, development, and overall township income. She noted that a great  deal of work has already gone 
into the project, including community meetings, interviews, and surveys, and emphasized that this is an 
unprecedented effort for Lower Saucon Township in terms of the number and range of outside professionals 
involved. She highlighted that experts in housing, finance, environmental and land development issues, and 
roads are contributing information and analysis that the township does not have in -house, and that their work 
is helping the township “design our future.” She clarified that the reports being presented are Phase One, that 
there will be a Phase Two for both the Meyner Center and LVPC later in the year, and that both efforts are 
ongoing. She closed by formally thanking both organizations for their time, effort, and expe rtise, stating that 
the Township is very grateful for their assistance, and then turned the meeting over to Acting Township 
Manager Jay Finnigan to serve as moderator. 
 
3. MODERATOR: JAY FINNIGAN 
Mr. Finnigan  thanked Ms. Opthof -Cordaro and the attendees and  framed the purpose of the workshop as 
“another step” in Council and the Township looking to the future, not the “rearview mirror.” He explained that 
the goal is to bring in  outside input and technical skills  to help keep the Township a great place to live.  He 
noted that both the Meyner Center and the Lehigh Valley Planning Commission have completed Phase One of 
their engagements, that the Meyner Center has already started Phase Two, and that LVPC will begin its Phase 
Two with another community meeting at Saucon Valley High School next Monday evening. Mr. Finnigan set 
basic meeting decorum expectations, asking attendees to refrain from side comments and conversations so 
everyone can hear the presenters and to treat them as they themselves would want to be treated. He also pointed 
out that the agenda has specific time allocations, said he would do his best to keep the meeting on track, and 
stated that the workshop has a hard end time of 10:00 p.m. He then opened Courtesy of the Floor for any items 
on the agenda before moving to the presentations.

Council Questions 
Ms. deLeon 
• The report contains “a substantial amount of well-organized, high-value information” that will support 
Council’s decision-making. 
• She s tated for  decades she has reviewed landfill information and agreements with  one objective: 
“protecting Lower Saucon Township and its residents. “Her position has been consistent since  1988: 
when an agreement  removes protections, limits oversight, or restricts the Township’s authority, she 
votes no. She recalled that in 1998 she opposed the Host Community Agreement because it eliminated 
protections, weakened enforcement,  removed restrictions on waste types,  reduced the Township’s 
ability to comment, and added a non-opposition clause limiting elected officials. 
• The 2023 Host Agreement “repeated the same pattern,” and she again voted no because it surrendered 
protections, narrowed the Township’s oversight role, and conditioned Township rights on criteria set 
by the landfill operator. 
• The Phase One study  “outlines the consequences of those decisions”  and “finally” provides clear 
external validation of concerns she has raised for many years. 
• Concluded that the Township must use its remaining authority to ensure  oversight, compliance, and 
enforcement are carried out “to the highest standard,” and thanked the Meyner Center, saying  “thank 
you for the validation. Made me feel good. Mixed decision on that, though.” 
 
Mr. Gress 
• Mr. Gress said he had a few questions and thanked the presenter for the report. He said the report is laid 
out with the numbers and the facts and is very clear and easy for anyone to read. 
• He raised his first question: noting there is a list of five priority recommendations and that the presenter 
mentioned EIT could potentially make up a large portion of the host fees that may be lost, he asked why 
a change or use of EIT was not mentioned in those priority recommendations. 
• After hearing the initial response, he said he is not entirely sure of the rules about EIT without looking 
into them, but using EIT seemed much more appealing than increasing property taxes. 
• His second question was about borrowing versus cuts: he recalled the comment that borrowing can be 
good and that having debt can be an asset and asked if it came down to  cutting services and lowering 
costs versus borrowing money to continue making those costs without increasing real estate tax, which 
route the presenter would personally recommend. 
• Mr. Gress acknowledged that the answer would  depend on what services  would be cut,  how much, 
and what kinds of things would be affected. 
• He closed by stated his appreciation, saying the report was very easy to understand and very thorough, 
and thanked the presenter.
