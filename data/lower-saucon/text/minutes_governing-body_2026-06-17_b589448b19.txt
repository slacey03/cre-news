Ms. Opthof-Cordaro said Township Business, item 6.A., is One More Steel City Variance 04-26/3679

MOTION BY: Ms. Ray moved to authorize the solicitor to write a letter of support to the Zoning Hearing Board 
solicitor, indicating Township Council's support of the application conditioned on the 
representations made by the applicant before Council, which included the historical mark er and 
historical kiosk, public access to the markers, as well as a conservation easement or deed restriction

SECOND BY: Ms. deLeon 
 
Kate Durso said she is from Fitzpatrick, Lentz and Bubba. 
 
Chris Brown said he is with Brown Design Corp and the designer for the project. 
 
Phil Malitsch said he is with Tuskes Homes and represents the developer for the project. 
 
Chris Brown said this project has been ongoing for some time.  They first came before the Township 
with a proposal for garden apartments. There are basically two residential options in the Village Center 
District, and this property is located in the Village Center District right across the street from the 
municipal complex.  The two options are multi-family rental or condominium type development and the 
other is a cluster residential development.  They started with the apartments, but that plan was not well 
received.  The only other option is under the residential cluster development, and that does allow for 
twins and town homes with a minimum lot area requirement of 10 acres.  To their knowledge, there's no 
10-acre parcel in the Village Center District in the Township. The property is sort of long rectangular 
property and right down the middle is a perennial creek or stream, an unnamed tributary  which is a 
physical limitation.  There are wetlands illustrated on the plan. Those obviously are areas of avoidance. 
They went further to define what would be the 100-year flood plain. There was no FEMA designated 
100-year floodplain, but it was something that came up in some of their prior presentations and hearings.   
They delineated the floodplain, that has very specific protections and there  are areas that are buffers, 
and one of the commenters in the beginning of the meeting tonight talked about the buffers.  They are 
seeking relief from a dimensional distance, an area that is defined by ordinance only.  The creek sort of 
annexes a portion of the property closer to 378 which, although developable, they cannot get to it, so 
almost 80% of the site is undevelopable.  They understand the variances are not taken lightly, but they 
also recognize that it's got to be proven burden of not their own doing.  Their proposal is to go with 19 
town homes, where mathematically, by the regulations and the zoning ordinance, even despite all of the 
encumbrances on the property, the math actually checks out to where 44 units would otherwise be 
allowed by right, but they can’t fit 44 units on this property.  The 19 units are in close proximity to the 
road which was keeping with the character of Old Philadelphia Pike as there are homes and businesses 
relatively close to the street.   They chose to do the rear loading concept with a private alley or driveway 
in the back, so that they wouldn't have numerous driveways coming out onto Old Philadelphia Pike and 
that would be the least amount of impact in terms of driveways. 
 
Kate Durso  said they learned that Council asked them to not necessarily advocate for the garden 
apartments, but to do these town homes.  It was brought to their attention by the Historic Committee 
that the property previously might have been a spot for the Underground Railroad.  They are working 
with the Historic Committee to locate where on the property that they could place a marker and that has 
now been included specifically into the project as a specific area to recognize the past history of the 
property.
