Patricia Kuronya said she’s the Director of Finance and we are here to discuss the 2027 budget, which 
is available on the online meeting portal.  She has a slideshow presentation because she will not be going

• The General Fund is the main operating fund of the Township. The money in this fund pays for 
the staffing of a fully accredited Police Department with 20 full -time officers. It also pays for 
the 14-member crew of the Public Works Department that maintains and plows approximately 
87 miles of Township roads, as well as maintains all Township parks.  At this time, no additional 
police officers, public works crew, or administrative positions are being requested, only filling 
the open spots.  There is no substantive change proposed to Township provided services in the 
coming year.  This funding of Township services comes from the following sources: real estate 
taxes, earned income taxes, landfill host fees, county, state, and federal funding, permits, 
charges for services, fines, interest income, as  well as an approximate $940,000 amount from 
reserves. The proposed budget includes a 1.75 mill tax increase. A property that is assessed at 
$86,867 would have a tax increase of $12.67 per month for 2027.  This slide shows Lower 
Saucon's tax rate compared with other Townships in the County that are similar in size or 
services provided. All the other municipalities listed provide the same services except for East 
Allen, who uses PSP for police services. The next slide is the tax history for the Township, 
which Jay had wanted to comment on. 
 
Jay Finnigan said if you look at the millage rate for the General Fund in 2021, the Township dropped 
its mill rate from 3.9 to 4.39, and then in 2024 to 4.24.  In his opinion, that was not appropriate cost, and 
if you just look at the consumer price index, that hurt the Township financially, because if that had not 
been done, the Township would have had at this point an additional $2.8 million dollars in reserves to 
be used to help fund ongoing operations.   Looking back at the financials of Lower Saucon he doesn’t 
think it was a wise decision to drop the mill rate for the general purpose twice in the last six years. 
 
Patricia Kuronya said this slide gives different options and how much we would take out of reserves 
at different mill rates.
