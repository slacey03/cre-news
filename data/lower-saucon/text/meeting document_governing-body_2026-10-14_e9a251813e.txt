Ms. Opthof-Cordaro thanked the candidates for their time and investment they put in for this evening. 
She stated they have a variety of backgrounds and felt each person had a wonderful set of qualities that 
would be helpful on Council. This is a very difficult decision because everyone had very thoughtful 
responses. She encouraged all of them to consider becoming involved in the various committees and 
boards in their community and attend meetings. 
 
Jay Finnigan explained that council could fill the vacancy through nominations, with a nominee 
needing three affirmative votes to be selected. If council did not reach a decision that evening, it could 
continue deliberations for up to 45 days; after that, the matter would go to the Northampton County 
Court of Common Pleas. The appointee would serve until the next municipal election and would need 
to run in the primary and, if successful, the general election. 
 
Mr. Heimbecker echoed what Ms. Opthof-Cordaro said, and respected and appreciated each one for 
coming this evening.  
 
Mr. Heimbecker NOMINATED Jennifer Helton. 
 
Jay Finnigan asked if there were any other nominations. 
 
Mr. Gress NOMINATED Eric Barnett. 
 
Jay Finnigan asked if there were any other nominations. 
 
Stacy Werkheiser conducted a roll-call vote: 
 
Eric Barnett: 1 vote (Mr. Gress) 
Jennifer Helton: 3 votes (Ms. Opthof-Cordaro, Mr. Heimbecker, Ms. Ray) 
 
Jennifer Helton received three votes and was selected to fill the unexpired council term through 
December 31, 2027.  
 
Jay Finnigan said she would be sworn in before the next Wednesday (October 21, 2026) evening 
council meeting, with the District Magistrate administering the oath.

9. COUNCIL REPORTS  
 
MS. RAY REPORT: 
 
Ms. Ray reported that the day after the previous council meeting, Stacy, Carol, and Acting Manager Jay 
Finnigan were recognized for their service to the township at a ceremony at the Northampton County 
Courthouse. The event also recognized county volunteers, including junior volunteers. She also 
described a well-attended EAC composting workshop where approximately 55–60 compost bins were 
distributed and noted continuing discussions about fire services. 
 
MR. HEIMBECKER REPORT: 
 
Mr. Heimbecker reported that the high school’s Environmental Club was holding a fall festival the 
next day and encouraged people to support it by purchasing the baked goods. He also attended Apple 
Fest at Lutz-Franklin earlier that week, where attendees painted rocks and sampled apple varieties; the 
apples were donated by Bechtold’s Orchard. He closed by wishing everyone a good night and safe trip 
home.  
 
MR. GRESS REPORT: 
 
Mr. Gress thanked the candidates for participating and encouraged them to remain involved in township 
activities and consider running in the next municipal election. He also thanked the Junior Council 
Members who attended. 
 
MS.  OPTHOF-CORDARO REPORT: 
 
Ms. Opthof-Cordaro thanked Township staff and highlighted upcoming community events, including 
the Halloween parade, a Heller Homestead Park ribbon cutting, and a 100-year celebration for the Se-
Wy-Co Fire Station. 
 
 
10. STAFF REPORTS 
 
 A. ACTING TOWNSHIP MANAGER 
 
Jay Finnigan reported that Easton Road paving was complete and the road was open in both directions; 
the Black River Road Bridge had also reopened. He reiterated the state process for filling vacancies if 
council cannot select a replacement and thanked staff and Council.
