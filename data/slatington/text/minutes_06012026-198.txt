Alexa Vreeland, 247 So. Walnut St., signed in and was recognized to speak.  Ms. Vreeland asked

Ms. Vreeland indicated that if the Borough posted its ordinances on its website, it would
dramatically reduce the Right-To-Know requests.  The Borough is currently working on

Jared Keichel – 535 Diamond Street.  Signed in and was recognized to speak.  Mr. Keichel
informed the Council that his dog was attacked and killed about a year ago. Mr. Keichel also
feels an animal control officer would be beneficial to the Borough.  
The Council discussed hiring an Animal Control Officer.  Solicitor Young indicated that the
State laws trump any and all Borough ordinances, but the ordinances do need to be up dated.
Solicitor Young added that the County currently does not have adequate funding for enough
animal control officers to assist municipalities, especially the smaller ones.  Moreover, many
municipalities do not have the funding for a full-time animal control officer.
Solicitor Young stated the difference between an animal control officer and a humane officer:
 A humane officer has police abilities – the ability to file charges and arrest someone for
violation of the dangerous dog provisions.  Solicitor Young suggested contacting the
Lehigh Valley Humane Society because their animal control officers are licensed
humane officers and they can investigate the care aspects, i.e., abandonment, cruelty,
neglect, etc.  
 The standard animal control officer does not have the ability to make arrests or bring
charges.  Solicitor Young stated there are different qualifications, certification, and
training for an animal control officer.
Councilor Schnaars stated that if the Borough were to hire an animal control officer, they
wouldn’t be able to bring charges, etc.  Therefore, they would need to ask for assistance from
the Police.  Councilor Schnaars asked if the code enforcement officer could also be the animal
control officer.  Solicitor Young indicated that they would need the necessary training to fulfill
the requirements of that position.
Police Chief Borst stated that if a municipality gets a dog and they are contractually working
with the Humane Society, those municipalities call and get a response in real time. Solicitor
Young mentioned it is possible to have a police officer also trained as an animal control officer.
Councilor Schnaars stated that the price for the humane society was expensive but is willing to
go back and look at it again.  Currently, when a dog is picked up in the Borough, it is taken to
the kennel at the Sewer Plant.  The Borough’s contracted kennel is then called to retrieve the
dog which is usually at the end of the day.
Gene Stalnecker described another incident with his neighbor’s dog that went to court.  He
indicated that after the court hearing and after a determination by the court to impose
restrictions on the dog, the dog was let outside its enclosure without the required supervision
and unleashed, as required.   The County warden is not in proximity with the Borough to
respond to issue that arise like this one.  If the Borough contracts with the Humane Society, the
Humane Society would be the organization to respond.  However, this is always dependent on if
staff was available.  The concern is always the response time.  Mr. Stalnecker suggested
contacting Walnutport to see if they would be interested in partnering with an animal control
officer and/or the Humane Society.
