chairing the Venture Group.  However, Ms. Treskot also stated that the Venture Group has
indicated that it will continue to maintain the Trailhead and have made those intentions clear to the
County.  The Venture Group is asking for a little curtesy and consideration from the Council to
move forward.  Before the Council makes any executive decision concerning the Trailhead, the
Venture Group requests that the Council notify her first.

A. FINANCE/PLANNING/ZONING
David Schnaars– Chairman; Members: Kevin Steckel, William Shippers
1. Committee Member discussion on listed topics:
a. Discussion of the financing options for the repaving of Shadow Oaks Lane.  Councilor Schnaars
stated the Council had approved a paving study which will provide details about the paving and
infrastructure needs of the road.  Councilor Schnaars stated that the Borough has contracted with
Transcend Finance to produce on a long-term capital/financing plan.   Given the amount of work
that is needed, there is a consideration for issuing a bond next year, then a secondary bond
several years after.  Then continue to alternate bond issuances in regular succession.  Manager
Hambrecht informed the Committee the auditors will have the 2024 audit completed the first
week of August, and the 2025 audit will be done by the end of the calendar year.  This will give
the Borough the ability to move forward with the bond issue in 2027.

2
b. Discussion of utility billing and ensuring that all invoiced utilities (Water/Sewer/Garbage) are
billed consistently.  For example, currently the garbage billing is January – March, while the
Water and Sewer billing is November – January. Councilor Schnaars indicated that this
inconsistency was primarily due to the timing of the signing of the garbage contract. Since the
Borough will be transitioning to a new utility billing system in the fall, and since the garbage
contract will be expiring at the end of the calendar year, it was suggested to have
water/sewer/garbage billed the same.
c. Manager Hambrecht will contact Solicitor Young to start putting an RFP (request for proposal)
together for the garbage company.   Councilor Schnaars would like this to be placed on the next
committee agenda.  
d. Budgeting.  Manager Hambrecht informed the Committee that the first budget meeting (Police)
was held this morning.  The Water and Sewer are scheduled for tomorrow, July 21, 2026.  These
meetings are to accomplish a couple of things, including current spend and targets for 2027.
e. Discussion of agenda/minutes/multi-media adjustments in Council room (attachment).   Manager
Hambrecht provided information from CivicPlus regarding upgrades to the Borough’s approach
to the agendas and minutes.  These upgrades would include a paperless agenda and minutes
program, live streaming options and a public portal for people to access.  The CivicPlus
upgrades would not include the monitors and upgrades to the audio system.  Those upgrades
would require contracting with a separate vendor.  The goal is to obtain two (2) additional
monitors, one (1) in each corner behind Council, upgrade the audio system, including a mic at
the podium and live streaming capabilities.  Manager Hambrecht explained that the AI
functionality would save the Borough a lot of time as it summarizes the audio and produces an
initial document that can be reviewed/edited.  Manager Hambrecht stated each councilor would
use a tablet rather than paper documents.  The Committee agreed to have a representative from
Civic Plus attend the meeting and provide a demonstration.  The initial investment would be
$14,550.00 for year 1; going forward would be $11,500.00, annually.  President Mayberry
would like a demonstration from Civic Plus.   
f. Main Street Zoning Overlay - Discussion of zoning classification (Residential/Commercial). Jim
Macort (KCE) attended the Council meeting on July 13, and it was agreed upon that the
Borough should move forward.  President Mayberry spoke with Solicitor Young and wanted to
make sure they have the exact properties that we are doing.  Manager Hambrecht to reach out to
Mr. Macort to secure a 8.5 x 11inch version of the map and a listing of all impacted properties.
g. Visioning.  Councilor Schnaars would like more information on the 100 steps project.  Manager
Hambrecht informed the Committee that the bid results came in last Thursday for the 100 steps
project.  He stated that the original amount was $400,000.00.  Due to delays in the process, the
Borough sought additional monies and got another $220,000, or a new total of $620,000.00.
The low bid came back at $845,892. This puts Borough at a projected deficit of $273,272.47.
Manager Hambrecht asked the Committee if the borough will be agreeable to pay the
$273,272.47 so the Borough can move forward with this project.   Councilor Schnaars feels we
have to move forward.  Since the work would be done next year, this can be calculated in the
2027 budget.  Manager Hambrecht brought up a possible concern.  He stated that E. Washington
St. is expected to be repaved this year.  Manager Hambrecht is concerned that the work that will
be done on the 100 steps, which is near E. Washington St., will impact the freshly paved road.
Councilor Schnaars indicated that it will be the responsibility of the contractor to ensure all
roads are returned to their original condition.  Councilor Schnaars would like this placed on the
next regular Council agenda to approve the $273,272 for the 2027 budget.  
Manager Hambrecht reached out to Community Leasing the regarding the $800,000.00 for the
new fire truck.  The agreement states the Fire Department will pay the $400,000 and the
Borough will pay $800,000.00 for a total of $1,200,000.00.   Manager Hambrecht provided
financing options for 10 years, 12 years, and 15 years.  The Borough’s payment responsibility
for the 10-year is $104,483.14, annually; 12-years is $92,382.29, annually; and the 15-year is
$79,247.93, annually.  The percentage associated interest rate for the 12-and 15-year options is
5.41% and 5.17% for the 10-years.  Manager Hambrecht reached out so quickly to secure the
financing so the Borough can benefit from the proposed discount of $80k. Manager Hambrecht
spoke with the Borough’s auditing firm, and he was told that the Pennsylvania Emergency
Management Agency (PEMA) also provided financing options at a rate as low as 2%.  Manager
Hambrecht reached out PEMA but was told the financing is only available for emergency
management organizations.  Councilor Schnaars would like to have a special meeting in order to
approve this.  
There were no questions from the committee members; the Finance Committee Meeting was
adjourned.
