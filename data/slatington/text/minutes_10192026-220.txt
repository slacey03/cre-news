PRESENTATIONS:
Charleah Sandt is requesting to open up a recovery house in the Borough for individuals with
addiction and mental health issues.  Ms. Sandt’s mission is to provide a safe, clean, and loving
environment for both males and females during their recovery. This mission is to build a long-term
foundation for individuals recovering from alcohol and/or drug addiction and mental health
diagnosis.  Ms. Sandt indicated that every individual has the right to reclaim their life and
rediscovering their true potential.  This project will help in that regard.
Accepted individuals would be required to attend a 12-step spiritual program as well as individual
counseling with an approved sponsor, in conjunction with other required services depending on the
individual’s personal situation.  Ms. Sandt would create a network of support for each individual
that extends beyond the walls of the recovery house.  The goal is to break down barriers, reduce
stigmas, promote a society where recovery is celebrated and embraced, and provide hope and build
a future for the individual.
Currently, the only treatment center in Slatington is Treatment Trends Inc., which opened in
September of 2025.  Ms. Sandt informed the Committee that from January 2026 to June 2026, there
were 540 opioid related products dispensed from the healthcare vending machine at the Trailhead.
From July 2026 to August 2026 there were 177 opioid related products dispensed from the same
machine.
Regarding the housing for people suffering from addiction in the Borough, there are 151 people
living with others temporarily, 37 are in transitional housing and sheltered programs, and 38 are
considered “unhoused” and are without stable housing.  There are no recovery houses in the
Borough’s area to meet the obvious need.  Ms. Sandt is working by herself and not affiliated with
any other organization.
Councilor Schnaars asked Ms. Sandt what her qualifications are and she stated she is a recovering
addict and has been a paralegal for 15 years.

Greg Kurtz, representing Advanced Code, provided information on its fee schedules for both
residential and non-residential buildings.  Mr. Kurtz offered two (2) fee scheduled:
 A square footage base fee schedule; and
 A percentage base fee schedule.
It was recommended by Mr. Kurtz that the Borough adapt the square foot-based fee schedule for the
residential work and the percentage-based fee schedule for the commercial work.  The
administration fees are a variable percentage, i.e. 10% for residential, 30% for commercial.
Manager Hambrecht noted that the fees proposed by Advanced Code are consistent with current
Borough fees.  Manager Hambrecht also informed the Committee the company the Borough is
currently using Key Codes and has experienced long delays in turning projects around.  Whereas, in
a test period with Advanced Code, the Borough experienced turnarounds of a couple of days.  In
fact, Advanced Code has a 5-day turnaround as a rule, with few exceptions.  Mr. Kurtz added they
are willing to work with the Borough concerning the fee schedule.
PUBLIC COMMENT:
Bryon Reed, 405 Diamond Street, signed in and was recognized to speak.  Mr. Reed questioned Ms.
Einolf regarding a letter he received stating that he had 30 days to schedule an appointment for an
inspection of his rental property.  Manager Hambrecht clarified that Mr. Reed was to call Ms.
Einolf and schedule the appointments for any of his rentals within 30 days. President Mayberry
informed Mr. Reed the Borough will be inspecting all rentals, not just new tenants at this time.  
Mr. Reed also revisited an earlier discussion regarding the proposed change to the Borough’s water
billings.  Mr. Reed stated it does not cost the Borough any more to provide water service to
buildings used for residential than it does to provide water service to a building used for a rental
unit.  Any proposal regarding apartments must treat all apartments equally.  There cannot be two (2)
classes of apartments as it would be illegal.  Therefore, units in George Dillard Manor and
Mountainview, in Seventh Street Village, should be billed the same as all other rentals.  Those
rentals are often occupied by the most economically challenged people in the town.  Therefore, any
rate changes on those rental units would penalize the poorest residents.  Mr. Reed stated many of
the rentals have an agreement with their landlords to pay for the water bill.  Some residents/renters
feel they are paying for water they do not use.  Additionally, having landlords install individual
water plumbing/meters would be a costly endeavor.

a. Anita Einolf to present status of Rental Inspection Program/State of Code: Ms. Einolf
completed 163 rental inspections, 102 have failed, 61 have passed.  Another 25 rental units were
inspected today and all passed.  Ms. Einolf displayed the inspection program she is using on her
laptop computer?  Manager Hambrecht stated it will take about one (1) year to obtain an
accurate number of rentals in the Borough because many have gone without inspections and
there has been little Borough enforcement prior to this effort.  An important item that needs to be
discussed and resolved is what constitutes a tenant within the Borough.  Also, Ms. Einolf is
recommending that the Borough extends the inspection window from 30 days to 90 days so
landlords would not be burdened by having to get a reinspection done within 30 days if a tenant
vacates a property soon after moving in.
Ms. Einolf also stated that the Borough is currently working under the 2003 International
Property Maintenance Code (IPMC).  The current IPMC is 2024 and the Borough must pass an
ordinance that reflects the use of the 2024 IPMC version.  The recommendation would be to
word the ordinance in such a way that the proposed ordinance updates when the IPMC is
issued/updated.  Ms. Einolf asked if the Borough would adopt the whole IPMC or just a portion
of it?
Ms. Einolf stated there are issues with parking spots.  Specifically, there are units that don’t have
the required two (2) spots for each unit.  President Mayberry stated there are some buildings that
are grandfathered in and maybe should be addressed.  
Ms. Einolf also discussed electric panels and how each unit should have their own panel or a
common area where each tenant can access.  Ms. Einolf also found multiple violations regarding
the electrical panels.
Regarding inspections in general, President Mayberry asked Ms. Einolf what would be a
reasonable frequency for inspections?  Ms. Einolf indicated that every three (3) years would
suffice.  Ms. Einolf suggested splitting the borough up into three (3) sections to accommodate
this plan. 
Discussion of the potential changes to Ord. 701, including rental unit inspection frequency, fees
schedule changes to include rooming units, and defining Rooming Units vs Rental Units.
Manager Hambrecht informed the Committee that the inspection fees are $65.00 per rental unit
but the ordinance does not speak to rooming units specifically.  As an example, Manager
Hambrecht cited the property at the corner of Main and W. Church that has 36 rooms.  These
rooms do not have a kitchen or a bathroom.  Therefore, if the Borough were to treat this property
like other rentals within the Borough, the cost would be over $2k, which would not be fair. The
draft changes for Ordinance 701 would define the requirements for inspection and cost between
a rental/housing unit and residential rooming unit.  Ms. Einolf informed the Committee of the
differences for inspections between a room and a residential housing/rental inspection.  Once
there is a defined number of rentals, the frequency of the inspections can then be determined.
Rental Inspection fees for Housing Units and Rooming Units.  The Committee agreed a rooming
unit is not the same as a housing unit. Manager Hambrecht suggested using the same fee
structure for the rooming units as is done for the housing units, except for a reduced cost.
