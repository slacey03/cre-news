SPECIAL PRESENTATION:
Lehigh Valley Planning Commission presentation
The Chief Planner with the Lehigh Valley Planning Commission (LVPC), joined with her colleague, and
presented to Council.  They both took turns explaining that the LVPC is really two (2) governmental entities
under one roof. LVPC is looking at county planning for Lehigh and Northampton Counties.  The LVPC does
a lot of work pertaining to land use, transportation, economic initiatives, and environmental challenges.  The
LVPC spends a good deal of its time fitting those pieces together through its planning efforts.  It is also the
Federally designated metropolitan planning organization that focuses on the transportation aspects of the
counties.  LVPC acts as Slatington’s municipal planners when there is a subdivision or land development
project proposed.
A PowerPoint presentation was distributed to the Council.  LVPC Staff indicated generally that the northern
Lehigh County area has remained relatively stable in its population. However, it is expecting job growth
within the six (6) municipalities to increase from around 5,800 to around 9,600 over the next several years,
with most of that growth in Weisenburg.  LVPC staff added that Weisenburg accounts for roughly seven (7)
out of ten of those additional jobs. Slatington is more modest with growth in the neighborhood of about 100
jobs over the next 30 years.  LVPC staff indicated that transportation to and from Slatington is critical due to
jobs mostly being outside of Slatington.
Mr. Wineburg informed the Council that with the population growth in the area, LVPC has looked at 143
residential lots, or units, for possible development across the six (6) municipalities from 2021 to 2025.
Slatington has not had any new land development.
For the non-residential side, there has been 1.3 million sq. feet of development.   Warehouses account for
nearly three-quarters of it.  Agricultural buildings also make up a significant portion of that.
The LVPC considers itself as a proactive planner for all the below items:
 Traffic, including:
o Safety;
o road congestion;
o conflicts at intersections;
 External pressures that can increase development pressure within the Borough, and
 Infrastructure capacity (water, sewer, storm water and emergency response).  
LVPC staff indicated that Slatington has been identified as an established center for planning purposes.  To
that end, LVPC staff wanted to know if there are any infrastructure aging concerns that the Borough has at
this time.  LVPC is here to help the Borough with any concerns.

6. Acela to lead discussion on Shadow Oaks Rd. repaving. Ms. Wieder has completed the analysis of
the existing system.  Acela has confirmed that the current infrastructure is inadequate.  The existing
system is too shallow and not pitched properly.  The pipe network that Acela is proposing requires
a greater pitch as per industry standards.  Because of that, the new pipes will not align properly with
the existing pipes. Therefore, to properly convey the storm water, the proposed work will have to
bypass the existing system and continue through the downstream to the outfalls. Easements for
construction and DEP approval may be required.
Manager Hambrecht offered the following thought: If the Borough simply repaves Shadow Oaks
Rd. without addressing the storm water issues, the rainwater will simply run off the newly paved
surface rather than seeping into the cracked road surface.  This could have the potential of flooding
property owners’ front yards.  This is not the prudent approach and the Borough should seriously
consider these facts, including the cost, before moving forward.  The correct approach to this
project is to address the infrastructure needs prior to the repaving. However, that would bring the
cost to about $2,000,000 vs just the paving at about $700,000.  Ms. Wieder suggested phasing the
project – instead of doing all the work at once, complete 1 (one) outfall at a time.   Ms. Wieder
suggested grant opportunities.  President Mayberry would like this on the next committee meeting.
