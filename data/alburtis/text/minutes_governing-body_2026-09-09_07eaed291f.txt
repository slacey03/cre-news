Macungie Ambulance Special Presentation-  
Chris Greb was in attendance to provide a brief presentation on an emergency situation that took place on August 15th at 
the Alburtis Fire Company Social Club.  
 
Each year more than 350,000 out of hospital cardiac arrests occur across the United States.  Less than 15% of those 
patients survive.  The American Heart Association, CDC, EMS and Healthcare agencies across the country work every 
day to improve this statistic.  While some gains have been made in recent years with an emphasis on chest compressions 
and hands-only CPR, the change of survival from cardiac arrest is still incredibly low. 
  
When someone does survive cardiac arrest, it is almost always because of a coordinated effort of bystanders, first 
responders, EMS, and hospitals.  Nobody survives cardiac arrest because of the actions of one person.  It is always a team 
effort. 
 
While inside, Mr. Greenawalt told others that he had really bad indigestion and felt as though he needed to go 
home.  Several minutes later the Alburtis Fire Company Social Club President Scot Smith, found him in his car, 
unresponsive, without a pulse, and in cardiac arrest.  Bystanders Nicole Lichtenwalner, who happens to also be a nurse, 
and Donald Smith Sr, who happens to also be a firefighter and former chief of the Alburtis Fire Department, advised 
others to call 911 and began CPR.Firefighters from the Lower Macungie Fire Department, Alburtis station were 
dispatched along with Macungie Ambulance Corps.  The initial arriving firefighters, Firefighter / Paramedic Andrew 
Miller and Firefighters Randy Trexler and Gary Arndt, arrived and found CPR in progress.  They continued CPR, applied 
for an AED, and delivered two shocks restarting Mr. Greenawalt’s heart.  Officer Bulger from the Alburtis Police 
Department also responded to offer assistance as needed. Two ambulances from the Macungie Ambulance Corps arrived 
shortly thereafter.  Paramedics Rachael Diehl and Jake Miller, along with EMTs Christopher Andrews and Yailis Baez 
continued advanced care.  During transport to the hospital, after regaining consciousness briefly, Mr. Greenawalt suffered 
cardiac arrest again and required another defibrillation shock. Upon arriving at the hospital Mr. Greenawalt’s care was 
transferred over to the staff of Lehigh Valley Health Network where he was further stabilized and cardiac catheterization 
was completed to alleviate the blockage that caused his cardiac arrest. 
  
Mr. Greenawalt survived his cardiac arrest when most others do not.  This was not because of luck, but because of the 
choreographed efforts of a team of people, from bystanders, first responders, EMS and hospital staff. I am here today to 
most importantly thank all of those involved in this amazing case of life-saving care.  But also, to let you all know that, 
when emergencies happen, it takes a team to save a life.  We urge every person to learn CPR so that, when the time 
comes, you can be part of a life-saving effort that will, hopefully, have an excellent ending like this story does today.
