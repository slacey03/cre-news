Public Works, Thomas McHugh 
 
 Mr. McHugh submitted the following report on the activities of the Street Department 
personnel during the month of July 2026: 
 
 During the month of July, the Street Department continued routine maintenance and 
infrastructure improvements throughout the Borough.  Department personnel inspected and 
replaced faded traffic signs, completed pothole repairs, maintained Borough-owned properties 
through regular mowing and weed control, repainted handicap parking spaces, and coordinated 
tree maintenance at the Community Park.  Preparations were also completed for PennDOT’s 
upcoming slurry seal project on Philadelphia and Reading Avenues, including the purchase of 
inlet risers for installation during paving.  In addition, curb replacement projects on East Fifth, 
East Sixth, and Shaner Streets are substantially complete, with only minor punch list items 
remaining.

Solicitor, Chris Hartman 
 
Mr. Hartman opened discussion regarding the underground electrical easement that was 
previously requested by Damore Holdings LLC.  Mr. Hartman reviewed that Damore Holdings 
LLC had asked the Borough for a utility easement to access Borough property to make an 
underground electrical connection between his property at 27 South Reading Avenue and an 
electrical box located on Peach Street.  In the process of requesting that easement, it was 
discovered that the location of the shared access easement on the deed plans was not where it is 
in actuality.  Mr. Hartman explained that in an agreement with the Borough, Mr. Damore agreed 
to finance the surveying by Aston Surveyors & Engineers in order to amend and restate the 
shared access easement as well as the legal fees for preparing the documents for the restatement 
and the easement agreement.  He reported that the surveying has been done, and the documents 
are complete.  Mr. Damore has advised that the electrical work will be entirely underground and 
will be done in segments as to not inhibit the flow of traffic through the rear of the Eastern Berks 
Regional Police parking lot.  Mr. Hartman asked Council to consider motions, individually, to 
approve both the shared access easement restatement as well as the electrical utility easement for 
Damore Holdings LLC.   
After minimal discussion, Mr. Cascino made a motion, second by Mrs. Rhodes, to 
approve the execution and recording of the Amendment and Restatement of Declaration of 
Shared Access Easement.  The roll call vote reflected the following members voting “yes”:  Mrs. 
Rhodes, Mr. McHugh, Mr. Cascino, Mr. Endy, Mr. Neubauer, Mr. Miller, and Mr. Deery.  The 
motion was approved. 
 
Mr. McHugh made a motion, second by Mr. Neubauer, to approve the underground 
electric utility easement for Damore Holdings LLC.  The roll call vote reflected the following 
members voting “yes”:  Mrs. Rhodes, Mr. McHugh, Mr. Cascino, Mr. Endy, Mr. Neubauer, Mr. 
Miller, and Mr. Deery.  The motion was approved. 
 
 
Public Comments 
 
Mr. Deery called for public comment and recognized a resident identified only as Nicole.  
She expressed her concern regarding the coke stockpile at the railroad tracks in the area to the 
rear of the Boyertown Community Ambulance building.  She stated that the noise of the 
operation affects residents’ sleep and wellbeing, with noise sometimes as early as 5:30 a.m.  The  
 

August 3, 2026 
         Page/8 
 
dust from the coke coats everything outside and can also be a health hazard to medically 
compromised residents.  She also heard that the coke is only being stored here, it is not even  
utilized locally.  Shaner Street resident Denise Trout reminded Council of her previous complaint 
of the weeds and overgrowth behind her property that borders the railroad tracks, and stated that 
the railroad would not be able to transport the coke beyond her property even if they wanted to.  
Attempts by Nicole and Ms. Trout to contact the DEP and the Berks Redevelopment Authority 
have gone unanswered, and Ms. Trout advised that she has contacted a local news outlet and they 
will be out next week to report on the issue.  Nicole reported that the DEP has issued notices of 
violation to the Colebrookdale Railroad every year but nothing seems to be remedied.  She gave 
copies of the notices of violation to Mrs. Loder.  Mr. Hartman explained that the railroad is 
federally regulated and is not under the jurisdiction of the Borough.  Therefore, there is very little 
that can be done at local level.  He advised that he will research what, if any, recourse the 
Borough would have in this situation.  Another resident in attendance, Matt Hiryak, suggested 
contacting the EPA at federal level, as they have strict rules on coke storage.
