Public Works, Thomas McHugh 
 
 Mr. McHugh submitted the following report on the activities of the Street Department 
personnel during the month of September 2026: 
 
 During the month of September, the Street Department completed four days of crack 
sealing on roadways, alleys, curb lines, and the Community Park walk path.  The 2026 street 
paving project progressed well, with most punch-list items completed, and curb painting was 
completed on newly paved and other faded curbs.  The department also repaired a catch basin at 
Walker Drive and South Reading Avenue, completed all planned 2026 thermoplastic line repairs, 
and performed annual rust protection treatments on department vehicles.  Department personnel 
assisted with preparation for several community events, while a student completed community

service work by painting park benches and tables, and by washing trucks.   Upcoming work 
includes street sweeping, property maintenance, Halloween Parade cleanup, and leaf collection.   
 
 Mr. McHugh then opened discussion regarding an ordinance to prohibit the depositing of 
grass clippings in the public right-of-way.  Reiterating previous discussion from the Public 
Works Committee meeting, he stated that he has received numerous complaints from Borough 
residents regarding the clippings in the street, which get slippery in the wet weather, clog the 
storm drains, and create a general eyesore.  He advised that door-to-door notices would do no 
good without an ordinance to support it and proposed an ordinance similar to the one regarding 
snow removal.  The ordinance would allow for a first-offense warning, to help educate and set 
residents up for success, rather than penalize residents from the first step.  Mr. Hartman stated he 
sees no difficulty in creating an ordinance that fits the situation, if that is the direction Council 
wants to take.  After some discussion, Mr. Deery respectfully suggested tabling the ordinance 
discussion until the next Council meeting when all members can be present and offer their 
thoughts, and Council agreed.

Mr. Deery recessed the regular meeting at 7:48 p.m. for Council to go into executive 
session regarding a utility billing issue. 
 
Mr. Deery reconvened the regular meeting at 7:57 p.m.  Mr. Hartman explained that the 
executive session was to discuss two utility accounts, 42 East Third Street and 25 East Fifth 
Street.  The owners of these two properties are seeking relief for a quarterly bill as they disagree 
with the water consumption and amount due.  On the basis of those discussions, Mr. Hartman 
recommended that Council motion to amend the agenda of this meeting to include these two 
situations to allow motions to be made on them.  The motion to amend the agenda was made by 
Mr. Deery, second by Mr. McHugh, and approved by the membership present.  
 
Mr. Hartman asked for any public comment specifically on this issue, and recognized 
Mark and Kristi Dumin, the owners of 25 East Fifth Street, who were seeking relief for their 
second quarter water and sewer bill which was higher than usual.  Mr. Dumin stated his 
contention that the increase in water consumption reflected on his bill was due to water hammer 
effect, or hydraulic shock, caused by road construction in the area.  He reported that the street, 
curb, and sidewalk were disrupted at his residence, therefore hydraulic shock could be the logical 
explanation for his excessive water consumption but gave other theories as well.  Mr. Dumin 
also expressed his opinion that since having received a new water meter, his consumption is 
more typical now, and therefore there must have been a problem with the previous water meter.  
Mr. Deery confirmed with Mrs. Loder that no other property owners in the road construction 
area reported issues of excessive water consumption on their accounts, and that the previous 
water meter was sent out for testing at the Borough’s expense and was found to be functioning 
within American Water Works Association standards.  Mr. Deery explained to Mr. Dumin that 
the two utility bills he received previous to the contested one were estimated bills due to the 
inability of Inframark to be able to read the meters over the winter, and that his increase in 
consumption could have been occurring over previous quarters and not noticed until the second 
quarter actual read was taken.  After much discussion, Mr. Hartman summarized that while Mr. 
Dumin’s theories are speculative, Council can only rely on the facts, the evidence they have 
seen, to make their determination.  With no one else experiencing a hydraulic shock issue during 
construction, and the meter being tested as accurate within standards, Council does not support

a relief or forgiveness of Mr. and Mrs. Dumin’s utility bill.  Mr. Hartman suggested that Council 
make a motion to enter this decision into record.   
 
Mr. Deery made a motion, second by Mrs. Rhodes, to deny relief for the second quarter 
water and sewer bill of 25 East Fifth Street, but to allow two quarters for repayment with no 
penalties incurred during that time.  The roll call vote reflected the following members voting 
“yes”:  Mrs. Rhodes, Mr. McHugh, Mr. Endy, Mr. Neubauer, and Mr. Deery.  The motion was 
approved.  
 
Mr. Neubauer made a motion, second by Mr. McHugh, to deny relief for the third quarter 
water and sewer bill of 42 East Third Street, but to allow four quarters for repayment with no

“yes”:  Mrs. Rhodes, Mr. McHugh, Mr. Endy, Mr. Neubauer, and Mr. Deery.  The motion was 
approved.
