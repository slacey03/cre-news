 Mr. Deery introduced Sue Landes of Recreation and Park Solutions to present her 
Boyertown Area Parks and Recreation Project report.  The purpose of the report was to ascertain 
if there was any area interest in creating a coalition of municipalities working together to provide 
better park and recreation services in the Boyertown Area School District. Ms. Landes explained 
that she collected various demographic statistics in the Boroughs of Bally, Bechtelsville, and 
Boyertown, as well as the Townships of Colebrookdale, Douglass (Berks), Earl, New Hanover, 
and Upper Frederick.  Douglass (Montgomery) and Washington Townships were not included in 
the survey as they already belong to another coalition.  Ms. Landes proceeded to give highlights 
of the report.  Boyertown Borough meets the nationwide standard of 10 acres of parkland per 
1,000 people, however Upper Frederick and Colebrookdale Townships do not.  Boyertown 
Borough is average in relation to the surrounding townships regarding net per capita spending on 
park and recreation, but all are below national average.  Ms. Landes then explained that surveys 
were sent to the officials of the previously mentioned seven municipalities to gather opinions on 
collaboration and goals.  Every municipality responded except Upper Frederick Township.  A 
brief summary of the results shows that most municipalities identified the negative issues of 
declining volunteer turnout and maintenance cost increases; the highest priorities are maintaining 
the amenities they already possess, reducing costs, and increasing volunteerism; the lowest 
priorities are the building of more athletic fields and acquiring more land; and the features that 
would best benefit from a regional effort would be community gardens, dog parks, and fully 
accessible playgrounds.  The summary of municipal interest shows 50 percent of the survey 
respondents were interested in collaborating, 72 percent of respondents were interested in 
attending meetings to discuss possible collaborative efforts, and 25 percent of respondents would 
support using municipal funds to collaborate on park and recreation initiatives.  After discussing 
the benefits of working together, Ms. Landes concluded that the Borough and the surrounding 
municipalities would benefit from a coalition, and that there appears to be a mutual sense of 
interest among this collection of municipalities.  Ms. Landes and Dave Kraybill from the 
Pottstown Regional Community Foundation did make it clear to Council that his foundation’s 
responsibility was putting together the report and providing the Borough with the information to 
move forward, but moving forward would be the responsibility of the Borough and the other 
municipalities working with each other, not being facilitated by the Pottstown Regional 
Community Foundation.  Council members extended their gratitude to Ms. Landes for the 
thorough work she did on the report.  After some discussion, Council decided to table the subject 
until the next meeting of Council on August 3rd to further delve into the report and develop ideas 
on how to proceed.

Solicitor, Chris Hartman 
 
Mr. Hartman opened discussion to review the amendment to the 
Boyertown/Colebrookdale/Pike Joint Zoning Ordinance proposed by Colebrookdale Township to 
provide regulations for Data Center use as a Special Exception use within the GI/Q General 
Industrial/Quarry District of Colebrookdale Township.  He advised that he has been working 
with Colebrookdale Township to develop an ordinance regarding data centers to be ready and 
have it in place in the event that it is needed.  He reported that the ordinance is very restrictive, 
and applicants would have to apply to the Colebrookdale Township Zoning Hearing Board for 
special exception use.  He advised that as a Joint Zoning Board of three municipalities, only one 
of the three municipalities has to develop an ordinance and get this in place.  Mr. Hartman stated 
he would like to package this ordinance with the Borough proposal for the zoning change 
regarding special event space, as well as a proposed Joint Zoning Board amendment regarding 
the use of facilities such as storage pods and containers on private property.  He reported that all
