Public Comments 
 
Mr. Deery called for public comment and recognized Denise Trout, Borough resident.  
She updated Council that she has tried to speak to the Berks County Redevelopment Authority 
regarding the overgrowth from the railroad track property that is encroaching upon her yard.  The 
Redevelopment Authority has yet to solve the issue.   
 
Mr. Deery then recognized a person who is still only known as Nicole.  Nicole gave a 
nutshell presentation of approximately nine years of railroad history, including ownership and  
leasing items, as well as mentioning a portion of the Redevelopment Authority property that the 
Borough leases from them.  She posed questions, as she did in a previous meeting, such as who 
is responsible for the railroad and who has authority and oversight over the activities occurring 
there.  She expressed her concern that the business that was supposed to be using the stored coke  

September 8, 2026 
         Page/7 
 
 
is no longer using it, as well as continued complaints of noise and coke dust.  Mr. Hartman 
reiterated that the Borough has no authority over the railroad, including the tracks, the property, 
and the operations.  He explained that the Redevelopment Authority owns the land, and the 
Colebrookdale Railroad leases it from them.  Mr. Hartman also clarified that the portion of 
Redevelopment Authority property that the Borough leases is the property that houses the salt 
shed, and that it was simply a real estate transaction that had nothing to do with the railroad.  
Mrs. Loder clarified that there two separate entities using those railroad tracks.  The 
Colebrookdale Railroad runs the passenger service, while the Eastern Berks Gateway Railroad 
operates the freight service, including the coke operation.  Nicole expressed concern over the 
types of freight materials being shipped on the train, and Mrs. Loder explained that the Federal 
Railroad Administration controls the materials shipped and the Gateway Railroad does not have 
the right to refuse shipments.  Mrs. Rhodes provided an update of information she gathered at the 
last meeting she attended with the Colebrookdale Railroad, such as the new conveyor system and 
the sprayer system they are testing to try to minimize the coke dust.  She reported that the DEP 
has checked in with them and is pleased with the progress they have made so far.  Mrs. Rhodes 
advised that her intention is to have monthly meetings with the Colebrookdale Railroad to stay 
informed.  Mr. Hartman cautioned that Mrs. Rhodes’ line of communication with the 
Colebrookdale Railroad is only a courtesy, not a business partnership, and that courtesy will only 
last as long as the Railroad wishes.   Mr. Hartman suggested that if the residents are not seeing 
resolution through contacting the Redevelopment Authority, perhaps their next step should be to 
contact the Berks County Board of Commissioners, who are elected County officials, and who 
may have some authority or influence with the Redevelopment Authority.
