 CENTRE TOWNSHIP BOARD OF SUPERVISORS 
MINUTES  
 May 26, 2026     
 
 
The regular meeting of the Centre Township Board of Supervisors was held on 
May 26, 2026 at the Centre Township Municipal Building, 449 Bucks Hill Road 
Mohrsville, PA.   Chairman James Fitterling called the meeting to order at 7:00 
P.M. and led the Pledge of Allegiance, followed by a Moment of Silence.  The 
following were present:  James Fitterling, Melissa Krishock Member, Daniel 
Unterkofler, Member, Solicitor Matthew Fessler, Ryan Rhode, Jeff Fiant, and 
Constable Kevin Noecker, residents Brett Thomas, Erica Kirkpatrick, and Jefferson 
Seidel.  
 
APPROVAL OF MEETING MINUTES: 
A motion was made by Melissa and seconded by Daniel to approve the 
minutes from April 28, 2026.    Motion passed.  
 
A motion was made by Melissa and seconded by Daniel to pay bills thru 
May 26, 2026. Motion passed. 
 
SOLICITOR’S REPORT:   NONE 
 
ROAD DEPARTMENT REPORT:  The road department has performed 
maintenance on the saws and other equipment used during tree 
trimming. Pot hole repairs were performed.  Walked and marked out 
Hickory and Grove for milling and paving.  Boom mowed several roads 
in the Township. 
 
ENGINEER’S REPORT: 
The bids were opened for the Grove Road project and New Enterprise 
Stone and Lime was awarded the paving contract after underbidding H 
& K by approximately $12K.  Work is to be funded with Liquid Fuels 
money.  The Oil and Chip bid was awarded to AMS at $133492 beating 

the second bidder by $5K.  This project will also be funded with Liquid 
Fuels money.  A motion was made by James and seconded by Melissa to 
accept the bid from New Enterprise.  Motion passed.  A motion was 
made by James and seconded by Melissa to accept the bid by AMS for 
the oil and chip road project.  Motion passed.  
 
The board approved the Escrow release #7 for the Tiny Pear 
development.  A motion was made by James and seconded by Daniel to 
approve the #7 escrow release in the amount of $15303.  Motion 
passed.  
 
Mr. Rhode addressed a resident’s concern regarding a private alley that 
was mistakenly identified as a public road due to 911 signage.  The 
Township clarified that the alley remains private, and the resident was 
advised to coordinate with neighbors to manage delivery traffic.   
 
The Engineering and Road department will finalize the schedule for fuel 
pump replacement and the investigation of 101 Railroad Rd’s retaining 
wall by next meeting.  
 
ZONING OFFICER REPORT:   
Jeff Fiant of Kraft Municipal Services reported there were 9 permits 
issued totaling $170772.50 in construction value. 29 inspections were 
completed. The court hearing for 236 Shannaleigh Way was held on  
May 7th.  The owner was granted a 30 day extension to clean up the 
property.  The continued hearing is scheduled to June 11, 2026. 
 
The Board discussed the burning ordinance following complaints about 
a fire at a local farm.  Current rules allow open burning in AP zoning 
district if attended by an adult and at least 50 feet from property lines.  
While the ordinance prohibits burning during drought emergencies or 
when smoke is hazardous, the current language is considered vague 
regarding nuisance enforcement. 

A resident raised concerns about the Mohrsville Post Office Roof and a 
collapsing building at the Martin farm on North End Rd.  The post office 
roof allegedly has a four-inch bow and the Board will check if 
appropriate permits and engineering plans were filed for recent repairs. 
 
A resident raised concerns about the deteriorating condition of the 
Mohrsville Playground, a private facility managed by an association.  
The Board suggested that a formal maintenance complaint would allow 
code enforcement to investigate property.  Information regarding the 
Hefner Trust, which funds the playground was shared, noting 
Wilmington Trust is the current manager of the Trust. 
 
Mr. Seidel requested the implementation of a noise ordinance to 
address persistent disturbances from neighborhood subwoofers.  The 
Township does not have currently a noise ordinance.  The Board will 
obtain sample ordinances for the Board to review at the next meeting. 
 
Ms. Kirkpatrick informed the Board of the possible illegal application of 
Food Processing Residuals (FPRs) on land and its impact on water 
quality.  A cease-and-desist order was reportedly issued by the DEP for 
a property on Berne Road for applying residuals over snow.    The Board 
noted that any local ordinance cannot be more restrictive than state-
level DEP regulations. 
 
Ms. Kirkpatrick also inquired about her prior complaint regarding the 
“half-way House” being operated along River Road, and she was 
informed this matter was already discussed at a prior meeting and she 
can locate the discussion by reviewing the meeting minutes o that 
meeting.  
 
OLD BUSINESS: 
A motion was made by Melissa and seconded by James to approve the 
Penny Hill Sewer Module.  Motion passed.  

 
NEW BUSINESS:  NONE 
 
With there being no further discussion a motion was made by James  
and seconded by Melissa to adjourn the meeting at 8:26 P.M.
