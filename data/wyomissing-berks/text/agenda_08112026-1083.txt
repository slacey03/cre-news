1. Financial Condition, Spending Priorities, and Budget Accountability
My concern is not that the June financial statement proves that the Borough is immediately running out 
of money, nor that every expenditure is improper. At midyear, General Fund expenditures were 53% of 
the annual budget, and a substantial portion of the year-over-year increase reflects the one-time debt 
payment. The concern is structural: cash and investment balances are lower than the prior month and 
below comparable earlier periods, the adopted budget deliberately relies on fund balance, and the 
public has not been shown a specific cost-reduction plan or credible year-end forecast before the next 
budget cycle.
June 30 financial indicators
 Total cash and investments declined from $11,174,959 on May 31 to $10,760,566 on June 30 - a 
reduction of $414,393, or approximately 3.7%, in one month.
 The packet's 24-month chart shows that cash and investment balances are below comparable 
prior spring and summer periods. A one-month decrease may reflect timing, transfers, debt 
service, or capital activity; the longer trend nevertheless warrants a public explanation.
 The adopted General Fund budget anticipates $19,070,000 in revenue and $19,834,000 in 
expenditures, a planned deficit of $764,000. Across all reported funds, the budget anticipates 
$29,436,000 in revenue and $30,419,000 in expenditures, a planned deficit of $983,000.
 The reported General Fund year-to-date surplus of $2,427,447 should not be treated as a year-
end projection. By June 30, 93% of budgeted real-estate-tax revenue and 85% of budgeted grant 
revenue had already been received, while significant expenses remain for the second half of the 
year.
 General Fund tax revenue was $655,719 below the same period in 2025. Real-estate transfer tax 
alone was $367,601 below 2025, and interest revenue was $79,992 below 2025.

2. Borough Communications Concerning Abrams and the Berkshire 
Mall
The Mayor has publicly relayed projected dates and expectations concerning Abrams Realty and the 
Berkshire Mall, while Abrams itself has provided very limited public information. Earlier public 
discussions included a possible May 15 closing date. At the June 9 Council meeting, however, the Interim 
Borough Manager stated that Abrams was not the property owner, that the Borough had received no 
formal land-development plan or even a sketch plan, and that the discussions remained informational. 
Special counsel similarly emphasized that Abrams had not acquired the property or submitted a formal 
plan.
Against that record, public statements about expected purchases, closings, tenants, financing, 
schedules, or redevelopment outcomes require clear attribution. Residents should be able to distinguish 
among a documented representation from the prospective purchaser, an informal conversation, the 
Mayor's personal expectation, and an official Borough position. Without that distinction, a Borough

appearance of governmental confirmation.
This is especially important because the Borough may later exercise zoning, land-development, 
infrastructure, financing, enforcement, or agreement-approval authority affecting this property. 
Accurate, neutral communication protects both public confidence and the integrity of the Borough's 
later decision-making.

 State whether the information has been independently verified and whether it represents an

3. 915 Penn Avenue: Notice Failure, Legal Expense, and Corrective 
Accountability
At the May 12 Council meeting, counsel for nearby property owners publicly stated that his clients had 
not received the certified-mail notice required for the March 30 conditional-use hearing. The issue was 
raised before Council voted. The public was then told that there had been testimony that the notices 
were provided as required. The Borough later acknowledged that the certified-mail procedure had not 
been followed. The matter is now in the Berks County Court of Common Pleas, and the developer has 
intervened to defend the approval.
The filing of the appeal demonstrates that the notice issue did not simply disappear. It produced 
concrete legal exposure and taxpayer expense. That observation does not predict which party will 
prevail, and it does not require disclosure of privileged advice or litigation strategy. It does require 
Council to examine the nonprivileged administrative failure that created the dispute and to account 
publicly for the resulting cost.
During my spoken comment, I referred to the Solicitor's statement that "nothing will happen." Whatever 
the intended legal meaning of that reassurance, the subsequent appeal establishes that something did 
happen: the Borough is now defending litigation and incurring legal expense. Council should evaluate 
whether the advice and procedural oversight it received were adequate, without requiring disclosure of 
privileged communications.
Council is responsible for overseeing both the administration and the solicitor. Approving legal invoices 
is not a substitute for determining how a required procedural step was missed, who was responsible for 
verifying compliance, whether counsel or staff relied on an incomplete record, and what safeguards 
have now been implemented. An ongoing lawsuit should not prevent an after-action review of 
ministerial procedures such as preparing an adjoining-owner list, sending certified mail, preserving 
receipts, and confirming the file before a hearing or vote.
Requested 915 Penn accountability measures
 Report the Borough's legal expenses for 915 Penn Avenue as a separate monthly matter total, 
beginning with the notice dispute and continuing through final disposition. A matter-level total 
can be disclosed without revealing privileged descriptions or strategy.
 Identify the Borough role responsible for assembling the notice list, sending required notices, 
preserving proof of mailing, and certifying that the record is complete.
 State whether an after-action review was conducted and publish its nonprivileged findings and 
corrective actions.
 Adopt a mandatory land-use notice checklist requiring documented verification of publication, 
posting, mailing lists, certified-mail receipts, returned mail, radius or adjoining-owner 
determinations, and dates of compliance.

4. Right-to-Know Delay for Routine Financial Records
On June 1, 2026, I submitted a Right-to-Know request for March, April, and May financial records. The 
Borough invoked the additional response period and did not provide its final response until July 9, 
essentially at the end of the five-business-day initial period plus the additional 30-calendar-day period. 
March and April were closed financial periods, and the requested materials were routine municipal 
financial records. To the extent May records were not yet complete on June 1, that did not explain 
withholding completed March and April records until July 9.
I am not alleging in this submission that the response missed the date stated in the extension notice, 
and I cannot prove that the delay was intended to deter access. The accountability concern is the effect 
of the practice: waiting until the final permissible date to provide routine, already-completed financial 
records makes the information less useful for current committee and Council oversight and has a 
deterrent effect regardless of intent.
Section 901 of Pennsylvania's Right-to-Know Law requires a good-faith determination and a response as 
promptly as possible under the circumstances, with five business days as the outer limit for the initial 
response. Section 902 permits additional time only when a specified statutory condition applies, and the 
notice must state the reason for review and a reasonable response date. The additional 30 days should 
not operate as an unexplained default waiting period for every request, particularly where responsive 
records are routine and can be produced in completed monthly groups.
Requested RTKL and proactive-disclosure measures
 Provide a written explanation identifying the specific Section 902 ground relied upon for the 
June 1 request, the date each requested monthly record became available, and why completed 
portions were not produced on a rolling basis.

Job Description 
POSITION SUMMARY 
Serves as the Department Director in charge of and administrator for the Recreation Department, including 
recreation facilities and grounds maintenance, sports league programs, activity programming, parades and 
special events, the Stone House, and the Wyomissing Pool, including budget submission, expense tracking, 
program development, operations, and reporting. Serves at the pleasure of the Borough Council or their designee 
and provides general administration, development, direction, on financial, organizational  and operational work; 
performs other related work as required. 
Serves as a member of the Borough Management team, in a trusted leadership position, reporting directly to the 
Borough Manager or their designee. Provides supervision of a variety of specific and general management tasks. 
Develops annual department objectives, supervising performance measurement & statistical evaluation of the 
quality and effectiveness of the Borough’s recreation services.  The work involves preparing and executing an 
annual budget, supervising the maintenance and construction of parks and recreation facilities, and directing the 
work of professional, technical staff, contract, or administrative staff in developing new and/or revising existing 
programs.  The incumbent works with the Recreation Board in establishing department goals and objectives and 
exercises considerable latitude in achieving these goals and objectives; and prepares and delivers reports and 
communications to the General Public, Rec. Board, Borough Manager, and Borough Council. 
 
ESSENTIAL FUNCTIONS OF THE JOB: 
• General administration, development, and direction of a dynamic park and playground system and recreation 
programs for people of all age groups, abilities, and interest levels within the greater Borough area; 
• Direct the work of professional, technical staff, contract, or administrative staff; 
• Recruits, selects, trains, supervises and evaluates all part-time/seasonal recreation staff and volunteers; 
• Develop new and/or revise existing programs for review by the Borough Manager; 
• Oversee the inspection of playground equipment and facilities for safety and planning purposes;

Dear Council Members,  
 
On December 22, 2025, PennDOT issued an LTAP Technical Assistance Report based on field 
observations to improve traffic conditions along Lawndale Road in Wyomissing Borough. Our office has 
reviewed the recommendation that PennDOT had provided and offered a summary of the proposed 
improvements on June 12, 2026. A site inspection of each of the intersections present in the LTAP report 
along Lawndale Road was performed on July 16, 2026. This inspection served to identify opportunities 
for improvement to sight lines outside of what was offered in the initial PennDOT LTAP Report.  
 
Intersections were evaluated at existing and proposed stop sign locations as recommended in the 
PennDOT LTAP Report for sight triangle obstructions. Per Wyomissing Borough Subdivision and Land 
Development Ordinance, sight triangles at intersections of roads that are posted at 25 miles per hour shall 
be a minimum of 150 feet where removal of obstructions proves to be prohibitively costly. In the interest 
of ecological conservation and to avoid excessive cost, mature trees were not considered viable candidates 
for removal as a part of this evaluation.  
 
Sight lines of the evaluated filter streets (i.e. side streets) to Lawndale Road were observed to be 
generally free of obstructing vegetation with the exception of a small number of mature trees that obscure 
portions of the sight triangle for the southbound lanes of travel at most of the Lawndale intersections that 
were evaluated. These intersections are identified in the table below.   
 
On-street parking was observed to be the main contributor to diminished sight lines at 
intersections as parked cars block the view of oncoming traffic on both Lawndale Road and its filter 
streets. The eastbound lane of Lawndale Road has existing ‘NO PARKING’ signs installed mounted to 
overhead electric power poles along the entire length of the road that prohibit parking on one side of the 
road. Signs that prohibit parking, per PennDOT design standards, should be placed at minimum 250-foot 
intervals. No cars were observed to be parked in the eastbound lane of travel along Lawndale Road during 
the site inspection , however if illegal parking becomes an issue additional signage may be needed.

lane before all intersection with filter streets. This will allow for the full sight distance to be realized for 
vehicles attempting to turn onto Lawndale Road. For 4-way stop intersections it is recommended that in 
addition to the 20 foot long section of no parking zone that filter streets have a 15 foot long buffer from 
the intersection of ‘no parking’  adjacent to the lane with the stop sign. This will ensure proper sight 
distance requirements are met by vehicles turning onto filter streets from Lawndale Road.

the posted speed limit of 25 miles per hour as indicated by the installed pole mounted speed monitoring 
devices. Traffic/Speed control devices per the Pennsylvania Traffic Calming Handbook (Pub. 383) may 
be warranted to reduce speed related incidents from occurring.

Safety Concern and Background 
In response to a technical assistance request from Wyomissing Borough, LTAP met with John 
Phillips, Police Chief, on September 23, 2025, to examine intersections along Lawndale Road as well 
as the intersection of Mayfair Road and Wingert Road. The Borough wants to ensure that the 
appropriate intersection traffic controls are being used. 
Figure 1 shows an aerial view of the study intersections with the existing intersection traffic control. 
The study intersections were assessed through walking, driving, and reviewing site conditions.  
 
Figure 1: Aerial View of the Study Intersections 
Field Observations 
Lawndale Road is a two-lane, two-way Borough road with no longitudinal pavement markings, and 
sidewalks exist on both sides of the road. Other pavement markings on Lawndale Road include 
Type A – Parallel crosswalk markings at all intersections except Edgedale Road and Vista Road as 
well as a westbound SLOW pavement marking between Wingert Road and Vista Road. The roadway 
width is approximately 24 feet from curb to curb. The roadway length per PennDOT’s Type 5B 
Map is 0.28 mile (1,478 feet). Per traffic data collected by the Borough, Lawndale Road has an 
average daily traffic (ADT) of 2,186 vehicles per day (vpd) in both directions. The posted speed limit 
is 25 MPH, and traffic data collected by the Borough shows an average 85th percentile speed of 25 
MPH in both directions.

Assessment 
Intersection Traffic Control 
An engineering and traffic study is required per Pennsylvania’s Vehicle Code, Title 75, Section 6109 
to establish traffic restrictions including stop-controlled intersections. Per Section 6109(e), the study 
must follow PennDOT’s regulations, which are contained in the Pennsylvania Code, Title 67, 
Chapter 212. Title 67, Chapter 212 is also known as PennDOT’s Official Traffic Control Devices, 
Publication 212 (Pub. 212). In addition to PennDOT’s regulations, the Federal Highway 
Administration’s (FHWA) Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices (MUTCD) has criteria on selecting 
the appropriate intersection traffic control. From the MUTCD, Section 2B.06, when selecting a 
form of intersection control, the following factors should be considered: 
A. Motor vehicle, bicycle, and pedestrian traffic volumes on all approaches; where the term 
units/day or units/hour is indicated, it should be the total of motor vehicle, bicycle, and 
pedestrian volume;  
B. Driver yielding behavior with regard to all modes of conflicting traffic, including bicyclists 
and pedestrians;  
C. Number and angle of approaches;  
D. Approach speeds;  
DRAFT

 Page 5 of 17                                            PennDOT LTAP: #24178 
 
E. Sight distance available on each approach;  
F. Reported crash experience; and  
G. The presence of a grade crossing near the intersection.

§501A. Specific Intent. 
1. In addition to the conventional commercial and industrial zoning districts established by this 
Chapter, there shall be established a Commercial and Industrial Redevelopment Overlay District 
(the “Overlay District”). 
2. The Overlay District shall not be an independent zoning district, but shall be a set of special 
zoning regulations to be applied over and above the zoning regulations otherwise controlling upon 
a tract of land, as enabled by and described in Section 605 of the Pennsylvania Municipalities 
Planning Code. The district upon which the Overlay District is superimposed is referred to herein 
as the “underlying district.” 
3. The purpose of the Overlay District is to provide standards for the comprehensive 
redevelopment of aging commercial and industrial sites within the Borough of Wyomissing, by: 
(A) permitting certain uses or combinations of uses not otherwise permitted in  the underlying 
district; (B) permitting a greater density and intensity of development than would otherwise be 
permitted in the underlying district; (C) permitting the fee -simple subdivision of individual 
buildings and building pads within a unified redevelopment site in the manner of a planned unit 
development; (D) permitting shared parking, shared access, shared stormwater management, and 
shared utility infrastructure among multiple lots under separate ownership, administered by a 
mandatory lot owners association; (E) providing reductions in required off -street parking in 
exchange for bicycle and pedestrian improvements that implement the Official Bike Route Map 
of the Borough of Wyomissing and the Borough of West Reading as well as other improvements; 
and (F) requiring enhanced greenspace, shade trees, and landscaping within parking areas, 
consistent with §604 of this Chapter, and streetscape improvements along adjacent public streets, 
so as to promote the public health, safety, and general welfare of  the residents and inhabitants of 
the Borough. 
4. A secondary purpose of the Overlay District is to encourage respect for the historic and 
architectural identity of the structures and neighborhoods in which redevelopment may occur 
under the terms of this Part, and to promote walkable, connected development that reduces reliance 
on automobiles. 
5. The Overlay District shall be generally apply throughout those portions of the Commercial and 
Industrial Districts of the Borough of Wyomissing located to the south of the Warren Street Bypass 
and bounded to the north by properties located adjacent to both sides of Paper Mill Road and to 
the west by properties located adjacent to both sides of Woodland Road, and shall include the 
entirety of the property known as the Berkshire Mall, 1665 State Hill Road (PIN 
No. 96439719529232), together with all commercially zoned properties adjacent thereto lying 
between State Hill Road, Woodland Road, Paper Mill Road, and Route 422 (SR 0422), as depicted 
DRAFT

on the Official Zoning Map, notwithstanding that portions of such properties may lie outside the 
boundaries otherwise described in this subsection. Where the boundary of the Overlay District 
divides a single lot, the entire lot shall be deemed to be within the Overlay District. A site is eligible 
for redevelopment under this Part provided the following requirements are met: 
A. A site proposed for redevelopment shall be under single ownership, or in the case of 
multiple ownership, the lot or lots shall be redeveloped according to a single overall plan 
(the “Master Redevelopment Plan”). 
B. The site proposed for redevelopment shall be a minimum of one (1) acre in area. If the 
site is not a single, contiguous parcel, no part of the site shall be separated by more than 
one hundred (100) feet from another part of the site. If a site containing a minimum of one 
(1) acre is approved under this Section, or was approved under a previously enacted version 
of a regulation on the same topic, smaller adjacent properties may qualify under this 
Section at a later date as long as the smaller adjacent properties are within the boundaries 
of the Overlay District and the other requirements of this Section (other than minimum site 
acreage) are met. 
C. The site proposed for redevelopment shall be occupied by existing commercial or 
industrial development, and the following shall apply: (1) the majority of the existing 
commercial or industrial development was originally constructed at least thirty (30) years 
prior to the proposed redevelopment; and (2) due to changing economic or technological 
conditions, the existing commercial or industrial development is no longer appropriate. 
D. The site is not required to be located within a single underlying zoning district, but all

be located within a commercial or industrial zoning district or within fifty (50) feet of such

E. Land may be added to an established redevelopment site, provided the revised 
redevelopment site, as modified by the addition of land, continues to meet the requirements 
of this Part. 
F. Plans for redevelopment shall demonstrate, to the extent practically achievable, respect 
for the historic and architectural identity of the structures and neighborhoods in which 
redevelopment may occur under the terms of this Part. 
 
G. Plans for redevelopment shall include a schedule for completion of the development, 
which schedule shall be subject to the approval of the Borough Council. 
6. To the extent any regulation in this Part conflicts with any regulation in any other Part of this 
Chapter, the regulation in this Part shall be controlling.

§507A. Parking Area Greenspace, Shade Trees, and Landscaping. 
1. Purpose. Large expanses of unbroken pavement are contrary to the purposes of this Part. Parking 
areas within the Overlay District shall incorporate substantial interior and perimeter greenspace, 
shade trees, and landscaping to reduce heat island effect, manage stormwater, provide safe 
pedestrian circulation, and improve the appearance of the redevelopment site. 
2. Interior Landscaped Area. Not less than ten percent (10%) of the interior area of each surface 
parking lot containing twenty (20) or more spaces (measured as the area devoted to parking stalls 
and drive aisles) shall consist of landscaped islands, medians, or planting strips. Landscaped areas 
within parking lots shall count toward the Open Area requirement of §504A. 
3. Landscape Islands. A landscaped island not less than nine (9) feet in width and not less than one 
hundred sixty (160) square feet in area shall be provided: (A) at each end of each parking row; and 
(B) at intervals such that no more than fifteen (15) contiguous parking spaces occur in any row 
without an intervening landscaped island. Landscaped medians not less than ten (10) feet in width 
DRAFT

shall be provided between every fourth double row of parking, and such medians shall, wherever 
practicable, incorporate the pedestrian walkways required by §506A.5.A(2). 
4. Shade Trees. Deciduous trees shall be provided within or immediately adjacent to each surface 
parking lot at a ratio of not less than one (1) deciduous tree for each ten (10) parking spaces, any 
fractional result being rounded up to the next whole tree. Deciduous trees located within interior 
landscaped islands shall be shade trees; deciduous trees located along the perimeter of a parking 
lot may be either shade trees or ornamental trees. Each shade tree shall be a minimum of two and 
one-half (2.5) inch caliper measured at six (6) inches above grade, shall be limbed up to six (6) 
feet in height, and shall be a species having a mature canopy spread of not less than thirty (30) feet 
selected from the recommended tree lists in §604 of this Chapter or otherwise approved by the 
Borough. Each ornamental tree shall meet the minimum size standards of §604. Each required tree 
shall be planted in a pervious area of not less than one hundred sixty (160) square feet (or in a 
structural soil or suspended pavement system providing not less than four hundred (400) cubic feet 
of soil volume per tree), within a landscaped island or within ten (10) feet of the parking lot 
perimeter, with the center of the tree located not less than three (3) feet from the edge of any curb 
or paving and not less than ten (10) feet from any underground or overhead utility. Tree species 
shall be diversified such that no single species comprises more than twenty-five percent (25%) of 
required parking lot trees, not less than sixty- six percent (66 %) of the required trees shall be of 
species native to the northeastern United States, and no species or cultivar deemed invasive by the 
Pennsylvania Department of Conservation and Natural Resources shall be planted. The design 
shall achieve, at tree maturity, canopy coverage of not less than thirty percent (30%) of the surface 
parking area. 
5. Perimeter Landscaping. A landscaped planting strip not less than ten (10) feet in width shall be 
provided between any surface parking area and any public street right -of-way, and not less than 
fifteen (15) feet in width between any surface parking area and any residential district boundary, 
planted with a combination of shade trees, ornamental trees, shrubs, and groundcover forming an 
effective visual buffer as approved by the Borough Planning Commission. Where a surface parking 
area abuts a residential zoning district or an existing residential use, the planting strip shall 
constitute a screen buffer meeting §604 of this Chapter, installed at a minimum height of six (6) 
feet and producing, within three (3) years, a complete and continuous visual screen of not less than 
six (6) feet in height. Screen buffer plantings shall be fully contained between the property line 
and the yard setback line. Perimeter landscaping shall be shown on the landscape plan required by 
subsection 7 and is subject to approval by the Borough Planning Commission. 
6. Stormwater Integration. Landscaped islands, medians, and perimeter strips shall, wherever 
feasible as determined by the Borough Engineer, be designed as rain gardens, bioretention areas, 
bioswales, or other vegetated stormwater best management practices  receiving runoff from 
adjacent pavement, and such facilities shall count toward both the landscaping requirements of 
this Section and the stormwater requirements of §509A. 
7. Landscape Plan; Maintenance. A landscape plan shall be submitted as part of a preliminary plan 
or final plan (if not preceded by a preliminary plan or zoning permit). The landscape plan shall be 
prepared by a Landscape Architect with current and active registration in the Commonwealth of 
Pennsylvania, shall meet the requirements of §604 of this Chapter, and is subject to approval by 
the Borough Planning Commission. All required landscaping and shade trees shall be perpetually 
DRAFT

maintained or replaced, and any plant material which is in decline or has expired shall be replaced 
in kind within six (6) months of observance, by the Lot Owners Association pursuant to §511A. 
8. Relationship to §604. The requirements of this Section are in addition to the landscaping 
requirements of §604 of this Chapter. Where a requirement of this Section and a requirement of 
§604 differ, the more restrictive requirement shall apply. Any lands cape requirement calculation 
under this Section which results in a fraction shall be rounded up to a whole plant. 
§508A.  Recreation Dedication. 
1. Applicability. For each redevelopment project under this Part which includes dwelling units, the 
applicant shall satisfy the recreation land dedication and fee requirements of §509.1 of the Borough 
Subdivision and Land Development Ordinance.  
2. Open space provided under this Section shall count toward the Open Area requirement of 
§504A, and publicly accessible plazas, greens, seating areas, and trail corridors within the 
redevelopment site may be credited toward this requirement in the discretion of Borough Council. 
§509A. Shared Stormwater Management. 
1. Redevelopment under this Part shall comply with the Borough Stormwater Management 
Ordinance and all applicable state and federal requirements, including NPDES permitting. 
2. Shared Facilities Permitted. Stormwater management facilities — including detention and 
retention basins, subsurface storage, rain gardens, bioretention areas, water quality devices, 
conveyance systems, and outfalls — may be designed, constructed, owned, and maintained on a 
shared basis serving multiple lots and multiple buildings within the redevelopment site, 
notwithstanding that such lots are under different ownership, and may be located on any lot within 
the redevelopment site. Rate, volume, and water quality compliance shall be computed on the basis 
of the overall redevelopment site. 
3. Easements and Responsibility. All shared stormwater facilities shall be located within recorded 
drainage and access easements benefitting each lot they serve and the Borough. The Lot Owners 
Association shall own or control, and shall be solely responsible for the inspection, operation, 
maintenance, repair, and replacement of, all shared stormwater facilities, pursuant to a recorded 
Stormwater Management Facilities Operation and Maintenance Agreement in form approved by 
the Borough Solicitor and Borough Engineer, which agreement shall: (A) run with the land and 
bind all present and future lot owners; (B) grant the Borough the right, but not the obligation, 
following notice and opportunity to cure, to enter upon the site, perform required maintenance, 
and lien each lot within the redevelopment site for its proportionate share of the cost; (C) require 
routine inspection and reporting to the Borough at intervals specified by the Borough Engineer; 
and (D) require the Association to maintain a dedicated reserve for stormwater facility repair and 
replacement. 
§510A. Utility Infrastructure. 
1. All new and relocated electric, telecommunications, and cable service lines within the 
redevelopment site shall be placed underground, except for transformers, switchgear, and similar 
at-grade appurtenances, which shall be screened by landscaping or architectural features. Existing 
electric utility service areas may remain and be upgraded in place. 
DRAFT

2. Shared private utility infrastructure — including private water and sanitary sewer laterals and 
mains, fire loops and hydrants, stormwater conveyance, site lighting, common signage electrical 
service, and communications conduit — serving more than one lot or building shall be permitted, 
shall be located within recorded utility easements benefitting each lot served, and shall be owned 
or controlled, operated, maintained, repaired, and replaced by the Lot Owners Association 
pursuant to §511A, except to the extent such facilities are accepted for dedication by the applicable 
public utility, authority, or the Borough. 
3. Public and Private Demarcation. The Master Redevelopment Plan shall clearly identify, for each 
utility, the point at which public facilities end and private facilities begin, as follows: (A) for water 
service, the Borough or the applicable authority or utility shall own, operate, and maintain the 
public main and the master meter, and all water distribution piping, valves, hydrants, submeters, 
laterals, and appurtenances located downstream of the master meter shall be private; (B) for 
sanitary sewer service, the Borough or the applicable authority shall own, operate, and maintain 
the public sewer main, and all collection piping, laterals, cleanouts, manholes, and appurtenances 
located upstream of the point of connection to the public main shall be private; and (C) all facilities 
identified as private shall be owned or controlled, operated, maintained, repaired, and replaced by 
the Lot Owners Association pursuant to §511A, and shall not be accepted for dedication by the 
Borough or any authority except by express written agreement. Where a master meter serves more 
than one lot or building, the Declaration shall provide for submetering or for equitable allocation 
of the metered charges among the lots and buildings served. 
4. The Master Redevelopment Plan shall include a composite utility plan demonstrating adequate 
capacity, metering arrangements, and allocation of maintenance responsibility for all shared utility 
infrastructure, subject to approval by the Borough Engineer and the applicable utility providers. 
Adequate capacity shall be demonstrated by written confirmation from each applicable utility 
provider or authority.  
§511A. Lot Owners Association. 
1. Association Required. Prior to or concurrently with the recording of any subdivision plan 
creating more than one lot within a redevelopment site, or the conveyance of any portion of a 
redevelopment site, the applicant shall form a lot owners association, business owners association, 
or functionally equivalent entity organized under Pennsylvania law (the “Association”), and shall 
record the Declaration establishing the Association and governing the redevelopment site. The 
Declaration and the Association’s  organizational documents shall be subject to review and 
approval by the Borough Solicitor for compliance with this Section. 
2. Mandatory Membership; Covenants Running with the Land. Every owner of a lot within the 
redevelopment site shall automatically be a member of the Association. The Declaration shall run 
with the land, shall bind all present and future owners, occupants, and mortgagees, and shall not 
be amended in any manner affecting the obligations described in this Section, nor shall the 
Association be dissolved, without the prior written consent of Borough Council. 
3. Required Responsibilities. The Declaration shall obligate the Association, at a minimum, to own 
or control and to perpetually operate, inspect, maintain, repair, replace, insure, and administer: 
A. All shared stormwater management facilities, in accordance with §509A and the 
recorded Stormwater Management Facilities Operation and Maintenance Agreement; 
DRAFT

B. All shared private utility infrastructure described in §510A, including private water, 
sanitary sewer, fire protection, storm sewer, site lighting, and communications facilities 
not dedicated to a public utility or authority; 
C. All shared parking areas, drive aisles, access driveways, service drives, and loading 
areas, including pavement maintenance, restriping, snow and ice removal, and enforcement 
of the shared parking arrangements approved under §506A; 
D. All common landscaping, parking lot greenspace, shade trees, buffer plantings, and open 
space areas required under §§507A and 508A, including replacement of dead or diseased 
material; 
E. All internal sidewalks, crosswalks, multi- use paths, bicycle parking, transit amenities, 
and other bicycle and pedestrian improvements for which a parking reduction credit was 
granted under §506A.5, and all streetscape improvements required under §512A.6; 
F. Common signage, common site lighting, and common refuse and recycling facilities; 
and 
G. Any other common elements identified on the Master Redevelopment Plan. 
4. Assessments and Reserves. The Declaration shall grant the Association the power to levy 
assessments against each lot sufficient to fund the responsibilities described in subsection 3, shall 
provide that unpaid assessments constitute a lien on the delinquent lot, and shall require the 
Association to maintain (A) an operating budget adopted annually and (B) a capital reserve fund, 
funded at a level reasonably sufficient, based on a reserve study updated not less than every five 
(5) years, for the repair an d replacement of shared stormwater facilities, shared utility 
infrastructure, and shared pavement. 
5. Borough Enforcement Rights. The Declaration shall name the Borough as a third- party 
beneficiary with the right, but not the obligation, to enforce the Association’s obligations under 
this Section. If the Association fails to perform any obligation relating to stormwater management, 
utility infrastructure, life safety, or required landscaping after thirty (30) days’ written notice (or 
immediately in the event of an emergency), the Borough may perform such obligation and recover 
its costs, together with re asonable attorneys’ fees and an administrative charge, from the 
Association, and may lien each lot within the redevelopment site for its proportionate share thereof, 
in the manner provided for municipal claims. 
6. Reporting. The Association shall provide to the Borough, upon request and in any event not less 
than annually, evidence of its continued good standing, its current budget and reserve balances, its 
stormwater inspection reports, and the name and contact information of its manager or responsible 
officer. 
§512A. Additional Performance Standards. 
1. The horizontal distance in feet at the closest place between any principal buildings on the same 
lot shall not be less than twenty- five (25) feet, provided that buildings separated by a lot line 
created under §505A, and attached buildings, shall be exempt from this requirement.

2. Wall Signs  and Freestanding Signs. Walls signs and freestanding signs shall be permitted in 
accordance with Section §606C, except that the following provisions shall apply: 
A. Freestanding Signs shall not exceed twenty-five feet in height and shall not exceed 100 
sq. ft. of gross sign area per face. Freestanding signs shall not have more than 2 faces. No 
more than one freestanding sign shall be permitted for each 1,500 feet of street frontage 
which the freestanding sign abuts.  
B. The total area of all sign limitation of §606C.2 shall not apply. 
C. Within the redevelopment site, a sign attached to a building wall is not required to face 
a public street. Said signs shall be subject to the requirement that the sign shall not have an 
area exceeding 10% of the building wall to which it is attached, or two square feet of area 
for each one foot of building height plus two square feet of area for each one foot of 
building length of the wall to which it is attached, whichever is less.  
D. Signs attached to a building walls shall not exceed 100 square feet for each separate use 
within the redevelopment site (not including the freestanding sign area).   
3. Directional Signs. Within the overall redevelopment site, one (1) sign facing each direction of 
travel, not exceeding six (6) square feet in area, is permitted at each street intersection and at each 
driveway intersection, outside the legal right-of-way, to identify the redevelopment site and to give 
directions. These signs are in addition to all other signs permitted under this Chapter and do not 
count toward any sign area limitation of §606. 
4. When a part of the overall redevelopment site is separated from another by no more than one 
hundred (100) feet in accordance with §501A.5.B: (A) the minimum distance between the 
centerline of a driveway where it intersects a street and the centerline of  another driveway on the 
same lot or another lot (including another lot which is outside of the overall redevelopment site) 
where that other driveway intersects the same street shall be one hundred (100) feet; (B) the 
centerline of a driveway where it intersects a street shall be a minimum of seventy -five (75) feet 
from a lot line, provided that it may be less than seventy- five (75) feet from a lot line if it will be 
at least one hundred (100) feet from the nearest driveway on the adjoining lot (including a n 
adjoining lot which is outside of the overall redevelopment site); and (C) the minimum distance 
between the centerline of a driveway where it intersects a street and the intersection of street 
cartway lines (except in the case of a street intersecting the street the driveway intersects directly 
opposite from the driveway) shall be one hundred (100) feet. The driveway locations and spacing

may require greater spacing than specified in this subsection where necessary for traffic safety.
