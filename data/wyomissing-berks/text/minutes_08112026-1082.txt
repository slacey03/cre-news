A concept plan is not a formal land-development application. It is nevertheless directly relevant to a
zoning rewrite. The public record should identify when that plan was submitted, who received and
reviewed it, what zoning issues were identified, and whether any element of the concept or review

Expanding the map does not answer who designed the text

Staff emphasized that the overlay also encompasses the Crowne Plaza and other commercial properties
and discussed possibly adding the Shops at Wyomissing and additional parcels. A broader map may
support a legitimate area-wide planning purpose. It does not, by itself, answer why the ordinance
contains this particular combination of uses, financing tools, subdivision rights, parking rules, and
phasing provisions.

The issue should not be reduced to a premature legal accusation of spot zoning. The more immediate
public-record question is simpler: was permanent public zoning independently designed from adopted
planning goals, or was it reverse-engineered around the requirements of one prospective purchaser and
then extended to surrounding properties? The Borough can answer that question by publishing the
provenance of the draft.

The closing delays make that disclosure more important

“In exchange for that, they pushed the outside closing date to May 15th, which gives us enough

2. Estevez matter — departmental accountability cannot end with one
officer

What the record establishes

When Council promoted John Phillips to Police Chief in February 2021, the minutes record my request
for greater transparency, diversity, accountability, and an effective complaint process. The Estevez
matter is a concrete test of that commitment.

Ina December 17, 2025 deposition, Officer Rubiel Estevez described the underlying incident as a “false
arrest.” He testified that he received a 15-day suspension after an internal investigation, that Lieutenant
Paul Baur conducted the investigation, and that he believed the Police Department determined he
should not have arrested Gulshan Chilashvili and Haldun Ekinci.

Estevez also testified that the decision to arrest was his and required no departmental approval. He had
no prior experience investigating the alleged “stringing” scheme. His described work consisted
principally of reviewing material and video supplied by Nicholas Damone, discussing the video with
other officers, and making follow-up calls to Damone. Court filings state that Ekinci was jailed for 10
days and Chilashvili for 15 days before release, and that the criminal charges were later withdrawn.

The Borough and Estevez entered a Settlement Agreement and General Release requiring payment of
$499,000, including attorneys’ fees and costs, while denying wrongdoing. On July 30, 2026, G&B
Amusements and Damone filed a joinder complaint alleging that the internal investigation found Estevez
did not perform a proper investigation and should not have made the arrests. That joinder allegation has
not been adjudicated; the deposition and settlement are records that speak for themselves.

independent counsel would provide a clean slate
