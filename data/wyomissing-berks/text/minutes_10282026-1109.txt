Written comments following the October 28, 2026
budget meeting

Adrian Jadic | Prepared October 30, 2026

This is a later edited written version of my oral comments, with the clarifications below. {tis not a
verbatim transcript.

| oppose the tax increases presented at the meeting. Council should first bring residents a
lower-spending budget. Zero additional mills deserves serious consideration: turn the
smaller-Borough scenario into specific cuts, annual net savings and service choices.

A smaller immediate tax increase supported by borrowing is not, by itself, a spending cut. Show
the full cash-versus-borrowing costs of the same projects, including annual repayments and
total cost over the loan term. Explain how debt service would be funded and whether future tax
increases would be needed. Council must own the long-term borrowing decision.

The preliminary 2027 budget proposes $600,000 across the identified management and
finance contract lines, plus $160,000 for communications, marketing and public relations.
These are proposed allocations. What measurable recurring savings has management
delivered, and what targets and deadlines has Council set?

When does interim management end, and what is the permanent staffing plan? | want a
defined scope, deliverables and an end point for interim work. | also questioned the value of
communications spending and asked for concrete evidence of its output, with consideration of
interns or other lower-cost approaches.

| objected to having interim management make records-destruction decisions and asked that
those decisions be left to the permanent manager.

| asked that the presentation distinguish average from median and use the terms accurately.
Eden acknowledged that the slide said median and that this was the word he should have used.
Residents need clearly identified statistical measures to understand the proposed tax impact.

Higher property taxes make homeownership more expensive and could make Wyomissing less
attractive to buyers. Telling residents we need more money is easy. We need a plan for better
value and lower costs. Review staffing, contracts, program subsidies and procurement options,
and show the costs, net annual savings and service effects before asking residents to pay
more.

Clarifications to my oral remarks

My phrase "7.75 plus interest" did not establish a like-for-like comparison of the scenarios.
They have different capital funding and reserve outcomes. My request remains a comparison of
the full cash and borrowing costs of the same projects.

The historical spending totals | cited combine records at different stages, including forecasts
and proposals, with unresolved differences in accounting scope and treatment. They do not, by
themselves, establish comparable recurring spending, explain the changes between years or
demonstrate management failure. | am not relying on that comparison to support these
requests.
