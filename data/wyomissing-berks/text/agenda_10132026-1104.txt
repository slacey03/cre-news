have continued under the previous haulers, the rate would have increased to $597 per year per 
household. Residents were lastly reminded that the hauler has a full 24 hours to service a 
missed stop.

PLANNING COMMISSION- OVERLAY OVERVIEW: 
Sam Wiser, Salzmann Hughes shared with Council that the proposed overlay has been revised 
to modernize the overly to add more opportunities for developers, to enhance the overlay 
district, as well as revising the Zoning and SALDO Landscaping Ordinances in synchrony. It 
was shared that the revisions are not meant to encompass the needs of any developer 
specifically, and that no concept plans have been provided for formal land development 
review.  
 
He further reviewed the proposed Commercial and Industrial Zoning Overlay District revisions 
noting that the existing district allows all uses permitted by right in commercial and industrial zones 
as before, but could expand to apartments and townhouses above non-residential use, 30% by right 
or 60% by exception, or age-restricted housing above non-residential uses. Adult uses are excluded 
from the special exception in this zone. This could also include shopping centers, discount stores, 
grocery stores, warehouse clubs, restaurants, convenience stores, and more. Accessory warehousing 
is permitted as long as it does not exceed 25% of the floor area, and cannot be a standalone 
warehouse. This was included to accommodate the online shopping and short delivery time retail 
experience.  
 
Relative to area, yard, and height in Section 504A, standards existing and proposed to remain 
include a height of 75 feet, maximum building coverage of 60 ft, maximum pervious paved area of 
75%, and minimum lot size and minimum lot width with the exception for interior lots which may 
have a smaller lot size. Minimum open area is proposed to change from 10% to 25%, and perimeter 
building set back from 66% of the underlying setback to the full underlying zoning setback. 
 
Planned unit development was outlined in the presentation, pointing out fee-simple subdivision, 
shared parking across site with the site encompassing the overall parking demand, shared 
stormwater facilities and utilities, and the requirement of easements, maintenance of landscape, and 
more, as a lot owners association. Overall, this encompasses site planning and maintenance for a 
particular redevelopment/development site in this overlay, and conveying to the Borough, the site’s 
master plan. It was reiterated that as part of the lot owners association, all owners/occupants within 
the site, must be members and is recorded at the Court House. Lot owners must contribute 
financially to keep up with facilities, maintenance, and more, and if not maintained, the Borough 
can maintain on their behalf, charge the association, and enforce through liens. The Borough would 
be responsible for reviewing any association agreements before recording occurs. Later in the 
meeting, it was shared that a developer can do as little as 1 acre based on the profile of the tax 
parcels in the proposed district. While the acreage is not a significant amount, parcels could be 
combined to complete a more expansive project, and a single parcel could have multiple overlay 
districts. A developer could choose the underlying development standards, partake in the RD3 
DRAFT

standards, or could follow the proposed incentives plan.   
 
The proposed overlay provides incentives in overall parking, no greater than 25%, in exchange for 
bike and pedestrian accessibilities. A parking demand analysis is required for the site based on 
proposed uses within. This ties in with enhancing greenspace within a site for beautification and 
environmental friendliness.

Spending controls and year end forecasts
At the meeting, I cited recreation at approximately 78% of its annual budget, administration at 94%, and legal 
spending at 90%, with four months remaining. Please publish the corresponding figures, explain any timing or 
classification differences, and provide the year-end forecast for each area. The important question is what

I had attended the Recreation Board meeting the previous evening. My concern was the lack of a clear public

West Lawn project costs and closeout
I asked about the final payment and release of retainage on the West Lawn project, the additional cost of

noncompletion. If a master plan or lot owners association is the answer, explain the precise duties and 
remedies. Shared maintenance arrangements and a promise to complete redevelopment are different

Budget decisions and spending restraint
Before Council asks residents to pay more, it must show what spending it has reviewed, what it can reduce,

million demands a plan that residents can examine, with clear choices and their effects on taxes and services. 
Presenting the size of the problem does not answer what Council has done to control it. [1]
Earlier warnings require an explanation of earlier decisions
At the January 8 Finance meeting, I raised debt and deficit concerns and requested a 90-day corrective plan 
and a review of the adopted budget. The February 5 Finance minutes subsequently recorded that Council had 
opted not to reopen the budget. They described possible expenditure reductions, stated that recreation 
publicity had already gone out and event contracts had been signed, and recorded a unanimous motion to

That history matters. Existing contracts may limit particular savings, but they do not explain why every later

reductions actually implemented, when they began, and the savings achieved or expected. If savings were 
rejected, explain why.
Explain the spending figures and the year end forecast
At the September 30 meeting, I cited August reports showing approximately $836,000 in administration 
spending, about 94% of its annual budget, with four months remaining. I also cited recreation spending 
approximately 14.4% above the comparable prior-year figure. Those figures call for an explanation of the 
spending trend, the corresponding revenues, and the remaining-year forecast. [1]
Publish the underlying August report, the adopted and revised budget amounts, and a reconciliation of any 
changes in accounting categories. Explain how the remaining months will be funded and what spending 
controls apply. A category may contain an early payment or a reclassified expense; if that explains a high 
percentage, show the adjustment rather than leaving residents to guess. The Borough should be able to 
distinguish a timing issue from a recurring shortfall.
Show the full cost of recreation and restore consistent reporting
Free admission does not make an event free. The public needs the full taxpayer cost of each major event and 
program, including recreation salaries and benefits, direct purchases, advertising, equipment, and support 
provided by Public Works, police, fire and EMS. Identify the hours worked, overtime where applicable, and the

A cost does not disappear because another department pays it.

library's operating budget. The discussion emphasized the continued importance of fundraising 
and membership support, while recognizing that additional strategies such as effective money 
management, legacy giving, and other donations will be necessary to achieve long-term 
sustainability. The board also discussed the importance of preserving and growing the reserve 
fund, which is currently nearly $600,000.  To achieve that the library must minimize our 
withdrawals to about 3% per year. 
4. David opened the floor to Steve Pottieger.  Mr. Pottieger provided background on his 40 years 
of experience as an investment advisor and his prior involvement in establishing the library's 
reserve fund. He discussed the potential benefits of creating an endowment, noting that some 
donors prefer contributing to an endowment rather than a general fund, while general fund
