Written Public Comments for the May 12,
2026 Borough Council Meeting

Agenda and Non-Agenda Public Comment Periods
Adrian Jadic, 48 Cardinal Road, Wyomissing

Please include these written comments in the official record for the May 12, 2026 Wyomissing
Borough Council meeting. These comments are intended to supplement and clarify the public
comments | provided during both the agenda-item and non-agenda public comment periods.

1. Kraft Municipal Group and Expanded Citation Authority

| want to be clear at the outset: | am not opposed to code enforcement. Property maintenance,
safe sidewalks, and consistent standards matter. My concern is with expanding enforcement
authority without enough transparency, reporting, and safeguards.

The May 12 agenda included action to ratify Kraft Municipal Group assuming responsibility for
writing and filing citations for violations of the International Property Maintenance Code, effective
May 1, 2026. That is a meaningful change. It gives a private, for-profit contractor a larger role in
the path from inspection to citation.

Before Council expands that authority, residents should know how the process will work:

Who receives a warning first?

Who receives a Notice of Violation?

Who receives extra time?

Who decides when a citation is filed?

What Borough employee reviews the matter before it goes to court?
How are errors corrected?

How are residents protected from inconsistent or selective enforcement?

These questions are not theoretical. The Borough does not publish a clear enforcement log
showing complaints, warnings, Notices of Violation, citations, withdrawn citations, appeals,
outcomes, or enforcement costs, even in aggregate form. Without that reporting, residents have
no practical way to evaluate whether enforcement is fair, consistent, or cost-effective. ot 1

If Kraft is going to inspect properties, prepare enforcement documents, conduct reingpections)

and file citations, Council should adopt written guardrails. At minimum, there should be a written _

enforcement procedure, Borough review before citations are filed, monthly aggregate reporting,
and clear cost tracking for contractor time tied to enforcement activity.




Rules should be enforced. But enforcement power must be accountable. A private contractor
should not receive expanded citation authority without public reporting and Borough oversight.

2. 915 Penn Avenue Conditional Use Decision

| also objected to approval of the 915 Penn Avenue conditional use request.

This is not because | oppose redevelopment of that property. The site can and should be
improved. The issue is whether Council should waive major standards at one of the most visible
corners on Penn Avenue without a broader plan for the corridor.

The applicant requested approval for a 30-unit apartment building with no first-floor office, retail,
professional services, or restaurant use, and with 32 off-street parking spaces instead of the 45
spaces normally required. That is not a minor adjustment. It goes directly to the purpose of the
mixed-use redevelopment framework.

This location is not an isolated back lot. It is at Penn Avenue and Park Road, a gateway location
for the Borough. The Borough's own zoning framework contemplates mixed-use redevelopment
in this area. That means residential development should strengthen the commercial corridor, not
replace it one waiver at a time.

The all-residential proposal also raises obvious parking concerns. Thirty apartments with only 32
spaces leaves almost no margin for visitors, second vehicles, workers, deliveries, or overflow.
Nearby residents and business owners raised those concerns. Ms. Murphy from Murphy
Jewelers and her attorney raised concerns. Other residents raised concerns about traffic, Court
Lane, alley access, parking spillover, and the future character of Penn Avenue.

Those concerns deserved more than a vote to approve.

The prior 2023 conditional use approval had expired. Council was not required to simply repeat
the same waiver. A prior approval under a different owner should not become an automatic
entitlement for a new application. If Council keeps granting exceptions to the mixed-use and
parking standards, then the exception effectively becomes the rule.

Council should have denied the request, tabled it, or required a revised plan with a stronger
public benefit: fewer units, a real first-floor commercial or professional component, better
parking, clearer traffic and delivery planning, and stronger conditions tied to the long-term
character of Penn Avenue.

Penn Avenue should be planned as a corridor, not pieced together one exception at a time. MG





3. Procedural Concern Regarding Notice to 902 Penn
Avenue / Murphy Jewelers

During the May 12 meeting, attorney Stephen Carponito appeared on behalf of Murphy Jewelers
of Wyomissing LLC and Mal Cody LLC, the owner and equitable owner of 902 Penn Avenue,
across from the 915 Penn Avenue site. He stated on the record that his client did not receive
notice of the March 30, 2026 conditional use hearing. He also cited the Borough's conditional
use notice requirement, including certified-mail notice to adjoining property owners, and stated
that if his client had received proper notice, they would have appeared at the hearing and sought
party or objector status.

That is not a small issue. Notice is not a courtesy. Notice is what gives affected property owners
a fair chance to appear, review the application, retain counsel if necessary, request party status,
cross-examine witnesses, and protect their interests before Council closes the evidentiary
record,

| am not stating here that the Borough definitively failed to send certified mail. | am stating that
once an affected property owner and its attorney raised that objection before the final vote,
Council should have paused and verified the record before voting.

The proper response should have been simple: table the decision briefly, produce the
certified-mail list, certified-mail receipts, returned-mail records if any, property-owner list,
radius/adjoining-owner determination, proof of publication, and proof of posting. If notice was
properly provided, the Borough could show it. If notice was not properly provided, then Council
should not treat the approval as clean.

It is especially troubling if the Borough’s position is that the vote would stand even if
certified-mail notice was not sent to an affected adjoining property owner. That should not be the
Borough's posture. A solicitor’s role is not only to defend a result after the fact. It is to help
Council avoid avoidable procedural defects before Council acts.

If the Borough later cannot prove compliance with its own notice requirements, the May 12
approval may be exposed to challenge. That is exactly why the issue should have been resolved
before the vote, not after.

For the record, | request that the Borough include the notice objection in the meeting record and

that the final written Findings of Fact, Discussion, Conclusions of Law, and Order for 915 Penn
Avenue specifically address:

4. Closing Request

Please include these comments in the public record for the May 12, 2026 Council meeting.

My request is straightforward: enforce rules fairly, document the process, respect public notice
requirements, and stop approving major exceptions at key locations without a broader plan.

Residents and nearby businesses should not have to guess how decisions are made. The
Borough should show the record, explain the standard, and follow the process.
