Written Public Comments for the May 12, 
2026 Borough Council Meeting 
Agenda and Non-Agenda Public Comment Periods 
Adrian Jadic, 48 Cardinal Road, Wyomissing 
Please include these written comments in the official record for the May 12, 2026 Wyomissing 
Borough Council meeting. These comments are intended to supplement and clarify the public 
comments I provided during both the agenda-item and non-agenda public comment periods. 
1. Kraft Municipal Group and Expanded Citation Authority 
I want to be clear at the outset: I am not opposed to code enforcement. Property maintenance, 
safe sidewalks, and consistent standards matter. My concern is with expanding enforcement 
authority without enough transparency, reporting, and safeguards. 
The May 12 agenda included action to ratify Kraft Municipal Group assuming responsibility for 
writing and filing citations for violations of the International Property Maintenance Code, effective 
May 1, 2026. That is a meaningful change. It gives a private, for-profit contractor a larger role in 
the path from inspection to citation. 
Before Council expands that authority, residents should know how the process will work: 
Who receives a warning first? 
Who receives a Notice of Violation? 
Who receives extra time? 
Who decides when a citation is filed? 
What Borough employee reviews the matter before it goes to court? 
How are errors corrected? 
How are residents protected from inconsistent or selective enforcement? 
These questions are not theoretical. The Borough does not publish a clear enforcement log 
showing complaints, warnings, Notices of Violation, citations, withdrawn citations, appeals, 
outcomes, or enforcement costs, even in aggregate form. Without that reporting, residents have 
no practical way to evaluate whether enforcement is fair, consistent, or cost-effective. 
If Kraft is going to inspect properties, prepare enforcement documents, conduct reinspections, 
and file citations, Council should adopt written guardrails. At minimum, there should be a written 
enforcement procedure, Borough review before citations are filed, monthly aggregate reporting, 
and clear cost tracking for contractor time tied to enforcement activity. 
DRAFT

Rules should be enforced. But enforcement power must be accountable. A private contractor 
should not receive expanded citation authority without public reporting and Borough oversight. 
2. 915 Penn Avenue Conditional Use Decision 
I also objected to approval of the 915 Penn Avenue conditional use request. 
This is not because I oppose redevelopment of that property. The site can and should be 
improved. The issue is whether Council should waive major standards at one of the most visible 
corners on Penn Avenue without a broader plan for the corridor. 
The applicant requested approval for a 30-unit apartment building with no first-floor office, retail, 
professional services, or restaurant use, and with 32 off-street parking spaces instead of the 45 
spaces normally required. That is not a minor adjustment. It goes directly to the purpose of the 
mixed-use redevelopment framework. 
This location is not an isolated back lot. It is at Penn Avenue and Park Road, a gateway location 
for the Borough. The Borough’s own zoning framework contemplates mixed-use redevelopment 
in this area. That means residential development should strengthen the commercial corridor, not 
replace it one waiver at a time. 
The all-residential proposal also raises obvious parking concerns. Thirty apartments with only 32 
spaces leaves almost no margin for visitors, second vehicles, workers, deliveries, or overflow. 
Nearby residents and business owners raised those concerns. Ms. Murphy from Murphy 
Jewelers and her attorney raised concerns. Other residents raised concerns about traffic, Court 
Lane, alley access, parking spillover, and the future character of Penn Avenue. 
Those concerns deserved more than a vote to approve. 
The prior 2023 conditional use approval had expired. Council was not required to simply repeat 
the same waiver. A prior approval under a different owner should not become an automatic 
entitlement for a new application. If Council keeps granting exceptions to the mixed-use and 
parking standards, then the exception effectively becomes the rule. 
Council should have denied the request, tabled it, or required a revised plan with a stronger 
public benefit: fewer units, a real first-floor commercial or professional component, better 
parking, clearer traffic and delivery planning, and stronger conditions tied to the long-term 
character of Penn Avenue.

certified-mail list, certified-mail receipts, returned-mail records if any, property-owner list, 
radius/adjoining-owner determination, proof of publication, and proof of posting. If notice was 
properly provided, the Borough could show it. If notice was not properly provided, then Council

certified-mail notice was not sent to an affected adjoining property owner. That should not be the

AN ORDINANCE OF THE BOROUGH OF WYOMISSING, BERKS COUNTY , 
PENNSYLV ANIA, AMENDING CHAPTER 21 ENTITLED “STREETS AND SIDEWALKS” 
TO ADD A PART 8 ENTITLED “SNOW AND ICE REMOV AL REGULATIONS”. 
 WHEREAS, the Borough has determined that it is in the best interests of its residents to 
establish snow and ice removal procedures for enforcement after snow events. 
 NOW THEREFORE BE IT ENACTED and ORDAINED by the Council of the Borough 
of Wyomissing, Berks County, Pennsylvania by the authority of the same as follows: 
SECTION 1. Addition of Snow and Ice Removal Regulations.  
Chapter 21 entitled “Streets and Sidewalks” is hereby amended and supplemented to add a 
Part 8 as follows: 
PART 8 
SNOW AND ICE REMOV AL REGULATIONS 
Section 801. Owner or O ccupant of Property to Remove S now and Ice from 
Sidewalk. 
1. I t shall be the duty of every person, entity, association, partnership 
or corporation owning or occupying any property fronting on any public street or 
alley within the Borough of Wyomissing to remove or cause to be removed from 
the sidewalks of said premises all ice, snow or sleet within a period of 36 hours 
after the same shall have ceased to fall thereon. Due to the increased pedestrian 
traffic on Penn Avenue  in the Borough, all ice, snow or sleet shall be removed 
within a period of 24 hours after the same shall have ceased to fall thereon. Said 
sidewalks shall be kept clear of such ice, snow and sleet thereafter, unless said snow 
and/or ice be of such a hardened or frozen condition that it cannot be removed 
without injury to the sidewalk, in which case the owner, occupant or tenant of each 
such property is required to place salt, sand or other abrasive or chemical material 
upon said snow and/or ice within the time provided above to render such sidewalks 
safe for travel.  In addition, school bus stop corners must have all snow and ice 
removed from them within 24 hours after the same shall have ceased to fall thereon. 
 2. For the purpose of complying with the provisions of Paragraph 1  
above, it shall be the duty of every such person, entity, association, partnership or 
corporation owning or occupying any property to conform to the requirements of 
this section, whether or not such owner is present on the property, and whether or 
not such owner has delegated such responsibility to a third party or tenant. In the 
instance of a building which is occupied by multiple residential or commercial 
tenants, any such snow and/or ice removal shall be the responsibility of the owner

of said premises, whether or not such responsibilities have been delegated to a third 
party. 
  (A) The owner of any property fronting on a public street or alley 
within the Borough of Wyomissing shall clear a path of at least 36 inches in width 
on the sidewalks fronting such premises. Sidewalks measuring less than 36 inches 
must be cleared entirely. 
(B) In order to accommodate increased pedestrian traffic on

payment shall not be made at Borough Hall within the ten-day period, that violator

Chapter. Notification by hand delivery shall include, among other things, affixing

Police Department: 
Police Chief John Phillips explained to the committee that the Magisterial District Justice’s 
office in West Reading is in jeopardy of permanent closure due to no current presiding judge. 
Municipalities were reassigned to various other MDJ offices, of which he expressed concern that 
West Reading and Wyomissing are now served by two different offices. Since both departments 
work hand in hand, this means extra time spent at one or the other MDJ office, and he pointed 
out that only one block is set aside to hear police matters. He also stressed that this is an 
inconvenience not only to the Police Department, but also business owners, residents, and any 
Borough related filings. Chief Phillips prepared a letter of opposition for the local MDJ office 
closure, to be provided during the County’s open public comment period, with the understanding 
that the County will be making a determination, however the PA Supreme Court must make the 
final decision. There was brief discussion that Council should draft a version, of which Borough 
DRAFT

Chief Phillips reviewed the packet prepared to address State Hill Road pedestrian safety matters, 
assessing various crosswalk, curbing and ramp challenges in the State Hill Road vicinity. He did 
however remind the committee that many of these concerns were to be addressed with the State 
Hill Road improvement phases over the next several years. He also pointed out various locations 
that indicate crossing is not permitted, and also noted the response from the PennDOT District 5 
representative that mid-block crossing needs a traffic study, the Borough is not permitted to 
install a crosswalk at a traffic signal without PennDOT reviewing, and signage would need 
PennDOT approvals. Based on the gathered statistics, there were two pedestrian fatalities in the 
vicinity of the Berkshire Square shopping complex, with both accidents being mutual fault driver 
and pedestrian. Chief Phillips shared that the area in which accidents are happening, Police 
enforcement is already occurring. It was pointed out that he can work with Public Works to see if

be putting information out on the Police social media pages pertaining to pedestrian safety.

the area, PennDOT must be involved if the Borough is leaning towards more crosswalks. It was

feels the equipment is obsolete since the new higher frequency was upgraded in 2013, and 
reached out to Radio Maintenance Inc., regarding removal of the old equipment. The committee 
questioned why the equipment needs to come out; allow WBWA to tear down the building and

Memorial Benches in the Parklands of Wyomissing 
Memorial benches offer another meaningful way to honor a loved one while enriching the 
parks for the entire community. Each bench provides a place to pause, reflect, and 
appreciate the natural splendor of the Parklands of Wyomissing. 
Cost & Inclusions 
The price for a memorial bench is $3,000.00, which includes: 
• A memorial plaque 
• A five-year warranty and maintenance 
o After the initial five-year period, donors may extend the maintenance 
program for $750.00, for an additional three-year period.   
• A personal meeting with a Parks representative to discuss and determine the best 
bench location 
• A personalized certificate signed by the Mayor 
• Acknowledgment on the Borough’s website under the Park & Shade Tree 
Commission page 
Ordering and Installation Timelines 
• Memorial Bench orders will be placed twice each year, once in February and once in 
August. To be included in either ordering period, all required paperwork, approvals, 
plaque information, and payment must be submitted before the Borough places the 
order. Applications or materials received after an ordering period has closed will be 
held for the next scheduled purchase window. 
• This ordering schedule allows the Borough to coordinate bench purchases, plaque 
production, delivery, and installation in an organized and cost-effective manner. 
Installation timelines may vary based on vendor availability, weather, ground 
conditions, staff scheduling, and the approved location within the Parklands. 
DRAFT

Bench Placement & Care 
• Donors are welcome to suggest a preferred location; however, the Borough will 
make the final determination to ensure each bench aligns with the overall design 
and visitor safety. 
• For the first five years, if a memorial bench is vandalized or damaged, the Borough 
will make efforts to repair it within available resources. 
• Outside the initial five years – without the maintenance program, if a bench 
becomes irreparable, hazardous, or visually unsuitable, Borough staff may remove 
it. 
• The Borough reserves the right to relocate memorial benches if necessary for 
safety, maintenance, or future park improvements; if relocation occurs, the donor 
will be notified of the new placement. 
• Donors may not remove, repair, modify, or decorate the bench without the prior 
written permission of the Park & Shade Tree Commission. 
Your contribution creates a lasting feature in the Parklands—an inviting spot where 
families, walkers, and visitors can rest and appreciate the quiet beauty around them. 
 
Program Disclaimer 
To preserve the integrity and long-term health of the Parklands, the Borough reserves the right to relocate or 
replace any memorial tree or bench if future park improvements require adjustments. Reasonable efforts will 
be made to contact the original donor before any relocation or replacement occurs.

LAND DEVELOPMENT 
PROCESS
Developer presents sketch plan to Borough for feedback and input
o There can be multiple iterations of this process
Developer submits a preliminary land development plan to the Borough
Multiple rounds of reviews by Borough staff, consultants, and Planning 
Commission
This process can lasts months or years and lead to negotiations over specific 
details, proposals, and needs for the developer and Borough
Preliminary plan is presented to Borough Counc il for review and approval
Developer submits a final development plan for same review and approval. Final 
plans have complete details on land use, layout, and infrastructure

LAND DEVELOPMENT PLANS
Approved land development plans are legally binding documents that 
"run with the land"
They can be sold to other persons who can choose to execute the plan or 
not
Assurances made by developers are not binding unless included in the 
land development plan or legally approved developer's agreement
Mistakes or oversights in this process negatively affect the community 
who is left with the project after it is completed
