ORDINANCE NO. 1464-2026 
AN ORDINANCE OF THE BOROUGH OF WYOMISSING, BERKS COUNTY, 
PENNSYLVANIA, AMENDING CHAPTER 27 (ZONING) OF THE CODE OF 
ORDINANCES OF THE BOROUGH OF WYOMISSING BY REPEALING AND 
RESTATING PART 5 -A, COMMERCIAL AND INDUSTRIAL REDEVELOPMENT 
OVERLAY DISTRICT, IN ITS  ENTIRETY; EXPANDING THE BOUNDARIES OF 
THE OVERLAY DISTRICT; ESTABLISHING PERMITTED USES, ESTABLISHING 
DEVELOPMENT STANDARDS; AND PROVIDING FOR SEVERABILITY, REPEAL 
OF INCONSISTENT ORDINANCES,  AN EFFECTIVE DATE, AND MODIFICATION 
OF CODE OF ORDINANCES. 
WHEREAS, the Borough of Wyomissing (the “Borough”) previously established the 
Commercial and Industrial Redevelopment Overlay District by Ordinance No. 1286, as amended by 
Ordinance No. 1381 and Ordinance No. 1397, codified at Chapter 27, Part 5- A, of the Borough Code 
of Ordinances; and 
WHEREAS, Borough Council finds that the comprehensive redevelopment of aging regional 
commercial sites requires flexible tools not fully provided by the existing overlay district, including 
provisions for fee -simple subdivision of individual buildings and building pads, shared parking and 
shared access among multiple lots under separate ownership, shared stormwater management and 
utility infrastructure administered by a mandatory lot owners association, incentives for bicycle and 
pedestrian improvements, and enhanced greenspace, shade tree, and landscaping standards within large 
parking areas and streetscape improvement standards along public streets adjacent to redevelopment 
sites, in each case coordinated with the Official Bike Route Map of the Borough of Wyomiss ing and 
the Borough of West Reading and with the landscaping regulations of §604 of the Zoning Ordinance; 
and 
WHEREAS, Section 605 of the Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning Code, 53 P.S. §10605, 
authorizes the creation of overlay zoning districts, and Sections 609 and 610 of the Municipalities 
Planning Code establish the procedures for enactment of zoning ordinance ame ndments, which 
procedures have been followed, including review by the Borough Planning Commission and the Berks 
County Planning Commission and a duly advertised public hearing; and 
WHEREAS, Borough Council finds that this Ordinance is consistent with the Borough 
Comprehensive Plan and will promote the public health, safety, and general welfare of the residents 
and inhabitants of the Borough. 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED AND ENACTED by the Borough Council of 
the Borough of Wyomissing, Berks County, Pennsylvania, as follows: 
SECTION 1. Chapter 27 (Zoning), Part 5- A, Commercial and Industrial Redevelopment Overlay 
District, of the Code of Ordinances of the Borough of Wyomissing is hereby repealed and restated in 
its entirety to read as set forth in Exhibit “A” attached hereto and incorporated herein by reference. 

SECTION 2. The Official Zoning Map of the Borough of Wyomissing is hereby amended to revise 
the boundaries of the Commercial and Industrial Redevelopment Overlay District as described in 
§501A.5 of Exhibit “A” and as depicted on the map attached hereto as Exhibit “B”, so that the overlay 
district includes, without limitation, the entirety of the properties identified on Exhibit “B”. 
SECTION 3. Severability. If any sentence, clause, section, or part of this Ordinance is for any reason 
found to be unconstitutional, illegal, or invalid, such unconstitutionality, illegality, or invalidity shall 
not affect or impair any of the remaining provisions, sentences, clauses, sections, or parts of this 
Ordinance, it being the intent of Borough Council that this Ordinance would have been adopted had 
such unconstitutional, illegal, or invalid provision not been included herein. 
SECTION 4. Repealer. All ordinances or parts of ordinances inconsistent with this Ordinance are 
hereby repealed to the extent of such inconsistency. 
SECTION 5. Effective Date. This Ordinance shall become effective at the earliest date permitted by 
law.  
SECTION 6.  Code of Ordinances .  The Code of Ordinances, as amended, of the Borough of 
Wyomissing, Berks County, Pennsylvania shall be and remain unchanged and in full force and effect 
except as amended, supplemented, and modified by this Ordinance.  This Ordinance shall become part 
of this Code of Ordinances upon adoption.

EXHIBIT “A” 
CHAPTER 27 – ZONING 
PART 5-A 
COMMERCIAL AND INDUSTRIAL REDEVELOPMENT OVERLAY DISTRICT 
(Repealed and Restated) 
§501A. Specific Intent. 
1. In addition to the conventional commercial and industrial zoning districts established by 
this Chapter, there shall be established a Commercial and Industrial Redevelopment Overlay 
District (the “Overlay District”). 
2. The Overlay District shall not be an independent zoning district, but shall be a set of 
special zoning regulations to be applied over and above the zoning regulations otherwise 
controlling upon a tract of land, as enabled by and described in Section 605 of the 
Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning Code. The district upon which the Overlay District is 
superimposed is referred to herein as the “underlying district.” 
3. The purpose of the Overlay District is to provide standards for the comprehensive 
redevelopment of aging commercial and industrial sites within the Borough of Wyomissing, 
by: (A) permitting certain uses or combinations of uses not otherwise permitted in the 
underlying district; (B) permitting a greater density and intensity of development than would 
otherwise be permitted in the underlying district; (C) permitting the fee-simple subdivision of 
individual buildings and building pads within a unified redevelopment site in the manner of a 
planned unit development; (D) permitting shared parking, shared access, shared stormwater 
management, and shared utility infrastructure among multiple lots under separate ownership, 
administered by a mandatory lot owners association; (E) providing reductions in required off-
street parking in exchange for bicycle and pedestrian improvements that implement the 
Official Bike Route Map of the Borough of Wyomissing and the Borough of West Reading 
as well as other improvements; and (F) requiring enhanced greenspace, shade trees, and 
landscaping within parking areas, consistent with §604 of this Chapter, and streetscape 
improvements along adjacent public streets, so as to promote the public health, safety, and 
general welfare of the residents and inhabitants of the Borough. 
4. A secondary purpose of the Overlay District is to encourage respect for the historic and 
architectural identity of the structures and neighborhoods in which redevelopment may occur 
under the terms of this Part, and to promote walkable, connected development that reduces 
reliance on automobiles. 
5. The Overlay District shall generally apply throughout those portions of the Commercial 
and Industrial Districts of the Borough of Wyomissing located to the south of the Warren 
Street Bypass and bounded to the north by properties located adjacent to both sides of Paper 
Mill Road and to the west by properties located adjacent to both sides of Woodland Road, 
and shall include the entirety of the property known as the Berkshire Mall, 1665 State Hill 
Road (PIN No. 96439719529232), together with all commercially zoned properties adjacent 
thereto lying between State Hill Road, Woodland Road, Paper Mill Road, and Route 422 (SR 
0422), as depicted on the Official Zoning Map, notwithstanding that portions of such 

properties may lie outside the boundaries otherwise described in this subsection. Where the 
boundary of the Overlay District divides a single lot, the entire lot shall be deemed to be 
within the Overlay District. A site is eligible for redevelopment under this Part provided the 
following requirements are met: 
A. A site proposed for redevelopment shall be under single ownership, or in the case of 
multiple ownership, the lot or lots shall be redeveloped according to a single overall plan 
(the “Master Redevelopment Plan”). 
B. The site proposed for redevelopment shall be a minimum of one (1) acre in area. If the 
site is not a single, contiguous parcel, no part of the site shall be separated by more than 
one hundred (100) feet from another part of the site. If a site containing a minimum of 
one (1) acre is approved under this Section, or was approved under a previously enacted 
version of a regulation on the same topic, smaller adjacent properties may qualify under 
this Section at a later date as long as the smaller adjacent properties are within the 
boundaries of the Overlay District and the other requirements of this Section (other than 
minimum site acreage) are met. 
C. The site proposed for redevelopment shall be occupied by existing commercial or 
industrial development, and the following shall apply: (1) the majority of the existing 
commercial or industrial development was originally constructed at least thirty (30) years 
prior to the proposed redevelopment; and (2) due to changing economic or technological 
conditions, the existing commercial or industrial development is no longer appropriate. 
D. The site is not required to be located within a single underlying zoning district, but all 
portions of the site which are planned to be redeveloped in accordance with this Part shall 
be located within a commercial or industrial zoning district or within fifty (50) feet of 
such a district. 
E. Land may be added to an established redevelopment site, provided the revised 
redevelopment site, as modified by the addition of land, continues to meet the 
requirements of this Part. 
F. Plans for redevelopment shall demonstrate, to the extent practically achievable, respect 
for the historic and architectural identity of the structures and neighborhoods in which 
redevelopment may occur under the terms of this Part. 
G. Plans for redevelopment shall include a schedule for completion of the development, 
which schedule shall be subject to the approval of the Borough Council. 
6. To the extent any regulation in this Part conflicts with any regulation in any other Part of 
this Chapter, the regulation in this Part shall be controlling. 
§502A. Uses Permitted by Right. 
Land and buildings in the Overlay District may be used for any one (1) or combination of the 
following purposes and no others, unless a special exception as provided in §503A or Section 
553 is granted: 
A. All uses and accessory uses permitted by right in any of the underlying districts which 
comprise the site. 
B. The following commercial uses, whether or not permitted in the underlying district: 

(1) Shopping center, retail store, and retail service establishment, including a 
department store, discount department store, free-standing discount superstore, and 
warehouse club, with no maximum gross floor area per establishment; 
(2) Grocery store, supermarket, and specialty food store; 
(3) Restaurant, café, coffee shop, and similar eating and drinking establishment, 
including such an establishment with a drive-through window, provided that stacking 
lanes shall be designed so that queued vehicles do not obstruct any parking aisle, 
driveway intersection, or pedestrian walkway; 
(4) Convenience store, with or without the retail sale of gasoline or other motor 
vehicle fuels, and automobile fuel or service station, provided that all fueling 
positions, canopies, and underground storage tanks comply with applicable federal 
and state regulations and are located at least one hundred (100) feet from any primary 
residential structure; 
(5) Medical office, medical clinic, walk-in clinic, urgent care facility, and outpatient 
medical facility; 
(6) Health and fitness club, gymnasium, and indoor recreation facility; 
(7) Business and professional offices, financial institutions with or without drive-
through facilities, and hotels; 
(8) Entertainment uses, including theaters and indoor amusement establishments; and 
(9) Multiple principal uses and multiple principal buildings upon a single lot, and any 
combination of the uses listed in this subsection within a single building. 
(10) Accessory storage for warehousing and off-site distributing of manufactured 
foods, goods, and materials shall be permitted as an accessory use provided that (1) 
the accessory storage is located in the same structure as the principal use and (2) said 
storage does not exceed twenty five (25) percent of the gross floor area of the 
structure where the principal and accessory use is located. 
C. Development containing apartments and/or townhouses, above one or more non-
residential uses permitted in any of the underlying districts which comprise the site, 
subject to the following: 
(1) No new residential use under this §502A.C shall be allowed on the first floor level 
of any property in the Overlay District. 
(2) Residential uses by right under this §502A.C shall not occupy more than thirty 
(30) percent of the combined area available for occupation within the redevelopment 
site. 
(3) Notwithstanding paragraph (2) above, development containing up to one hundred 
(100) percent apartments and/or townhouses shall be permitted without regard to the 
percentage of the combined area available for occupation where all of the following 
are satisfied: (a) the redevelopment site includes only a single existing building; (b) 
the existing building is being renovated rather than demolished and replaced; (c) the 
redevelopment site is under five (5) acres in area; (d) the redevelopment site is 
adjacent to a ten (10) acre or greater mixed use redevelopment site previously 
approved under this Part 5-A; and (e) pedestrian circulation in and through the 

redevelopment site is integrated with adjacent and adjoining existing or planned 
development in accordance with a sidewalk and street light plan acceptable to the 
Borough. 
(4) No single building may include a use under this §502A.C together with a use 
under §502A.D. Where uses under this §502A.C and §502A.D occur within a single 
redevelopment site, the uses shall be located within separate buildings. 
D. Development containing apartments and/or townhouses intended to be “55 or Over 
Housing” within the meaning of the Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C.A. §3601 et seq.), so as 
to qualify as “housing for older persons” within the meaning of the Fair Housing Act, 
above one or more non-residential uses permitted in any of the underlying districts which 
comprise the site, subject to the following: 
(1) No new residential use under this §502A.D shall be allowed on the first floor level 
of any property in the Overlay District. 
(2) The restriction shall be subject to all applicable Federal and State laws concerning 
“housing for older persons.” Occupancy of any dwelling unit shall be restricted to 
persons fifty-five (55) years of age or older (“age qualified”), provided, however, that 
a person who is age qualified may occupy such unit with such person’s spouse, 
regardless of age, and with a child nineteen (19) years of age or older who is not 
enrolled in a secondary school. No occupancy shall be permitted by any person under 
the age of nineteen (19) unless such person is a handicapped dependent protected by 
the provisions of the Fair Housing Act. A permanent resident is a person who resides 
in a dwelling unit for ninety (90) or more consecutive days. Permitted visitations shall 
not exceed ninety (90) consecutive days. 
(3) Accompanying the plan for redevelopment shall be a declaration of restrictive 
covenants (the “Age Restriction Declaration”) which must be approved by the 
Borough Council and recorded by the developer. The Age Restriction Declaration’s 
restrictions shall insure that the proposed development will function as depicted on 
the plan for redevelopment and be in compliance with the Federal Fair Housing Act 
Amendments of 1988, or as subsequently amended. The following are the minimum 
requirements for the Age Restriction Declaration: 
(a) Residency qualifications applicable to the development shall be included in 
the Age Restriction Declaration. Residents shall be limited by deed and lease to 
households including one permanent resident age fifty-five (55) years or older, 
and permanent occupancy by any person age nineteen (19) or younger shall be 
prohibited, except as follows: (i) individual units may occasionally house persons 
younger than nineteen (19) years of age, such as grandchildren, provided they 
reside within the unit for less than ninety (90) days in any calendar year; or (ii) 
members of a household shall not be required to vacate a dwelling unit if a 
resident age fifty-five (55) or older leaves the household due to death, divorce, or 
placement in a nursing or assisted care facility and the remaining household 
members would qualify for residency if the departed resident were still present in 
the household. 

(b) The applicant shall prove to the satisfaction of the Borough Council that an 
appropriate entity will have authority to assure compliance with the age 
limitation. 
(4) Residential uses by right under this §502A.D shall not occupy more than eighty 
(80) percent of the combined area available for occupation within the redevelopment 
site. Where uses under §502A.C and this §502A.D are combined within a single 
redevelopment site, the eighty (80) percent combined area regulation under this 
paragraph shall apply to the sum of the areas of the two uses, but in no event shall the 
use under §502A.C exceed thirty (30) percent of the total combined area available for 
occupation within the redevelopment site. 
(5) No single building may include a use under §502A.C or §503A.B together with a 
use under this §502A.D. Where such uses occur within a single redevelopment site, 
the uses shall be located within separate buildings. 
E. Any combination of the above uses, subject to the limitations set forth in this §502A. 
§503A. Uses Permitted by Special Exception. 
The following uses are permitted when special exceptions are granted by the Zoning Hearing 
Board in accordance with the applicable provisions of this Part: 
A. All uses and accessory uses permitted by special exception in any of the underlying 
commercial or industrial districts which comprise the site, excluding the adult uses listed 
in Section 553(A). 
B. Development containing apartments and/or townhouses above one or more non-
residential uses permitted in any of the underlying commercial or industrial districts 
which comprise the redevelopment site, subject to the following: 
(1) No new residential use under this §503A.B shall be allowed on the first floor level 
of any property in the Overlay District. 
(2) Residential uses under §502A.C and this §503A.B, together, shall not occupy 
more than sixty (60) percent of the combined area available for occupation within the 
redevelopment site. 
(3) Where uses under §502A.C and this §503A.B are proposed to occur along with 
the use under §502A.D, the eighty (80) percent combined area regulation under 
§502A.D(4) shall apply to the sum of the areas of the three uses, but in no event shall 
the use under §502A.C and the use under this §503A.B, together, exceed sixty (60) 
percent of the total combined area available for occupation within the redevelopment 
site. 
(4) No single building may include a use under §502A.C or this §503A.B together 
with a use under §502A.D. Where such uses occur within a single redevelopment site, 
the uses shall be located within separate buildings.

§505A. Fee-Simple (Planned Unit Development Style) Subdivision; Financial Subdivision. 
1. Purpose. It is the intent of this Section to permit, within an approved redevelopment site, 
the division of land into individual fee-simple lots corresponding to individual buildings, 
building pads, or portions of the site, including divisions made for legal, financing, taxation, 
or accounting purposes without physical separation of buildings or site improvements (a 

“financial subdivision”), while requiring that the redevelopment site continue to function, 
and be regulated, as a single unified development. 
2. Subdivision Permitted. Notwithstanding any contrary provision of this Chapter or of the 
Borough Subdivision and Land Development Ordinance, land within an approved 
redevelopment site may be subdivided into fee-simple lots, and buildings may be conveyed 
on separate lots, subject to the following: 
A. Lot lines may be drawn at or around the perimeter of a building or building pad, along 
the face of a building wall, or through a party wall separating attached buildings or 
attached units within a building; 
B. Interior lots (lots which do not abut a public street) shall be permitted, and no 
minimum lot size, minimum lot width, minimum street frontage, minimum yard, or 
maximum building coverage requirement shall apply to any individual interior lot, 
provided that each such lot has guaranteed vehicular and pedestrian access to a public 
street by way of recorded easement over the common driveways and access ways of the 
redevelopment site; 
C. Zero (0) foot setbacks shall be permitted along any lot line internal to the 
redevelopment site; and 
D. Off-street parking, loading, stormwater management facilities, signage, landscaping, 
and open space serving any lot may be located on any other lot within the redevelopment 
site, provided such facilities are secured by recorded cross-easements and are subject to 
the Lot Owners Association documents required by §511A. 
3. Unified Compliance. Compliance with the use, area, bulk, coverage, open area, parking, 
landscaping, stormwater, and all other requirements of this Part shall be determined on the 
basis of the overall redevelopment site as shown on the approved Master Redevelopment 
Plan, and not on the basis of individual lots. No subdivision, conveyance, or lease of any lot 
or building shall create or increase any nonconformity with respect to the overall 
redevelopment site, and no lot shall be conveyed except subject to the Declaration required 
by §511A. 
4. Required Documents. As a condition of final approval of any subdivision under this 
Section, the applicant shall record, in form approved by the Borough Solicitor: (A) a 
Declaration of Covenants, Conditions, Restrictions and Easements (the “Declaration”) 
satisfying §511A; (B) reciprocal easement agreements (or equivalent provisions within the 
Declaration) providing perpetual cross-access, cross-parking, utility, drainage, stormwater, 
construction, and maintenance easements benefitting and burdening every lot within the 
redevelopment site; and (C) a plan note on the record plan stating that the lots shown thereon 
are part of a unified redevelopment site under Part 5-A of the Borough Zoning Ordinance and 
may not be developed, used, or conveyed except in accordance with the approved Master 
Redevelopment Plan and the Declaration. 
5. Subsequent Changes. Following recording, individual lots may be further subdivided, 
consolidated, or reconfigured, and lot lines may be adjusted, by revised subdivision plan, 
provided the overall redevelopment site remains in compliance with this Part and the 
Declaration is amended as necessary.

4. Alternative Parking Demand Analysis. In lieu of or in addition to the computations under 
subsections 1 and 3, the Borough Council may approve a parking supply based upon a 
parking demand analysis prepared by a qualified professional traffic engineer utilizing site-
specific parking counts and/or the parking generation methodologies of the Institute of 
Transportation Engineers (ITE), current edition, including a time-of-day shared parking 
analysis (a “Parking Demand Analysis”). The approved Parking Demand Analysis for a 
redevelopment site shall establish the minimum parking floor for that site under subsection 
5.D below. 
5. Parking Reductions for Bicycle and Pedestrian Improvements. It is the express intent of 
this Part to permit the reduction of required off-street parking in exchange for bicycle and 
pedestrian improvements. The number of off-street parking spaces otherwise required under 
this Section shall be reduced by the following credits, subject to the limitations of subsection 
5.D: 
A. Credits. The following credits may be combined (said credits are also reflected in the 
table set forth below): 
(1) Bicycle parking and support facilities: five (5) required automobile spaces waived 
for each installed and maintained rack, locker, bicycle room, or other secure facility 
accommodating at least four (4) bicycles; five (5) required automobile spaces waived 
for each installed and maintained public bicycle repair station equipped with, at a 
minimum, a fixed tool array and an air pump; and five (5) required automobile spaces 
waived for each installed and maintained bus stop shelter; in each case up to a 
maximum aggregate reduction under this subsection (1) of three percent (3%) of the 
base requirement; 
(2) Internal pedestrian network: a reduction of five percent (5%) of the base 
requirement where a continuous internal sidewalk and crosswalk network at least five 
(5) feet in width connects every principal building entrance to every other principal 
building, to all parking areas serving the building, and to the perimeter public 
sidewalk system, with raised or high-visibility crosswalks at all driveway and aisle 
crossings; 

(3) Perimeter and off-site connections: a reduction of two percent (2%) of the base 
requirement for each publicly accessible pedestrian or bicycle connection constructed 
by the applicant linking the redevelopment site to an adjoining public street, adjoining 
residential neighborhood, transit stop, or adjoining trail or planned trail (including 
sidewalks and multi-use paths along State Hill Road, Woodland Road, and Paper Mill 
Road, coordinated with PennDOT where applicable), up to a maximum reduction of 
eight percent (8%). To qualify for a credit under this subsection (3), a connection 
shall connect to, or extend, a bicycle route, trail, pedestrian facility, or transit stop 
shown on the Official Bike Route Map of the Borough of Wyomissing and the 
Borough of West Reading, as adopted by ordinance of Borough Council and as 
thereafter amended (the “Official Bike Route Map”), or shall be identified as a 
priority connection by the Borough Planning Commission. All connections 
constructed under this subsection shall be designed to be consistent with the Official 
Bike Route Map; 
(4) Multi-use path or bicycle lane: a reduction of five percent (5%) of the base 
requirement where the applicant constructs a multi-use path at least ten (10) feet in 
width, or dedicated bicycle lanes, along the full frontage of the redevelopment site on 
at least one abutting arterial or collector road; 
(5) Pedestrian amenities: a reduction of up to three percent (3%) of the base 
requirement, in the discretion of Borough Council, for pedestrian-scale lighting, 
benches, shade structures, bus shelters, and similar amenities installed along the 
internal pedestrian network; and 
(6) Electric vehicle and micromobility infrastructure: a reduction of one (1) space for 
each two (2) installed electric vehicle charging spaces and for each shared 
micromobility (bicycle or scooter share) station, up to a maximum reduction of two 
percent (2%) of the base requirement. 
B. All improvements for which a credit is claimed shall be shown on the Master 
Redevelopment Plan, shall be constructed prior to or concurrently with the phase of 
development they serve, and shall be perpetually maintained by the Lot Owners 
Association. Failure to maintain any such improvement shall render the corresponding 
parking reduction void, and the Borough may require restoration of the improvement or 
provision of the waived parking. 
C. Maximum Reduction. The aggregate reduction under this subsection 5 shall not 
exceed twenty-five percent (25%) of the base parking requirement computed under 
subsections 1 through 3. 
D. Parking Floor. Notwithstanding any other provision of this Section, in no event shall 
the total number of off-street parking spaces provided within the overall redevelopment 
site, after application of all shared parking computations and all reduction credits, be 
fewer than the peak parking demand identified in an approved Parking Demand Analysis 
for the redevelopment site. 
E. Consistency with the Official Bike Route Map. The Master Redevelopment Plan shall 
depict the redevelopment site in relation to the Official Bike Route Map, shall identify 
each bicycle route, trail, pedestrian facility, and transit stop shown on the Official Bike 
Route Map which abuts the redevelopment site or lies within one-half (1/2) mile of it, and 

shall show how the bicycle and pedestrian improvements proposed under this subsection 
5 connect to those facilities. Where the Official Bike Route Map depicts a route along or 
across the frontage of the redevelopment site, the applicant shall construct the portion of 
that route lying within or along the redevelopment site to the standards of the Official 
Bike Route Map and other applicable regulations. 
6. Parking Design. Parking stalls shall be a minimum of nine (9) feet by eighteen (18) feet 
with twenty-four (24) foot two-way aisles, except that accessible spaces, parallel spaces, and 
oversize spaces shall be dimensioned in accordance with applicable law and sound 
engineering practice as approved by the Borough Engineer.

§507A. Parking Area Greenspace, Shade Trees, and Landscaping. 
1. Purpose. Large expanses of unbroken pavement are contrary to the purposes of this Part. 
Parking areas within the Overlay District shall incorporate substantial interior and perimeter 
greenspace, shade trees, and landscaping to reduce heat island effect, manage stormwater, 
provide safe pedestrian circulation, and improve the appearance of the redevelopment site. 
2. Interior Landscaped Area. Not less than ten percent (10%) of the interior area of each 
surface parking lot containing twenty (20) or more spaces (measured as the area devoted to 
parking stalls and drive aisles) shall consist of landscaped islands, medians, or planting strips. 
Landscaped areas within parking lots shall count toward the Open Area requirement of 
§504A. 
3. Landscape Islands. A landscaped island not less than nine (9) feet in width and not less 
than one hundred sixty (160) square feet in area shall be provided: (A) at each end of each 
parking row; and (B) at intervals such that no more than fifteen (15) contiguous parking 
spaces occur in any row without an intervening landscaped island. Landscaped medians not 
less than ten (10) feet in width shall be provided between every fourth double row of parking, 
and such medians shall, wherever practicable, incorporate the pedestrian walkways required 
by §506A.5.A(2). 
4. Shade Trees. Deciduous trees shall be provided within or immediately adjacent to each 
surface parking lot at a ratio of not less than one (1) deciduous tree for each ten (10) parking 
spaces, any fractional result being rounded up to the next whole tree. Deciduous trees located 
within interior landscaped islands shall be shade trees; deciduous trees located along the 
perimeter of a parking lot may be either shade trees or ornamental trees. Each shade tree shall 
be a minimum of two and one-half (2.5) inch caliper measured at six (6) inches above grade, 

shall be limbed up to six (6) feet in height, and shall be a species having a mature canopy 
spread of not less than thirty (30) feet selected from the recommended tree lists in §604 of 
this Chapter or otherwise approved by the Borough. Each ornamental tree shall meet the 
minimum size standards of §604. Each required tree shall be planted in a pervious area of not 
less than one hundred sixty (160) square feet (or in a structural soil or suspended pavement 
system providing not less than four hundred (400) cubic feet of soil volume per tree), within 
a landscaped island or within ten (10) feet of the parking lot perimeter, with the center of the 
tree located not less than three (3) feet from the edge of any curb or paving and not less than 
ten (10) feet from any underground or overhead utility. Tree species shall be diversified such 
that no single species comprises more than twenty-five percent (25%) of required parking lot 
trees, not less than sixty-six percent (66%) of the required trees shall be of species native to 
the northeastern United States, and no species or cultivar deemed invasive by the 
Pennsylvania Department of Conservation and Natural Resources shall be planted. The 
design shall achieve, at tree maturity, canopy coverage of not less than thirty percent (30%) 
of the surface parking area. 
5. Perimeter Landscaping. A landscaped planting strip not less than ten (10) feet in width 
shall be provided between any surface parking area and any public street right-of-way, and 
not less than fifteen (15) feet in width between any surface parking area and any residential 
district boundary, planted with a combination of shade trees, ornamental trees, shrubs, and 
groundcover forming an effective visual buffer as approved by the Borough Planning 
Commission. Where such planting strip includes a sidewalk or pedestrian or bicycle path, the 
planting strip shall include landscaped areas on either side of such sidewalk or path.  Such 
sidewalks, pedestrian, or bicycle paths shall not count as impervious cover under Section 
504A above. Where a surface parking area abuts a residential zoning district or an existing 
residential use, the planting strip shall constitute a screen buffer meeting §604 of this 
Chapter, installed at a minimum height of six (6) feet and producing, within three (3) years, a 
complete and continuous visual screen of not less than six (6) feet in height. Screen buffer 
plantings shall be fully contained between the property line and the yard setback line. 
Perimeter landscaping shall be shown on the landscape plan required by subsection 7 and is 
subject to approval by the Borough Planning Commission. 
6. Stormwater Integration. Landscaped islands, medians, and perimeter strips shall, wherever 
feasible as determined by the Borough Engineer, be designed as rain gardens, bioretention 
areas, bioswales, or other vegetated stormwater best management practices receiving runoff 
from adjacent pavement, and such facilities shall count toward both the landscaping 
requirements of this Section and the stormwater requirements of §509A. 
7. Landscape Plan; Maintenance. A landscape plan shall be submitted as part of a preliminary 
plan or final plan (if not preceded by a preliminary plan or zoning permit). The landscape 
plan shall be prepared by a Landscape Architect with current and active registration in the 
Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, shall meet the requirements of §604 of this Chapter, and is 
subject to approval by the Borough Planning Commission. All required landscaping and 
shade trees shall be perpetually maintained or replaced, and any plant material which is in 
decline or has expired shall be replaced in kind within six (6) months of observance, by the 
Lot Owners Association pursuant to §511A. 
8. Relationship to §604. The requirements of this Section are in addition to the landscaping 
requirements of §604 of this Chapter. Where a requirement of this Section and a requirement 

of §604 differ, the more restrictive requirement shall apply. Any landscape requirement 
calculation under this Section which results in a fraction shall be rounded up to a whole 
plant. 
§508A.  Recreation Dedication. 
1. Applicability. For each redevelopment project under this Part which includes dwelling 
units, the applicant shall satisfy the recreation land dedication and fee requirements of §509.1 
of the Borough Subdivision and Land Development Ordinance. 
2. Open space provided under this Section shall count toward the Open Area requirement of 
§504A, and publicly accessible plazas, greens, seating areas, and trail corridors within the 
redevelopment site may be credited toward this requirement in the discretion of Borough 
Council. 
§509A. Shared Stormwater Management. 
1. Redevelopment under this Part shall comply with the Borough Stormwater Management 
Ordinance and all applicable state and federal requirements, including NPDES permitting. 
2. Shared Facilities Permitted. Stormwater management facilities — including detention and 
retention basins, subsurface storage, rain gardens, bioretention areas, water quality devices, 
conveyance systems, and outfalls — may be designed, constructed, owned, and maintained 
on a shared basis serving multiple lots and multiple buildings within the redevelopment site, 
notwithstanding that such lots are under different ownership, and may be located on any lot 
within the redevelopment site. Rate, volume, and water quality compliance shall be 
computed on the basis of the overall redevelopment site. 
3. Easements and Responsibility. All shared stormwater facilities shall be located within 
recorded drainage and access easements benefitting each lot they serve and the Borough. The 
Lot Owners Association shall own or control, and shall be solely responsible for the 
inspection, operation, maintenance, repair, and replacement of, all shared stormwater 
facilities, pursuant to a recorded Stormwater Management Facilities Operation and 
Maintenance Agreement in form approved by the Borough Solicitor and Borough Engineer, 
which agreement shall: (A) run with the land and bind all present and future lot owners; (B) 
grant the Borough the right, but not the obligation, following notice and opportunity to cure, 
to enter upon the site, perform required maintenance, and lien each lot within the 
redevelopment site for its proportionate share of the cost; (C) require routine inspection and 
reporting to the Borough at intervals specified by the Borough Engineer; and (D) require the 
Association to maintain a dedicated reserve for stormwater facility repair and replacement. 
§510A. Utility Infrastructure. 
1. All new and relocated electric, telecommunications, and cable service lines within the 
redevelopment site shall be placed underground, except for transformers, switchgear, and 
similar at-grade appurtenances, which shall be screened by landscaping or architectural 
features. Existing electric utility service areas may remain and be upgraded in place. 
2. Shared private utility infrastructure — including private water and sanitary sewer laterals 
and mains, fire loops and hydrants, stormwater conveyance, site lighting, common signage 
electrical service, and communications conduit — serving more than one lot or building shall 
be permitted, shall be located within recorded utility easements benefitting each lot served, 

and shall be owned or controlled, operated, maintained, repaired, and replaced by the Lot 
Owners Association pursuant to §511A, except to the extent such facilities are accepted for 
dedication by the applicable public utility, authority, or the Borough. 
3. Public and Private Demarcation. The Master Redevelopment Plan shall clearly identify, 
for each utility, the point at which public facilities end and private facilities begin, as follows: 
(A) for water service, the Borough or the applicable authority or utility shall own, operate, 
and maintain the public main and the master meter, and all water distribution piping, valves, 
hydrants, submeters, laterals, and appurtenances located downstream of the master meter 
shall be private; (B) for sanitary sewer service, the Borough or the applicable authority shall 
own, operate, and maintain the public sewer main, and all collection piping, laterals, 
cleanouts, manholes, and appurtenances located upstream of the point of connection to the 
public main shall be private; and (C) all facilities identified as private shall be owned or 
controlled, operated, maintained, repaired, and replaced by the Lot Owners Association 
pursuant to §511A, and shall not be accepted for dedication by the Borough or any authority 
except by express written agreement. Where a master meter serves more than one lot or 
building, the Declaration shall provide for submetering or for equitable allocation of the 
metered charges among the lots and buildings served. 
4. The Master Redevelopment Plan shall include a composite utility plan demonstrating 
adequate capacity, metering arrangements, and allocation of maintenance responsibility for 
all shared utility infrastructure, subject to approval by the Borough Engineer and the 
applicable utility providers. Adequate capacity shall be demonstrated by written confirmation 
from each applicable utility provider or authority. 
§511A. Lot Owners Association. 
1. Association Required. Prior to or concurrently with the recording of any subdivision plan 
creating more than one lot within a redevelopment site, or the conveyance of any portion of a 
redevelopment site, the applicant shall form a lot owners association, business owners 
association, or functionally equivalent entity organized under Pennsylvania law (the 
“Association”), and shall record the Declaration establishing the Association and governing 
the redevelopment site. The Declaration and the Association’s organizational documents 
shall be subject to review and approval by the Borough Solicitor for compliance with this 
Section. 
2. Mandatory Membership; Covenants Running with the Land. Every owner of a lot within 
the redevelopment site shall automatically be a member of the Association. The Declaration 
shall run with the land, shall bind all present and future owners, occupants, and mortgagees, 
and shall not be amended in any manner affecting the obligations described in this Section, 
nor shall the Association be dissolved, without the prior written consent of Borough Council. 
3. Required Responsibilities. The Declaration shall obligate the Association, at a minimum, 
to own or control and to perpetually operate, inspect, maintain, repair, replace, insure, and 
administer: 
A. All shared stormwater management facilities, in accordance with §509A and the 
recorded Stormwater Management Facilities Operation and Maintenance Agreement; 

B. All shared private utility infrastructure described in §510A, including private water, 
sanitary sewer, fire protection, storm sewer, site lighting, and communications facilities 
not dedicated to a public utility or authority; 
C. All shared parking areas, drive aisles, access driveways, service drives, and loading 
areas, including pavement maintenance, restriping, snow and ice removal, and 
enforcement of the shared parking arrangements approved under §506A; 
D. All common landscaping, parking lot greenspace, shade trees, buffer plantings, and 
open space areas required under §§507A and 508A, including replacement of dead or 
diseased material; 
E. All internal sidewalks, crosswalks, multi-use paths, bicycle parking, transit amenities, 
and other bicycle and pedestrian improvements for which a parking reduction credit was 
granted under §506A.5, and all streetscape improvements required under §512A.6; 
F. Common signage, common site lighting, and common refuse and recycling facilities; 
and 
G. Any other common elements identified on the Master Redevelopment Plan. 
4. Assessments and Reserves. The Declaration shall grant the Association the power to levy 
assessments against each lot sufficient to fund the responsibilities described in subsection 3, 
shall provide that unpaid assessments constitute a lien on the delinquent lot, and shall require 
the Association to maintain (A) an operating budget adopted annually and (B) a capital 
reserve fund, funded at a level reasonably sufficient, based on a reserve study updated not 
less than every five (5) years, for the repair and replacement of shared stormwater facilities, 
shared utility infrastructure, and shared pavement. 
5. Borough Enforcement Rights. The Declaration shall name the Borough as a third-party 
beneficiary with the right, but not the obligation, to enforce the Association’s obligations 
under this Section. If the Association fails to perform any obligation relating to stormwater 
management, utility infrastructure, life safety, or required landscaping after thirty (30) days’ 
written notice (or immediately in the event of an emergency), the Borough may perform such 
obligation and recover its costs, together with reasonable attorneys’ fees and an 
administrative charge, from the Association, and may lien each lot within the redevelopment 
site for its proportionate share thereof, in the manner provided for municipal claims. 
6. Reporting. The Association shall provide to the Borough, upon request and in any event 
not less than annually, evidence of its continued good standing, its current budget and reserve 
balances, its stormwater inspection reports, and the name and contact information of its 
manager or responsible officer. 
§512A. Additional Performance Standards. 
1. The horizontal distance in feet at the closest place between any principal buildings on the 
same lot shall not be less than twenty-five (25) feet, provided that buildings separated by a 
lot line created under §505A, and attached buildings, shall be exempt from this requirement. 
2. Wall Signs and Freestanding Signs. Wall signs and freestanding signs shall be permitted in 
accordance with §606C, except that the following provisions shall apply: 
A. Freestanding Signs shall not exceed twenty-five feet in height and shall not exceed 
100 sq. ft. of gross sign area per face. Freestanding signs shall not have more than 2 

faces. No more than one freestanding sign shall be permitted for each 1,500 feet of street 
frontage which the freestanding sign abuts. 
B. The total sign area limitation of §606C.2 shall not apply. 
C. Within the redevelopment site, a sign attached to a building wall is not required to face 
a public street. Said signs shall be subject to the requirement that the sign shall not have 
an area exceeding 10% of the building wall to which it is attached, or two square feet of 
area for each one foot of building height plus two square feet of area for each one foot of 
building length of the wall to which it is attached, whichever is less. 
D. Signs attached to a building wall shall not exceed 100 square feet for each separate use 
within the redevelopment site (not including the freestanding sign area). 
3. Directional Signs. Within the overall redevelopment site, one (1) sign facing each direction 
of travel, not exceeding six (6) square feet in area, is permitted at each street intersection and 
at each driveway intersection, outside the legal right-of-way, to identify the redevelopment 
site and to give directions. These signs are in addition to all other signs permitted under this 
Chapter and do not count toward any sign area limitation of §606C. 
4. When a part of the overall redevelopment site is separated from another by no more than 
one hundred (100) feet in accordance with §501A.5.B: (A) the minimum distance between 
the centerline of a driveway where it intersects a street and the centerline of another driveway 
on the same lot or another lot (including another lot which is outside of the overall 
redevelopment site) where that other driveway intersects the same street shall be one hundred 
(100) feet; (B) the centerline of a driveway where it intersects a street shall be a minimum of 
seventy-five (75) feet from a lot line, provided that it may be less than seventy-five (75) feet 
from a lot line if it will be at least one hundred (100) feet from the nearest driveway on the 
adjoining lot (including an adjoining lot which is outside of the overall redevelopment site); 
and (C) the minimum distance between the centerline of a driveway where it intersects a 
street and the intersection of street cartway lines (except in the case of a street intersecting 
the street the driveway intersects directly opposite from the driveway) shall be one hundred 
(100) feet. The driveway locations and spacing proposed under this subsection shall be 
supported by a traffic impact study prepared by a qualified professional traffic engineer and 
submitted with the Master Redevelopment Plan, addressing driveway location and spacing, 
sight distance, internal circulation, and, where applicable, Pennsylvania Department of 
Transportation Highway Occupancy Permit requirements. Borough Council, upon the 
recommendation of the Borough Engineer or the Borough’s traffic engineer, may require 
greater spacing than specified in this subsection where necessary for traffic safety. 
5. Improvements within or affecting the right-of-way of State Hill Road (SR 3055), Route 
422 (SR 0422), or any other state highway, including sidewalks, multi-use paths, traffic 
signals, and corridor improvements, shall be coordinated with and subject to the approval of 
the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation. 
6. Streetscape Improvements. Streetscape improvements shall be provided along the entire 
frontage of the redevelopment site on each public street adjacent to the redevelopment site, 
within or immediately adjacent to the street right-of-way, in the locations shown on the 
approved Master Redevelopment Plan, as follows: 
A. Street Lights. Street lights meeting the Borough standard shall be installed along each 
such public street frontage, at spacing approved by the Borough Engineer. Each street 

light shall consist of a decorative pole and luminaire matching, in pole design, height, 
color, and luminaire style, the decorative street lights installed along Park Road and Penn 
Avenue, or a Borough-approved equivalent. All street lights installed under this 
subsection shall comply with §605 of this Chapter. 
B. Street Trees. Street trees shall be provided along each such public street frontage in 
accordance with §604 of this Chapter, at a ratio of not less than one (1) shade tree for 
each forty-five (45) feet of street frontage which demonstrates the open space to 
accommodate the tree, planted within twenty (20) feet of the curbed cartway in non-
grated tree wells or within a planting strip located between the curb and the sidewalk. 
Each street tree shall be a minimum of two and one-half (2.5) inch caliper measured at 
six (6) inches above grade, shall be limbed up to six (6) feet in height, and shall be of a 
species selected from the recommended tree lists in §604 or otherwise approved by the 
Borough. Street trees shall be planted in an evenly spaced, straight row unless a staggered 
or naturalistic alignment is approved by the Shade Tree Commission. Required street 
trees shall be shown on the landscape plan required by §507A.7, and the applicant shall 
make payment to the Borough for each required street tree in accordance with the 
Borough fee schedule prior to the release of plans for recording, whereupon the Borough 
shall purchase and install the trees as provided in §604. No existing tree within a public 
right-of-way shall be removed or disturbed without the approval of the Shade Tree 
Commission. Street trees provided under this subsection may be counted toward the 
perimeter landscaping requirement of §507A.5, but shall not be counted toward the 
parking area shade tree requirement of §507A.4. 
C. Pedestrian Benches and Seating Areas. Pedestrian benches shall be installed along 
each such public street frontage at intervals of not more than three hundred (300) feet, 
with each bench centered between a pair of street trees, and with at least one bench 
located at each transit stop along the frontage. Each bench shall match, in design, 
material, and color, the standard public bench installed along the north side of the Penn 
Avenue and Park Road intersection, or a Borough-approved equivalent. In addition, along 
each public street frontage exceeding one thousand (1,000) feet in length, at least one (1) 
landscaped pedestrian seating area shall be provided at a location shown on the Master 
Redevelopment Plan. Each seating area shall contain not less than four hundred (400) 
square feet, shall be directly accessible from the public sidewalk, and shall include not 
fewer than two (2) benches, shade trees or a shade structure, ornamental planting, and 
pedestrian-scale lighting. Seating areas provided under this subsection shall count toward 
the Open Area requirement of §504A. 
D. Coordination with PennDOT. Streetscape improvements within or affecting the right-
of-way of State Hill Road (SR 3055), Route 422 (SR 0422), or any other state highway 
shall be coordinated with and subject to the approval of the Pennsylvania Department of 
Transportation in accordance with subsection 5 of this §512A. 
E. Plan; Construction; Maintenance. All streetscape improvements shall be shown on the 
Master Redevelopment Plan, shall be constructed prior to or concurrently with the phase 
of development they serve, and shall be perpetually operated, maintained, repaired, and 
replaced by the Lot Owners Association pursuant to §511A, except to the extent any such 
improvement is accepted for dedication and maintenance by the Borough or the 
Pennsylvania Department of Transportation. Street trees purchased and installed by the 

Borough pursuant to subsection B shall be maintained by the Borough, and the 
maintenance obligation of the Lot Owners Association under this subsection shall not 
extend to those trees. 
§513A. Master Redevelopment Plan; Procedure. 
1. An applicant seeking to develop under this Part shall submit a Master Redevelopment Plan 
depicting, for the overall redevelopment site: existing and proposed lots and lot lines; 
existing and proposed buildings, uses, and gross floor areas; parking areas, parking 
computations, and any shared parking plan and Parking Demand Analysis; the bicycle and 
pedestrian improvements for which credits are claimed under §506A.5, together with the 
depiction of the redevelopment site in relation to the Official Bike Route Map required by 
§506A.5.E; landscaping, parking area greenspace, and shade tree plans under §507A, 
including the landscape plan required by §507A.7; open space under §508A; shared 
stormwater management facilities under §509A; the composite utility plan under §510A, 
including the public and private demarcation required by §510A.3; the streetscape 
improvements required under §512A.6; the traffic impact study required under §512A.4; a 
phasing schedule under §501A.5.G; and the proposed Declaration and Association 
documents under §511A. 
2. The Master Redevelopment Plan shall be reviewed by the Borough Planning Commission 
and approved by Borough Council in conjunction with land development approval under the 
Borough Subdivision and Land Development Ordinance. Minor modifications consistent 
with the approved Master Redevelopment Plan, as determined by the Zoning Officer in 
consultation with the Borough Engineer, shall not require reapproval; other modifications 
shall be submitted as revisions to the Master Redevelopment Plan.

 §604. Landscaping.  
 
1.  Introduction 
A.  When a Landscape Plan Submission is Required 
(i) A Landscape Plan shall be submitted as part of a preliminary plan or final 
plan (if not preceded by a preliminary plan).  
(ii) The Landscape Plan shall be prepared by a Landscape Architect. 
(iii) When landscape requirements are provided within a specific zoning 
district, the more restrictive landscape requirement shall apply.  
B. What is Required of the Landscape Plan 
(i) The Landscape Plan shall meet the requirements of preliminary and final 
plans as required within the Subdivision and Land Development 
Ordinance.  
(ii) Emphasis shall be placed upon an accurate demonstration of existing 
trees and vegetation to be removed and to remain, as well as, a proposed 
Landscape Plan. 
(iii) Existing and proposed utilities, including site lighting, shall be depicted 
on the Landscape Plan.  
(iv) Any clear sight triangles shall be depicted on the Landscape Plan. 
(v) The Landscape Plan shall clearly indicate what landscape requirements 
the proposed plantings (and existing plantings to remain) are designated 
to, as well as, the calculations utilized to determine the quantity of plants 
within specific requirements. Any landscape requir ement calculations 
that result in a fraction, shall be rounded up to a whole plant.  
(vi) When landscape requirements within a specific zoning district vary from 
this Section, the more restrictive landscape requirement shall apply. 
(vii) Installation details and notes for proposed plants shall be provided. 
 
2. Protection of Existing Trees  
A. What Trees are to be Protected 
(i) Trees located within the public right -of-way shall not be removed or 
disturbed without approval by the Shade Tree Commission. 
(ii) A temporary, sturdy physical barrier, such as a snow fence, shall be 
erected a minimum of one foot outside the dripline or a minimum of 20 
feet from the tree's trunk, whichever is greater, on all sides of freestanding 
trees, tree masses or woodlands prior to major clearing or construction. 
The barrier shall be placed to prevent disturbance to or compaction of soil 
inside the barrier and shall remain until construction is complete. When 
construction is complete, a note on the approved Landscape Plan  shall 
direct the contractor to remove the barrier.  
(iii) Trees to remain which require or incur disturbance during construction, 
will be subject to review by the Borough Arborist to determine if the tree 
can be salvaged as a result of this disturbance. A note to this activity shall 
appear on the approved Landscape Plan if work occurs in a public right -
of-way.  

3 
 
B. Existing Trees to Remain Towards Landscape Requirements  
(i) Existing trees to remain, that are 2 1/2 inches or more in diameter 
(measured at 6” above grade), may be utilized towards respective 
landscape requirements, as may be appropriate. 
(ii) When possible, it is encouraged to preserve and utilize existing 
woodlands and hedges towards landscape requirements for perimeter 
buffers and screens. The quality and density of the vegetation to remain 
will be reviewed upon a case-by-case basis. 
 
3. Required Street Trees 
A. When Street Trees are Required  
(i) Any proposed subdivision or land development shall provide the required 
street trees within twenty feet of the curbed cartway provided there is space 
to accommodate the tree. The Borough is responsible for purchasing and 
installing any required street trees. The developer shall make a payment for 
each required street tree to the Borough, in conjunction with the current fee 
schedule, prior to the release of plans for recording. The developer shall show 
the required street trees on the Landscape Plans to demonstrate coordination 
with the project site.  
(ii) Quantity of Street Trees Required  
a. One s hade tree shall be provided per 45 feet of street frontage which 
demonstrates the open space to accommodate the tree.  
b. An evenly spaced, straight row of trees is preferred to match the existing 
character of Wyomissing Borough. Staggered alignment of trees is 
permitted with approval by the Shade Tree Commission. 
 
4. Required Screen Buffers and Required Site Element Screens 
A. When Screen Buffers are Required 
(i) Along the side and rear yards between residential zoning districts (including 
existing residential uses) and any non-residential zoning district. 
(ii) Along the side and rear yards between residential zoning districts (including 
existing residential uses) and multifamily dwellings, including apartment 
buildings, condominiums, and townhouses. 
(iii) Along the rear yard of any residential reverse frontage lots as required per the 
Subdivision and Land Development Ordinance. 
(iv) Along the front yards of industrial zoning districts and uses. 
B.  When Site Element Screens are Required 
(i) When any of the following items are located within 100 feet of a property line 
or road right-of-way 
a. Dumpsters, trash disposal or recycling areas 
b. Service or loading docks 
c. Truck and trailer parking areas 
d. Outdoor storage 
e. Outdoor mechanical yards 
f. Sewage treatment plants and pump stations 
g. Vacuums at car washes 

4 
 
h. Solar fields 
i. Ground based HVAC units 
j. Ground based water storage elements 
C. The Level of Screening Required by the Screen Buffers and Site Element Screens 
(i) Screen buffers shall consist of a dense mass of trees, shrubs or other plant 
material or fences or walls, or a combination of fences, walls, vegetation and 
landform, extending the full length of the property to serve as a barrier to 
visibility, glare and noise. 
(ii) Screen buffer and site element screens consisting of plantings shall be installed 
at a minimum 6’ height and shall produce within three years a complete, 
continuous visual screen of at least six feet in height. 
(iii)A screen buffer may be substituted with a 6’ height solid screen fence or wall.  
When space allows, additional trees and shrubs shall be included to soften the 
fence or wall, and to buffer views above six feet in height. 
(iv) A solid 6’ height screen fence or wall may be utilized to screen site elements. 
D. Where the Screen Buffer Plantings and Site Element Screens are to be Located 
(i) Screen buffer plantings shall be fully contained between the property lines and 
the yard setback lines. 
(ii) Site element screens may be located outside of the yard setback lines and close 
to the element to be screened. 
 
5. Required Softening Buffers 
A. Purpose of a Softening Buffer 
(i) The p urpose of a softening buffer is to provide additional partial visual 
mitigation and accent while allowing the Landscape Architect flexibility in its 
design. 
(ii) When Softening Buffers are Required 
a. Along all yards of any industrial district or office/research park 
district that are not along residential districts or residential uses. 
b. Along all yards of residential communities consisting of more than 
three dwelling units. 
c. Along the perimeter of stormwater management open basins. 
B. Quantity of Softening Buffer Plantings Required 
(i) 1 Shade tree (minimum 2 1/2” caliper) per 50 linear feet; or 
(ii) 10 Shrubs (minimum 24” height) per 50 linear feet; or 
(iii) 2 Evergreen trees (minimum 6’-8’ height) per 50 linear feet 
C. Where the Softening Buffer Plantings are to be Located 
(i) Softening buffer plantings shall be fully contained between the right-of-way or 
property lines and a point fifteen feet beyond the yard setback lines. 
(ii) The design of a softening buffer is the responsibility of the project landscape 
architect.  A more, naturalistic, clustered grouping of plant material is desired, 
particularly along the perimeter of a stormwater management open basin. 
 
6. Required Parking Lot Landscaping 
A. When Parking Lot Landscaping is Required 
(i) For any parking facility with 5 or more parking spaces. 

5 
 
B. Quantity of Parking Lot Plantings Required 
(i) 1 Deciduous tree for every 10 parking spaces.  Deciduous trees proposed within 
interior parking islands shall be shade trees.  Deciduous trees proposed along 
the perimeter of parking lots may be either shade or ornamental trees. 
(ii) A grouping of shrubs shall be provided along the perimeter of parking facilities 
to prevent headlights from glaring onto adjacent streets and any incompatible 
abutting land uses.  The shrub grouping shall consist of predominantly 
evergreen shrubs or heavil y branched deciduous shrubs and shall be designed 
to provide an immediate impact. 
(iii) Shrubs, ornamental grasses, perennials, and groundcover plantings are 
encouraged within interior parking islands, but not required.  Any such 
plantings shall be located so that their mature growth will not impact sight lines 
of parking circulation.  
C. Where the Parking Lot Plantings are to be Located 
(i) Trees shall be planted within landscape parking islands or within 10’ of the 
parking lot perimeter. 
(ii) The center of trees shall be installed minimally 3’ from the edge of curb or 
paving. 
 
7. General Landscape Requirements 
A. Minimum Size of Required Plantings 
(i) Shade trees shall be minimally 2 ½” caliper (measured at 6” above grade) and 
limbed up to 6’ height. 
(ii) Ornamental trees shall be minimally 2 ½” caliper (measured at 6” above grade) 
for single trunk trees and minimally 8’-10’ height for multi-stem trees. 
(iii) Evergreen trees shall be minimally 6’ height. 
(iv)  Shrubs utilized for screening and buffering shall be minimally 2’ height.   
B. Recommended Plant Species 
(i) The species of proposed street trees will be subject to review by the Borough. 
(ii) A minimum of 66% of each plant type (shade tree, ornamental tree, shrub, 
groundcover) shall be of a species native to the northeastern region of the 
United States of America. 
(iii) Any plant species (including cultivars) deemed to be invasive by the 
Commonwealth of Pennsylvania Department of Conservation and Natural 
Resources (PA DCNR) shall be prohibited. 
(iv)  Non-native species are permitted under Item (B)(ii) above, as long as they are 
not invasive. 
(v) Monocultural planting designs shall be avoided.  Biodiversity shall be 
encouraged for range of seasonal interest and protection from disease and pest 
outbreaks. 
C. Required Tree Offsets from Utilities 
(i) The center of any proposed tree shall be offset minimally 10’ from any 
underground or overhead utility. 
(ii) All plantings, other than lawn, shall adhere to any restrictions as may be set 
forth by the utility authority. 
 

6 
 
D. Any plantings proposed within clear sight triangles  shall adhere to the height 
restrictions for clear sight triangles established in this Zoning Ordinance. 
E. All plants shall be in accordance with the latest edition of the American Standard 
for Nursery Stock. 
F. Any landscape area within fifteen feet of a building footprint that is not planted in 
lawn shall contain a natural, fire -retardant, weed deterrent surface.  Fire retardant 
products (natural and manmade) with a maintenance strategy for long term 
fertilization and weed control shall be required. 
G. Guarantee 
(i) All required plantings shall be maintained or replaced permanently. 
(ii) Any plant material which is in decline or has died shall be replaced within six 
months of observance.
