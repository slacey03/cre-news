ORDINANCE NO. 1462-2026 
AN ORDINANCE OF THE BOROUGH OF WYOMISSING, BERKS COUNTY , 
PENNSYLV ANIA, AMENDING CHAPTER 21 ENTITLED “STREETS AND SIDEWALKS” 
TO ADD A PART 8 ENTITLED “SNOW AND ICE REMOV AL REGULATIONS”. 
 WHEREAS, the Borough has determined that it is in the best interests of its residents to 
establish snow and ice removal procedures for enforcement after snow events. 
 NOW THEREFORE BE IT ENACTED and ORDAINED by the Council of the Borough 
of Wyomissing, Berks County, Pennsylvania by the authority of the same as follows: 
SECTION 1. Addition of Snow and Ice Removal Regulations.  
Chapter 21 entitled “Streets and Sidewalks” is hereby amended and supplemented to add a 
Part 8 as follows: 
PART 8 
SNOW AND ICE REMOV AL REGULATIONS 
Section 801. Owner or O ccupant of Property to Remove S now and Ice from 
Sidewalk. 
1. I t shall be the duty of every person, entity, association, partnership 
or corporation owning or occupying any property fronting on any public street or 
alley within the Borough of Wyomissing to remove or cause to be removed from 
the sidewalks of said premises all ice, snow or sleet within a period of 36 hours 
after the same shall have ceased to fall thereon. Due to the increased pedestrian 
traffic on Penn Avenue  in the Borough, all ice, snow or sleet shall be removed 
within a period of 24 hours after the same shall have ceased to fall thereon. Said 
sidewalks shall be kept clear of such ice, snow and sleet thereafter, unless said snow 
and/or ice be of such a hardened or frozen condition that it cannot be removed 
without injury to the sidewalk, in which case the owner, occupant or tenant of each 
such property is required to place salt, sand or other abrasive or chemical material 
upon said snow and/or ice within the time provided above to render such sidewalks 
safe for travel.  In addition, school bus stop corners must have all snow and ice 
removed from them within 24 hours after the same shall have ceased to fall thereon. 
 2. For the purpose of complying with the provisions of Paragraph 1  
above, it shall be the duty of every such person, entity, association, partnership or 
corporation owning or occupying any property to conform to the requirements of 
this section, whether or not such owner is present on the property, and whether or 
not such owner has delegated such responsibility to a third party or tenant. In the 
instance of a building which is occupied by multiple residential or commercial 
tenants, any such snow and/or ice removal shall be the responsibility of the owner 
DRAFT

2 
 
of said premises, whether or not such responsibilities have been delegated to a third 
party. 
  (A) The owner of any property fronting on a public street or alley 
within the Borough of Wyomissing shall clear a path of at least 36 inches in width 
on the sidewalks fronting such premises. Sidewalks measuring less than 36 inches 
must be cleared entirely. 
(B) In order to accommodate increased pedestrian traffic on 
Penn Avenue, a path of at least 48 inches in width shall be cleared. Where certain 
Penn Avenue properties have a sidewalk width of less than 48 inches, a path of 36 
inches in width shall be cleared. 
(C) The owner of any property fronting a public street or alley 
with a bus shelter, handicap ramp, or fire hydrant shall clear sidewalks surrounding 
those areas. 
 3. Exceptions. 
(A) State of emergency/government closure. In the event that 
snow or ice, or combination thereof, causes a declared state of emergency or the 
closure of the Borough of Wyomissing office, the occupant, owner or other person 
in charge of a property shall have an additional 48 hours to fulfill the requirements 
of Paragraphs 1 and 2 above. 
(B) Extreme winter conditions. In the event that snow or ice 
cannot be removed due to packed ice or other conditions and a reasonable effort 
has been made during the specified time frame to render the sidewalks safe for 
travel, a fine will not be assessed. Once the snow has melted or is able to be 
removed, the provisions of Paragraphs 1 and 2 above shall apply. 
 4. Penalties. Authorized officials of the Borough of Wyomissing 
engaged in the enforcement of the regulations established in Paragraphs  1 and 2 
shall have the authority to notify (by first-class mail, hand delivery or posted on the 
property) any such person, association, partnership or corporation violating 
Paragraphs 1 or 2 that he or she shall pay at the office of the Borough Hall within 
10 days from the time of the notice, the sum of $25 for the first offense, $50 for the 
second offense, and $100 for the third or subsequent offense. Each day that a 
violation of this  section continues shall constitute a separate offense. If such 
payment shall not be made at Borough Hall within the ten-day period, that violator 
shall be subject to prosecution and to the penalty prescribed in Section 803 of this 
Chapter. Notification by hand delivery shall include, among other things, affixing 
a notice to the front door of the office or residence of any such person, entity, 
association, partnership or corporation violating Paragraphs 1 or 2 above. 
Section 802. Snow and Ice Not to be Deposited in Streets.

2. In residential districts, snow/ice should be deposited onto the lawn 
or yard of the property.  
3. In commercial districts, snow/ice should be deposited on half the

4. Snow/ice cleared from around parked vehicles should be deposited

PERSONNEL COMMITTEE— MEETING MINUTES

The Borough of Wyomissing Personnel Committee held their regular meeting in the Borough of 
Wyomissing Council Chambers on the above date and time. Committee Chair Kristen Gajewski 
called the meeting to order with the following persons present: Committee members  
Jana Barnett, Stephen Brunner, Christopher Valente, and Jennifer Reimer; Borough Manager & 
Economic Development Coordinator Michele Bare, Borough Solicitor Heidi Masano, and 
Borough Secretary Melissa Miller.

Roll Call 
 
 
Public Comments: 
No public comment was provided.  
 
Public Works Job Classification Adjustments: 
Greg Capriolo, Assistant Public Works Director explained to the committee, the process for 
determining wage increases as permitted by the current labor contract, noting the contract 
incentivizes Public Works employees to continue learning and getting licensed. This also 
includes evaluations by the Leads, and collective discussions among all Leads, to approve. He 
pointed out that two generic increases were added to the 2026 budget, however there is 
unused funding in the parkland equipment category to cover the wage adjustment proposed 
for the third employee. He clarified that while Cory Fererri was scheduled for an increase in 
2023, his starting rate was higher since he was grandfathered by the old contract of which his 
skill set attributed to a higher rate. He was then not eligible for a wage increase in the old 
contract. The committee briefly discussed ensuring that the Public Works team is planning 
ahead for future budgeting, not necessarily coming mid-year with unbudgeted requests.  
Stephen Brunner made a motion, seconded by Jennifer Reimer to recommend Council 
authorize the job classification requests for Public Works employees Cory Fererri, Jake 
Rebholz, and Christopher Conlon, retroactive March 3, 2026, all in favor.  
 
DRAFT

Resignation Notice of Blaze Yasso: 
Borough Manager & Economic Development Coordinator Michele Bare shared that a 
resignation letter was submitted by Firefighter/EMT Blaza Yasso, however after careful 
consideration and the onset of a new Fire Chief, Blaze has requested to rescind his resignation. 
He feels it is pertinent to give the new Chief the opportunity to see if the Chief can make the 
department what it needs to be.

Hiring Processes: Fire Chief, Borough Manager, and Business Manager: 
Michele shared that a conditional offer letter has been sent to a Fire Chief candidate. A special 
Council meeting will need to be held to appoint the Chief.  
The Director of Finance hiring has been placed on hold at this time, with Transcend Finance

RECREATION BOARD – MEETING MINUTES  
APRIL 21, 2026 – 7:00 P.M. 
 
The Borough of Wyomissing Recreation Board held their regular meeting in the Borough of 
Wyomissing Council Chambers on the above date and time. Board President Richard Weinhoffer 
called the meeting to order with the following persons present: Board members Sally Donato, Pamela 
Wetzel, Karim Davidson, Jessica Christman, and Christopher Valente; Recreation Director Randy 
McKinley, Borough Manager & Economic Development Coordinator Michele Bare, and Borough 
Secretary Melissa Miller. The following persons were absent: Board members Jess Leinbach 
Michael Sharer, and Liz Toigo. 
 
Councilmember Visitors: 
None 
 
Visitors: 
Christian Avalos – Wyomissing Area Soccer Club 
 
Board President Richard Weinhoffer indicated that the meeting was being recorded.  
 
Public Comment: 
No public comment was provided.  
 
Approval of Meeting Minutes: 
Pamela Wetzel made a motion, seconded by Karim Davidson to approve the March 2026 meeting 
minutes, all in favor.  
 
Approval of Financial Report: 
Recreation Director Randy McKinley indicated that no financial report was received from the Finance 
Director, however not a lot of money was expended for February. He pointed out two large 
expenditures for American flags; $8,200 for July 4th, and $3,600 for Memorial Day. He noted that the 
budget for egg hunt was $2,500 but due to the diligent work by Jess Leinbach and the subcommittee, 
the event costs were $1,044. He also reminded the Board that a majority of the Materials and Supplies 
category that is shown on both the Rec and Public Works budget, is allocated to the Public Works 
Buildings and Grounds maintenance.  
 
Jessica Christman made a motion, seconded by Sally Donato to table the February financial report, all 
in favor.  
 
Subcommittee Reports: 
Christopher Valente shared that the Playground program is a little behind last year with registrations; 
247 currently, and 260 in 2025, however this year’s income is approximately $38,000 compared to 
2025’s income of $32,500. He explained that due to some changes necessary for the Playground 
registration literature, the program registration was 30 days behind schedule for opening. Randy 
pointed out there is a glitch in the vendor system with enrolling 12-year-olds that he is working 
through with the vendor, but manually entering at this time. Chris mentioned that for next year’s 
program, the subcommittee is considering expanding the program to 13 year olds, which would help 
DRAFT

with revenue, and better prepare the adolescents to become Playground leaders when turning 14.  In 
regards to hiring, Erin and Nikki received multiple applications, with some needing to be turned away. 
During the hiring, they better defined the hiring process to ensure it was more objective. All positions 
have been filled.  
 
Chris shared that the subcommittee also discussed potentially opening Wyomissing’s Playground 
program to West Reading since both municipalities fall under the same school district. The West 
Reading program has dwindling enrollment, and more costly registration. If absorption of their 
program was approved, their counselors could apply for Wyomissing’s program. There were some 
concerns from the Board, including Wyomissing tax dollars funding West Reading students, other 
municipalities could request to join the program as well, and that West Reading residents were never 
permitted to use the pool. It was addressed that a different fee structure could be implemented for 
West Reading residents for Playground to alleviate the tax dollar concern, and only West Reading 
would be able to participate as they fall under the same district. West Reading also had a non-resident 
fee for non-resident participants, of which Wyomissing does not. The Playground subcommittee will 
further review and discuss with the Board at a later date.  
 
Jessica Christman reported on Earth Day pointing out several of the previously discussed events and 
activities, noting that more help is needed to push out the macroinvertebrate creek study event. She 
also requested more assistance with setting up and cleaning up the day of the event, as well as needing 
help with hiding the painted rocks. There was a concern with the weather forecast, of which the Board 
was reminded the rain date is the next day, at the same time.  
 
In Jess Leinbach’s absence, Randy provided an event recap on the egg hunt, noting that the bunny was 
good, various in-kind donations were received, totaling around $4,600 to support the event, as well as 
a donated backdrop for photos. He thanked Jess L. and Mike for their efforts. There were 
approximately 3,000 people in attendance, and new this year was a golden egg prize where children 
could win a large gift basket prize, as well as the event featuring both candy and non-candy items. 
Randy thanked Prospectus Berco for their assistance filling the plastic eggs again this year. He further 
pointed out that the prize claim area flowed better than prior years.  
 
Some event improvements include continue monitoring the event to ensure children are in the right 
age groups and entering the field at the appropriate times. There was enough help for the event, but 
eggs for two age groups were accidentally placed together. Parking will continue to be an issue for 
events held at this location since it is street parking only. Way-finding signage for the age groups 
needs improvement, as does the sound for announcing rules and age groups. The Wyomissing Hills 
Citizens Social group provided 100 silver dollar coins as they always do, however kids were confused 
as to what to do with them. Maybe the group could consider a different prize contribution next year. It 
was lastly pointed out that the ending was a little challenging with people leaving and cleanup 
occurring.  
 
Rick reminded the Board that this year the parades were solicited together, with both parades being 
managed as their own separate events. The subcommittee has selected the gold star family, the parade 
stager will be participating again, and he pointed out the desire for more community groups, not just 
vendors. He noted that he will continue to work various Recreation events to continue gaining interest 
DRAFT

for the parade, by having a table and interactive opportunity to engage with event goers. In regards to 
parade staging, it was noted there would be a small adjustment, making the intersection of Woodland 
and Lawndale Roads a drop-off zone. Handicapped parking will be a small designated lot to ensure 
accessibility to the event.  
 
For July 4th, there was discussion to do bi-monthly planning meetings. Rick noted the desire to make 
this more of a community event as well, with the idea to push a “Remember When” campaign on 
social media for community engagement and participation. He feels the Board should make a float for 
the parade, of which the task of handing out flags could be passed to Councilmembers. Randy shared 
that the Reading Pops Orchestra received a grant associated with America 250 for community 
outreach, and have asked if they could play during the July 3rd fireworks display. He indicated the best

their official parade. They will participate in 2026 and 2027, and will have a float that will feature

Parks & Recreation Director’s Report: 
Randy shared that rental revenue so far in 2026 totals $3,000, the year-to-date fundraising total is 
$8,750, and $400 has been collected in the vendor fee category. He noted that refuse and recycling 
containers that were budgeted, will be installed. Randy thanked the Earth Day monetary and in-kind 
donors for their support towards the event. Similarly, MasterCraft Cabinets (Eli Peters) donated 
$2,500 to offset the increased price of the fish for the fishing rodeo. The price increase was due to the 
harsh weather conditions.  
 
Randy spoke about upcoming events including Star Gazing, Memorial Day parade, and Touch-A-
Truck. Playground statistics were discussed earlier in the meeting, but he shared that the pool is 
currently at 75 registrations compared to 66 last year, with $23,615 income versus 2025 of $20,150. 
Swimnastics is at 10 registrants for $1,100 compared to one registration last year at this time. 
Swimming lessons are at 31 registrants for $4,300, compared to 15 registrants last year for $1,900. For 
tennis lessons, Randy regretfully announced the passing of instructor Craig Conrath, and indicated that 
he will need to see if anyone will step up to volunteer as there are currently 11 registrations for $830. 
Lastly, he had no facility updates, other than reiterating the various groups utilizing the Quarry field 
over the next few months.  
 
Old Business: 
There was no old business to discuss.  
 
New Business: 
Chrisian Avalos, Wyomissing Area Soccer Club President, spoke about the inception of the soccer 
program in 1978, being run by parents and volunteer coaches. The program supports children ages 3 to 
12, with around 300 kids in the program. The soccer Board changes approximately every four to five 
years, with Board meetings being held remotely the last Monday of each month. The program is the 
Fall and Spring seasons, with participation in the Reading-Berks Junior Soccer League for traveling 
soccer. The program spans outside of Wyomissing to include other schools in the area, as long as all 
Wyomissing kids are first taken into consideration for open spots. The club used to have a large 
classic, but due to needing so many people and parking be a concern, Christian indicated they are

registrations and the snack bar, with extra revenue for Summer camps. They intend on offering a “Day

The club currently participates in the Borough’s July 4th and Memorial Day parades, as well as Spooky 
Hollow, and is considering hosting their own viewing party at the Quarry Field for the upcoming 
World Cup games. Randy indicated the group could borrow the Borough’s inflatable screen. Lastly it

FIRE & POLICE COMMITTEE – MEETING MINUTES 
APRIL 27, 2026 – 12:00 PM 
 
The Borough of Wyomissing Fire and Police Committee met in the Borough of Wyomissing 
Council Chambers on the above date and time. Committee Chair Jana Barnett called the meeting 
to order with the following persons present: Committee members Mayor Frederick Levering, 
Stephen Brunner, and Kristen Gajewski; Police Lieutenant Paul Baur, Borough Manager & 
Economic Development Coordinator Michele Bare, Borough Solicitor representative Michael 
Gombar, and Borough Secretary Melissa Miller. The following person was absent: Committee 
member Adam Berkeley.  
 
Councilmember Visitors: 
Jennifer Reimer 
Richard Weinhoffer 
 
Visitors 
Azahel Henriquez – 105 Woodland Road 
Thomas Bausher – Westside Regional EMA Coordinator 
Tony Tucci – Western Berks Ambulance Association 
Jim Babb – Public Works Director 
David Reeser – 1110 Fairview Avenue 
 
Public Comment: 
No public comment was provided. 
Committee Chair Jana Barnett announced that the meeting was being recorded.  
Western Berks EMS: 
Tony Tucci, Western Berks Ambulance (WBAA) provided a brief overview of the recent Berks 
County Medical Society event WBAA participated in, teaching hands only CPR and other 
important EMS skills.  
 
Westside Regional EMA: 
Thomas Bausher, Westside Regional EMA Coordinator, shared that the Westside Regional EMA 
(WSREMA) Emergency Operations Plan has been updated with all municipalities served by 
WSREMA, completed the necessary Resolutions. He also shared that they are progressing with 
CodeRed, and he is going to call to follow up to see how many people have re-enrolled. There 
was discussion about having CodeRed enrollment reposted on the Borough’s website and social 
media. Tony indicated he could also share on the WBAA social media page.  
 
Police Department: 
Police Lieutenant Paul Baur shared with the committee that they have been contacted by Berks 
County Department of Emergency Services (DES) regarding the radio fleet refresh program 
which is the replacement of existing radios in the fleet. The County has again agreed to the 
financing program for municipalities for radios being replaced to maintain the fleet, not enhance 
DRAFT

the fleet. It was discussed that the Borough maintains a tri-band frequency from prior use for in-
house dispatching, that is no longer needed, and is more costly when considering radios as well 
as paying yearly for the frequency. It was noted that Borough crews still use the KGI frequency, 
of which the Borough should consider maintaining when the time comes to renew. He pointed 
out that over the last few years, seven handheld radios failed, but had extended warranties of 
which the department now has seven newer radios. The department is checking on extending the 
warranties for the newer seven radios, and will opt to extend the warranty instead of purchasing 
new radios if that is the case. Lieutenant Baur shared that three pricing options are available 
including a $253,000 option which includes the tri-band. The department and committee were in 
agreeance that option three could be removed. The other two options include the replacement of 
18 handheld units at the discounted price with the warranty, and the addition of in-car cradle 
units for the Detective’s vehicles, for a cost of $139,000. The second option includes the items 
from option one, but also the seven additional radios if the extended warranty previously 
discussed is not available. The cost would be $160,000. 
 
Borough Manager & Economic Development Coordinator Michele Bare shared that the Fire 
Department had also priced out radios, and noted that a more rugged radio is available to handle 
higher temperatures, but the anticipation is that costs will be $220,000 to $230,000 for only the 
Fire Department.  
 
Both Tony and Tom indicated that they are looking to replace handheld units only, not mobile 
radios.  
 
Lieutenant Baur informed the committee that a concern was raised regarding parking at the 
intersection of Upland Road and Upland Road, with vehicles blocking the intersection. It was 
noted that parking on this street is restricted to one side only, so the remedy to the concern would 
be to potentially eliminate parking for residents. It was mentioned however, that any parking 
modifications would require a traffic study. Lieutenant Baur explained that 26 years worth of 
data was reviewed for vehicular and pedestrian accidents, of which neither have occurred. The 
committee was not in favor of moving forward with any remedial actions at this time due to no 
supporting crash statistics. 
 
Stephen Brunner made a motion, seconded by Frederick Levering to deny the request 
for limiting parking in the intersection of Upland Road and Upland Road, all in favor.  
 
Lieutenant Baur also spoke of the Approved Storage Garages; Bonding; Towing, and Storage 
Ordinance Amendment of which the committee discussed at the March 2026 Fire and Police 
committee meeting. The Ordinance has been prepared and approved by Police Chief Phillips. 
There was brief discussion about why Council has to pass such an Ordinance, and Solicitor 
Gombar shared this is customary in many municipalities, and best practice to have in place if 
discrepancies arise. The Ordinance affords the Chief to make a best judgement on who should be 
appointed as primary and secondary towing. As an example, it was noted that it does not make 
sense to have a tow company out of county if they are not close enough to clear up an accident 
scene quickly.  
DRAFT

Frederick Levering made a motion, seconded by Stephen Brunner to approve 
recommending Council authorize advertisement of Ordinance No. 1463-2026 as to 
designation of approved storage garages; bonding; towing and storage, all in favor.  
 
Regarding prior discussions of the Snow Emergency Route Ordinance, Public Works Director 
Jim Babb spoke to the selection of roads in the Ordinance. He feels it is not necessary to 
designate a snow emergency route as the largest issues are only a day or two after a storm. If the 
committee were to move forward with such Ordinance, he suggests only keeping South Park 
Road between Penn and Wayne Avenues due to emergency vehicle traffic to the hospital. He 
explained that prior to the merger in the early 2000’s, Wyomissing Hills had a designated snow 
emergency route, of which the signs have been taken down since this is not a plan that is 
enforced. He explained that by implementing a snow emergency, the Public Works team can 
mandate overtime for snow removal. The committee then discussed necessary steps for removing 
roads from the Ordinance, of which Solicitor Gombar indicated that a separate Ordinance is 
needed to do such. A question arose as to why the Borough would not want to have a Snow 
Emergency Route Ordinance in the Borough Code, and it was explained that it is not necessary if 
it is not going to be enforced. It was also noted that signage would need to be installed. The 
committee was in favor of tabling discussions of the Snow Emergency Route Ordinance and 
work towards creating an Ordinance amendment to remove streets.  
Frederick Levering made a motion, seconded by Stephen Brunner to table discussions 
relative to the Snow Emergency route, all in favor.  
Fire Department: 
 
Monthly report review:  
Michele shared that the month-to-date report was not available at this time, but will be included 
in the Council packet. She noted that to-date, the average response time was 1 minute 08 
seconds, with 87 responses under 90 seconds. She indicated there were 133 responses so far this 
month, with mutual aid given 63 times, and received 28 times. She noted the department logged 
80 hours of training, with another 40 hours to be added due to a course completed by one of the 
personnel.   
It was noted that there was no further update on the Fire Chief hiring process, as the new Chief 
will be appointed during the special Council meeting.  
 
Michele briefly explained to the committee that the Fire Chief has requested a command cabinet 
for the rear of the Chief’s vehicle. This would allow for organization during a command 
operation. She explained that this is an unbudgeted expenditure and would need to go to the 
Finance and Administration committee prior to Council. The cost for the cabinet is $3,246.  
 
Stephen Brunner made a motion, seconded by Jana Barnett to recommend the Finance 
Committee review the request, and further recommend Council approve the purchase 
of a command drawer with accessories for the Fire Chief’s vehicle, in the amount of 
$3,246, all in favor.

FOR DES IGNATING APPROVED STORAGE GARAGES  AND THE CRITERIA FOR 
SELECTION. 
 WHEREAS, the Borough has determined that it is in the best interests of its residents to 
designate and establish one primary and one secondary approved storage garage for the towing 
and storage of vehicles illegally parked, damaged, or otherwise rendered inoperable on roadways 
or parking lots in the Borough, and to outline the criteria for selection of approved garages.  
 NOW THEREFORE BE IT ENACTED and ORDAINED by the Council of the Borough 
of Wyomissing, Berks County, Pennsylvania by the authority of the same as follows: 
SECTION 1. Designation of Approved Storage Garages, Bonding, Towing, and Storage. The Code 
of the Borough of Wyomissing, Chapter 15 entitled “Motor Vehicles and Traffic”, Part 5 entitled 
“Removal and Impoundment of Illegally Parked Vehicles ”, Section 504 entitled “Designation of 
Approved Storage Garages; Bonding; Towing and Storage” is hereby amended and restated in its 
entirety as follows:  
Removal and impounding of vehicles under this Part shall be done only by “approved storage 
garages” that shall be designated from time to time by the Borough Council  as a professional 
service. The Borough Council shall designate one (1) primary and one (1) backup storage garage 
based upon the recommendation of the Chief of Police. The backup storage garage shall be utilized 
if the primary storage garage is unavailable to perform the work needed at the time it is required. 
If the Chief of Police determines that a change or update to the current approved storage garage s 
is necessary, in his discretion, he may solicit interest  from storage garages which meet the 
following criteria:  
1. The storage facility shall be located no more than ten (10) miles from the Borough and 
in Berks County;  
2. Have the ability to tow commercial motor vehicles;  
3. Have secured storage facilities;  
4. Hold current state salvor authorization; and  
5. Be bonded and  have liability insurance in an amount satisfactory to the Borough 
Council as sufficient to indemnify owners of impounded vehicles against loss or 
damage to those vehicles while in the custody of the garage keeper for the purpose of 
towing or storage.

ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT COMMITTEE –MEETING MINUTES 
APRIL 30, 2026 – 8:00 A.M. 
 
The Borough of Wyomissing Economic Development Committee held their regular meeting in 
the Borough of Wyomissing Council Chambers on the above date and time. Committee Chair 
Frederick Levering called the meeting to order with the following persons present: Committee 
members George Zeppos, Rick Weinhoffer, and Mark Dougherty; Borough Manager & 
Economic Development Coordinator Michele Bare, and Borough Secretary Melissa Miller. The 
following person was absent: Committee member Adam Berkeley.

Jana Barnett

Mike Chervanik – 575 Spring Street 
 
 
Public Comment: 
No public comment was provided.  
 
Spotlight PA Article – Gov. Shapiro’s Housing Initiative: 
Fred Levering shared with the committee that to-date housing stock in Wyomissing is at 21 
homes, and very few rental properties. He identified that the proposed initiative in the Housing 
Initiative article, focuses on opportunities for housing in Pennsylvania. Looking at the Borough, 
the committee pointed out that the area is already built out, with some lots available, and some 
residents having taken on teardowns and rebuilds. The article explains the concept of alternate 
dwelling unit (ADU), of which there was expressed concern that the Borough would need to be 
mindful of the mission to support residents, and to assess both positive and negative impacts of 
implanting such concept.  
Borough Manager & Economic Development Coordinator Michele Bare shared that low and 
medium residential zoning districts allow residential by right, TC and C1 zoning districts allow 
single family residential by right, and R3 and R3A are apartment complexes and high density 
residential, with R4 being retirement communities. She cautioned that the Borough may want to 
stay away from allowing ADUs in low density residential, however noted TC and C1 could 
allow for higher density, with consideration in R2 which encompasses semi-detached and two-
family units, and allowing ADUs by right or special exception.  
The committee agreed that a mutual conversation between the Economic Development 
committee and Planning Commission is needed to look at the logistics of the impact of ADUs. 
There was also a larger scale conversation as to reviewing and redoing Zoning in the Borough. 
Michele indicated the Borough at one time had a Zoning Task Force to review Zoning 
regulations, and pointed out that Zoning Hearing Board hearings were being held more 
frequently, but has since dwindled down.  
DRAFT

There was discussion on several municipal call to action items including modernizing zoning and 
streamlining the process. It was expressed that the Borough should have ways to make doing 
business easier, potentially using a digitized and streamlined permitting process. Michele 
indicated that under the Uniform Construction Code, no electronic submissions are permitted. It 
was briefly noted that Wyomissing has been easier to do business with than other municipalities. 
Relative to zoning, a scenario was provided of the current zoning regulations that allow 
accessory use structures to total no more than 40% of the size of the principal structure. 
Accessory structures include detached garages, sheds and pools. In the particular scenario, the 
resident had a larger property, but was unable to install the desired sized shed due to the size of 
the principal structure. There was also brief mention of property lines and setback requirements, 
and Michele explained that setbacks are also a built-in protection for other surrounding homes 
and structures, as part of the fire code, as well as mindfulness of structures that other neighbors 
may have to look at.  
The question arose as to whether or not the current zoning meets the needs of Wyomissing 
residents in 2026, and if there could be more investments and/or improvements if there were less 
barriers. It was mentioned that zoning should continue to be handled on a case by case basis, but 
there were concerns that it entails a $1,000 application for residential properties, to partake in a 
Zoning Hearing Board hearing. It was further pointed out that current zoning is not necessarily 
one size fits all since there are various zoning districts, and to also take into consideration 
revising portions of zoning that are often proved by developers to be outdated, including parking 
assumptions. The suggestion was to look at the zoning districts first, if the committee wants to 
make changes, and also taking into consideration that many of the Wyomissing properties are 
non-conforming areas.  
Questions arose as to converting a garage to a studio apartment space, and renting it out. It was 
explained that depending what district it were in, it would be allowed. Building permits would be 
required as well as rental inspection program compliance. Boarding rooms are also permitted in 
certain zoning districts, as are short-term rentals with appropriate permitting. Michele noted that 
the Borough has a Disruptive Conduct Ordinance included with lease addendums for rental 
properties, identifying that after three disruptive conduct calls to a property in a calendar year, 
the landlord is required to evict the tenant. As far as rental counts, Michele estimated there are 
300 rental properties in the Borough with 1,200 total units available, with a total of 3,500 
residential properties overall. It was lastly mentioned that despite the Borough’s willingness to 
work with a developer, the Borough still holds them accountable for plan and building aspects.

Note 1—Summary of significant accounting policies  
 
The Borough of Wyomissing (the “Borough”), Berks County, Pennsylvania, is a borough created in 1906.  The 
Borough is governed by seven (7) elected council memb ers in accordance with laws of the Commonwealth of 
Pennsylvania.  The council members must be residents and registered voters  who are elected every two years, 
on a staggered basis, for a four-year term.  The council members have the power to establish policies to be 
carried out by the Borough.  The Borough council manages  the Borough with the assistance of the Borough 
manager who is appointed. 
 
As described further in Basis of Accounting , these financial statements are presented on a modified cash basis 
of accounting, which is a basis of accounting other than accounting principles generally accepted in the United 
States of America (“U.S. GAAP”) established by the Governmental Ac counting Standards B oard (“GASB”).  
These modified cash basis financial statements generally meet the presentation and disclosure requirements 
applicable to U.S. GAAP, in substance, but are limited to the elements presented in the financial statements and 
the constraints of the measurement and recognition criteria of the modified cash basis of accounting. 
 
Financial Reporting Entity – As required by generally accepted accounting principles, the financial statements of 
the reporting entity include those of the Borough (the primary government) and its component units. 
 
The Borough used guidance contained in U.S. GAAP to evaluate the possible inclusion of related entities 
(authorities, boards, councils, fiduciary activities, etc.) wi thin its reporting entity.  Accounting principles generally 
accepted in the United States of Am erica require that the reporting entity consists of the primary government 
and legally separate entities for whic h the primary government is financially accountable.  In addition, the 
primary government may determine through the exerci se of management’s professional judgment that the 
inclusion of a legally separate entity that does not meet the financial accountability criteria is necessary in order 
to prevent the reporting entity’s financial statements fr om being misleading.  In such instances, that legally 
separate entity should be included as a component unit if the nature and significance of their relationship with 
the primary government or other component units are such  that the exclusion from the financial reporting entity 
would render the financial reporting entity’s financial statements incomplete or misleading.  In evaluating how to 
define the reporting entity, management has considered all potential component units.   
 
Based on the foregoing criteria, the Borough has deter mined it has three fiduciary component units.  The 
Borough’s pension plans are considered fiduciary component units and are reported as pension trust funds in 
the fiduciary fund financial statements. 
 
Jointly Governed Organizations – Governments often enter into special arrangements with each other to provide 
or obtain needed services.  A common type of such an arrangement is a jointly governed organization whereby 
governments enter into an arrangement to plan for and addre ss certain activities for their mutual benefit.  The 
following are such relationships: 
 
Western Berks Water Authority –  The Borough, in conjunction with the boroughs of Shillington and West 
Reading, has created the Western Berks Water Authorit y.  The Western Berks Wa ter Authority’s board is 
composed of two members from each of the municipalit ies.  The primary purpose of the Authority is to 
provide water utilities to the member municipalitie s. The Borough paid $1,439,149 to the Western Berks 
Water Authority for water for the year ended December 31, 2025. 
 
Joint Municipal Authority of Wyomissing Valley – The Borough, in conjunction with the borough of West 
Reading, has created the Joint Municipal Authority of Wyomissing Valley.  The Joint Municipal Authority of 
Wyomissing Valley’s board is composed of three memb ers from each of the municipalities.  The primary 
purpose of the Authority is to provide sewer utilit ies to the member municipalities. The Borough paid 
$1,464,629 to Joint Municipal Authority of Wyomissi ng Valley for sewer utilities for the year ended 
December 31, 2025.

The statement of activities demonstrates the level to which the direct expenses of a given function of the 
Borough are offset by the program revenues related to that function.  Direct expenses are those that are directly 
related to and clearly identified with a function.  Progr am revenues include: 1) char ges to customers or others

contributions that are restricted to m eet the operational or capital requireme nts of a function.  Taxes and other 
items not includable in program revenues are reported as general revenues.

by the applicable measurement focus and basis of accounting.  Measurement focus is a term used to describe

Measurement Focus  – In the government-wide statement of net position and statement of activities, both 
governmental and business-type activities are present ed using the economic resources measurement focus, 
within the limitations of the modified cash basis of accounting. 
 
In the fund financial statements, the current financial resources measurement focus or the economic resources 
measurement focus, as applied to the modified cash basis of accounting, is used as appropriate: 
 
All governmental funds utilize a current financial resour ces measurement focus within the limitations of the 
modified cash basis of accounting.  Only current fi nancial assets and liabilities are generally included on 
their balance sheets.  Their operating statements present sources and uses of available spendable financial 
resources during a given period.  These funds use f und balance as their measure of available spendable 
financial resources at the end of the period. 
 
The proprietary fund utilizes an economic resources measurement focus within the limitations of the 
modified cash basis of accounting.  The accounti ng objectives of this measurement focus are the 
determination of operating income, changes in net posit ion (or cost recovery), net financial position, and 
cash flows.  All assets, deferred outflows of resources, liabilities, and deferred inflows of resources (whether 
current or noncurrent or financial or nonfinancial) associated with their activities are generally reported 
within the limitations of the modified cash basis of accounting. 
 
The fiduciary funds utilize an economic resources measur ement focus within the limitations of the modified 
cash basis of accounting.  All assets and liabilitie s (whether current or noncurrent or financial or 
nonfinancial) associated with their activities are generally reported within the limitations of the modified cash 
basis of accounting.

accounting involves modifications to the cash basis of accounting to report in the st atement of net position or 
balance sheet cash transactions or events that provide a benefit or result in an obligation that covers a period 
greater than the period in which the cash transaction or event occurred.  Such reported balances include 
investments, interfund receivables and payables, and devel oper escrows arising from cash transactions or 
events. 
 
This modified cash basis of accounting differs from  U.S. GAAP primarily because certain assets and their 
related revenues (such as accounts re ceivable, and revenue for billed or provided services not yet collected, 
and other accrued revenue and receivables) and certain li abilities and their related expenses or expenditures 
(such as accounts payable, and expens es for goods or services receiv ed but not yet paid, and other accrued 
expenses and liabilities) are not recorded in these finan cial statements.  In additi on, other economic assets, 
deferred outflows of resources, liabilities, and deferred inflows of re sources that do not arise from cash 
transactions or events are not reported, and the measur ement of reported assets and liabilities does not involve 
adjustment to fair value, except investments which are reported at fair value or amortized cost, which 
approximates fair value. 
 
If the Borough utilized accounting principles generally accepted in the United States, the fund financial 
statements for governmental funds would use the modifi ed accrual basis of account ing, and the fund financial 
statements for the proprietary and fiduciary fund type s would use the accrual basis of accounting.  All 
government-wide financial statements would be presented in accordance with the accrual basis of accounting.

Committed – Amounts that can be used only for a specific purpose determined by a formal action

action to commit fund balance to a specific purpose must occur prior to the end of  the reporting period, but

balance are made by the Borough council. 
 
Unassigned – Amounts that are not included within any other classification noted.  Unassigned amounts are 
the portion of fund balance not obligated or specifically designated and is available for any purpose.

existing facility that will remain in service during the project.

for the project, was also allowed to bid on the plumbing contract.  Mr. Hughes confirmed that this 
is permitted and will likely reduce administrative costs.

the loan funds do not earn interest.  The Pennvest loan is a drawdown loan.  The Authority will 
not receive all of the funds up front.  The Authority will make interest only payments on the loan 
during construction.  Mr. Leonard E. Bilger said that the Authority will receive roughly 
$2,000,000.00 from Pennvest for an engineering cost reimbursement.  These funds will be placed 
into the Authority’s CRIM account and will earn interest there.
