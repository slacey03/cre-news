C
ommercial and Industrial Overlay District: 
Sam Wiser, Salzmann Hughes, reviewed the proposed Commercial and Industrial Zoning 
Overlay District revisions. Sam noted that while there is an existing Zoning overlay in place, the 
new proposal identifies modifications to section 5A. This aids properties beyond useful life and 
incentivizes redevelopment to enhance usage.  
T
he existing district allows all uses permitted by right in commercial and industrial zones as 
before, but could expand to apartments and townhouses above non-residential use, 30% by right 
or 60% by exception, or age-restricted housing above non-residential uses. Adult uses are 
excluded from the special exception in this zone. This could also include shopping centers, 
discount stores, grocery stores, warehouse clubs, restaurants, convenience stores, and more. 
Accessory warehousing is permitted as long as it does not exceed 25% of the floor area, and 
cannot be a standalone warehouse. This was included to accommodate the online shopping and 
short delivery time retail experience.  
R
elative to area, yard, and height in Section 504A, standards existing and proposed to remain 
include a height of 75 feet, maximum building coverage of 60 ft, maximum pervious paved area

of 75%, and minimum lot size and minimum lot width with the exception for interior lots which 
may have a smaller lot size. Minimum open area is proposed to change from 10% to 25%, and 
perimeter building set back from 66% of the underlying setback to the full underlying zoning 
setback. 
 
Planned unit development was outlined in the presentation, pointing out fee-simple subdivision, 
shared parking across site with the site encompassing the overall parking demand, shared 
stormwater facilities and utilities, and the requirement of easements, maintenance of landscape, 
and more, as a lot owners association. Overall, this encompasses site planning and maintenance 
for a particular redevelopment/development site in this overlay, and conveying to the Borough, 
the site’s master plan. It was reiterated that as part of the lot owners association, all 
owners/occupants within the site, must be members and is recorded at the Court House. Lot 
owners must contribute financially to keep up with facilities, maintenance, and more, and if not 
maintained, the Borough can maintain on their behalf, charge the association, and enforce 
through liens. The Borough would be responsible for reviewing any association agreements 
before recording occurs. Later in the meeting, it was shared that a developer can do as little as 1 
acre based on the profile of the tax parcels in the proposed district. While the acreage is not a 
significant amount, parcels could be combined to complete a more expansive project, and a 
single parcel could have multiple overlay districts. A developer could choose the underlying 
development standards, partake in the RD3 standards, or could follow the proposed incentives 
plan.   
 
The proposed overlay provides incentives in overall parking, no greater than 25%, in exchange 
for bike and pedestrian accessibilities. A parking demand analysis is required for the site based 
on proposed uses within. This ties in with enhancing greenspace within a site for beautification 
and environmental friendliness. There was also discussion about electric vehicle charging, and it 
was noted that while this was an incentive in the proposal, it is also a topic that should be 
reviewed Borough-wide. There was also discussion about ride-share and drop-off areas as it 
pertains to parking needs, and it was noted this would be reviewed for the proposal.  
 
Streetscape was mentioned, noting that it will be helpful to point to a design guide rather than 
adding directly to the Ordinance which may need more frequent revisions. Streetscapes focuses 
on aspects such as trees, seating, and streetlights, and is formatted based on the Park Road 
corridor. The use of a notation versus matrix was also pointed out with the ULI parking matrix, 
recommending that the Ordinance refer to the ULI parking document, not the actual matrix in the 
Ordinance. 
 
While the aspect of a unified site plan, or single master redevelopment plan is important, it was 
pointed out that this does not have to happen at one time, and can be in phases. The proposed 
developer would of course need to move through all aspects of the land development process, 
including items such as reviews, PennDOT coordination, necessary studies, lot owners 
association review recording, and more. These areas would be reviewed by the Borough 
Engineer and necessary Borough personnel, the Planning Commission, and Council. 
 
The Commission spoke about extending the proposed overlay district to the Shoppes at 
Wyomissing to incentivize redevelopment within the entire corridor to route 222.

State Hill Apartments 
Project Narrative 
 
 
This site is located on the south side of State Hill Road (SR 3023) in Wyomissing 
Borough. This lot is currently developed with a commercial building and macadam parking 
lot that covers the majority of the property. Runoff from this site drains to the Schuylkill 
River (WWF, MF). 
The owners propose to demolish the existing commercial building and construct a 
new apartment building on the property. Access and parking areas will be improved to 
meet Borough Ordinance requirements. Overall impervious coverage will be reduced by 
the proposed development. This property will be serviced by public water supply and 
public sewer. The plan for development includes a small commercial café on the ground 
floor facing State Hill Road. Structured parking will be provided at ground level, with 
apartments on floors above. The first floor will include several apartments, gym, pool, 
maintenance/utility rooms and a small office for the building manager.

Dimmerling  Consulting,  Inc.  has  completed  the  following  trip  generation  comparison  for  the 
property located at 1713 State Hill Road in Wyomissing Borough,  Berks County.  The purpose of 
this comparison is to show the proposed apartment building land  use will generate significantly

The  Institute  of  Transportation  Engineers’  (ITE)  Trip  Generatio n,  12th  Edition  (2025),  is  the 
standard  publication  used  to  determine  anticipated  trips  generated  by  a  proposed  site.    The 
site's previous restaurant use falls into ITE Land Use Code (LU C) 932 (High‐Turnover (Sit‐Down) 
Restaurant).  For the proposed apartment building, LUC 221 (Multifamily Housing (Mid‐Rise)) is 
the appropriate land use code.  T he following table illustrates  the estimated average daily trip 
generation for the previous and proposed uses. 
Average Daily Trip Generation Comparison

traffic of the previous restaurant use during the AM and PM peak hours and total daily trips.
