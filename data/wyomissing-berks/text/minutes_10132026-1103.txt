ACTION ITEMS:
No items were presented to Council for action.

MAYOR’S REPORT:

Mayor Frederick Levering noted that he had no report highlights for the month. He did
congratulate the Wyomissing School District for ranking in the top 2,000 in the nation, top 80
in the state, and top 2 in the County. He also spoke to Council about the Stoltzfus Homestead
along Tulpehocken Road, ultimately reminding all to not lose sight of history and traditions.

Mayor Levering also questioned the grant received for the streambank restoration and
parkland trails, and it was pointed out this was relevant to work set forth in the parkland and
forestry study. There was also an area discussed for controlling invasive species and
incorporate deer management.

STAFF REPORTS:
The Library, Police, Fire, Public Works/Engineering, and Tax Collector’s reports were
included in the packet for review.

INTERIM BOROUGH MANAGER & INTERIM FINANCE DIRECTOR REPORT:

Interim Borough Manager Ratliff shared with Council that the proposed overlay district was
sent to the Berks County Planning Commission, with no comments received. This Ordinance
should be on course to have the public hearing during the November Council meeting. He also
pointed out there are no updates on 1714 State Hill Road, 915 Penn Avenue, or the Crown
Plaza. Interim Borough Manager Ratliff also reminded Council of Spooky Hollow event being
held on October 17, 2026 at 2:00 pm.

2027 BUDGET DEVELOPMENT:
Interim Borough Manager Ratliff first shared the 2027 budget preparation schedule with the
public.

During the presentation, there was discussion about the restructuring of the general fund,
leaving a deficit of $2.4 to $2.7 million, and the fact that some of the enterprise funds were
drawn on to pay the deficit, of which there are specific regulations that must be followed when
moving funds as overhead transfers. It was noted that in 2026, there were $750,000 in capital
projects, and the Borough was only responsible for paying $300,000. It was noted that the
Borough is financially healthy, and wise financial decisions were made previously. Staff will
continue to review the cost of operations, debt service arrangements, capital contributions, and
contributions to reserve accounts.

As per the presentation, the 2027 preliminary general fund revenues are $14,442, 200;.
expenditures are $20,517,601, with a negative net of $6,075,401. ay 7 “ia on, z

The presentation further reviewed operational drivers including bargaining, other ‘wages, | ; :

2027. To impact the budget, the Borough would have to decrease service levels.



For the library program, the proposed expenditure is $254,253, of which the Borough funds a
portion of the library.

Summer Playground revenues were $75,692 expenditures were $106,762, leaving a negative
net of $31,070. There was much discussion around the program offering including fees,
expanding the program times, timeline, and ages, inclusion of West Reading youth, as well as
how much tax burden should be placed on residents for the program.

Similarly, the pool was discussed next, with revenues of $156,550, expenditures of $220,019,
leaving a negative net of $63,469. It was noted that major capital work is needed, as well as an
overall needs assessment conducted. There was discussion of the efforts by the pool
subcommittee to increase awareness of the pool and activities, West Reading youth possibly
joining the Wyomissing swim team, and the need to review staffing levels.

The presentation also touched base on code enforcement services, noting that all are
outsourced at this time due to staff changes. The cost is $109,000 to the Borough, with an
aspect of customer service that is lacking. The Borough currently participates in proactive
code enforcement 3 times per week, but has reviewed switching to reactive enforcement. It
was pointed out that most municipalities are using a reactive enforcement approach. If the
aspects completed by the third-party services are brought in-house, the Borough would break
even and improve customer service. It was discussed that building inspections would still be
outsourced due to the lack of volume with inspections. It was pointed out that planning,
zoning, and permitting would need to be fulfilled by two full-time people at the market rate
established in Berks County.

There was discussion that it was difficult to consider raising taxes years ago when the Borough
had a significant fund balance, and the now declining commercial locations, were thriving.

The general fund beginning in 2026 was $3.4 million with 20% available for operating costs.
In December 2026, the general fund will decrease to $3.04 million with 18.4% available for
operating costs, with the same general fund balance to begin 2027. It is estimated that the
ending general fund balance for December 2027 will be negative $3.036 million, with -18%
available for operating costs. At the next Council meeting, three options will be brought to the
table for discussion, about next steps moving forward. There was also brief discussion about
restricting the chart of accounts to better align expenditures with their appropriate
classifications. A tax bill sample was shown to the public for better understanding of the
taxation obtained by the Borough.

The public was reminded of the public feedback forum available, as well as following
budgeting progress on the Borough’s website.

To Melissa Miller, Borough Secretary, and Wyomissing Borough Council:

Please add these expanded written comments to the October 13 meeting record and publish
them with the Council materials. The recording remains the record of my oral remarks. Updates
after the meeting are dated below.

1. Give public proposals a decision.

When a resident makes a practical proposal, say what happens next: a committee referral, a
vote, a decision date, or a reason for rejecting it. Residents should not have to return month
after month for an answer.

| asked for evening committee meetings on January 13. Nine months later, committees still
meet during the working day. Put the remaining daytime meetings to a Council vote. If a
committee needs a different arrangement, explain why. Council and Recreation already meet
in the evening. [1]

The Council president clarified that her response concerned my broader criticism of Council,
not specifically records preservation. That clarification belongs in the record. My concern
remains: critical comments deserve the same respectful consideration as favorable ones. A
resident does not need to speak for a majority to raise a valid question. [5]

Adopting the agenda did not approve the consent items. Before the consent vote, make clear
that residents may ask for separate consideration and Council may grant it. Give contested
matters their own discussion and decision. [5]

2. Set goals and a timetable for interim management.

Publish the interim assignment's goals, responsible people, deadlines, results and expected
remaining cost. Include the permanent-manager recruitment timetable. Report progress
publicly and explain delays.

Cumru appointed an interim manager and began a permanent-manager search in January. It
appointed a permanent manager in September. Its refuse procurement drew several
companies and included another bidding round. What will Wyomissing learn from that process?
Show residents the timetable and results here. [2]

3. Show taxpayers what they are getting for their money.

The September 16 draft minutes identify purchasing reform as a priority. Publish the proposed
rules, competition requirements and adoption date. [3]

The circle-wall records show Spayd's earlier $28,500 proposal and New Castle's $41,613 formal
bid. Explain the scope and price differences, and why proceeding with the single formal bid
offered good value. The price difference alone does not establish a saving. [4]

The towing packet contains proposals and rates. | found no explanation there for the primary

4. Preserve financial records before they are lost.

Council approved Resolutions 2026-28 and 2026-29 on October 13. That does not establish
that records have been destroyed. Defer disposal while the Borough documents what would be
lost, what survives and the preservation options. [3, 5]

The briefing anticipates no significant financial impact but supplies no inventory of the actual
files. Publish a file inventory and date ranges, who reviewed the records, the criteria used, and
how records requiring longer retention or having historical value will be protected. [3]

A check register can show who was paid and how much. Invoices and vouchers can show what
was purchased and why payment was approved. Identify the surviving records that preserve
that detail and explain how residents can obtain them.

Show the staff time and cost of disposal compared with preservation. Seek archival advice and
assess scanning and grant options. My no-charge offer remains available for an agreed batch,
indexed scans and return of originals under Borough-approved terms. Any plan must account
for the Borough's staff work.

As of October 19, the Borough has acknowledged my October 12 records request and given
November 18 as its response target. That does not confirm a preservation hold. Confirm in
writing how responsive records are being protected and which custodians were notified. My
existing request remains unchanged. [6]

5. Make code enforcement fair and open to scrutiny.

Judge code enforcement by consistent treatment, response, cost and accountability, whether
the work is done by employees or contractors.

Publish enforcement policies, service standards, complaint and appeal procedures, and reports
on notices and outcomes. Release records where lawful, protect private information, and
explain specific grounds for withholding records. Residents need a way to check whether
comparable cases receive comparable treatment.

6. Show the full budget costs and test alternatives.

Before asking households to pay more, separate recurring operations from grants, transfers,
capital, debt and other one-time items. Show the cost of current services, proposed changes
and outside contracts, including fees and transition costs. Do not present a year-to-date result
as the recurring operating position.

Compare taxes for the same year and on the same basis. Include separately imposed municipal
levies and explain whether fire and EMS are covered. Show mandatory service charges
separately. General-purpose millage alone does not show a household's full burden.
