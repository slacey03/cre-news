Jim McCarthy shared that the base paving for the West Lawn interceptor will be completed Friday,
June 12, 2026 and concrete work a little later in June. Testing of the lines has been completed and
passed, and the project is on budget, with $115,000 remaining to be paid on the project. In regards

POLICE DEPARTMENT & MAYOR’S REPORT: On motion duly made, seconded and passed,
Council approved the Police Department & Mayor’s report for May 2026.

Police Chief John Phillips provided an update noting that 793 calls in the month of May is the sixth
busiest time, and anticipates a busy Summer. In May the department has also participated in several
community relations events. He spoke about the Felony Lane Gang, those who go through the
drive-thru at a bank and perform some type of fraud. In May, Detectives were able to make an
arrest in a recent case, pointing out that the suspect was involved in other incidents around the
county. Chief Phillips also spoke of another fraudulent matter, where the individual was attempting
fraud in another county, was identified at a Wyomissing bank, and arrested. Regarding the
discussion in May about micromobility enforcement, warnings have been issued, with citations
potentially impending.

There was discussion in regards to solicitation calls, and the public has been advised to call the
Police when solicitors are in the area. He reminded Council that no solicitation is permitted in the
Borough except organized youth related groups and solicitors only hanging door hangers.

Wyomissing Borough Council
June 9, 2026

Dear Members of Council and Concerned individuals,

lam here tonight to formally express my deep concern regarding the continued deterioration of conditions
at the Wyomissing Hills Basketball Courts and surrounding area. What was once intended to serve as a
valued recreational asset has increasingly become a source of frustration, Safety hazards, anda
diminished quality of life for nearby residents — particularly families with young children.

Please Note this is not just an isolated complaint — it is the beginning of an organized community effort to
address ongoing concerns.

ISSUE 1: Ongoing Disruptive and Inappropriate Behavior

The courts and surrounding area have become a consistent gathering place for conduct that is
incompatible with a family-friendly recreational space,

Specific documented and witnessed behaviors include:

* Drug and alcohol use on or adjacent to public playground equipment and recreational facilities

* Excessive profanity and aggressive behavior that intimidates families and children

* Loud music at unreasonable hours, disturbing nearby residents well into the evening

* Littering and debris, including used condoms and other inappropriate materials found throughout the
playground area

* Additional inappropriate conduct/ntimidation that prevents families and children from safely and
comfortably utilizing the public space

These are not isolated incidents, They represent a recurring pattern that has persisted for years without
adequate resolution.

ISSUE 2: Reckless Driving and Dangerous Parking Within a School Zone

The traffic conditions in and around this area have become a serious and ongoing public safety concern
in two primary ways:

Reckless Driving: Vehicles are routinely observed speeding through the area at unsafe speeds with little
regard for pedestrians, children, or residents. This is not an accasional occurrence — it Is a regular and
ongoing issue.

Dangerous Parking: Vehicles frequently park in hazardous and inconsiderate ways, blocking sight lines,
obstructing access, and creating unsafe conditions for pedestrians. During peak evening hours, the
volume and manner of parking significantly worsen an already dangerous situation.

Both of these issues are occurring In and around a K-4 school zone. The combination of high foot traffic,
children at play, reckless driving, and obstructive parking creates a foreseeable and serious public safety
risk, Currently, there are little to no adequate physical barriers, traffic caiming measures, or enforceable’: ,, 7
parking restrictions in place to properly protect pedestrians in this area. Ss Gl hlaous!

ISSUE 3: Children and Families Are Being Displaced From Public Space

The cumulative effect of these ongoing conditions is that the very residents this facility was intended to:
serve — nelghborhood families and young children — are increasingly unable to use it safely.


Participants in the Borough’s summer playground program have already been directly impacted, On the
evening of June 8, 2026, police were called to the area on multiple occasions, and playground program
Staff were forced to intervene in order to clear the space so children could safely use It. This should never
occur. A public recreational space funded and maintained by the Borough should remain accessible, safe,
and welcoming for all residents — especially the children it was designed to serve.

Given the persistent nature of these issues, | respectfully urge Council and the appropriate Committees to
evaluate whether the current use and oversight of this location are adequately best serving the
community. We need a solution that preserves positive recreational opportunities while reducing — and
ideally eliminating — the recurring Issues currently associated with the site.

We are open to any and all reasonable solutions that would help restore this area as a safe community
space. At this time, our primary concern is ensuring the safety of children participating in the playground
program. Some immediate suggestions include increased police presence during playground program
hours and/or temporarily removing the basketball rims and nets, as was done during Covid, to determine
whether conditions improve while a more permanent solution is considered.

The residents — and more importantly, the children — of Wyomissing deserve a clean, safe, and peaceful
environment. | respectfully ask that this matter be placed on Council's agenda for discussion and that
meaningful action be taken before conditions worsen further,

| would also respectfully ask Council to address the following questions:

1, June 8 Incident: is Council aware of the incidents that occurred on the evening of June 8, during
which police were called to the area multiple times and playground staff were forced to intervene
so children could safely use the space? What is the Borough's official account of what occurred?

2 Complaint History: How many formal complaints has the Police Department received regarding
public safety concerns at the Wyomissing Hills Park, and over what timeframe? Has the volume
of complaints increased in recent years?

3. Actions Taken to Date: Beyond periodic police patrols, what specific steps has the Borough
taken in response to these concerns? Are any long-term solutions currently being explored, and if
so, who is responsible and what is the projected timeline?

4. Traffic and Parking: Has the Borough formally assessed the parking and traffic conditions
surrounding this area, particularly given its designation as a school zone? Are any traffic calming
or parking enforcement measures currently under consideration?

5. Path Forward: What is Council's plan to address these conditions in a meaningful and lasting
way, and what timeline and accountability measures can residents reasonably expect moving
forward?

Thank you for your attention to this matter. The families and children of this neighborhood deserve a safe,
clean, and welcoming recreational space, and they are counting on this Council to act. We look forward to
partnering with the borough and our neighbors to develop a solution that serves the best interests of
current and future residents.

Respectfully submitted,

Herbert and Laura Lovett
