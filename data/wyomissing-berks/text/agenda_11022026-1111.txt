1714 St
ate Hill Road: 
Sal Folino, legal counsel for 1714 State Hill Road spoke on behalf of the applicant, explaining 
why the proposed apartment complex complies with the standards set forth in the Borough’s 
Redevelopment Mixed Use Overlay encompassing this site. He identified the project as all 
residential, or residential above commercial, of which both fall within conditional use set forth in 
the RAMUI. The project also satisfies the minimum acreage for redevelopment, the maximum 
coverage, the open space, the height requirements, residential buffers, and the fact that no 
variances are required for the project. A traffic study was completed for the project utilizing the 
aspect of having commercial space, of which Sal indicated having commercial space would 
increase the traffic impact versus strictly residential use. He further noted that the impact to 
DRAFT

schools would be minimal, as the target audience for such project is those with no children. Sal 
pointed out that the Met and B2 apartment complexes have small numbers of children in their 
complexes. Sal assured the attending public that the owner/applicant lives in the Borough, and 
would not want to bring forth a project that lowers values in the neighborhood.  
The project was further detailed, noting that it fits within the area, pointing out Woodland 
Apartments across the street, and the Valley Green condos behind the proposed development 
site. It was reiterated that parking would be to the rear of the facility per the Ordinance, and there 
is a proposal for on-site amenities such as a gym and pool, and possibly a café open to the public, 
however the developer was not opposed to rescinding the café plan, depending on the input of 
the Commission. Sal lastly pointed out, Shapiro’s action housing plan, noting the push in 
Pennsylvania to add more housing.  
The public was reminded that a Conditional Use hearing is required before any formal land 
development occurs. Borough Engineer Jim McCarthy shared that the apartments are allowed by 
conditional use, of which Council would hold a hearing to determine approval or denial of such 
project. If the project were to be approved, the developer will begin the formal land development 
process to essentially refine their plan to meet the parameters set forth in the Borough 
Subdivision and Land Development code. It was also pointed out that public has opportunities to 
participate in the public meetings if the developer gets to the land development process.  
Rob Hain, Berks Surveying and Engineering, reviewed some of the existing site conditions, 
noting that macadam covers a large portion of the current site, and the goal is to increase 
structured parking and eliminate the extensive surface parking. Structured parking would be in 
the first level of the building, and the site would have an access point as far South as possible due 
to the impending State Hill Road round-a-bout project, causing a right in, right out scenario to 
the site. Rob pointed out that the parking ratio for the project using structured parking rules, is 
1.2 spaces per unit, resulting in the parking proposal of 178 spaces. He shared that the apartment 
bedroom count is mostly two bedroom units, with a few three bedroom units. The question arose 
as to possibly assigning at least one space per unit, and the concern was raised that the 
neighborhood cannot take on any more vehicles. The question also arose as to the access point 
before the installation of round-a-bouts, and it was shared that residents would need to access the 
site by means of Wingert to Valley.  
More discussion ensued as to the uses permitted if the commercial space of the project were to 
occur; professional services, restaurant, offices, or retail, as well as the feasibility of adding even 
a café. Due to the minimal accessibility to the location, it was noted the space allotted for the 
café could be turned into more residential space, as the developer would prefer not to have any 
commercial space. It was additionally pointed out that the site would have a leasing office, 
mailroom, and package room, as well as multiple side canopied entrances for accessibility into 
the building. The lot would feature guest parking spaces, a loading area in the rear of the 
building, sidewalk front to back, one elevator, and two-way traffic under the building in the 
structured parking area, along with ADA parking.  
The Commission expressed concerns about traffic and parking, of which it was pointed out the 
Commission could prepare a memo to Council for consideration during the Conditional Use 
hearing for this project. There was further concern about the PennDOT project as it relates to the 
proposed 1714 project, however it was explained that the Borough does not have legal authority 
to hold the project due to an impending PennDOT project that has no identifiable timelines. The 
developer briefly spoke about trip counts, noting that restaurant use would generate 1,200+ trips

per day, with housing at 540 trips per day. They were waiting until school was back in session to 
obtain a more accurate traffic count. It was noted the developer will have the preliminary study 
available by the Conditional Use hearing, and it was also reiterated that PennDOT will require a 
Highway Occupancy Permit.  
While the Commission was not opposed to recommending the proposed project to Council for 
Conditional Use hearing, as it meets the requirements set forth in the overlay, and will undergo 
further land development phases, it was pointed out several raised concerns of parking, traffic, 
and infrastructure that should be attached to Council’s Conditional Use decision. The 
Commission stood as a non-recommendation for the café portion, as that is a business decision, 
not Commission decision.  
Scott Miller made a motion, seconded by Michael Herr to recommend Council support 
the application with the following considerations; traffic analysis, stormwater impact, 
building design information and internal structure for pedestrian and vehicles, utilities 
impact, geotechnical study pertaining to sink holes, structural analysis, and all aspects 
of land development including bike and pedestrian accessibility, all in favor.

Dear Borough Members, 
 
In accordance with the Borough’s request, we have reviewed the above-referenced plan, application, 
and supporting documents to determine compliance with the Borough’s Zoning and Subdivision and

consisting of the church and parking lot (Lot 1), and the other lot consisting of the community center 
(Lot 2). The proposed lot line between Lot 1 and Lot 2 also serves as the zoning district boundary 
between the R1 Low Density Residential District and the TC Town Center Residential/Office District. 
The Borough Zoning Map was recently amended to reflect this district boundary change.  
 
The documents received and reviewed by our office include the following: 
• Subdivision Plan (Sheet 1 of 1) prepared by C2C Design Group, dated September 12, 2025, last
