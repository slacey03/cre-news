Interim Borough Manager Ratliff spoke about the trash and recycling services, pointing out 
several concerns received by residents about the hauler services. Residents were assured that 
constant communications are occurring with the haulers to improve services, but also pointed 
out issues faced on their end such as labor shortages. The hauler also identified several areas of 
concern during collection such as untagged bulk items, improper refuse and recycling 
preparation by residents, items not set far enough into the collection area, items not set out the 
night before, items set in areas between two cars, and more. Even the aspect of no standard 
trash/recycling receptacles and the process of a person manually dumping the trash, is seen as 
an issue. Interim Borough Manager Ratliff indicated the Borough cannot dictate where the 
hauler compacts the trash, and the Borough has been told numerous times that when the hauler 
intermingles trash and recycling, the items are separated out at the facility. Residents are 
reminded the hauler has the entire day to make the collection, and have sometimes worked 
later into the evening to finish routes, especially during extremely hot days. 
Some remediations being worked through included the use of forward facing cameras on the 
hauler's trucks, working towards a communications publication, integrating Borough 
addresses into route mapping, and exploring the creation of a route inspector. The public was 
reminded that the agreements are in place until 2028, and it was pointed out that in 2025 
services with the previous haulers equated to $575 per year per household, in 2026 under the 
new haulers, services dropped to $504 per year per household, and in 2026, if services would 
have continued under the previous haulers, the rate would have increased to $597 per year per 
household. Residents were lastly reminded that the hauler has a full 24 hours to service a 
missed stop.

PLANNING COMMISSION- OVERLAY OVERVIEW: 
Sam Wiser, Salzmann Hughes shared with Council that the proposed overlay has been revised 
to modernize the overly to add more opportunities for developers, to enhance the overlay 
district, as well as revising the Zoning and SALDO Landscaping Ordinances in synchrony. It 
was shared that the revisions are not meant to encompass the needs of any developer 
specifically, and that no concept plans have been provided for formal land development 
review. 
He further reviewed the proposed Commercial and Industrial Zoning Overlay District revisions 
noting that the existing district allows all uses permitted by right in commercial and industrial zones 
as before, but could expand to apartments and townhouses above non-residential use, 30% by right 
or 60% by exception, or age-restricted housing above non-residential uses. Adult uses are excluded 
from the special exception in this zone. This could also include shopping centers, discount stores, 
grocery stores, warehouse clubs, restaurants, convenience stores, and more. Accessory warehousing 
is permitted as long as it does not exceed 25% of the floor area, and cannot be a standalone 
warehouse. This was included to accommodate the online shopping and short delivery time retail 
expenence. 
Relative to area, yard, and height in Section 504A, standards existing and proposed to remain 
include a height of 75 feet, maximum building coverage of 60 ft, maximum pervious paved area of 
75%, and minimum lot size and minimum lot width with the exception for interior lots which may 
have a smaller lot size. Minimum open area is proposed to change from 10% to 25%, and perimeter 
building set back from 66% of the underlying setback to the full underlying zoning setback. 
Planned unit development was outlined in the presentation, pointing out fee-simple subdivision, 
shared parking across site with the site encompassing the overall parking demand, shared 
stormwater facilities and utilities, and the requirement of easements, maintenance of landscape, and 
more, as a lot owners association. Overall, this encompasses site planning and maintenance for a 
particular redevelopment/development site in this overlay, and conveying to the Borough, the site's 
master plan. It was reiterated that as part of the lot owners association, all owners/occupants within 
the site, must be members and is recorded at the Court House. Lot owners must contribute 
financially to keep up with facilities, maintenance, and more, and if not maintained, the Borough 
can maintain on their behalf, charge the association, and enforce through liens. The Borough would 
be responsible for reviewing any association agreements before recording occurs. Later if) .the 
meeting, it was shared that a developer can do as I ittle as 1 acre based on the profile of the tax 
parcels in the proposed district. While the acreage is not a significant amount, parcels could be 
combined to complete a more expansive project, and a single parcel could have multiple overlay 
districts. A developer could choose the underlying development standards, partake in the RD3 

standards, or could follow the proposed incentives plan. 
The proposed overlay provides incentives in overall parking, no greater than 25%, in exchange for 
bike and pedestrian accessibilities. A parking demand analysis is required for the site based on 
proposed uses within. This ties in with enhancing greenspace within a site for beautification and 
environmental friendliness.

Written Public Comments 
Adrian Jadic I Wyomissing resident 
Council meeting of September 16, 2026 
Date October 9, 2026 
To Melissa Miller, Borough Secretary, and Borough Council 
Please retain and publish this written version and supplementation of my public comments at the September 
16, 2026 Council meeting, which continued into the first capital budget discussion. It develops the issues I 
raised during the opening and closing public comment periods. The recording remains the record of my oral 
remarks. 
Spending controls and year end forecasts 
At the meeting, I cited recreation at approximately 78% of its annual budget, administration at 94%, and legal 
spending at 90%, with four months remaining. Please publish the corresponding figures, explain any timing or 
classification differences, and provide the year-end forecast for each area. The important question is what 
controls prevent optional spending from continuing while the Borough faces an operating shortfall. [1] 
I had attended the Recreation Board meeting the previous evening. My concern was the lack of a clear public 
accounting of program and event costs and the lack of direct answers when residents requested it. Council 
should pause new discretionary event spending until it has the full cost and has set spending limits. Include 
salaries and benefits, other departments' support, equipment, advertising, donations, fees and other income. 
Publish the review and say when it will be available. (1, 2] 
West Lawn project costs and closeout 
I asked about the final payment and release of retainage on the West Lawn project, the additional cost of 
approximately $95,000 I cited, and the reported subcontractor payment dispute. Explain the change-order 
basis, the work covered, how final quantities were verified, and the protections in place before final payment 
or retainage release. Identify the grant and contribution funding as well as any Borough share. Outside funding 
does not remove the need for a proper closeout. [ 1] 
I also asked when the temporary storage associated with that project would be removed and the site cleaned 
up. Please identify the responsible party and the completion date. 
Berkshire Mall zoning and completion safeguards 
Following the September 15 Planning Commission discussion, I asked Council to explain the proposed zoning 
changes in plain English: what they are intended to achieve, why these particular changes were selected, and 
where a comparable approach has worked. Residents should be able to distinguish the zoning framework from 
any developer's concept or an actual approved redevelopment plan. (1, 3] 
The ability to divide and sell individual parcels makes the completion question especially important. Explain 
what prevents the valuable pieces from being sold or developed while the mall core remains unresolved. 
Identify the enforceable obligations, any phasing or completion security, and the consequences of 
noncompletion. If a master plan or lot owners association is the answer, explain the precise duties and 
remedies. Shared maintenance arrangements and a promise to complete redevelopment are different 
obligations. 
I also asked how the proposed changes relate to ideas previously presented by Peter Abrams and what the 
Borough is doing to attract other qualified developers. Publish the relevant concept material and explain the

Borough's objectives. A permanent zoning change should have a public justification that residents can evaluate 
beyond its usefulness to one prospective developer. 
On fuel stations, my concern was permitting them by right without a clear explanation of their role in the 
Borough's redevelopment objectives. I suggested allowing a proposal to be evaluated through a special­
exception process rather than simply opening that use generally. Explain the scope of the proposed permission 
and why the selected review process adequately addresses traffic, access, safety and neighboring uses. [1] 
Public comment timing and access to presentations 
Residents should not have to remain through a lengthy agenda simply to make a general public comment. I 
observed people leaving before the later comment period. Provide an early opportunity for comments on 
nonagenda matters and retain a later opportunity for issues arising during the meeting. Working residents and 
parents should have a practical chance to be heard. 
When a presentation contains material that was not available before the meeting, residents also need a way to 
ask a relevant question while that subject is being discussed. Publish the material in advance and provide an 
orderly opportunity for clarification. A meeting is less useful if residents cannot understand the proposal until 
after the discussion has moved on. [1] 
Nature play proposal and the continuing cost of recreation 
I questioned the proposed nature play area, including whether mud play or other features previously discussed 
had returned to the proposal. Council should require an accurate description of what is actually proposed, 
what was removed, and what remains. My concern was the suitability of the site and activities, the safety 
review, and the ongoing responsibility for inspection and maintenance. (1, 2] 
Before approval, explain the solicitor's and insurer's review, the approach to supervision and inspection, and 
which department will maintain the site. Natural materials and volunteer installation do not eliminate 
recurring costs or potential hazards. The proposal should identify those responsibilities and their cost. 
Public Works time also has a cost even when no separate recreation invoice is created. Time spent maintaining 
a new play area is time unavailable for trees, streets and other existing duties. Council should consider that 
tradeoff before expanding facilities while discussing a budget shortfall. A worthwhile idea still has to tit the 
Borough's finances and staffing. 
Meeting room arrangements and recording rules 
I asked about changing the Council seating arrangement so members would face residents, and about the rules 
governing residents who record meetings. Please provide clear, practical rules that allow someone recording to 
check equipment, adjust the camera when needed, and briefly leave the room without creating an 
unreasonable barrier to recording. 
I recognize that room changes may require scheduling and staff work. Please identify the planned changes and 
their timing, and explain how the layout and audio arrangements will make proceedings easier for the public to 
follow. These comments describe the concern as it stood on September 16; any subsequent improvements 
should also be recorded. [1]

using complaint records, missed collections, contract compliance and cost, rather than treating dissatisfaction
