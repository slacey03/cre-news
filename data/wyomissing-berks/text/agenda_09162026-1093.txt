A concept plan is not a formal land-development application. It is nevertheless directly relevant to a 
zoning rewrite. The public record should identify when that plan was submitted, who received and

Expanding the map does not answer who designed the text 
Staff emphasized that the overlay also encompasses the Crowne Plaza and other commercial properties 
and discussed possibly adding the Shops at Wyomissing and additional parcels. A broader map may 
support a legitimate area-wide planning purpose. It does not, by itself, answer why the ordinance 
contains this particular combination of uses, financing tools, subdivision rights, parking rules, and 
phasing provisions. 
The issue should not be reduced to a premature legal accusation of spot zoning. The more immediate 
public-record question is simpler: was permanent public zoning independently designed from adopted 
planning goals, or was it reverse-engineered around the requirements of one prospective purchaser and 
then extended to surrounding properties? The Borough can answer that question by publishing the 
provenance of the draft. 
The closing delays make that disclosure more important 
On January 29, Abrams said he had waived due diligence and placed a $1 million nonrefundable deposit. 
He described the May 15 outside closing date as time to obtain the two anchor leases, secure Boscov’s

He also said a TIF was not formally a condition of closing, but that without it his odds of reaching the

“In exchange for that, they pushed the outside closing date to May 15th, which gives us enough

The May 15 closing did not occur. In a June 11 Reading Eagle report, Mayor Fred Levering said he had

2. Estevez matter — departmental accountability cannot end with one 
officer 
What the record establishes 
When Council promoted John Phillips to Police Chief in February 2021, the minutes record my request 
for greater transparency, diversity, accountability, and an effective complaint process. The Estevez 
matter is a concrete test of that commitment. 
In a December 17, 2025 deposition, Officer Rubiel Estevez described the underlying incident as a “false 
arrest.” He testified that he received a 15-day suspension after an internal investigation, that Lieutenant 
Paul Baur conducted the investigation, and that he believed the Police Department determined he 
should not have arrested Gulshan Chilashvili and Haldun Ekinci. 
Estevez also testified that the decision to arrest was his and required no departmental approval. He had 
no prior experience investigating the alleged “stringing” scheme. His described work consisted 
principally of reviewing material and video supplied by Nicholas Damone, discussing the video with 
other officers, and making follow-up calls to Damone. Court filings state that Ekinci was jailed for 10 
days and Chilashvili for 15 days before release, and that the criminal charges were later withdrawn. 
The Borough and Estevez entered a Settlement Agreement and General Release requiring payment of 
$499,000, including attorneys’ fees and costs, while denying wrongdoing. On July 30, 2026, G&B 
Amusements and Damone filed a joinder complaint alleging that the internal investigation found Estevez 
did not perform a proper investigation and should not have made the arrests. That joinder allegation has 
not been adjudicated; the deposition and settlement are records that speak for themselves. 
The command question remains unanswered 
I found no evidence that Chief Phillips ordered the arrests. That is not the end of the inquiry. Section 341 
of the Borough Code makes the Chief the Police Department’s chief executive and, under the Mayor, 
places him in charge of the force with supervision over its members’ exercise of their powers, duties, 
and authority. 
The public record shows individual discipline. It does not show a public command-level review of how 
this investigation passed through the department, what supervisory controls existed, what failed, or 
what changed. The department had nearly two weeks between the January 9 report and the January 22 
arrests. Estevez testified that at least one officer and possibly a sergeant saw or discussed the video. The 
public deserves to know whether any supervisor reviewed the evidence, charging theory, warrant work, 
probable-cause basis, or risks associated with an unfamiliar alleged scheme.

Paragraph 12 requires the Borough to conduct a de novo hearing within 60 days of the court’s approval,

including certified-mail notice to all adjoining property owners. Paragraph 13 requires the appellants,

Those provisions disclose the core procedural exchange: the matter starts over with proper notice, and 
the appellants end their appeal. That relief may constitute the complete consideration for resolving the 
case. The remaining question is whether paragraphs 11–15 are the complete and exclusive agreement, 
including all terms concerning fees or costs, releases, waivers, side agreements, or other commitments. 
Mr. Gombar subsequently stated that the applicant waived the agreement’s 60-day hearing 
requirement while a new hearing date is arranged. That later waiver is not reflected in the court-
approved agreement, and his explanation does not say what prompted the applicant to relinquish a 
deadline that would ordinarily allow its project to move forward promptly or how the waiver was 
obtained. This observation does not assume a hidden bargain; it identifies a material development not 
explained in the public record. 
Independent counsel would provide a clean slate 
My request for independent counsel is directed to the solicitor’s institutional role, not to Council’s 
decision to correct the notice defect or to Borough staff’s work in arranging a new hearing. Masano 
Bradley participated in the original conditional-use proceeding, advised Council when the notice issue 
was raised before the vote, represented the Borough in the resulting appeal and settlement, and would 
ordinarily advise Council during the de novo proceeding required to correct the challenged process. 
This comment does not allege adjudicated misconduct or claim personal bias. Those overlapping roles 
nevertheless create an avoidable appearance concern: the same firm should not assess its earlier advice, 
determine what correction is sufficient, and then serve as substantive counsel during the corrective 
proceeding. Independent counsel would protect Council, the Borough, the applicant, the appellants, the 
public, and Masano Bradley itself from reasonable doubt about the process. 
The April 6 Planning Commission meeting adds to that appearance concern. The posted action for

independent process now.

Information Briefing Summary 
 
From: Eden Ratliff, Interim Borough Manager  
To: Infrastructure Committee and Borough Council  
Date: August 17, 2026  
RE: Maintenance of Proposed Nature Play Area  
 
 
Action Forcing Event  
 
Local resident and member of the Wyomissing Borough Recreation Board has requested 
authorization to raise money and install a Nature Play Area on Borough property at 53 Valley 
Road.  
 
Background  
 
The Borough of Wyomissing owns and operates an expansive suite of parks and 
recreational facilities. Resident Jess Christman, who is a member of the Borough’s 
Recreation Board, has requested authorization to raise money and install a Nature Play 
Area on Borough property. The presentation outlining the site plan and proposed recreation 
facility is attached. The facility would be built completely out of natural items including 
fallen trees, rocks features, dirt, etc. The costs to install the facility would be funded by 
donations and volunteers.  
 
Analysis  
 
The proposal for the Nature Play Area is for its installation to be funded by donations and 
built by volunteers that the resident is organizing. At the completion of installation, the 
facility would become a recreation asset managed and maintained by the Borough’s Public 
Works Department. The proposed Nature Play Area is completely made up of natural 
materials they do not require regular or irregular maintenance. Examples of the play 
“equipment” include fallen trees and other natural materials. Maintenance responsibilities 
by the Department of Public Works may require annual weeding, tree trimming, and 
inspection to ensure no dangerous conditions exist. The parking space at the entrance to 
the proposal play area will also require maintenance including painting, and eventually, 
resurfacing.  
 
The maintenance required for the proposed nature play area is minimal and can be 
absorbed with the existing resources of the Department of Public Works. No additional 
resources are required in order to maintain the existing facility.

Borough staff supports any decision made by Borough Council as it relates to the 
installation of the Nature Play Area and does not require additional resources to support its 
maintenance requirements.
