Written Public Comments 
Adrian Jadic I Wyomissing resident 
Council meeting of September 30, 2026 
Date October 9, 2026 
To Melissa Miller, Borough Secretary, and Borough Council 
Please retain and publish this written version and supplementation of my public comments at the September 
30, 2026 Council special budget meeting. It covers both my opening budget comments and my closing 
comments concerning the September 17 Atonement zoning hearing. The recording remains the record of my 
oral remarks. 
Budget decisions and spending restraint 
Before Council asks residents to pay more, it must show what spending it has reviewed, what it can reduce, 
and why the remaining costs are necessary. A reported General Fund structural operating deficit of $2.4-$2.7 
million demands a plan that residents can examine, with clear choices and their effects on taxes and services. 
Presenting the size of the problem does not answer what Council has done to control it. [1] 
Earlier warnings require an explanation of earlier decisions 
At the January 8 Finance meeting, I raised debt and deficit concerns and requested a 90-day corrective plan 
and a review of the adopted budget. The February 5 Finance minutes subsequently recorded that Council had 
opted not to reopen the budget. They described possible expenditure reductions, stated that recreation 
publicity had already gone out and event contracts had been signed, and recorded a unanimous motion to 
table recommending actions on 2026 budget cost savings. [2, 3] 
That history matters. Existing contracts may limit particular savings, but they do not explain why every later 
optional commitment or purchase had to proceed. Council should identify which commitments were already 
binding, which could still be reduced, and what action followed the February discussion. Please publish the 
reductions actually implemented, when they began, and the savings achieved or expected. If savings were 
rejected, explain why. 
Explain the spending figures and the year end forecast 
At the September 30 meeting, I cited August reports showing approximately $836,000 in administration 
spending, about 94% of its annual budget, with four months remaining. I also cited recreation spending 
approximately 14.4% above the comparable prior-year figure. Those figures call for an explanation of the 
spending trend, the corresponding revenues, and the remaining-year forecast. [1] 
Publish the underlying August report, the adopted and revised budget amounts, and a reconciliation of any 
changes in accounting categories. Explain how the remaining months will be funded and what spending 
controls apply. A category may contain an early payment or a reclassified expense; if that explains a high 
percentage, show the adjustment rather than leaving residents to guess. The Borough should be able to 
distinguish a timing issue from a recurring shortfall. 
Show the full cost of recreation and restore consistent reporting 
Free admission does not make an event free. The public needs the full taxpayer cost of each major event and 
program, including recreation salaries and benefits, direct purchases, advertising, equipment, and support 
provided by Public Works, police, tire and EMS. Identify the hours worked, overtime where applicable, and the 
Adrian Jadic I September 30, 2026 I 1 

accounts to which those costs were charged. Separately show donations, sponsorships, fees and other income. 
A cost does not disappear because another department pays it. 
Attendance is useful information, but it does not establish affordability. Council needs to know the net cost 
and the staff time being diverted from other work before approving an expansion. Please explain the increase 
in recreation spending and which services or events Council considers essential, which can be reduced, and 
which can wait. 
Financial oversight also requires reports that arrive before they are approved. The June 16 Recreation Board 
minutes state that the May financial report had not been received, then record approval of that same report. 
Please explain what was reviewed and approved, and put a consistent reporting schedule in place. Each report 
should show budget, actual spending, income and the expected year-end result, with a comparable prior-year 
period. [4] 
Publish the numbers before asking for public input 
As I stated at the September 30 meeting, the Agenda Center linked the agenda that afternoon but did not 
provide a supporting budget packet. Seeing figures on a screen for the first time is not a reasonable 
opportunity to review them and make an informed comment. Publish presentations, calculations and 
supporting schedules before the hearing, with enough time for residents to examine them. Posting material 
afterward helps the archive; it does not provide that earlier opportunity. [1] 
I am asking Council to pause new discretionary event commitments and nonessential purchases while it 
completes this review. Publish the expected savings and explain each exception. Protect necessary services, 
safety and maintenance, while identifying optional spending that can be postponed. Residents need to see the 
restraint before they are asked to pay more. 
Demonstrate staffing and equipment needs and explain vehicle use 
For police and fire requests, show workload, overtime, the full cost of staffing, equipment condition and the 
alternatives considered. If comparisons with neighboring municipalities are used, account for differences in 
services and responsibilities. Demonstrate the need for each request and explain its priority within the 
Borough's finances. 
I also asked a question based on reports I had received, which I could not independently verify: does the 
Borough authorize the Fire Chief to use a department vehicle for out-of-state commuting, including to 
Maryland? This is a request for confirmation, not a statement that such an arrangement exists. If authorized, 
publish the policy or agreement, who approved it, the permitted use and mileage, the annual fuel and 
maintenance costs, and any employee contribution. If not authorized, say so clearly. [1] 
Problems inherited from previous councils still require decisions by this Council. The record should show what 
action followed the warnings and what residents receive in return for any additional taxes or charges. 
The Atonement decision and stormwater responsibilities 
My closing comment concerned the September 17 Zoning Hearing Board hearing for Lutheran Church of 
Atonement at 5 and 65 Wyomissing Boulevard. Residents deserve a competent, independent decision with 
evidence and an explanation they can review. Familiarity with the community cannot replace understanding 
the ordinance and testing the basis for an exception. 
The percentages reflect a voluntary change in the lot boundaries 
The Borough's R-1 regulations set maximum building coverage at 25% and maximum paved area at 20%. The 
August 26 zoning officer's memorandum identifies existing building coverage of 26.7% and existing paved 
Adrian Jadic I September 30, 2026 I 2 

coverage of 36. 9%, increasing after the proposed subdivision to 28.6% and 41.8%, respectively. My reference 
to approximately 42% at Council was the rounded paved-area figure. Building coverage and paved coverage 
are separate measures. [S, 6] 
The hearing explanation was that no additional paving or construction on the remaining church parcel was 
proposed: the percentages would rise because the lot area would become smaller. The engineer nevertheless 
acknowledged that the subdivision would increase the existing nonconformity. That is the issue I was raising. 
Calling the change arithmetic does not explain why an owner should receive relief to redraw a lot in a way that 
increases its departure from the ordinance. [7] 
The zoning memorandum also says the same request had been denied on February 25. The public should be 
able to compare the earlier and later decisions and understand what evidence, circumstances or findings 
justified the different result. Publish the written decision, the earlier decision, the plans and calculations, and 
the exhibits relied upon. [6] 
Explain the drainage review and the safeguards 
I recognize that a boundary line alone does not add pavement or automatically increase runoff. My concern is 
the opportunity to review an already heavily paved site when granting new relief, the safeguards considered, 
and the consistency of the Borough's treatment of applicants. Residents who are required to address drainage 
deserve an explanation of how those responsibilities were considered here. 
At the September 16 capital discussion, Public Works described deteriorating storm sewer pipes, the need for 
preventive inspections and repairs, and flooding and inlet-capacity problems. Those are public costs. The 
Borough should explain how its zoning and subdivision decisions fit with its stated need to maintain drainage 
infrastructure. [8] 
At the September 17 hearing, I did not hear a substantive review of site drainage or a specific stormwater 
mitigation condition in the announced approval. The motion did include conditions concerning residential use 
and compliance with applicable requirements. Please identify any separate stormwater or subdivision review 
that supplies the missing analysis, and explain who must complete and enforce it. General conditions are not a 
substitute for a clear public explanation. [7] 
I was not asking the Borough to reject preservation of the historic building. I was asking why practical 
measures to reduce runoff or improve the site were not meaningfully considered or explained. Where 
appropriate, those could include reducing unnecessary pavement, planting trees, infiltration measures or 
other improvements supported by a qualified drainage review. Their suitability must be assessed for this site. 
Make the evidence and relevant relationships visible 
The zoning officer's August 26 memorandum was introduced as Board Exhibit 8 at the hearing. I asked why the 
officer did not give sworn testimony explaining it, as had occurred with the prior zoning officer in hearings I 
attended. Please publish the memo and the complete exhibit set and explain how the Board evaluated that 
evidence and how residents could examine it. I am asking for an explanation of the procedure, not asserting 
that the memo was necessarily inadmissible. [7] 
I also asked for a direct answer about relevant relationships between the applicant and those deciding or 
advising on the application. I had received an unverified report about the Chair's affiliation with the church. I 
did not present it as an established fact, and it should not be treated as one. Please state whether a relevant 
affiliation exists and explain any disclosure, participation or recusal determination. Apply the same approach to 
other Board members and advising counsel where relevant. Residents should not have to rely on rumors to 
understand the decision.

The request is straightforward: make the decision, evidence, drainage responsibilities and safeguards visible. 
The Borough cannot expect residents to accept higher costs for infrastructure while leaving reasonable 
questions about its treatment of individual properties unanswered.
