CONSULTING ENGINEER’S REPORT: On motion duly made, seconded and passed, Council 
approved the Consulting Engineer’s Report for May 2026. 
 
Borough Engineer Jim McCarthy reviewed the report.  
 
Jim McCarthy shared that the base paving for the West Lawn interceptor will be completed Friday, 
June 12, 2026 and concrete work a little later in June. Testing of the lines has been completed and 
passed, and the project is on budget, with $115,000 remaining to be paid on the project. In regards 
to the Berkshire Mall, there was court mandated agreement correspondence, of which Michelle 
Mayfield will be responding to the request. 
 
POLICE DEPARTMENT & MAYOR’S REPORT: On motion duly made, seconded and passed, 
Council approved the Police Department & Mayor’s report for May 2026. 
 
Police Chief John Phillips provided an update noting that 793 calls in the month of May is the sixth 
busiest time, and anticipates a busy Summer. In May the department has also participated in several 
community relations events. He spoke about the Felony Lane Gang, those who go through the 
drive-thru at a bank and perform some type of fraud. In May, Detectives were able to make an 
arrest in a recent case, pointing out that the suspect was involved in other incidents around the 
county. Chief Phillips also spoke of another fraudulent matter, where the individual was attempting 
fraud in another county, was identified at a Wyomissing bank, and arrested. Regarding the 
discussion in May about micromobility enforcement, warnings have been issued, with citations 
potentially impending.  
 
There was discussion in regards to solicitation calls, and the public has been advised to call the 
Police when solicitors are in the area. He reminded Council that no solicitation is permitted in the 
Borough except organized youth related groups and solicitors only hanging door hangers.  
 
FIRE CHIEF’S REPORT: On motion duly made, seconded and passed, Council approved the Fire 
Chief’s report for May 2026. 
 
Fire Chief Eric Needle reviewed the report.  
 
Chief Needle shared the monthly and year-to-date statistics, invited Council to stop by the fire 
station to learn more about their department and see the new air packs, and briefly mentioned the 
desire to host a public CPR class in September 2026 at station; more information to follow.

ORDINANCE NO. 1462-2026 
AN ORDINANCE OF THE BOROUGH OF WYOMISSING, BERKS COUNTY, 
PENNSYLV ANIA, AMENDI NG CHAPTER 21 ENTITLED “ STREETS AND 
SIDEWALKS” TO ADD A  PART 8 ENTITLED “ SNOW AND ICE REMOV AL 
REGULATIONS”. 
WHEREAS, the Borough has determined that it is in the best interests of its residents to 
establish snow and ice removal procedures for enforcement after snow events. 
NOW THEREFORE BE IT ENACTED and ORDAINED by the Council of the Borough 
of Wyomissing, Berks County, Pennsylvania by the authority of the same as follows: 
SECTION 1. Addition of Snow and Ice Removal Regulations. 
Chapter 21 entitled “Streets and Sidewalks” is hereby amended and supplemented to add a 
Part 8 as follows: 
PART 8 
SNOW AND ICE REMOV AL REGULATIONS 
Section 801. Owner or O ccupant of Property to Remove S now and Ice from 
Sidewalk. 
1. I t shall be the duty of every person, entity, association, partnership
or corporation owning or occupying any property fronting on any public street or 
alley within the Borough of Wyomissing to remove or cause to be removed from 
the sidewalks of said premises all ice, snow or sleet within a period of 36 hours 
after the same shall have ceased to fall thereon. Due to the increased pedestrian 
traffic on Penn Avenue  in the Borough, all ice, snow or sleet shall be removed 
within a period of 24 hours after the same shall have ceased to fall thereon. Said 
sidewalks shall be kept clear of such ice, snow and sleet thereafter, unless said snow 
and/or ice be of such a hardened or frozen condition that it cannot be removed 
without injury to the sidewalk, in which case the owner, occupant or tenant of each 
such property is required to place salt, sand or other abrasive or chemical material 
upon said snow and/or ice within the time provided above to render such sidewalks 
safe for travel.  In addition, school bus stop corners must have all snow and ice 
removed from them within 24 hours after the same shall have ceased to fall thereon. 
2. For the purpose of complying with the provisions of Paragraph 1
above, it shall be the duty of every such person, entity, association, partnership or 
corporation owning or occupying any property to conform to the requirements of 
this section, whether or not such owner is present on the property, and whether or 
not such owner has delegated such responsibility to a third party or tenant. In the 
instance of a building which is occupied by multiple residential or commercial 
DRAFT

2 
 
tenants, any such snow and/or ice removal shall be the responsibility of the owner 
of said premises, whether or not such responsibilities have been delegated to a third 
party. 
  (A) The owner of any property fronting on a public street or alley 
within the Borough of Wyomissing shall clear a path of at least 36 inches in width 
on the sidewalks fronting such premises. Sidewalks measuring less than 36 inches 
must be cleared entirely. 
(B) In order to accommodate increased pedestrian traffic on 
Penn Avenue, a path of at least 48 inches in width shall be cleared. Where certain 
Penn Avenue properties have a sidewalk width of less than 48 inches, a path of 36 
inches in width shall be cleared. 
(C) The owner of any property fronting a public street or alley 
with a bus shelter, handicap ramp, or fire hydrant shall clear sidewalks surrounding 
those areas. 
 3. Exceptions. 
(A) State of emergency/government closure. In the event that 
snow or ice, or combination thereof, causes a declared state of emergency or the 
closure of the Borough of Wyomissing office, the occupant, owner or other person 
in charge of a property shall have an additional 48 hours to fulfill the requirements 
of Paragraphs 1 and 2 above. 
(B) Extreme winter conditions. In the event that snow or ice 
cannot be removed due to packed ice or other conditions and a reasonable effort 
has been made during the specified time frame to render the sidewalks safe for 
travel, a fine will not be assessed. Once the snow has melted or is able to be 
removed, the provisions of Paragraphs 1 and 2 above shall apply. 
 4. Penalties. Authorized officials of the Borough of Wyomissing 
engaged in the enforcement of the regulations established in Paragraphs  1 and 2 
shall have the authority to notify (by first-class mail, hand delivery or posted on the 
property) any such person, association, partnership or corporation violating 
Paragraphs 1 or 2 that he or she shall pay at the office of the Borough Hall within 
10 days from the time of the notice, the sum of $25 for the first offense, $50 for the 
second offense, and $100 for the third or subsequent offense. Each day that a 
violation of this  section continues shall constitute a separate offense. If such 
payment shall not be made at Borough Hall within the ten-day period, that violator 
shall be subject to prosecution and to the penalty prescribed in Section 803 of this 
Chapter. Notification by hand delivery shall include, among other things, affixing 
a notice to the front door of the office or residence of any such person, entity, 
association, partnership or corporation violating Paragraphs 1 or 2 above. 
Section 802. Snow and Ice Not to be Deposited in Streets. 
DRAFT

3 
 
1. It shall be unlawful for any person, entity, association, partnership 
or corporation to throw, cast, or deposit, or cause to be thrown, cast or deposited, 
any snow, ice, hail or sleet into any streets or public highways situate within the 
Borough of Wyomissing. 
2. In residential districts, snow/ice should be deposited onto the lawn 
or yard of the property.  
3. In commercial districts, snow/ice should be deposited on half the 
sidewalk. 
4. Snow/ice cleared from around parked vehicles should be deposited 
on the lawn, yard, or the half sidewalk nearest the street. 
5. Snow/ice must not be deposited on any storm drain inlet, as this may 
block the snow melt and create ponding and possibly flooding.  
Section 803. Violations and Penalties; Abatement of Nuisances. 
1. Any person who violates or permits a violation of this Part 8 of 
Chapter 21 shall, upon being found liable therefor  in a prosecution outlined in 
Section 801, Paragraph 4, pay a fine of not more than $1,000, plus court costs and

If the penalty is not paid, the Borough shall initiate a civil action for collection in 
accordance with the Pennsylvania Rules of Civil Procedure. Each day a violation 
exists shall constitute a sepa rate offense, and each section of this Part 8  that is 
violated shall also constitute a separate offense. In addition to or in lieu of 
enforcement under this Section 803, the Borough may enforce this Part 8 in equity 
in the Court of Common Pleas of Berks County. 
 2. All matters referred to as being unlawful in this Part 8  are declared 
to be nuisances and removable as such by the Borough. If the cost of removal is not 
promptly paid, a municipal lien shall be filed therefor or otherwise collected as 
authorized by law. 
 3. If a property owner  or occupant  shall fail, neglect, or refuse to 
comply with its obligations under this Part 8 of Chapter 21 within twenty-four (24) 
hours of being issued a notice of violation, the Borough may proceed to clear all 
snow and/or ice and collect the expenses therefor and any additional amount s 
allowed by law. These sums may be in addition to any fine or penalty imposed 
under this Section 803.

Public Comment for the Record - Recreation Board - June 16, 2026
Submitted by Adrian Jadic, 48 Cardinal Road, Wyomissing, PA
Director report.
1. Wyomissing Hills basketball courts: safety complaints require a written 
response plan
At the June 16 Recreation Board meeting, residents living near the Wyomissing Hills Memorial Park 
basketball courts described a pattern of late-night noise, marijuana odor, litter, alcohol bottles, used 
condoms, profanity, fighting, speeding vehicles, and situations requiring police response. These are 
resident reports and allegations; the Borough should verify them through police call data, incident 
reports, patrol observations, and resident documentation. But the volume and specificity of the 
comments make clear that residents no longer feel safe using or living next to this park area.
The concern is not merely whether people should be allowed to play basketball. The concern is 
whether the courts, as currently managed and enforced, are still serving neighborhood families or 
whether they are functioning as a draw for activity that the park and neighborhood were not 
designed to absorb.
This is also not a brand-new concern. The Recreation Board discussed similar complaints in 2022: 
foul language, loud music, littering, alcohol/drugs, rule signage, police contact information, cameras, 
and possible rim removal. If similar issues are appearing again in 2026, the Board should treat this 
as a recurring management failure requiring a written plan, not a one-night resident complaint.
Requested action:
 Ask the Mayor and Police Chief to provide a written short-term enforcement plan for 
Wyomissing Hills Memorial Park, including patrol frequency, hours of concern, who residents 
should call, what to report, and what ordinance violations can be enforced.
 Publish a simple public reporting protocol for residents near the courts.
 Request monthly police-call data and public-safety updates for the Wyomissing Hills and Happy 
Hollow basketball-court areas during warm-weather months.
 Consider interim measures if issues continue, including time restrictions, temporary rim/net 
removal, additional signage, camera review, or other enforceable controls.
 Before spending capital funds to resurface or replace court facilities, conduct a resident survey 
and facilities-use review to determine whether basketball courts remain the best use of this 
neighborhood park space.
2. Recreation communications: families should not need five sources and a 
phone call
A separate resident concern is that recreation-event information is scattered and inconsistent. 
Parents should not have to compare a recreation guide, the Citizen newsletter, MyRec, Facebook 
events, facility-reservation calendars, and phone calls to Borough Hall to know whether a family 
event is happening.
This is especially important because the Borough now has a Recreation and Communications 
Director. The public record shows that the full-time position was expanded to include Borough 
informational updates on the website and social media, and Council later changed the title from 
Recreation and Marketing Director to Recreation and Communications Director. With that role 
comes an expectation of accurate, timely, centralized event information.

The June 16 Director report itself lists “Moonlight Swim | Swim Under the Stars” as “Friday, June 
29th – 8:00 to 10:00 PM.” June 29, 2026 is a Monday. The same report lists the July 2 Moonlight Swim, 
July 3 fireworks, and July 4 activities. Resident-provided screenshots also show event information 
spread across multiple public-facing channels, with some events appearing on one channel but not 
another.
Requested action:
 Publish one corrected official schedule for the July 2-4 Independence Day events and identify it
as the authoritative source.
 State publicly which platform is the official source of truth for recreation events: Borough
website, MyRec, Facebook, the recreation guide, the Citizen newsletter, or another source.
 Require a monthly communications checklist in the Recreation Director report: event name,
date/time/location, where it was posted, when it was posted, whether the MyRec calendar
matches the website and Facebook, and whether corrections were needed.
 When a public correction is made, document the correction date and update every official
channel, not just Facebook.
3. Recreation finances: “free” events still have public costs
The Recreation Director report includes a section labeled financials, but the public-facing report is 
mostly a list of revenues, registrations, rentals, donations, and sponsorships. It does not show a 
complete monthly event-level financial analysis. A separate finance report may exist, but the public 
packet and public discussion do not give residents a clear view of net event cost.
For each major event, the Board should be able to see revenue, direct expenses, sponsorships, 
donations, advertising costs, staff labor, Public Works labor, police/fire/EMS staffing, overtime, 
traffic-control costs, equipment use, and net taxpayer cost. That matters because residents just 
experienced a major tax increase and the Borough repeatedly discusses financial pressure. 
Recreation should not be exempt from ordinary budget discipline.
This is also connected to event advertising. The Board previously discussed large event advertising 
signs, and the minutes indicate that signs are “figured into” the Recreation budget. That is not 
enough. Residents should know what signs cost, whether they are reusable, whether they can be 
read safely from a vehicle, whether QR codes are usable, and whether advertising free events 
outside the immediate neighborhood creates traffic, parking, and public-safety costs that exceed the 
intended benefit.
The larger strategic question is simple: are neighborhood parks being managed primarily for nearby 
residents and Borough families, or are they being promoted as regional event destinations? If the 
Borough intentionally advertises free events to draw large crowds from outside the neighborhood, 
the Board should calculate the cost and the impact before doing so.
Requested action:
 Require an event-level budget-to-actual report for each major recreation event.
 Include direct costs, staff labor, Public Works labor, public-safety costs, sponsorship revenue,
donations, grant funding, and net taxpayer cost.
 Create a separate line item or report category for event signs, advertising, printing, graphics, and
related communications costs.

RESOLUTION NO.  2026-23     
A RESOLUTION OF THE COUNCIL OF THE BOROUGH OF 
WYOMISSING, BERKS COUNTY, PENNSYLV ANIA SUPPORTING 
DIRECT EMS REIMBURSEMENTS LEGISLATION 
 
WHEREAS, longstanding billing issues continue to plague our emergency medical 
providers in our county and throughout the Commonwealth; and 
WHEREAS, payments from insurance companies for emergency medical services 
rendered often go directly to the policy holder and not to the EMS provider; and 
WHEREAS, indirect payments by insurance companies lead to difficult collections and 
often times leads to uncollected bills; and 
WHEREAS, Act 84 of 2015 created a "Registry" to be maintained by the Department of 
Health, which out-of-network ambulance companies could select annually from which insurance 
carriers would be willing to receive reimbursement; and 
WHEREAS, when a 911 emergency medical services agency responds to a legitimate 
911 emergency call and renders emergency medical services to a patient covered by a health 
insurance policy that includes this coverage as a benefit, network membership should not be a 
hurdle to an ambulance company receiving due recompense for services provided; and 
WHEREAS, House Bill 1152 and Senate Bill 1342 amend the Insurance Company Law 
to repeal Act 84 and require insurance companies to reimburse EMS agencies for emergency 
medical services provided when dispatched by 911. 
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, the Borough of Wyomissing calls on the 
Pennsylvania General Assembly to pass House Bill 1152 and Senate Bill 1342 which repeals Act 
84 of 2015 and requires insurance companies providing coverage in the Commonwealth of 
Pennsylvania to reimburse ambulance companies directly for 911 emergency medical services 
provided regardless of whether the EMS agency is part of the insurance company’s network; and 
FURTHER BE IT RESOLVED, that a copy of this resolution be sent to our state 
representative, our state senator and the Pennsylvania State Association of Boroughs.
