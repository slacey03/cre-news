PERSONAL APPEALS, PART I  
 
 Scott Seibert, 111 N. 7th Street, stated that he would like no parking signs around his area.  
He complained that high school students are making it very difficult for residents to park.  Mr. 
Pepe acknowledged that Mr. Seibert reached out previously in April, and the matter was 
discussed at the Public Safety Committee.  The matter was referred back to the Public Safety 
Committee for further consideration. 
 
 Melissa Ballas, 618 Walnut Street, issued concerns regarding her neighbors.  She stated 
that she does not feel safe living next to the tenants in the attached home.  There is garbage all 
over, she has had issues with trespassing, threat, and harassment.  She stated that she has had 
rocks thrown at her, and that there is trash and high weeds throughout the property.  She has 
concerns about her family’s safety and there are patterns of escalation.  There have been multiple 
incidents where her family has been threatened and multiple calls to police by multiple 
neighbors.  She stated that she should not be afraid to live there in fear.  Mr. Pepe stated that, 
while most of these issues are police issues, there is a type of ordinance that can be considered to 
address rental properties with problem tenants.  This matter can be discussed at committee.

 Borough Council interviewed four (4) candidates to fill the remainder of the term vacated 
by Katherine Watt.  The term expires December 31, 2027.   
 
 Tim Roland moved to the Borough in 2013.  He lives at 749 Walnut Street.  He used to 
live in East Coventry Township, where he served on the Board of Supervisors for 7 years.  He 
was on the Regional Planning Committee as well.  He got involved in the Borough’s Joint 
Environmental Advisory Committee and participated in hydrant painting as well.  He owns a 
recycling business out of his house.  He is 61 years old and weening off of his business.  He 
wants to benefit the community.  When asked about dealing with controversy during his time on 
the Board of Supervisors, Mr. Roland stated that he was the 1st Independent on an all Republican 
Board.  A member would drag meetings out for 2 – 3 hours.  He stated that it took a lot of 
patience.  He also discussed some land development challenges he dealt with.  When asked about 
2 – 3 things he would improve, he stated that he would like to address traffic and pedestrian 
safety and calming strategies, as well as trail and parks connections, which make the town even 
more desirable.  Lastly, he stated that, like all communities, he’d like to address some of the 
noise issues, especially with oversized mufflers and truck brakes.  He stated that he’d like to be 
part of the EAC, public safety, and anywhere else he’s needed.  He’s already read our minutes 
and watched the meetings over the past year.  He is able to attend meetings, as he sets his own 
schedule.   
 
 LeaAnn Buchman was born and raised in the Borough.  Her great grandfather owned the 
original Public Works garages.  She has seen a lot of changes in town.  She wants to learn more 
about behind the scenes.  She has a 15-year-old daughter and wants to be more involved in the 
community.  She served on the Fire Board for 3 years and enjoys crafts and activities.  She 
knows a lot of people and would be a good asset.  When asked about how many meetings she has 
attended, Mrs. Buchman stated that she has watched a few online.  She has been to 10 – 12 
meetings over the years.  When asked what she believed was the biggest challenge for the 
community over the next 5 – 10 years, she responded that she feels that the population growth is 
a concern.  The Borough has experienced a lot of development, but we don’t have enough roads 
to support it.  She is concerned with the safety of the town, as she has seen a difference in town.  
When asked what she believed were the hot issues of her neighbors, Mrs. Buchman stated that 
she didn’t believe they had any hot issues, but all have safety concerns.  The environment 
downtown has changed.  Everyone around her are renters.  When asked what changes could be 
made to make a difference, Mrs. Buchman stated that the population and development of the 
land could change.  Every free lot is an apartment.  She wants to keep Emmaus wonderful.  She 
appreciates that the Borough is always open to hearing the community.  She also stated that she 
works full-time.  When asked what one single issue she has researched in detail, Mrs. Buchman 
stated that she has not researched any single issue.  She has watched the meetings and she 
appreciates that we take care of the issues that are brought up at meetings.  When asked about 
her guiding principles, Mrs. Buchman stated that it depends on what the vote is on.  They will 
always be honest and will always think about the Borough in every vote.  When asked why she 
applied, Mrs. Buchman stated that she saw it on Facebook several times and thought about it.  
She would be excited to learn about what everybody does.  Mrs. Buchman was asked about her 
relative that works for the Borough.  She stated that she would do what’s best for the Borough 
and that she has no problem keeping confidentiality.  She will be able to attend meetings as long

 Donna Bok is a proud grandmother.  She is also a bookkeeper.  She retired in 2000 as an

 Kyle Vogeler moved to the Borough in 2021 with his wife.  He has 2 young daughters.  
He is a Penn State University graduate and attended Southern Lehigh School District.  He is a

investor, as he owns 110 units, none of which are close to the Borough.  He buys underdeveloped

would be a great opportunity to make an impact here.  He stated that he should have the time to

Mr. Vogeler stated that his number one issue is sidewalks and roads, as they are terrible with

nerd” and “history nerd”.  He would have an emphasis on increasing education and preserving 
history.  When asked about any potential conflicts, Mr. Vogeler stated that he doesn’t invest in 
the area, as his closest property is 4 hours away.  He would vote on matters without emotion.  He 
issued concerns about sidewalks, traffic on North Street and noise.  When asked how he would 
get up to speed, Mr. Vogeler stated that he would speak with every Council member.  When 
asked about the “old town” vs. “new town” philosophy, Mr. Vogeler warned that simply trying 
to stop development is bad.  He stated that we can still preserve our history by requiring a certain 
look and standard.  When asked about availability, Mr. Vogeler stated that he is available every

SPECIAL PRESENTATIONS  
 
 Allison Hudak and Marian Schneck presented a proposal to Borough Council to install a 
metal roof on the 1803 House.  Ms. Schneck stated that the Historical Commission unanimously 
voted to recommend the proposal.  She stated that the Friends of the 1803 House have raised 
over $51,000 for the project.  She stated that metal roofs existed in 1803.  She stated that the 
Commission had previously suggested examining synthetic shake shingles, since the original 
roof was made from cedar shake shingles.  Ultimately, the Friends of the 1803 House voted for a 
standing seam metal roof and made the recommendation to the Commission.  The cost for the 
metal roof is $51,000.   
 
 Mr. Labenberg stated that he supports a metal roof for the building.  A metal roof will 
protect the building longer.  He stated that he likes the synthetic look, but he understands the 
reason for the metal roof, and the organization raised the funds for the project.  Mr. DeFrain 
stated that he researched metal roofs during the time period as well.  Metal roofs were typically 
installed on wealthy family owner’s homes at the time.  He questioned if the mission is to keep 
the integrity of the historical look.  Ms. Hudak stated that the front of the house is federal style.  

63 
 
It's not necessarily original, but progressive.  Ms. Schneck stated that about 20% of the funds 
were raised through See My Legacy, but the platform definitely reached a wider audience that 
sent funds to the organization.  Mr. Brown questioned if the organization had the full $70,000 to 
install a synthetic roof, perhaps they would change their minds.  Ms. Schneck stated that the 
organization is committed to a metal roof, because the synthetic is not real. 
 
 Motion by Mr. Labenberg, seconded by Mr. Balliet, to approve the Friends of the 
1803 House to install a metal roof on the 1803 House.  There were 7 ayes.  Motion carried.
