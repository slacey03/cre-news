PUBLIC HEARING ON VACATION OF PERKIOMEN STREET
Mr. DeFrain stated a Public Hearing regarding the vacation of Perkiomen Street will take 
place prior to the Council Meeting and referred to Attorney Ulrich to run the hearing.
SOLICITOR COMMENTS
Attorney Ulrich stated that this is a public hearing with respect to the street vacation 
petition of CSW, 300 Furnace Street Emmaus LP also known as Iron Furnace Foundry Flats. He 
explained that the petition is to vacate a portion of Perkiomen Street and the application was 
received on May 4, 2026. Attorney Ulrich stated that the property address was 300 Furnace 
Street with property ID 549474474892 1. Attorney Ulrich recognized Attorney McLean as the 
applicant’s legal counsel.
Attorney Chris McLean, Fitzpatrick, Lentz, and Bubba, confirmed that the Public 
Hearing is a continuance of the July 20, 2026, Public Hearing. Attorney Ulrich responded 
affirmatively.  Attorney McLean inquired on the necessity to repeat his case for requesting the 
vacation of a portion of Perkiomen Street. He stated that his previous statements are included in 
the transcripts and are part of the record. Attorney McLean explained that he and his client are in
attendance to answer any questions. Attorney Ulrich asked Attorney McLean if anything has 
changed since the submission of their application requesting the vacation. Attorney McLean 
responded that there have been no changes. 
Attorney Ulrich inquired if Council had any questions for Attorney McLean in regard to 
the request for vacation. He then asked if any members of the audience or anyone who had 
indicated that they wish to be an objector had any questions for Attorney McLean.

Attorney Carly Sebia stated that she was in attendance on behalf of Christopher and 
Barbara Romig, owners of 450 S 2nd Street. She inquired if the proper procedure had been 
followed. Attorney Sebia stated that it is their stance that the petition is procedurally deficient 
under Section 1732.A of the Borough Code. She explained that the statute requires a petition of a
majority in interest and in number of abutting property owners. Attorney Ulrich explained that 
the percentage of ownership by the petitioner is 53.87% of street frontage. Attorney Sebia 
responded that the amount was not enough and that there needed to be more than one abutting 
property owner included in the petition.
Attorney Sebia stated that should the vacation be granted, they would request that all 
recorded plans and public documents clearly state that the vacation is subject to the private 
easement rights held by Mr. and Mrs. Romig and the other abutting property owners.  Attorney 
Sebia stated that she understands that the Borough can vacate the street on its own volition.
Attorney Ulrich stated that the Borough has little interest in Perkiomen Street since it can 
no longer be activated or used because the plot plans have gone unused for more than 21 years. 
He explained that the abutting property owners will obtain half of the ownership right via quiet 
title action.
Becky Bradley, Executive Director, Lehigh Valley Planning Commission, explained 
Pennsylvania Municipal Code 304 states that the Lehigh Valley Planning Commission has the 
right to review the street vacation proposal as an adviser to the Borough prior to action by 
Council. 
Mr. DeFrain questioned the difference for the Borough if the vacation was handled as 
Attorney Sebia requested, by having the Borough initiate the vacation of the street rather than the
petitioner. Attorney Ulrich stated the only difference is procedural. Attorney Ulrich explained the
procedural differences and the outcome of each potential action. Mr. Labenberg inquired about 
the cost to residents and requested acting in the best interest of the residents with the least cost to
taxpayers. Attorney McLean stated that he and his client would like a decision that evening. Mr. 
DeFrain asked what the standard is for vacating streets. Attorney Ulrich referred to Mr. Pepe 
regarding codified Ordinances vacating paper alleys. Mr. Pepe stated that currently there are 
more than 400 paper alleys within the Borough and that the Borough has vacated alleys in the 
past, however, it is not a recurring occurrence. Attorney Sebia stated that her clients would like 
the law to be followed.  Attorney Ulrich stated that this will simply delay the vote, however, 
Council can readvertise and vote again. 
Ms. Bradley requested the application be sent to the Lehigh Valley Planning Commission for 
review at their next Commission meeting.
