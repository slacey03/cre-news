Presentations:  
G. Terpstra – Environmental Advisory Council (EAC) Presentation. 
G. Terpstra presented the results of an EAC survey that received 76 responses from Township residents 
regarding environmental concerns and development priorities. She explained that the EAC is a volunteer 
advisory group that assists the Township Supervisors with environmental research, policy 
recommendations, grant opportunities, and public input. 
The survey results showed that residents’ primary concerns include water quality, wildlife habitat 
preservation, air quality, open space preservation, and noise pollution. Respondents also expressed 
strong opposition to data centers, sludge and biosolids applications on agricultural land, and industrial 
growth, while indicating support for agricultural growth. Popular outdoor activities included gardening, 
walking, hiking, birdwatching, and wildlife observation. Although the survey had a limited number of 
responses, the EAC noted that the findings provide useful insight into community priorities. Terpstra 
offered the EAC’s assistance with future research, studies, surveys, and environmental policy 
recommendations. Chairman Ascani thanked the EAC for the presentation. 
 
J. Mauser – Tour of Data Centers update and sound studies. Supervisor Mauser provided an 
overview of Talen’s Martins Creek and Lower Mount Bethel power plants along the Delaware River, 
which supplies water to approximately 14.2 million people. He reviewed the plants’ operating units, 
fuel sources, and combined generating capacity of 2.24 gigawatts. 
 
 
 

 
 
 
J. Mauser also presented preliminary noise measurements taken at various locations using a 
calibrated sound-level meter in response to residents’ concerns. Readings varied based on plant 
operations, location, and other noise sources. Martins Creek Units 3 and 4 were identified as louder 
than the newer Lower Mount Bethel facility. J. Mauser noted that the noise measurements were 
preliminary and not intended for legal use. He will discuss potential noise mitigation measures with 
Talen, conduct additional nighttime noise surveys, and research the court case and agreements 
related to the plant’s construction. He will report his findings at the November 2026 meeting. 
The proposed data center tour discussion was tabled for further research.

Old Business  
1.  Farmland Preservation Policy – P. McInerney presented the Open Space Committee’s proposal to use 
Earned Income Tax (EIT) funds for secondary, zoning -neutral farmland appraisals to ensure fair 
preservation payments. The proposal would cover appraisal costs and differences in property values, 
with landowners reimbursing appraisal costs if they chose not to preserve their  land. Legal review is 
needed to determine whether the proposal conflicts with the existing Open Space resolution. EIT funds 
cannot be used to retroactively increase payments for completed preservation agreements. 
 

 
 
 
 
MOTION: J. Mauser moved, seconded by D. Ascani, to table the proposal pending Solicitor Mark Fisher’s 
legal review of the existing Open Space ordinance/resolution. Vote: 5-0. 
During public comment, K. Brewer criticized the “90/10” act, stating that its restrictions negatively affect 
farmers. He asked the Board to determine how many other Pennsylvania Townships have similar 
restrictions. 
2.  Gravel Hill Enterprises (GHE) request to modify hours of operation  Gerald Domenico, owner of 
Gravel Hill Enterprises was present requesting to change operating hours to start and end one hour

current request to simply shift the existing hours. Solicitor Fisher mentioned A 2015 -2016 court order 
and agreement explicitly define the current operating hours and prohibit operations outside those times 
and advised that any change would require court approval and notification of prior objectors.  
D. Ascani mentioned he went on a tour of the property with J. Mauser and M. Mastrogiovanni. The 
property is cleaned-up and the owner has been cooperating with issues such as trucks going the wrong

Gravel Hill Road. The account balance is $75,110. An initial $30,000 was set aside for legal expenses, 
leaving a current balance of approximately $45,000 generated from operations. It was clarified that the 
proposed time change would not increase the contractually agreed-upon number of truck trips. MOTION 
by D. Ascani was made to proceed with the court approval process for the time change, contingent on 
Gravel Hill Enterprises covering all associated attorney fees. MOTION died due to a lack of a second.
