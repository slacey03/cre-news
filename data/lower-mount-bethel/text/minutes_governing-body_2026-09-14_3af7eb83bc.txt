Public Hearing – Adoption of Ordinance 2026-01 
Solicitor Uldrich clarified that the ordinance sets foundational regulations for potential future data 
centers, no plan has yet been submitted. This Ordinance may be amended later. 
D. Ascani noted  the public comment rules: there is a three-minute limit and the public comment is 
for Township Residents and taxpayers only.  
MOTION by J. Mauser, second by C. Cook to adopt Ordinance 2026-01. 
Public Comments on the Data Center Ordinance 
1. Raymond Dilione: The board received a formal objection outlining 27 deficiencies from 
Denise Webster that expressed her concerns that adopting the proposed ordinance would bind 
the Township to its current form if an application were submitted. He suggested a three-month 
delay to revise the proposed Ordinance. 
2. Dominic Buscemi: The data center offers no public value and will sap resources, raise 
electricity bills, use excessive water, and cause job loss to AI. Urged the board to stand up for 
the community. 
3. Todd Rehrig: Applauded the board for bringing the ordinance to a vote and hoped for its 
approval, with a commitment to proactively create amendments as technology changes. 
4. Gary Dorfner: Stated that a delay would be a serious mistake and advocated for passing the 
proposed ordinance as is. 
 

 
 
 
5. Adelise Bruen: Expressed concerns about the impact of sound and vibrations on livestock, 
wildlife, habitats, and water quality. 
6. Julie Bruen: Described existing noise issues from the nearby power plant, which have 
worsened. Voiced concerns about additional data center noise affecting property values, private 
wells, and resident health. 
7. Kevin Swanson: Reiterated concerns about power plant noise and asked if the proposed 
ordinance protects property values. Solicitor Ulrich explained that a future plan must comply 
with the subdivision and land development ordinance (SALDO). 
8. Catherine Buehler: Questioned the board's decision not to adopt a curative amendment, which 
would have provided 180 days to work on the ordinance. 
9. Rod Ruland: Current decibel readings are 61-63 dBA near the Talon power plant, questioned 
how the ordinance's lower limits (40-55 dBA) could be enforced. Another resident three miles 
away can hear the plant and fears a data center will be louder. 
E. Richards advocated for passing the ordinance to establish protections immediately rather than 
having not having any guidelines for another three months. 
The MOTION to adopt Ordinance 2026-01 was approved with a 5-0 vote. 
D. Ascani directed the Planning Commission to review all public comments and input from the Lehigh 
Valley Planning Commission.
