Farmland Preservation Policy - Ryan Shaw, Northampton County Farmland Preservation
Administrator, reported that the county's second application round was approved on October 6, 2026.
Lower Mount Bethel had four qualified applications covering 207 acres across four properties.
Appraised values are not available. The Board considered the Open Space Committee's proposal to use
Township Earned Income Tax {EIT) funds to bridge differences between appraisals and support
farmland preservation. A discussion was held regarding the Township's 9010 Ordinance, appraisal
methodology, state review, available funds, spending limits, and the need for a longer-term approach.
M. DeBerardinis noted that a $2,000-per-acre difference across 207 acres could amount to $414,000.
The Township’s financial figures and annual EIT revenue were discussed as estimates.

R. Shaw explained that appraisals must be prepared by Pennsylvania-licensed general real-estate
appraisers under applicable uniform standards. He described comparisons involving current zoning and
sales of unencumbered and preserved properties. He also said the largest discrepancy among the
seventeen appraisals received that year was $9,000: none in Lower Mount Bethel Township. Solicitor
Fisher advised that the policy expressly address reimbursement of the Township's second-appraisal
cost if an applicant withdraws after that appraisal. The Board discussed having a different appraiser
conduct a second appraisal and comparing properties with equivalent Townships. The proposal was
described as a temporary measure while the Township considers the longer-term effects of the 9010
Ordinance. MOTION by J. Mauser, second by D. Ascani to move forward with the policy to use EIT

Consider awarding the Sanitation Bid. The current sanitation contract will expire October 31, 2026.
Bids were received from Waste Management Inc., J.P. Mascaro and Sons, and Sanico Inc. The
specifications included automated and manual service, with weekly and every-other-week collection
options.

The Board discussed an October 19, 2026 letter from J.P. Mascaro raising bid-compliance concerns,
particularly about Sanico's proposed Warren County landfill in Oxford, New Jersey. The solicitor
focused on whether the proposed facility met the bid specification requiring a Pennsylvania DEP-
approved landfill. Sanico representatives said the Northampton County solid-waste plan permits
county-generated waste to go to permitted in-state or out-of-state facilities and requested time to
provide supporting documentation. J.P. Mascaro disputed whether Warren County, New Jersey is
among the facilities identified in the county plan.

The Board reviewed the bids received:

e Sanico's manual-service bid was cited as $574,080 annually, described by Waste Management
as about 12% above current costs.

e J.P. Mascaro's 2027 manual price was cited as $677,181, compared with an approximate
current cost of $511,000.

e Waste Management described its automated bid as the lowest responsive bid for automated
service at the current service level and said its price would be about 1.5% below its 2026 price
for 2027.

* Waste Management described automated collection as using a mechanical arm and compatible
carts. The specifications included 200 additional carts, 100 for trash and 100 for recycling.
Participants questioned whether the number would be sufficient; Waste Management said it
had carts available and would work with the Township.

e J.P. Mascaro advocated retaining manual collection and described its approach to extra
containers and recyclable materials. Sanico also described its manual collection and recycling
capabilities. .

operating expenses.

on like this; they need the support of the Township.

Call to Order — The October 26, 2026 Special Meeting of the Lower Mount Bethel Township Board
of Supervisors was held at 6984 S. Delaware Drive, Martins Creek, Pennsylvania, 18063. The meeting
was called to order by Chairman D. Ascani at 7:00 P.M. Also in attendance were M. Deberardinis, C.

there was an Executive Session held earlier tonight for potential litigation.

New Business — None.

Old Business

1. Sanitation bids - tabled from October 19, 2026. MOTION by C. Cook, second by J. Mauser to
approve the low responsible bid from J.P. Mascaro & Sons for a three year-contract for manual
trash pickup weekly/bi-weekly recycling. Contract service begins on November 1, 2026. Vote: 5-0.

2. 2027 Budget - The Board of Supervisors reviewed the proposed budget presented incorporating
an overall 2 mill general real estate tax increase. The increase will cover Lower Mount Bethel
Township Sandt’s Eddy Fire Company’s operating expenses of $157,363.77. There will be no
increase in the Fire Tax. The remaining funds from the adjustment were balanced into municipal
building maintenance and a used vehicle for the office. D. Ascani asked if any funds were allocated
for the Roadmaster pay. M. Mastrogiovanni confirmed no funds were set aside for the
Roadmaster position.

J. Mauser explained the Township needs to fully fund the operating expenses of the Fire Company,
they shouldn’t be spending 50% of their time fundraising. The final draft budget for November 2,
2026 is to be prepared with a 2 mill increase incorporating the Sandt’s Eddy Fire Company’s
budgeted general expenses in the amount of $157,363.77 for Monday, with possible
advertisement for the mandatory 20 day public inspection period.

Public Comment — Catherine Buehler asked for explanation of the 2 mill increase. J. Ulrich
explained that the 2 mill increase is for the General real estate tax increase, not the Fire Tax.

Correspondence -— All correspondence are located in the Township office.

Approval of the Bills - MOTION by J. Mauser, second by D. Ascani to pay the bills in the amount of
$4,380.99. Vote: 5-0.
