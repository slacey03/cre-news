Board Of Supervisor’s Public Hearing Minutes
Monday, August 17, 2026 @ 7:00 p.m. Mount Bethel Fire Hall
2341 N Delaware Dr, Mt Bethel, PA 18343

CALL TO ORDER - The August 17, 2026 Public Hearing of the Lower Mount Bethel Township
(LMBT) Board of Supervisors was held at the Mount Bethel Fire Hall, 2341 N Delaware Dr, Mt
Bethel, PA 18343. The meeting was called to order by Chairman D. Ascani at 7:00 P.M. Also in
attendance were: M. DeBerardinis, C. Cook, J. Mauser, S. Mack, stenographer, M. Fisher,

M. Mastrogiovanni and J. Lemak. There were approximately 200 plus attendees in the audience.
The Pledge of Allegiance was recited. D. Ascani turned the public hearing over to M. Fisher,
Solicitor.

Proposed Ordinance #2026-01. M. Fisher noted for the record that the Public Hearing was
advertised, as required in the Morning Call on August 3, 2026, and August 10, 2026 and the draft
Ordinance was sent to the LVPC for review. The Solicitor stated that the purpose of tonight’s
hearing is to discuss whether the ordinance should be adopted in its current form or be revised
and is limited to this topic. The Township has not received a formal application for Land
Development. He further stated that the Board of Supervisors will not be voting on this tonight,
but this will be on the agenda to be potentially adopted at the September 14, 2026, Board of
Supervisors regular meeting. There will be three minutes per person allotted for public
comment. Each person speaking will receive a thirty-second warning when they are
approaching the end of their three-minute time limit. If a comment or concern has already been
expressed, the next speaker may choose not to repeat the same point. There will be no back-
and-forth discussion with the Supervisors during public comment. Everyone that signs in will
‘have the opportunity to speak, and he requested that speakers are respectful of the time they
are allotted.

M. Fisher read the proposed ordinance; An ordinance of the Township of Lower Mount Bethel,
Northampton County, Pennsylvania, amending Chapter 27 (Zoning) of the code of Lower Mount
Bethel, providing for the regulation of digital infrastructure facilities, hyper digital infrastructure
facilities and digital infrastructure accessory uses; providing definitions, conditional use
requirements, and performance standards and related regulations. He stated the August 14,
2026 Lehigh Valley Planning Commissions review letter with recommendations shall be
incorporated into the record. M. Fisher read correspondence from Marge Holmes dated August
3, 2026, and correspondence from Marie Perkowski dated August 13, 2026, which shall be
incorporated into the record. He further commented that a twenty-one-page letter with
exhibits from Wester Law Office, a thirty-five-page memo from E. Schock & G. Davis Esq., which
was received today and an email from J. Guest, which all will be entered into the record.

Public Comment — The following residents gave public comment at the Public Hearing:
1. Cosimo Buscemi-— Mr. Buscemi noted multiple Townships turned down data centers, referred
to the green amendment, and suggested a total ban of data centers in LMBT.

2. John Callahan, Peron Development — Mr. Callahan noted that Peron Development’s goal is to
pursue the proper process to bring the data center to the Township. He explained that the
project could be accommodated within the current Industrial Zone, with a portion of the
Agricultural District to be revised. Mr. Callahan further explained that the proposed data center
would be located south of Martins Creek Belvidere Highway. The farmland would remain


Agricultural. He also stated that the proposed data center would utilize a closed loop cooling
system.

3. Sandra Newman — Ms, Newman thanked the Planning Commission for their timely work on
the proposed Ordinance. She also stated that she would like the Township to slow the
developers down to allow sufficient time to update the Zoning Ordinance to address and
incorporate provisions for data centers.

4. Lorraine England — Ms. England expressed concerns regarding potential pollutants associated
with data centers, stating that even small amounts of pollutants could pose a cancer risk. She
also raised concerns regarding groundwater remediation and potential decreases in property
values. She requested that the Supervisors establish a trust to provide compensation to the
residents for potential financial losses resulting from decreased property resale values. She
further requested that the Board consider prohibiting data centers from locating within the
Township.

5. Rob Kucharcezck — Mr. Kucharczck raised concerns regarding the water usage and stormwater
run-off, and their potential effects on the Delaware River. He stated that he values the quiet
nature of the community, and requested the Township not rush creating the Ordinance. He also
noted that, in his view, there are currently no studies demonstrating the potential side effects
of data centers.

6. Matthew Taylor — M. Taylor stated that he considers the proposed Ordinance to be a good
first step. However, he expressed concerns that data centers could decrease property values
and questioned the overall benefit of a data center for LMBT. He stated that, in his opinion, the
primary financial benefit would go to the technology companies rather than the Township and
its residents.

7. Denise Wester — D. Wester stated that she is an attorney and homeowner just off DePues
Ferry Road. She expressed concern that the proposed Ordinance is not sufficiently restrictive
and contains too many potential loopholes. She further mentioned that Upper Mount Bethe!
Township (UMBT) is located nearby and suggested that the two Townships coordinate and
conduct joint studies.

8. Raymond Dilione — R. Dilione stated that he lives right across the street from the proposed
data center site. He expressed his concerns that the proposed Ordinance contains numerous
potential loopholes and does not include provisions addressing potential increases in local
energy costs. He noted that other municipalities have expressed significant increases in energy
costs and is concerned that similar impacts could occur within the Township. He is also
concerned regarding the water usage associated with data centers. He referenced the quarry
clean-up and is concerned about the ingress and egress associated with the quarry and its
potential relationship to the potential data center.

9. Dominick Buscemi — Mr. Buscemi referenced the Pennsylvania State Constitution and stated
that in his view, protection of the environment should take priority. He also mentioned that
pensions and taxes are used to fund data centers.

10. Joann Walz — Ms. Walz indicated she lives in Hillendale and 50+ homes are on one well
system.

11. Melanie Thiel — M. Thiel stated she agrees with everything discussed in the meeting and
added that she feels there are a lot of loopholes in the proposed Ordinance. She questioned


how noise levels and wells would be monitored and stated that she doesn’t want the lownship
to turn into Forks or Nazareth with large warehouses.

12. Donald Dafmao — Mr. Dalmao stated he is proud of this Township. He noted his concern
with noise decibels at the property line and this should be included in the ordinance.

13. Todd Rehrig — Mr. Rehrig stated that he applauds the Planning Commission for completing
the draft Ordinance. He acknowledged there are loopholes in the draft Ordinance but felt that
it would be better to pass a faulty ordinance than to continue waiting. Mr. Rehrig discussed
concerns regarding property values and hopes that they will adopt an ordinance that protects
the Township.

14. Annette Van Rossum — Ms. Van Rossum read the Green Amendment and asked if the
Supervisors will uphold the constitution.

15. Georgianna Frostick ~ Ms. Frostick stated that UMBT cancelled their meeting scheduled for
this evening until next Friday due to a large crowd. Ms. Frostick also mentioned she has 35
years’ experience in the technology industry and has visited more than 1,000 data centers. She
expressed concerns regarding water usage and the need to protect livestock from data centers.

16. Kenneth Brewer — Mr. Brewer mentioned he has lived in LMBT all his life. He noted that the
Supervisors represent all residents of LMBT and should consider the best interest of the entire
community. Mr. Brewer commented that Mr. Callahan gave a nice presentation. He expressed
concern that a particular interest group is not receptive to the opinions of others and noted that
artificial intelligence will continue to advance. He also apologized for becoming a little out of
hand at the previous meeting.

17. Matt Hackenburg — Mr. Hackenburg mentioned UMBT infrastructure would run new
transmission lines through LMBT. The Planning Commission did a good job preparing the
proposed ordinance. He emphasized the importance of knowing who the end user of the
potential data center will be, whether it will be Google, Microsoft or Amazon.

18. Kevin Swanson — Mr. Swanson mentioned he feels that there are loopholes in the proposed
Ordinance and has numerous grey areas. He suggested that the Township speak with other
municipalities that have opposed data center developments to understand why their efforts
were unsuccessful and learn from their experience.

19. Ron Angle — Mr. Angle indicate he has no position on data centers. He mentioned that the
passage of 9010 has contributed to problems facing the Township today. He noted that the
average farmer is struggling with the rising cost of fertilizer and low appraisals. He further stated
that the Township has taken 90% of the farmers’ land.

20. Bridget Folts — Ms. Folts indicated she lives in Belvidere N.J. but believes she will still feel the
negative impact of a data center in the Township. She expressed her concerns regarding the
protection of drinking water and the potential for companies to establish operations and then
leave the community with a vacant building. She stated that she would like to see the proposed
Ordinance passed.

21. Nicole Chiappa — Ms. Chiappa indicated she is a resident of Roseto and is an Environmental
Scientist. She described the proposed development as a massive infrastructure project that is
new to the community. She expressed her concerns with stormwater management, including
the potential risks associated with increased runoff and the lack of adequate areas for the water


to drain. She also noted that local ecosystems could be adversely aitected. Ms. Chippa urged
the Township to stand firm in the face of potential legal pressure and recommended an
environmental review.

22. Shane Savant — Mr. Savant noted UMBT and LMBT are separated only by a municipal
boundary and that he would object to LMBT changing the Zoning District from Agriculture to
Industrial. He noted that he moved to this area because of its rural characteristic and currently
lives in between LMBT and UMBT. Mr. Savant emphasized that the vote for this will affect
everyone lives.

23. Joe Guest - Mr. Guest thanked the Board of Supervisors for putting this together. He
suggested to not re-zone the land, because they spend years developing a Comprehensive Plan.
Agricultural land protects LMBT and its residents. Data centers do not belong in our Township.
Mr. Guest noted that he has not read one good thing about data centers. He suggested that the
proposed Ordinance specify that the noise levels do not exceed 0 decibels. He also raised
concerns regarding the size of industrial buildings permitted under the proposed Ordinance.

M. Fisher closed the Public Hearing at 8:30 P.M.

Chairman Ascani resumed the meeting and stated that he is not in favor of changing any Zoning
from Agricultural to industrial.

Payment of bills - MOTION by J. Mauser second by C. Cook to approve the bills in the amount
of $75,718.50. Vote: 4-0.

Public Comment — Gary Dorfner — recommended the proposed ordinance is passed because
having some protection is better than not having any and hopefully the Township can
~ accommodate the Lehigh Valey Planning Commission’s recommendations without resubmitting.

Adjournment — MOTION by J. Mauser second by C. Cook to adjourn the public hearing at 8:35 p.m.

Respectfully submitted,
Juli Lemak, Township Secretary / Planning Administrator
LMBT
