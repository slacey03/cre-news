Subdivision and Land Development Plans 
Bernville Business Center ingress and egress on site  
 
Mark Koch on behalf of Bernville Business Center, along with Brian Johnson from Kinsley 
Construction, Matt Close from Bernville Business Center, Steve Walsh and Kevin Savage from 
Dynamic Engineering are here to provide information and discussion regarding the ingress and 
egress for the Bernville Business Center.  This information comes from the review letter dated 
February 20, 2025, particularly comment # 5.  This letter indicated that a Use Variance was 
needed with respect to the warehouse use.  After working through numerous variations for a 
solution a Variance Request was submitted.  Atty. Cleary clarified that the Townships position 
initially was the property was two lots and the plan that was initially submitted maintained two 
(2) lots even after the warehouse was developed.  The only use on the lot in the residential 
district was a driveway and stormwater basin, the only use on the lot is principal use and it took a 
while, but as of December, these two parcels have been combined into a single lot.  
 
A suggestion was made by the Township to use Route 183 as the primary access to keep the 
traffic off of Leiscz’s Bridge Road.  Review of potential access onto Route 183, would be from a 
small sliver of property, of which two issues were found; one is geometric and the second is 
operational.  Regarding the geometric issue, this property is only 100 feet wide, and access itself 
would be larger than the 24 feet currently proposed and with the radii that is required to facilitate 
the truck access to and from 183 the actual curb cut would be in excess of 150 feet so this would 
be wider than the length of the frontage.  And the second issue being operational; an analysis 
found that access would operate with very poor level of service.  In impact traffic terms, Level of 
Service is how an intersection performs on a scale from A to F and found the southbound 
movement onto Route 183 would operate at a Level F, which is not permitted or recommended 
by PennDOT.  Mr. Savage discussed it informally with Mr. Boyer from PennDOT and they both 
expressed similar concerns about the access required to facilitate truck access into and out of the 
property as well as the potential impact to the adjacent properties on Route 183.  Ms. Burkovich 
inquired about the ingress and egress effects on Leiscz’s Bridge Road.  Mr. Savage stated there 
will not be much impact, because of the business that already exists in this area.  The plan has 
always been to have access onto Leiscz’s Bridge Road, because acquiring access onto Route 183

Alternative Energy Ordinance discussion and recommendation 
 
Mr. Tricarico provided copies of the most recently revised Alternative Energy Ordinance. One 
subject of concern for this ordinance was to facilitate solar energy within Bern Township, and we 
felt that some of the written language of the revised ordinance was restrictive in not allowing the 
commercial alternative energy system to be used in the majority of our zoning districts.  
Obviously in some of the districts it does not make sense to have commercial levels, which is 50 
kilowatts or bigger by our definition in a residential area. But the concern is, this is being only 
allowed in (2) two industrial areas, which are about 400 acres of Bern Township within these 
zoning districts.  AB and RR are two of the larger zoning districts, with almost 10 times more 
acreage than what is in these two industrial districts; we felt landowners within these two 
residential districts should have the opportunity to be able to use their properties to generate 
electricity for sale.  Fortunately, districts AB and RR already have a lot coverage restriction 
which means you cannot have more than 10% of the lot having a structure on it and solar panels 
are considered by definition in the ordinance as a structure.  The Planning Commission is aware 
of the concerns the Supervisors had in regard to the setback distances for the installation of the 
solar structures.  There was additional lengthy discussion regarding setback distances regarding 
what is considered “centrally located” off road and parcel boundaries and this needs to be well 
defined. Mr. Becker made a motion to approve the 100-foot setback from a road and 150-foot 
setback from any adjacent property lines, seconded by Mr. Colflesh.   Motion carried.
