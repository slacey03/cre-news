Marissa Loeb from Schuylkill Valley Community Library – Ms. Loeb provided a Summer 
Learning Program Report along with the 2025 report to show how much the numbers have 
increased in 1 year.  Ms. Loeb highlighted some upcoming events.  One event she wanted to 
touch on is the “Rally for the Ramp” campaign.  The ADA ramp currently at the library needs to 
be replaced.  The library has secured a $16,000.00 grant though the Berks Community 
Foundation and is looking for support from the community for any added expenses. Ms. Loeb 
also provided the 2026 Funding Formula and thanked Bern Township for their continued 
financial support.   
 
Mike Worley, Bern Township Planning Commission- Mr. Worley read a section from the 
Planning Commission meeting minutes from July 2026, with regards to not allowing AB & RR 
districts to install commercial solar panels, which the Planning Commission does not agree with.  
The primary reasons stated is to not allow commercial solar within these districts are primarily 
focused on aesthetics, but this may not be a valid agreement, since the proposed ordinance does 
state screen previsions. The Planning Commission still respectfully recommends that the Board 
of Supervisors include both the AB & RR district as allowable areas for installation of 
commercial solar energy systems.  Mrs. Reed read what a variance is, which indicates that 
anyone could apply for variance for solar panels.  Mr. Potts also stated when he received the 
review letter from the Berks County Planning Commission, he asked the reviewer if it was 
appropriate to locate large commercial solar panels in AB & RR districts, and the response from 
the reviewer was No.  The reviewer felt solar should not be near prime agriculture lands, or 
residential areas.   
 
Steve Tricarico, Bern Township Planning Commission - Mr. Tricarico knows one of the main 
concerns for the solar panels is the way they look.  Mr. Tricarico provided information showing 
two (2) very large solar panel fields that already exist and because of the way they are sited it 
makes it difficult to know they exist. Mr. Tricarico also wanted to point out that solar panels can 
be installed on the roofs of buildings and not just installed on the ground.  When he spoke with 
the Executive Director of Berks County Department of Agriculture regarding solar panels and 
their impact and questioned about putting solar panels on roofs in what is considered “forever 
farm” and found out that there is no impact because it does not take away from the agricultural 
land.  This could potentially alleviate the visual site of solar panels. Mr. Tricarico also made his 
last point, that if only 39 of our major farms would have a chicken house on their property, and 
would install solar panels on that chicken house, this produces enough energy to satisfy 3 times 
the amount of our residential consumption in our Township.

PUBLIC HEARING 
 
Alternative Energy Ordinance - Atty. Mooney stated that today was date and time advertised 
for the Public Hearing on the Alternative Energy Ordinance which amends the Bern Township 
Zoning Ordinance.  Public notice was in the Reading Eagle on August 19 and August 24 of 2026 
and was posted at the Township Building.  The Ordinance was also available for inspection by 
the community.  The Ordinance, if passed this evening would amend the Bern Township Zoning 
Ordinance by providing rules and regulations for the sighting and locating of alternative energy 
systems, primarily solar within the Township for accessory use and commercial. This Ordinance 
added some definitions to the Bern Township Zoning Ordinance, unrelated to the alternative 
energy systems.  Amended the definition of “accessory building and group home” and created a 
definition for “grazing animals.” Atty. Mooney asked the audience if there were any questions or 
comments.  Let the record show there were no questions or comments from the audience. Atty. 
Mooney asked if the Board of Supervisors had any additional questions or comments, with none, 
Atty. Mooney closed the Public Hearing.  Dr. Wagner made a motion to close the public hearing, 
seconded by Mr. Thompson.   Dr. Wagner also made a motion to accept the Alternative Energy 
Ordinance, seconded by Mr. Thompson YES: Dr. Wagner, Mr. Eveland, Mr. Thompson and Mrs. 
Reed.  NO: Mrs. Stork. Motion carried.

Discussion/Approval of Reading One Three, LLC Conditional Use Agreement -Atty. 
Mooney explained there was a Conditional Use Hearing for the Reading One Three, LLC 
proposed warehouse at 1067 West Leesport Road.  The Board of Supervisors issued a 
Conditional Use decision denying their request to increase the height of the proposed warehouse 
from 35 feet to 45 feet.  They filed an appeal with the Court of Common Pleas. There was an

Condition.  The Township has the option tonight whether to accept these 32 Conditions or not.

regarding many of the topics in this document. Mrs. Reed asked the audience for their feedback 
and simplified the question at hand; do we accept 35 feet with no conditions or 45 feet with 
conditions?  Ms. Burkovich asked if there could be a weight limit put on West Leesport Road.  
Mr. Potts did reply that this would be very difficult to enforce.  Mr. Thompson made motion to 
reject the offer of settlement put forth by the developer, seconded by Mrs. Stork.  YES: Mr. 
Eveland, Mrs. Stork, and Mrs. Reed.  NO: Dr. Wagner, Mr. Thompson.  Motion carried.
