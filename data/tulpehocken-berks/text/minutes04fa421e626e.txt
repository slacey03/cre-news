Tulpehocken Township 
Board of Supervisors 
 Meeting of August 12, 2026 
 
The Tulpehocken Township Board of Supervisors met on August 12, 2026, in the Township meeting 
room at 7:00 P.M.  In attendance and voting were Supervisors: Chairman Gary Deck, Vice Chairman 
Lester Feick and Harold Zimmerman.  Also present were Bryan Dronick, Police Chief; Kathy Judy, 
Township Secretary / Treasurer; and Atty. Christopher Hartman, Township Solicitor. 
Members of the audience included Roger Yiengst, Melvin Miller, Thomas Gruber, Susan Wolfe, Jeff 
Hahn, Bruce & Connie Bashore, Bill Dewart, Scott Klopp, Wilmer Hurst, Craig Hershey, Jordan Hurst, 
Mike Najarian and Bryant Semenza. 
CALL TO ORDER:  Chairman Deck called the meeting to order at 7:01 P.M.  The meeting continued 
with the pledge to the American Flag.   
PUBLIC COMMENTS:  Many members of the audience were present to share concerns about safety 
on Wintersville Road and talk about what could be done to help reduce speed and improve safety (e.g. 
speed lines, increased police presence, fines, repainting stop bars, additional chevrons, etc.)  
Supervisor Zimmerman made a motion to approve a traffic study for a stop sign at Deck Road and 
speed limits for the entire length of Wintersville Road within the Township - - seconded by Vice 
Chairman Feick and passed unanimously (3-0). 
 
A member of the audience reported that because there are no signs giving motorists advanced notice 
of the road closure on Route 419, vehicles, including tractor trailers, are driving right up to the work 
zone and are having to back up on the road and make tight turn-arounds.  The Roadmaster said he will 
call the State to request more detour ahead and/or road closed ahead signs be placed.  
 
Mike Najarian shared the latest statistics information and totals showing that the Bethel-Tulpehocken 
Public Library was nearly double the County average in both physical circulation and event 
attendance in 2025.  He also reported they had approximately 450 people attend their Summer 
Carnival this year. 
 
In response to an inquiry, it was explained that when a motion is made, if there is no conflict of 
interest, a Supervisor cannot abstain and must cast a vote for or against the motion.  
APPROVAL OF MINUTES: Supervisor Zimmerman made a motion to approve minutes from the July 
8, 2026 Board of Supervisors Meeting - - seconded by Vice Chairman Feick and passed unanimously 
(3-0).

- 20 Deck Road, Earl Ray Martin – The property went through Land Development in 2018 for a 
Poultry Operation.  A Waiver of Land Development and Drainage Plan has now been received for 
a proposed 12,600sf steer barn accessory structure.  In accordance with the Planning Commission

passed unanimously (3-0).  Stormwater and Zoning are still pending. 
Solicitor Legal Discussion:   
- Update Comcast Franchise Agreement – The Solicitor reported that according to the most recent

- Omnibus Traffic and Road Ordinance – Supervisor Zimmerman made a motion to enact 
Ordinance 2026-1; Traffic and Parking Ordinance - - seconded by Vice Chairman Feick and 
passed unanimously (3-0). 
 
There was a discussion about mobile phone service and dead zones becoming worse in some areas 
in the Township.  The Solicitor advised that complaints to the carriers can be made, but there aren’t 
any official or legal actions that can be made. 
Escrow Release:  None 
Zoning:  No update was received for the month of July. 
- Review Zoning Appeal Application for 106 Wintersville Road - The Zoning Hearing is August 20, 
2026; the application is for a dimensional variance from section 844.022 to allow a reduced side 
yard setback for a proposed 28’x35’x1-½ story garage to be situated 5 feet from the side lot 
boundary line.  After reviewing the application, project and the Planning Commission’s 
recommendation to the Zoning Hearing Board, Vice Chairman Feick made a motion to recommend 
to the Zoning Hearing Board that, if they were to approve the request, they should consider 
including the following conditions: - to have the property surveyed to assure they meet whatever the 
approved setback is; - to require that stormwater is directed away from the neighboring property, 
and - that no living quarters are permitted in the accessory structure - -  seconded by Supervisor 
Zimmerman and passed unanimously (3-0).

SEWER OPERATIONS: 
- Judgements and Delinquent Sewer Accounts.  The Board reviewed the report and also a letter from 
Teen Challenge advising that they intend on making weekly payments of $5,000 until their 
account’s outstanding balance is paid in full – Supervisor Zimmerman made a motion to accept Teen 
Challenge’s proposed payment schedule - - seconded by Vice Chairman Feick and passed 
unanimously (3-0). 
- 469 Godfrey Street – The Board reviewed an application to extend the waiver of Sewer Service fees 
since a 50% waiver granted last year expired on July 1, 2026.  Supervisor Zimmerman made a 
motion to approve the request and continue the 50% waiver of sewer service fees, effective July 1, 
2026 and good for up to one year or until the property is restored and no longer qualifies for relief - 
- seconded by Supervisor Zimmerman and passed unanimously (3-0). 
- Equipment Repairs and purchases, Vice Chairman Feick reported: 
• A pipe near the Lancaster Avenue Pump Station broke/blew out.  Dan Hershey is recommending 
that we install an air release valve to help prevent it from happening again.   
• On the panel at Market Street Pump Station - they were able to repurpose the original panel 
from the Lancaster Avenue pump station and install it here; it still needs a voltage regulator, but 
appears to be working.   
• At the Village Estates Pump Station the break away coupler that holds the two pumps has 
broken.  One of the pumps had to be pulled for repair, the spare was installed in its place and 
Slaymaker quoted $5,376.00 for installation of a new coupler.
