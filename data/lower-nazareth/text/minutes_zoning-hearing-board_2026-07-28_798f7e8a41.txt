HEARINGS
ZA2026-04  K&T  Property  Holding  Company,  LLC
The  advertisement  for  the hearing  was  read  aloud  and all  parties  who  would  testify  in  the hearing
were  sworn.  Present  for  the Application:  Applicant  Anthony  Collina  and Attorney  John  P.
Karoly,  III
Exhibit  Z-1 - Township  folder  containing  the original  application  and supporting  materials,
affidavit  of  publishing,  proof  of  posting  and certification  of  mailing  was requested  to be entered
into  the  record  by Seese. Karoly  asked  for  clarification  on the folders  contents  and  no objection.
Karoly  introduced  the application.  The applicant  is requesting  that  the proposed  use is not
defined  with  the Zoning  Ordinance  and  is asking  for  an interpretation.  Alternatively,  they  would
be seeking  a Special  Exception  or Variance  for  the  use.
Collina  provided  testimony.  K&T  Property  Holding  Company,  LLC  has owned  the property
since  March  of  2026  and  intends  to use the property  to house  inventory  and company  vehicles
for  Snap  On  Tools.
Exhibit  A  -  (3)  photographs  depicting  586  Nazareth  Pike.  The  building  is approximately  3,000 to
4,000  s.f. with  two  small  offices,  4 garage  bays  and parking.  It previously  housed  an auto  repair
shop.  Collina  painted  and  cleaned  the  building.  No  other  changes  are proposed.

Collina  owns  a Snap On  franchise,  which  is a mobile  tool  distributor  of  professional  hand  tools.
They  visit  places  of  business  requiring  professional  hand  tools  to sell and service  tools  on site.
No  sales  will  be conducted  on the property.
Exhibit  B -  photographs  showing  the interior  of  a Snap  On Truck.  Each Snap On  truck  contains
a work  center,  sales display,  sale computers  and  acts as a mobile  store.  All  sales occur  at the end
use location.
No  manufacturing  or repairs  of  equipment  will  take  place  on the property.  There  will  not  be a
retail  showroom.
The  business  currently  has three  employees,  including  Collina.  Hours  will  generally  be 7:00AM
to 4:00PM,  with  employees  working  off  site. Employees  will  load  vehicles  in the morning  and
return  at the end of  the workday.  No  outdoor  storage  is proposed.  The  trucks  will  be parked  in
the existing  garage  bays.
Exhibit  C -  photographs  showing  Snap  On  trucks  parked  inside  the garage
Collina  currently  operates  from  his  home  and  has outgrown  the space.
Snap  On  does  not  permit  retail  franchises  and  retail  stores  are not  permitted.
Neighboring  uses include  an auto  body  and repair  facility,  a dentist,  a diner,  an accountant,  a tree
service  and  a painting  company.  Collina  believes  the use fits  in with  the general  character  of  the
neighborhood.
The  prior  use of  the property  was  by Mark's  Automotive.  The  proposed  use will  not  generate
additional  traffic,  impact  neighbors  or hold  customer  gatherings
The  location  of  the property  is in  the  middle  of  the Snap  On service  area  and  is well  suited  to the
use.
If  denied,  Collina  expects  to rent  the property  out  for  another  auto body  and  repair  shop,  which
will  create  noise  and  traffic  in  the  neighborhood.
A Zoning  Use and Occupancy  application  was previously  submitted.  Seese categorized  the use
under  "Wholesale  Sales".
Nordahl  asked  how  the Snap On inventory  will  be replenished  and how  often  deliveries  are
received.  95% of deliveries  come  from  UPS and are placed  immediately  on the trucks.
Shipments  are dependent  on customer  orders  and  come  1 to 3 times  a week.  Collina  will  install  a
rack  of  shelving  inside  the garage  bays.
Nordahl  asked  if  inventory  is stockpiled.  Some  excess  tools  are kept  but  not  large  quantities.
Nordahl  noted  that  Appendix  B of  the original  application  states "customer  visits  expected  to be
extremely  infrequent  averaging  less than  1 visit  per week"  and  contradicts  the prior  testimony.
Collina  replied  that  he cannot  promise  a customer  will  never  arrive  at the site. Customers  will
never  be invited  to the site. Collina  aims  to keep  the business  low  key  and  avoid  theft.
Nordahl  asked  if  they  plan  to put  up a Snap On sign.  Collina  has already  replaced  the existing
sign  panel  with  a black  and white  sign  saying  "TTC"  for  Tony's  Tool  Crib.  There  will  be no
public  advertisement  of  Snap On. The  Snap  On  franchise  does not  permit  signs.

Reyes  asked  if  any tool  work  is done  at the property.  Small  repairs  are done  at the customer  site
and  otherwise  sipped  back  to Snap  On  for  repair.
McGinnis  noted  in Exhibit  C that  there  appears  to only  be room  for  one row  of  storage  shelves.
Collina  confirmed.
Reyes  asked  if  the side garage  door  will  be used.  It will  not  be utilized  at this  time  but  may  in the
future.
Reyes  asked  about  security  features.  Security  bars are placed  inside  all windows,  and the
building  is equipped  with  an alarm  system.
Nordahl  asked  if  any  vehicle  maintenance  is performed  on site. No  maintenance  is performed.
Nordahl  asked  about  weekend  hours.  Employees  may  opt  to enter  the shop  on the weekend,  but
there  are no set hours  of  operation.
Reyes  questioned  why  only  the first  line  of  the definition  of  "Wholesale  Sale"  was read. When
read  in entirety,  the definition  notes  selling  to industrial,  commercial  and  professional  business
users.  The  definition  seems  to meet  what  they  are doing.  Reyes  asked  for  the opinion  of  Karoly.
Karoly  replied  that  no selling  is engaged  on site and the definition  does  not  apply.  The  use was
compared  to that  of  a plumber.
Seese asked  for  clarification  on what  use they  are proposing  to meet.  Collina  replied  they  believe
they  meet  the definition  of  a Contractor  Office/Yard.  Seese asked  for  an explanation  on how  that
conclusion  was  made. Seese pointed  out  that  they  still  meet  the definition  of  Wholesale  Sales,  as
they  sell  to end  users.  Karoly  stated  that  the building  itself  is not  used  for  sales and is being  used
as a Contractor  Office/Yard.
Seese requested  that  the record  is made  very  clear  on what  the use is and  is not.  There  needs  to
be clarity  on what  the approved  use will  be to avoid  issues in the future  should  Snap On no
longer  occupy  the building  one day.
Cordts  suggested  adding  conditions  if  an approval  is granted.  Reyes  asked  if  they  would  agree  to
a condition  not  to perform  any sales on site.
Reyes  noted  that  a Contractor  is not defined.  Seese directed  the Board  to Zoning  Ordinance
Section 105.2. Use Not Specifically Regulated. There are five criteria items the applicant needs
to meet.
Crook  asked  what  would  happen  if  the business  outgrew  the site. The  property  allows  for  room
to expand,  specifically  space for  two  additional  trucks.
Statements  in support  of  the application  were  received  from  William  Newhatd  of  571 Nazareth
Pike,  Mark  Presby  of 1368 Bangor  Road (previous  owner  of the property  and Mark's
Automotive),  Bill  Hughes  of  592  Nazareth  Pike  and  Pat Hughes  of  592  Nazareth  Pike.
Board  Deliberation
Nordahl  called  for  an executive  session.  Motion  for  an executive  session  moved  by Reyes  and
seconded  by  McGinnis.  The  motion  carried  unanimously.  Executive  session  at 7:32PM.

Motion  by  the Board
Motion  by Reyes  to make  the determination  that  the proposed  use meets  the definition  of  a
Contractor Office/Yard and meets the criteria for Uses Not Specifically Regulated under
Ordinance  Section  105.2,  and the Board  grants  approval  of  a Variance,  with  the approval  based
upon  the unique  business  and operational  model  of  the use along  with  testimony  and evidence
provided,  and  with  the following  conditions:
1.  No  sales are made  on site
2. No  orders  are placed  on site
3. If  the  business  model  or operating  model  changes,  the applicant  must  return  to the Board
for  further  relief.
Seconded  by  McGinnis.  The  motion  carried  unanimously.
