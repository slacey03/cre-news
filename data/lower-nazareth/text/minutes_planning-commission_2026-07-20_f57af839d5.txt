McDonald's  Nazareth  Subdivision  & Land  Development  Preliminary/Final  Plan
Present  for  the Application:  Plan  Preparer  Justin  Klee  of  Bohler  Engineer
Klee  provided  background  on the project.  The parcel  will  be subdivided  from  the existing
shopping  center  while  remaining  under  the ownership  of  Laneco,  LP. The  applicant  will  require
zoning  relief  and  is only  looking  for  feedback  at this  time.
The proposed  McDonald's  is a 3,781 s.f. building  with  31 seats and 2 drive  thru  lanes. 35
parking  spaces are proposed,  along  with  site lighting,  a trash  enclosure  and street  trees.  The
existing  stub entrance  from  the shopping  center  parking  lot will  be utilized.  No direct  access
from  Route  248  is proposed.
Dulac  asked  for clarification  on where  the entrances  to the building  were.  Klee  provided  an
explanation  of  how  the parking  spaces  are intended  to be used  between  guests  and  employees.
Capecci  asked  for  clarification  on the flow  of  traffic  into  the site. Traffic  from  Route  248 will
free  flow,  while  traffic  within  the shopping  center  will  be stop controlled.
A  diversion  swale  and  underground  basin  are proposed  for  stormwater  management.
Coyle  reviewed  the July  17, 2026  Carroll  Engineering  review  letter.  No  exceptions  were  taken  to
the requested  waivers.  It was  suggested  that  the waiver  request  for  sidewalks  along  Route  248 is
deferred  rather  than  eliminated.
Klee  will  be requesting  a variance  for  the requirement  to have  curbing  in the drive  thru  lane  and
requested  input  from  the PC. Klee  stated  that  curbing  would  disrupt  the flow  of  traffic  on the
site.  No  curbing  in  the drive  thru  is a standard  design  for  McDonald's.
Capecci  expressed  that  she was  fine  with  not  requiring  curbing.  Harris  agreed.  Crook  asked  for
background  on why  the Zoning  Ordinance  requires  curbing.  Schwartz  suggested  an alternative  is
utilized,  such  as mountable  curbing  or a rumble  strip.
Klee  is against  a mountable  curb  and believes  a rumble  strip  may  disrupt  business.  However,
McDonald's  would  install  a rumble  strip  if  deemed  necessary.  Dulac  thought  a rumble  strip  was
a fair  compromise.

SUBDIVISION  AND  LAND  DEVELOPMENT  ORDINANCE  AMENDMENT
Draft  Update
Present  for  the Discussion:  Planning  Consultant  Andrew  Schwartz
Schwartz  provided  the PC with  an overview  of  key changes  and the proposed  timeline  for
adoption  of  the SAI,DO.  Flow  charts  are no longer  being  included  as appendixes  and are being
kept  for  staff  and administrative  use.  Minor  policy  updates  have been included,  such as
clarification  that  only  one application  may  be submitted  for a property  at a time.  Technical
requirements  have  been  reviewed  and updated,  such  as utilities  and sanitary  sewer  coru'iections.
Portions  of  Appendix  F and Article  7 have  been  combined  to eliminate  redundancy.  Removal  of
construction  standards  that  do not  belong  in an Ordinance  are being  reviewed  and removed  with
assistance  of  the Township  Engineer.
Seese believes  that  the abundance  of  specific  design  standards  for sanitary  sewer  was added
during  development  of  Hanoverville  Road.  The  duplicated  information  will  need  to be removed.
No other  utilities  have  the amount  of  detail  as sanitary  sewer.  Coyle  noted  the information
appears  to have  been  written  for  a specific  project  and  suggested  it be removed  entirely  because
the  owner  of  the sanitary  system  will  have  their  own  design  and  construction  standards.  Schwartz
agreed.
Capecci  asked  for  clarification  on the administrative  review  process.  Schwartz  explained  that
requests  for  an administrative  review  will  be reviewed  by  staff  members.
Dulac  asked  how  plan  submissions  are reviewed  for  completeness.  Seese explained  that she
verifies  the correct  number  of  copies  and required  reports  are submitted.  No  technical  review  is
performed  at that  time.  If  the submission  is deemed  incomplete,  it is returned  to the applicant  in
entirety  with  application  fees.
Seese provided  examples  of  conflicts  when  multiple  plans  are submitted  for  the same property
and running  concurrently.  Harris  questioned  how  applicants  will  be required  to handle  the

requirement  of  only  having  one active  plan  for  a property  at a time.  Schwartz  will  update  the
draft  to include  instructions  on withdrawing  applications.
Capecci  questioned  the difference  between  right-of-way  and cartway,  citing  a lack  of  clarity  in
their  definitions  and  the design  standards  included  in Article  7. Schwartz  will  provide  corrections
under  Article  7.
Capecci  asked  why  24/7  continuous  lighting  was  referenced  instead  of  dusk  to dawn,  as there  is
not 24-hour  darkness.  Crook  asked  how  the provision  can be enforced  during  the daytime.
Schwartz  explained  that 24-hours  is often  used because  some lighting  systems  have motion
detectors.  The  lighting  requirement  will  be rephrased.
Capecci  asked  that  standard  plan  notations  are required  to be placed  on the right  side  of  plans.
Under  definitions,  Dulac  suggested  "Accessory  Building"  be  referred  to  as "Accessory
Structure"  and  that  the term  "Code  Enforcement  Officer"  be included.
Dulac  asked  why  the list  of  neighboring  property  owners  receiving  notification  increased  from
those  within  200 feet  to 300 feet. Seese replied  that  it was to be consistent  with  the Zoning
Ordinance
Dulac  questioned  why  there  was  an exemption  to notify  neighboring  property  owners  on residual
tracts  greater  than  2 acres. Seese explained  the exemption  is based  on the idea  that  the residual
land  will  not  be changed.  Schwartz  will  check  into  improving  the wording.
Dulac  asked  what  qualifies  a third-party  agency  to receive  copies  of  a plan  submission.  Schwartz
and Seese explained  that  each  specific  agency  has their  own  requirements.  Capecci  asked  if  plans
are sent  to neighboring  municipalities  and Seese confirmed  they  are if  the property  is located
within  a certain  distance  of  the boundary.  Plan  submissions  are also shared  with  the Nazareth
COG.
Dulac  expressed  he was happy  that  requirements  for  an Environmental  Impact  Assessment  were
included.  Capecci  and Dulac  both  expressed  that increasing  the distance  to  1,500  feet for
connection  to public  sewer  and  water  was  a positive  change.
Schwartz  asked  that  any additional  comments  be submitted  by the end of  July.  The  final  draft
will  be in front  of  the PC for  recornrnendation  to the Board  of  Supervisors  in August.  Seese
advised  the goal  is for  the Board  of  Supervisors  to approve  forwarding  the SALDO  to the LVPC
for  review  in September
