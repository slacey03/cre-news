I. Call to Order — Pledge of Allegiance
The Macungie Borough Council meeting was called to order on October 5, 2026 at 7:30 p.m. at the
Macungie Institute, 510 E. Main Street, Macungie, PA 18062. In attendance were Carl Sell, Jr.,
President; Stan Landis, Vice President; Greg Hutchison, Member; Megan Sell, Member; Greg
Reinbold, Member; Lynette Sell, Member; Tom Ashelman, Member; Bob Sell, Jr., Mayor; Patrick
Armstrong, Esq., Solicitor; Joshua Fry, Borough Engineer; and Kalman Sostarecz, Jr., Borough
Manager.

to the Flag of the United States of America.

A. Jerry Simpson, PCPA — Police Consultation Update

Mr. Simpson commented on the PA Chiefs search for a new Chief; a meeting he held with the
Officer in Charge and Mayor regarding scheduling. Mr. Ashelman asked about their final report —
Mr. Simpson stated that a draft would be made available in November and will be finalized in
December. They will speak to Council about staffing and budget sooner than that in an upcoming
meeting. Mr. Hutchison asked from what areas the Chiefs of Police candidates are coming from —
Mr. Simpson stated that most are local or regional in Eastern PA. Vice President Landis asked what
the steps will be in the hiring process — Mr. Simpson stated that the resume’s will be accepted until
October 23", at which time an audit team reviews and grades them, then ten are chosen by the
Council to be invited to the video interview stage. From the video stage six are moved on to the
exercise stage which will be conducted by assessors from the PA Chiefs, after which time a report
is generated and provided to Council. Council chooses three or four individuals to conduct in person
interviews. President Sell asked when the Civil Service Commission would get involved — Mr.
Simpson stated that it’s the Council decision whether or not they involve the Commission.


B. Borough Engineer

Mr. Fry reviewed his written report. Vice President Landis commented on the start date of larger
Hickory Street Bridge and Church Street project, as well as the overgrown vegetation near the
bridge and asked that it be looked into. He also commented on a mailbox post that’s in the middle
of the sidewalk near the Mavis Tire development. Mr. Fry stated they will look into the issues. Mr.
Hutchison commented on the MS4 report. President Sell asked about the Lehigh Street Bridge —
Mr. Fry stated that PennDOT lists the bridge on their inventory and inspects it; he will request their
inspection reports. Ms. Megan Sell commented on the additional documents that were submitted
regarding the Lumber Street project.

Wendy Horn, resident, commented on the potential paper alley known as Green Street behind her
property, Ordinance #96 from 1931, and asked the Council to stay the Lumber Street project
approval. Solicitor Armstrong stated that a condition was put into the conditional approval that the
dispute between the property owners over the paper street.is handled to the satisfaction of the
Borough prior to the plans being released for recording.

Tim Romig, business owner, commented onthe potential paper alley known as Green Street, the
documents that have been found by the homeowners at the Lehigh County Courthouse, and he feels
that the Council needs to take action. Mr. Romig also commented on the appraisals being conducted
for the Hickory Street Bridge as well as the outlet structure in the creek that is a safety issue. He
commented on the Mavis Tire sidewalk construction project, that he has spoken with PennDOT
officials, and feels that BIA is not overseeing the project properly. Mr. Fry stated that as the
Borough Engineer he can only enforce the Borough’s Ordinances and the Development Agreement;
PennDOT enforces their rules and regulations.

Karen Billger, resident, asked why there has been no movement on the South Walnut project; Mr.

A. Fields at Brookside — Kay Builders Proposed Alterations
Mr. Sostarecz stated that Kay Builders had presented to Council earlier in the year some alterations
to their final plan as requested by residents. Council asked the applicant to prepare a sketch plan
outlining all of the requested changes. Solicitor Armstrong stated that there is no formal action
required by Council at this point and the applicant would need to come back with a formal
application for a Revised Final Plan which would need to go through the planning process.


Jordan Woods, of Colliers Engineering representing Kay Builders, presented the sketch plan which
shows removal of a portion of the walking trail, removal of fencing, and installation of alternate
fencing and a walking trail along the access road.

Mr. Hutchison commented on the fencing material; Mr. Woods stated that the fencing being
proposed is as solid vinyl privacy fence. The fence would be owned and maintained by the HOA.
Matt Hoerner from Kay Builders stated that the homeowners, the HOA, and Brookside Country
Club are on board with the plan.

Solicitor Armstrong stated that it is also proposed that the pedestrian easement be removed from
the portion of the walking path that is being removed from the plan.

Amanda Robbins, resident, commented on the gates that were installed for the emergency access
easement. They are very large and industrial looking = she asked that something is done to make it
more aesthetically pleasing. Mr. Hoerner stated that they can look into it.

Ben Parupalli, resident, commented on people driving on the access path and walking on the path
in the middle of the night. Mr. Ashelman asked if signs were posted. Mr. Parupalli stated they had
not yet but were planning on it.

Council had no object to the sketch plans and Kay Builders would proceed with engineered plans
for submission at a future date:

Authorizing Conditional Offer of Employment for Public Works Employee

Resolution #2026-19 — Council Agenda Policy & Meeting Code of Conduct

Mr. Sostarecz stated that Council has been discussing different aspects of how meetings are
conducted, including public comment periods, how agendas are created, and other items. He has
developed a draft policy for Council to discuss and revise as necessary, and a new draft can be
brought back to a future meeting for adoption. Solicitor Armstrong stated that he has gone through
the draft and has recommended changes, which he outlined for Council.

Council members discussed various changes to the draft that they recommended. Mr. Ashelman
commented on maintaining audio recordings on the website indefinitely. Vice President Landis
commented on the public comment period and the ability of the public to ask questions and receive


answers if possible. He also commented on adding items to the agenda during the meeting. Mr.
Hutchison commented on maintaining the public comment period at the end of the meeting and
rules of decorum during the meeting. Mr. Ashelman commented on the other committees and
boards of the Borough and an agenda item for a report on their actions be given during the meeting.
Ms. Megan Sell commented on adding items to future agendas.

Karen Billger, resident, commented on residents being able to ask questions during the public
comment period and being able to get answers to questions.

Jeff Hartmann, resident, commented on the public comment period and the need to increase three
minutes to five minutes. He also stated that the audio files should be kept indefinitely.

Terry Buss, resident, commented that the Council is elected to represent the citizens.

President Sell stated that he feels the public comment period should be toward the end of the agenda
as it is now, so that important business items are taken care of first. Ms. Megan Sell stated that she
would rather people not have to wait thr ough an entire meeting just to make a comment during the
public comment period.
Motion by Mr. Hutchison to table, seconded by President Sell. Motion passed
unanimously. : :

RE: Main Street Streetscape Bumpout Assessment
Findings and Recommendations

Dear Council Members,

In response to the Borough's request, the following report presents the results of our assessment of
the landscaped curb bumpouts along Main Street and identifies alternatives for restoring the areas in
a manner that reduces routine maintenance while maintaining an appropriate streetscape
appearance. This report documents the apparent original function and current condition of the
bumpouts, discusses factors contributing to their deterioration, and provides recommendations for
corrective action. The assessment is based upon review of the original project plans, available
construction and closeout records, the attached investigation photos, and current conditions reported
by Borough staff.

Background
The original project included 15 curb bumpouts. During construction, one bumpout was removed

from the project scope. Of the 14 bumpouts constructed, 13 contained landscaping and 11 were
designed to convey and provide pretreatment for stormwater runoff.

The Main Street streetscape project incorporated curb bumpouts containing landscaped stormwater
planting areas. The original plans indicate that the improvements included amended planting soil,
shrubs and herbaceous plugs, riverstone channels, drainage openings, subsurface drainage
features, and storm sewer inlets. The planting areas were intended to receive roadway runoff,
provide pretreatment through settlement and filtration, and convey treated runoff toward the storm
sewer system.

Project records and photographic documentation indicate that the landscaping and associated soil
media were installed, accepted through project closeout, and remained established for a period
following construction. Later conditions show progressive loss of vegetation and deterioration of the
landscaped areas. At some locations, plantings were subsequently replaced with stone. Sediment
and weed growth have since accumulated within the stone, and the bumpouts no longer provide the
intended appearance or effective pretreatment function.

We note that the Borough does not claim municipal separate storm sewer system (MS4) pollutant-
reduction credit for these facilities. However, the improvements were constructed using grant funding
that may have been awarded in part based on the Borough's commitment to improve the
appearance of Main Street. Grant agreements and agency approvals should therefore be reviewed
before the Borough selects an alternative that materially changes the original landscaped character.


Borough Council 2 October 14, 2026

SITE ASSESSMENT

The assessment focused on the 11 bumpouts designed to convey stormwater. The assessment was
based on the original project plans, available construction and closeout records, the attached
investigation summary, and current conditions reported by Borough staff. No subsurface
investigation, hydraulic analysis, materials testing, or detailed survey was performed.

Findings
Based on the review of available project records and the conditions described by Borough staff, the
following findings are presented:

All 11 bumpouts designed to convey stormwater are not operating as designed and require repair
or remediation by the Borough.

The original project included intentionally landscaped bumpouts rather than simple stone-filled
islands. The plans identify amended soil, riverstone drainage channels, shrubs, plugs, erosion-
control matting, and related drainage components.

Available construction and closeout documentation supports that the plantings and soil media
were installed and that no unresolved landscaping deficiency remained at project closeout.

Replacing vegetation with loose stone did not eliminate maintenance. Sediment deposited by
roadway runoff filled voids within the stone, supported weed growth, reduced infiltration and
filtration capacity, and made removal of accumulated material more difficult.

Any alternative that continues to accept untreated street runoff will require some recurring
sediment and debris removal. The practical objective should be to make maintenance
predictable, accessible, and less dependent on specialized horticultural care.

Complete concrete conversion would provide the lowest landscape-maintenance burden but
would eliminate the pretreatment function and substantially change the visual character of the
grant-funded streetscape improvements.

The Borough does not rely on these facilities for MS4 pollutant-reduction credit, reducing one
potential regulatory constraint. Separate confirmation of PennDOT, drainage, accessibility, utility,
and grant requirements would still be necessary during design.

°

Recommendations

The following alternatives are presented for Council's consideration. Final improvements may vary
by location based on bumpout geometry, drainage connections, pedestrian features, utilities, and the
condition of the remaining concrete. Opinions of probable cost should be prepared following
selection of the alternatives to be advanced for preliminary design.

Alternative 1 - Simplified Bioretention Restoration

Under this alternative, accumulated stone, sediment, weeds, and unsuitable surface material would
be removed. The engineered soil profile and drainage features would be evaluated and restored as
necessary. The bumpouts would then be replanted using a limited palette of durable, salt-tolerant
species, with a clearly defined stone inflow strip or sediment collection area at each runoff entry
point.

This alternative would preserve the landscaped character of Main Street and retain stormwater
pretreatment. Maintenance would be moderate but predictable and would include routine trash and
sediment removal, establishment watering, periodic weeding, annual vegetation cutback, and
replacement of failed plants.

Alternative 2 - Stone Forebay with Reduced Vegetated Cell

Under this alternative, each bumpout forebay would be restored with a grouted stone sediment
forebay. The forebay would concentrate coarse sediment in a defined area where Borough staff
could remove it without disturbing the planting media or established vegetation. Downstream

This alternative would reduce the total area requiring horticultural maintenance while protecting the

Alternative 3 - Manufactured Tree-Box or Media-Filter System

Under this alternative, selected bumpouts would be reconstructed using proprietary tree-box or
media-filter systems connected to the existing storm sewer. The visible surface could generally
consist of a tree opening, grate, or limited planting area, while stormwater treatment would occur
within replaceable engineered media below the surface.

This alternative would provide a compact and orderly streetscape solution with relatively low routine

periodic media replacement, and need to be evaluated for utility conflicts, hydraulic capacity,
structural loading, and maintenance access. We note that this solution would likely not be approved
by PennDOT at locations where a tree could block driver visibility of pedestrians at crosswalks.

Alternative 4 - Full Concrete Conversion and Direct Drainage

Under this alternative, the landscaped and stone-filled areas would be removed, the interiors of the
bumpouts would be reconstructed as concrete, and runoff would be routed directly to existing
drainage structures or maintained within a defined surface flow path within the island.

This alternative would provide the lowest landscape-maintenance burden by eliminating vegetation,
treatment media, and weed growth. It would also eliminate the stormwater pretreatment function and
materially change the original streetscape character. This is likely the least attractive alternative and
may require review by the original grant agency and PennDOT. Detailed design would also need to
address concrete joints, drainage, pedestrian clearances, existing utilities, differential settlement,
and compatibility with the remaining improvements.

Recommended Direction

Based on the present information, we recommend that the Borough advance Alternative 2, Stone
Forebay with Reduced Vegetated Cell, for preliminary design and cost development. This alternative
directly addresses the observed maintenance issue by separating routine sediment removal from the
planting media while retaining visible landscaping and a measure of stormwater pretreatment.
Alternative 1 should be retained as the lower-cost restoration approach. Alternative 3 may be
appropriate at constrained or highly visible locations where available funding supports a
manufactured system. Alternative 4 should be considered only where site constraints, maintenance
access, or lifecycle cost make continued treatment impractical.

+ Prepare concept plans and opinions of probable cost for the preferred alternative and at least
one comparison alternative before final Council authorization.

* Include a written operation and maintenance plan with defined frequencies, responsible Borough
staff or contractor, disposal procedures, and an annual budget line. No runoff-receiving
alternative will remain functional without recurring sediment and debris removal.

Conclusion

The available project records indicate that the Main Street bumpouts were constructed as
landscaped stormwater features intended to provide both streetscape and pretreatment functions.
The present condition is consistent with progressive plant loss, accumulation of roadway sediment,
and subsequent placement of stone without a sustainable sediment-removal strategy. The existing
stone-filled condition should not be considered maintenance-free because sediment and debris will
continue to accumulate within any facility that receives untreated roadway runoff.

The Borough has several viable alternatives, each with different maintenance, cost, and streetscape
implications. Alternative 1 would preserve the original bioretention concept at comparatively modest
reconstruction cost but retain routine landscape care. Alternative 2 would better isolate sediment,
reduce the maintained planting area, and retain an attractive streetscape treatment. Alternative 3
could reduce visible maintenance but would involve greater capital cost and specialized lifecycle
requirements. Alternative 4 would minimize landscape maintenance but eliminate pretreatment and
materially alter the aesthetic nature of the original the grant-funded improvements.

Based on this assessment, the stone forebay with reduced vegetated cell provides the most
balanced approach and should be advanced for concept design, agency coordination, and
preparation of an opinion of probable cost. Selection of a full concrete conversion should be deferred
until the original grant obligations, PennDOT requirements, drainage impacts, accessibility
considerations, and utility constraints have been confirmed.

WHEREAS, the Pennsylvania Sunshine Act, 65 Pa.C.S. § 701 et seq., requires agencies of political
subdivisions to conduct deliberations and official action.in an open and public manner and provides
members of the public with a reasonable opportunity to comment on matters of concern, official action, or
deliberation that are or may be before the agency; and

WHEREAS, Section 710 of the Pennsylvania Sunshine Act authorizes agencies to adopt rules and
regulations necessary for the conduct of their meetings and the maintenance of order, provided that such
rules do not violate the intent of the Act; and -

WHEREAS, Borough Council desires to establish consistent procedures for the preparation and
organization of its meeting agendas and f for public participation at Borough Council meetings; and

WHEREAS, porough Council fecognizes that teasonable rules concerning the order of business,
public comment, time limitations, decorum; and meeting procedures benefit both Borough Council and the
public by allowing all-interested persons a fair and reasonable opportunity to be heard while permitting
Council to conduct the business of the Borough in an orderly manner.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that the Council of the Borough of Macungie hereby
establishes the following rules and regulations regarding the Agenda for public meetings, public comment,
and conduct during such public meetings.

SECTION 1. PURPOSE

The purpose of this Resolution is to establish uniform procedures governing:

The preparation and organization of Borough Council meeting agendas;

The submission of matters for consideration by Borough Council;

The distribution and public posting of meeting agendas and supporting materials;

The order of business at regular meetings;

Public comment and public participation;

Rules of decorum and orderly conduct;

The authority of the Council President or other presiding officer during meetings; and
Other procedures reasonably necessary to facilitate orderly and effective Borough Council
meetings.

SECTION 2. APPLICABILITY

This Resolution shall apply to all regular and special meetings of the Borough Council to the extent
consistent with applicable law and the particular purpose of a meeting.

The Council President or other presiding officer may modify the order of business when circumstances
reasonably require, provided that such modification does not conflict with applicable law.

Work sessions, committee meetings, public hearings, and other meetings may utilize a different agenda
structure when appropriate for the purpose of the meeting.

SECTION 3. RESPONSIBILITY FOR PREPARATION OF AGENDAS

A. Borough Manager
The Borough Manager, or the Manager's designee, shall be responsible for compiling the proposed
agenda and supporting materials for regular meetings of Borough Council.
The Borough Manager shall coordinate with Borough staff, the Borough Solicitor, Borough
Engineer, and other appropriate officials regarding matters proposed for Council consideration.

B. Council President ,
The Council President shall have the general responsibility f for establishing the final agenda for
each Council meeting in consultation with the Borough Manager, The Council President may
request that an item be added, removed, postponed, or moved to another portion of the agenda.

C. Council Members i.
Any Council member may request that a matter be placed ona efature Council agenda. Requests
should be submitted to the Borough Manager sufficiently in advance of the meeting to permit
preparation of any necessary staff report, resolution, ordinance, financial information, legal review,
or other supporting material. Whenever practicable, Council members should submit agenda-item
requests no later than five (5). business days before the meeting. When deemed necessary, a
majority \ vote of Council shall determine if a matter will be placed on a future Council agenda.

D. Staff Recommendations
When an agenda item requires Council action, the responsible Borough staff member should
provide the Borough Manager with a written description of the matter, the requested Council
action, and supporting documentation sufficiently in advance to permit inclusion in the agenda
packet.

Where appropriate, staff recommendations should identify:
The issue requiring Council consideration;

Relevant background information;

The recommended action;

Fiscal implications;

Legal or regulatory considerations; and

Any supporting documentation.

SECTION 6. PUBLIC COMMENT POLICY

A.

General Policy

Borough Council recognizes the importance of public participation in municipal government and
shall provide a reasonable opportunity for public comment consistent with the Pennsylvania
Sunshine Act.

Public comment is intended to allow residents and taxpayers to comment on matters of concern,
official action or deliberation that are or may be before Council prior to taking official action.

Public comments and other remarks made during a Borough Council meeting shall be directed to
Borough Council as a governing body rather than personally direeted.toward individual Council
members.

. Public Comment Periods

Regular Council meetings shall generally include two opportunities for public comment:

1. Public Comment on Non-Agenda Items
This comment period shall generally occur near the beginning of the meeting and shall provide
an opportunity for members of the public to comment on matters not listed as action items on
the agenda.

2. Public Comment — Agenda Items
An opportunity for public comment shall be made available before Council takes official action
on a particular matter, in-which case the presiding officer shall provide the required opportunity
for comment before the actiomis taken.

Eligibility to Comment

The public comment opportunity shalhbe administered in accordance with the Pennsylvania
Sunshine Actandvother applicable law. Comments shall be limited to residents or taxpayers of the
Borough.

Prior to providing public comment, individuals are required to identify themselves by name and
indicate whether they are a resident or taxpayer of the Borough.

Time Limits |

To provide a fair andeasonable opportunity for all persons to be heard, individual speakers shall
be limited to three-four (64) minutes per public comment period, on both agenda and non-agenda
items.

Groups

When several persons wish to address Council on behalf of the same organization, neighborhood,
group, or collective interest, the Council President may request that the group designate a
spokesperson. A group spokesperson shall be limited to five (5) minutes per public-comment
period.

This provision shall not be used to prevent individual members of the public from addressing
Council on matters that are independently within the scope of public comment.

4


F. Questions and Requests for Information
The public-comment period is not intended to function as a general question-and-answer session.
Council members or Borough staff may respond to questions at their discretion.

A failure of Council or Borough staff to immediately respond to a question shall not be considered
a denial of the speaker's opportunity to comment.

Council will make a reasonable effort to have staff provide answers to inquiries subsequent to the
public meeting, subject to legal, logistical, informational, and other lintitations.

themselves in a civil, orderly, and respectful manner.

SECTION 8. AUTHORITY OF THE PRESIDING OFFICER

The Council President shall preside over meetings of Borough Council in accordance with the
Pennsylvania Borough Code, applicable parliamentary authority, and this Resolution.

The presiding officer shall have authority to:

SPAS we we Se

Recognize persons wishing to speak;

Maintain the order of business;

Enforce reasonable time limits;

Rule on procedural questions;

Request that a speaker remain germane to the matter being discussed;

Request that a person cease disruptive or disorderly conduct;

Request that a speaker yield the floor;

Direct the manner in which public comment shall be received; and

Take other reasonable measures necessary to maintain order and permit the meeting to proceed.

The presiding officer shall exercise these powers in a fair, reasonable, and content-neutral manner.

SECTION 9. DISRUPTIVE CONDUCT


If an individual engages in conduct that materially disrupts the meeting, the presiding officer may issue
a warning and provide the individual with a reasonable opportunity to comply with the rules of
decorum.

If disruptive conduct continues, the presiding officer may direct the individual to cease the disruptive
conduct and/or proceed with other action and/or instruction in accordance with applicable law.

Nothing in this section is intended to prohibit or terminate protected public comment merely because
the speaker expresses criticism, disagreement, or an unpopular viewpoint.

Where necessary to maintain public safety or order, the presiding officer may request assistance from
the Borough Police Department or other appropriate law-enforcement personnel to maintain order
during a meeting.

SECTION 10. RECORDING OF MEETINGS

Members of the public shall have the right to record, photograph, or otherwise obserye public meetings
as provided by applicable law.

Reasonable rules may be established concerning the placementand operation of recording equipment
when necessary to prevent obstruction, disruption, or interferencé»with the conduct of the meeting.

SECTION 11. PUBLIC HEARINGS

Nothing in this Resolution shall supersede the specific procedures, notice requirements, participation
requirements, or other requirements applicable to ‘a public hearing under the Pennsylvania Borough
Code, the Pennsylvania Municipalities, Planning Code, the Pennsylvania Sunshine Act, or any other
applicable law.

SECTION 12. PARLIAMENTARY AUTHORITY

Except where otherwise provided by law, ordinance, resolution, or specific Council rule, the current
edition of Robert's Rules of Order Newly Revised may be used as a parliamentary guide for the
conduct of Borough Council meetings but shall not be required to be strictly followed.

SECTION 13. CONSTRUCTION AND SEVERABILITY

Nothing in this Resolution shall be construed to:

1. Limit or expand the substantive authority of Borough Council;

2. Limit any right granted to the public by applicable law;

3. Authorize official action in violation of the Pennsylvania Sunshine Act;

4. Require Borough Council to take action on a matter merely because public comment has been
received; or

5. Supersede any specific statutory or regulatory requirement governing a particular meeting,
hearing, ordinance, resolution, or other proceeding.

6


If any provision of this Resolution is determined to be invalid or unenforceable, the remaining
provisions shall remain in full force and effect to the fullest extent permitted by law.

SECTION 14. REVIEW OF POLICY

Borough Council may periodically review this Resolution and may amend the procedures established
herein when experience, changes in law, or other circumstances demonstrate that revisions are
appropriate.

The Borough Manager and Borough Solicitor may recommend revisions to Borough Council as
necessary.

SECTION 15. REPEALER

Any prior resolution, policy, rule, or portion thereof adopted by Borough Council:-that is inconsistent
with the provisions of this Resolution is hereby repealed.to the extent of such inconsistency.

SECTION 16. EFFECTIVE DATE
This Resolution shall become effective immediately upon adoption.

AGENDA ITEM: Boy Scout Troop 71 Request — Macungie Institute Flooring Upgrade

Action/Motion to Be Considered

Motion to be formulated at the meeting.

Reason Why This Issue Needs Consideration

Borough Council previously authorized the Boy Scout Troop 71 to contract for an upgrade
to the flooring in their leased space in the basement of the Macungie Institute, the cost of
which was to be borne exclusively by the Boy Scouts. The Scouts were presented with a
draft indemnification agreement prepared by the Borough Solicitor to which they proposed
some modifications. Certain modifications were made to that agreement by the Solicitor to
satisfy the Scouts concerns; however, the Scouts are still not comfortable with signing the
indemnification. They are proposing that the Borough complete the upgrade itself for
which they would pay for.

Borough forces are not able to complete the work in-house and it would need to be
contracted out. The Borough would need to follow the requisite purchasing and bidding
requirements if it is to undertake the project.

Current Policy or Practice
Borough Council authorizes contracts for work.

Other Background Information

Three quotes are required for work that is above $13,200 but under $24,500; full bidding is
required for work that is above $24,500; prevailing wages must be paid for projects
exceeding $25,000.

Financial Impact
None

Recommendation

If the cost of the proposed work is under $13,200, authorize it to be undertaken by the
Borough. If the work is above $13,200 but under $24,500, require the Scouts to obtain at
least three quotes, at which point the work would be undertaken by the Borough. If the
work is $24,500 or greater, require the Scouts to complete the work.
