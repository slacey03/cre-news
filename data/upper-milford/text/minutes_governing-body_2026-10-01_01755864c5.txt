Upper Milford youth Association (UMYA) Presentation – 
 
Matthew Mecouch, President of the Upper Milford Youth Association (Stone Croft Cir), accompanied 
by James Hayes, presented an update on the organization. Run by an 11-member volunteer board, 
UMYA provides youth sports opportunities – including baseball, softball, soccer, volleyball, basketball, 
and kickball – across two main seasons per year, alongside off-season training sessions. Matthew 
thanked Township office staff and Public Works for keeping local parks safe and well maintained. He 
highlighted significant growth over the past three to four years (2023 – 2026), with participation nearly 
doubling in key programs: baseball grew from 83 to 146, softball from 46 to 100, soccer from 207 to 
233, volleyball from 62 to 77, basketball from 105 to 135, while kickball remained steady (65 to 63). 
To handle this growth, UMYA currently rents fields outside the Township and is seeking Township 
assistance, specifically requesting permission for sponsorship signs on fences and the installation of 
field lights to allow night games. 
 
During the discussion, Supervisor Zgura asked if rising registration coincided with increased 
operation costs. James Hayes confirmed higher expenses for uniforms, equipment, and field rentals, 
noting that 2023 numbers were still recovering from post-COVID trends. Supervisor Ashbrook 
inquired about residency requirements and pricing tiers; Matthew and James confirmed UMYA has no 
residency restrictions or higher fees for non-residents. Supervisor Zgura also inquired about the 
request for support regarding Macungie Memorial Park fields, where 24 tons of infield mix are needed 
for rehabilitation. UMYA noted they would only proceed with requesting Township investment if a 
Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) guaranteed them primary field access; under the proposed 
arrangement, the Township would drop off the materials while UMYA would spread them and supply 
all nets and equipment.

1. Western District Fire Company LSA Grant Funding Discussion  
 
President of the Upper Milford Western District Fire Company, Michael Kline, thanked the Board for 
their support in securing Phase I funding for the new firehouse and requested approval to apply for a 
Phase II Grant due November 30th, 2026. To meet the tight deadline given current staffing levels, 
Michael proposed that the Fire Company prepare the application using Phase I documents provided 
by the Township, with the Township submitting it on their behalf. Manager Carter estimated that 
former employee Kyle Walbert spent 30 to 40 hours gathering information, narratives, and support 
letters for Phase I. The Phase II application represents a $1 million grant to continue building the 
project. 
 
Supervisor Zgura noted this was the first he had heard of Phase II and requested to review a 
comprehensive plan, timeline, cost structured, and contingency options. Manager Carter suggested 

 
 
Page 3 of 6 
pausing the Phase II discussion to resolve open questions regarding Phase I, which secured $1 
million toward the estimated $3 million total cost. Phase I covers a functioning building shell with truck 
parking, a mechanical room, and a bathroom. Responding to requests for updated figures, Michael 
explained that firm construction numbers are on hold pending mechanical, electrical, plumbing, 
structural, and civil engineering drawings. Requests for bids for these design services are currently 
underway, and the team is awaiting official paperwork from the Department of Community and 
Economic Development (DCED) following the grant announcement.  
 
Addressing timeline concerns from Supervisor Ashbrook, Michael noted that grant funds typically 
allow two years to start, with continuance options available. Solicitor Fisher and Manager Carter 
clarified that professional engineering design services do not require public bidding, though expenses 
incurred prior to formal grant execution may not be covered. Michael stated that final costs are 
pending responses from engineering firms. Design plans are expected by December 1st, 2026, 
followed by a 7-to-9-month period to prepare construction bid packages, meaning groundbreaking is 
at least a year away. While building in phases may increase total costs, Michael emphasized it is the 
only viable path for the Fire Company. Engineering drawings will encompass all three phases. 
Supervisor Zgura added that the upcoming November election could impact future grant availability. 
Michael noted that Phase I will at minimum secure a functional shell. 
 
Joseph Terrible (St. Peters Rd), representing the Emergency Services Committee (ESC), expressed 
support for Phase II and offered the committee’s assistance. To meet the November deadline, 
Solicitor Fisher recommended adding a supporting resolution to the Board’s next agenda. Manager 
Carter suggested bypassing the next ESC meeting to place the resolution directly on the October 
15th, 2026, Board meeting; two ESC members present in the audience voiced support for this 
approach. Manager Carter requested a written project description for the resolution, noting that Phase 
II includes balancing the rooms from Phase I and a second-story addition over the four engine bay 
garage doors. 
 
Supervisor Ashbrook offered to assist with Phase II application prep, and Manager Carter confirmed 
access to the Phase I template files. Solicitor Fisher advised framing Phase II as an extension of 
Phase I rather than a new project. Manager Carter noted that finalizing the engineer’s cost estimate is 
the most time-sensitive requirement, which Michael expects to receive within two weeks. Regarding 
letters of support for the local LSA grant, Manager Carter recommended contacting Senator 
Coleman, State Representative Milou Mackenzie, and Congressman Ryan Mackenzie using 
Township-prepared templates. Supervisor Zgura volunteered to handle those support requests. 
 
In a final update, Michael reported that the Fire Company’s new truck is expected in one to two weeks 
for final outfitting, with delivery and final invoicing anticipated around December 1st, 2026. Township 
approval and check issuance will be required upon receipt of the final invoice. Michael noted they are 
currently awaiting final photos verifying requested adjustments before signing off on shipment.

3. LCA Buss Acres Public Water Supply Permit Application 
 
Planning Coordinator Miller read the prepared letter: 
 
The Upper Milford Township Planning Commission reviewed Lehigh County Authority’s  
(LCA) notice of intent to seek approval from the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental 
Protection (DEP) to construct a new water transmission main. As part of LCA’s Central Lehigh 
District, the proposed main would serve Buss Acres, an existing residential community in 
Upper Milford Township.  
        The Future Land Use Plan in the current Southwestern Lehigh County Comprehensive 
Plan identifies the proposed route as an area intended for rural uses, agricultural preservation, 
and natural resource protection. The plan recommends that these areas continue to rely on on-
lot sewer and water systems. It also records residents’ strong desire to protect agricultural land 
and natural areas from development and identifies the preservation of farmland, open space, 
and natural areas as the region’s greatest challenge.  
        The Planning Commission is concerned that installing the water line at the proposed 
location would exceed the need to address LCA’s existing Buss Acres water-system 
customers and inadvertently facilitate future development in those adjoining areas which are to 
be protected from development. That could threaten the rural uses, agricultural preservation, 
and natural resource protection, intended in the Comprehensive Plan for them.   
The Planning Commission therefore adopted a motion recommending that the Board of 
Supervisors submit the following concerns to DEP’s Permitting and Technical Services 
Section:  
1. The copy of the DEP’s General Information Form – Authorization Application provided by 
Edward DiMond of Buchart Horn, Inc. contains the following apparent omissions or 
inaccuracies: 
• The application answers the question about addressing community concerns as not 
applicable when the community has concerns. 
• Under Land Use Information, Question 6 asks whether municipal and County land-use 
letters are attached. Neither “Yes” nor “No” is selected. 
• Under Coordination Information, Question 4.02 appears to be incorrectly marked that 
the project does not discharge or drain to a HQ water area, although the Little Lehigh 
Watershed is designated HQ-CWF. 
2. The proposed extension would run through and along areas designated Rural, Natural 
Features, and Agricultural, where on-lot wells and septic systems are recommended in the 
Southwestern Lehigh County Comprehensive Plan. The project therefore conflicts with the 
adopted Comprehensive Plan and the uses which it designated for the adjoining areas. The 
Township and LCA should discuss a municipal agreement restricting future connections to, or 
extensions of, the transmission main to aid in the protection of those areas. 

 
 
Page 5 of 6 
3. Alternatives Analysis, the application materials do not indicate that alternatives were 
evaluated, including treatment of the existing water system, alternate routes, or other water 
sources for Buss Acres. 
4. No cost analysis or funding information was provided to explain how the water-line 
extension would be financed. 
5. Fire Protection, the proposed water main does not appear to include fire-service protection 
features. 
 
Supervisor Ashbrook requested removal of item number 5 from the proposal, expressing concern that 
it would lead to a higher volume of water runoff and inadvertently facilitate future developments in that 
area. Planning Coordinator Miller agreed and confirmed that the item could be removed.   
 
MOTION: Supervisor Ashbrook made a motion for Planning Coordinator Miller to send a letter to 
DEP. Supervisor Zgura seconded the motion.  
 
During public comment on the motion, Michael Kline asked whether fire hydrants could be installed 
along the proposed line. Planning Coordinator Miller explained that current water pressure is barely 
sufficient to support the supply and that the storage tank will fill overnight, noting that hydrants can be 
discussed further with the Lehigh County Authority (LCA) at a later time. 
 
All were in favor, motion carried.
