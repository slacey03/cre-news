1. Resolution 2026-028 – Red Hill Water Authority Intergovernmental Agreement  
 
Manager Carter read the Red Hill Water Authority Memo: 
 
For many years, the Red Hill Water Authority has provided water service to 16 township 
properties where the water line crosses the property or runs along the adjacent roadway. This 
service extends from the Kemmerer Spring location at 6841 Sigmund Road to Route 29, where 
the line leaves the Township and enters Hereford.  
The Red Hill Water Authority has no record of an intergovernmental agreement and would like 
to enter into one with the Township to formalize this long-standing arrangement for providing 
water service to these 16 Upper Milford Township customers. 
 
Planning Coordinator Miller provided a map showing where the line runs. Solicitor Fisher added this is 
simply putting in writing what has been going on for years. The code requires an intergovernmental 
agreement between the Township and the Red Hill Water Authority.  
 
Supervisor Ashbrook asked if this agreement would allow Red Hill in the future to run more sewer and 
water lines in the Township? Solicitor Fisher answered no, only the current 16 houses and added Red 
Hill is responsible for maintenance.  
 
MOTION: Supervisor Zgura made a motion to approve resolution 2026-028 Red Hill Water Authority 
Intergovernmental Agreement.  Supervisor Mohr seconded the motion.  Hearing no questions or 
comments, all were in favor, motion carried.

3. Truck 17(1999 GMC) Discussion  
 
Manager Carter explained during the May 7th, 2026, meeting a request had been made to sell Truck 
17 (a 1999 GMC Sierra 3500) on Municibid. However, public works employee Lee Marks 
subsequently performed a pre-inspection on Truck 3, a 1999 F350 Super Duty with a dump insert. 
Lee discovered a fuel tank leak and broken manifold bolts on the engine blocks; the truck would also 
require four new tires to pass inspection. Manager Carter noted that based on these findings, Truck 3 
will not pass its upcoming inspection at the end of August. While the original intention was to keep 
Truck 3 in service due to the dump body insert, this discovery changed plans. To avoid being short a 
vehicle, the Township will retain Truck 17, which is currently inspected through June 2027 and only 
requires two new tires. Once the inspection on Truck 3 expires at the end of August, a request will be 
made to sell that one instead.  
 
Supervisor Zgura asked if Truck 17 was already on Municibid? Manager Carter answered it was 
never posted.  
 
MOTION: Supervisor Zgura made a motion to rescind the motion to sell the 1999 GMC Truck.  
Supervisor Mohr seconded the motion.  Public Comment was made by Phil Casey – Phil asked if the 
dump body could be transferred? Manager Carter answered no it is old and beat up. The goal is to try 
to go without it and adjust. All were in favor, motion carried.

Emergency Services Committee Report:  
 
1. Funding for Western District Fire Truck 
 
The Board reviewed the final payment structure for the new pumper tanker expected to arrive at the 
end of August. The total remaining balance due upon delivery is $873,313.41. Joe Terrible led a 
discussion regarding a low-interest state loan available at a 2% interest rate for a 20-year term in the 
amount of $353,547. If approved, this loan would leave a remaining balance of $519,766.41 to be 
disbursed from the Major Firefighting account.   
 
Supervisor Ashbrook requested clarification on the total purchase price of the fire truck. Joe stated 
the total purchase price is $1,400,414.00, noting that final reconciliation and discounts would be 
applied when the fire company takes delivery. In response to Supervisor Ashbrook’s question 
regarding potential cost overruns, Michael Kline stated that no additional change orders are 
anticipated. Joe and Mike confirmed that the $519,766.41 lump-sum payout from the Township 
remains a firm figure if the 2% loan is utilized. 
 
Supervisor Zgura recommended that the Township pay the full $873,313.41 outright from available 
funds rather than securing the 2% loan. There was a brief discussion regarding current fire service 
fund accounts. Manager Carter provided an overview of the five distinct accounts currently 
maintained for the two fire companies.  
 
Supervisor Zgura noted that the payment agreement needs to include a provision stating that Upper 
Milford Township will acquire title to the truck in the event the Western District Fire company 
dissolves or defaults.  
 
Joe Terrible and Mike Kline discussed the financial advantages of leveraging the state loan. Joe 
noted that because the Township’s PLGIT (Pennsylvania Local Government Investment Trust) 
accounts are currently earning 3.96%, utilizing the 2% loan creates a positive interest spread of 
roughly 1.5%. Forgoing the loan would mean sacrificing interest earnings that could otherwise benefit 
the fire companies. Mike added that the state note carries no prepayment penalties and could be 
retired early. Joe agreed that protective language regarding asset recovery could be drafted into the 
Township agreement with the fire company.   
 
Supervisor Ashbrook expressed concern that maintaining a high cash balance in the account rather 
than spending it on the truck outright might expose the funds to be reallocated for other purposes in 
the future budget cycles.  
 
Supervisor Zgura asked about the feasibility of consolidating the various fire company accounts? 
Manager Carter explained that the two Major Firefighter accounts pre-date the active Fire Tax and no 
longer receive new revenue. Active fire tax revenues are deposited into a General Operating account 
shared by both companies, with separate savings accounts maintained for each. Manager Carter 
stated that while he believes these accounts must remain separated for their legal lifespan, he will 
verify with the Township auditors to confirm whether consolidation is permissible.  
 
Supervisor Zgura maintained his preference to fund the full $873,313.41 outright upon delivery of the 
fire truck. Manager Carter asked if the 2% loan could be structured on a shorter 10-year term? Mike 
and Joe confirmed a 10-year term is allowable. Manager Carter recommended that the Emergency

2. Volunteer Fire Fighter Gift Card Incentive Program 
 
Joe Terrible presented the revised qualification criteria for the Volunteer Firefighter Gift Card 
Incentive Program, noting that the guidelines had been updated based on the Board’s requests at the 
April 2nd meeting. Joe thanked Manager Carter for his assistance in drafting the refined qualification 
language. 
 
On behalf of the Emergency Services Committee (ESC), Joe requested approval of the program as 
presented. The ESC recommended that the incentive amount be the average residential fire tax bill, 
which is estimated to be $125.00 for 2026. 
 
Supervisor Ashbrook asked for clarification regarding whether responses tracked by the Lehigh 
County 911 Center would include calls outside of Upper Milford Township boundaries? Michael Kline 
and Joe confirmed that the program counts any incident for which the volunteer are paged out, noting 
that the companies are dispatched to assist neighboring municipalities.  
 
Solicitor Fisher advised the Board that if they agree with the qualification criteria, a formal resolution 
would be required to establish the program legally. The resolution will define the program’s effective 
state date, disbursement timelines, and the framework. The ESC recommended the gift card value be 
the average fire tax residential assessment in the year of payment, which would require an annual 
assessment figure if approved by the Board. 
 
Supervisor Ashbrook asked if this would be retroactive back to January 1st, 2026? Solicitor Fisher 
stated that would be the Board’s decision, noting his understanding that the fire companies have 
already been tracking responses for the current year. Mike confirmed that both fire companies 
maintain comprehensive call and response records.  
 
Supervisor Mohr asked whether the Township or the fire companies would be responsible for 
generating tax forms for the recipients? Solicitor Fisher clarified that tax compliance would be the 
responsibility of the individual fire companies. The Township’s role is limited to issuing gift cards 
directly to the fire companies based on the certifications. 
 
Solicitor Fisher requested direction on whether the Board was comfortable tying the incentive value to 
the average residential fire tax assessment each year. Following a brief discussion, the Board agreed 
that the incentive rate should be reviewed and set annually by the Board during the fall budget 
process.
