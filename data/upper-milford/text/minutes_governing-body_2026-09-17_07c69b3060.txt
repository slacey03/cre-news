2. Emmaus Library Presentation Annual Report  
 
Emmaus Public Library Director Allison Floray presented the Annual Report, accompanied by Upper 
Milford Library Board Representatives Robin Rotherham and Ruk Chisty. 
 
Director Floray reported that 2,067 registered users reside in Upper Milford Township and highlighted 
that Emmaus Public Library ranks #1 among the 11 Lehigh Carbon Library Cooperative (LCLC) 
libraries for highest percentage of population registered for library cards. Completed 2026 upgrades 
include a redesigned website with a new logo and a renovated front entrance. Planned initiatives for 
2027 include renewing the strategic plan, completing a space utilization assessment, improving public 
restrooms, and continuing technology upgrades. 
 
The proposed 2027 budget includes a 3.5% cost-of-living adjustment for wages and taxes, based on 
the end-of-June 2026 Consumer Price Index. The budget request from Upper Milford Township is 
$55,372.00 
 
During discussion, Supervisor Ashbrook asked whether the 62,654 recorded visits included online 
users; Director Floray confirmed the number reflects physical walk-ins. In response to Supervisor 
Ashbrook’s inquiry regarding total investment account balances, Director Floray agreed to forward the 
details to the Board. Supervisor Ashbrook noted that the 3.5% adjustment aligns with prior budget 
expectations. 
 
During public comment, Lauren Haponski praised the library’s continued quality of service during 
entrance construction and voiced support for the upcoming restroom renovations.

1. Jasper Ridge Parking Plan  
 
Supervisor Ashbrook introduced the item and requested that speakers avoid repetitive statements. 
Bill Erdman (Keystone Consulting Engineers) and John Duvoe (HOA Vice President) presented a 
revised parking plan for Jasper Ridge outlining permitted parking areas, updated signage, and street 
markings.  
 
Manager Carter and Solicitor Fisher reviewed the history of the project, noting that the plan received 
conditional approval on December 18, 2025. However, the condition to re-record the subdivision plan 
presents logical difficulties, as it requires adding PIN numbers and securing signature from every lot 
owner. Solicitor Fisher advised that the Board needs to formally resolve whether this plan is a 
temporary trial and amend the original motion to remove the re-recording requirement. Manager 
Carter explained that once the Township accepts dedication of the roads, the Board can enforce 
parking via ordinance regardless of whether the plan is recorded. 
 
During public comment, Gabriel Natalini and Julia Jurkiewicz (Stole Rd.) and Lauren Haponski 
expressed safety concerns regarding street parking, double parking, and snow obstruction, noting 
that weekly HOA notices have not deterred violations. Conversely, Scott Strahler and Joelle Strahler 
(Mink Rd.) reported that street parking has decreased significantly under HOA enforcement and 
noted that 87% of residents voted in favor of the revised plan. Colt Hershinger (St. Peters Rd.) 
questioned liability regarding pedestrian safety, which Solicitor Fisher noted is not a Township matter.  
 
In response to Board inquires regarding enforcement, Solicitor Fisher reiterated that the Township 
has no legal authority on private roads until dedication. Once dedicated, Township personnel will 
handle enforcement; Solicitor Fisher will research whether enforcement costs or fines could be 
assessed to the HOA.
