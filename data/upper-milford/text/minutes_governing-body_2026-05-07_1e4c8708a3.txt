1. W.B. Homes Proposed Culvert change for walking trail 
 
Chris Canavan apologized for missing the April 2nd meeting for the Time Extension Request for Maple 
Ridge Development and thanked the Board for the extension. He explained the issue at hand is 
regarding the walking trail at the end of the development leading into Linda Ln for pedestrian access 
wide enough for emergency UTV and allows access for LCA sewer and water lines. They have been 
working with Township staff, Engineer, and Regulators of DEP for a different conveyance method for 
storm water and run off going under the pedestrian path. He is asking for a plan modification for a 
substitution of materials underneath the walking trail. The goal is to start working on dedication items 
this summer into fall hoping to top the road before the winter months. Trail work would be done in 
June or July 2026. 
 
Supervisor Zgura and Chris Canavan had a brief conversation regarding who reviewed the plans. 
Chris emphasized it has been thoroughly reviewed by Ott Consulting the Township Engineer with 
ARRO Consulting. He explained they will have an 18-month maintenance bond once completed so 
they are responsible for making sure it is working properly. Supervisor Zgura praised Chris Canavan 
saying he is one of the best builders we have in the Township. 
 
Supervisor Ashbrook asked if this would get us closer to a resolution for the issues with Linda Ln cul-
de-sac. Manager Carter answered once this is finished we can address the cul-de-sac.  
 
MOTION: Supervisor Ashbrook made a motion to approve the Culvert change.  Supervisor Mohr 
seconded the motion.  Hearing no questions or comments, all were in favor, motion carried.   
 
Randy Kraft asked a question after the motion was carried. He asked what the interim ideas for 
protecting pedestrians and bicyclists using the trail and the long-term ideas? Planning Coordinator 
Brian Miller answered two Bollards. Chris Canavan added there are Bollards that will block cars but 
allow pedestrians and bikes. Removable Bollards so the trail can be made available for maintenance 
UTV or utility truck. Chris explained that four wheelers and motorcycles are not supposed to be there.

3. Lehigh County Authority-Buss Acres Water Line Extension 
 
Representatives from LCA were in the audience and introduced themselves; Charles Volk Chief 
Capital Works Officer, Amy Kunkel Project Manager, and they were accompanied by Ed DiMond from 
Buchart Horn the Design Engineer attained by LCA.  
 
Ed DiMond presented on behalf of LCA in front of the Board regarding a problem with PFAS a 
chemical in the water supply that the DEP and EPA are starting to regulate. He explained this is in the 
Buss Acres water system provided by two wells and there are also low levels of Radon in the water 
as well. He explained that LCA hired Buchart Horn to do a study to figure out the best way to solve 
the problem, treating or a water main extension from the Central Lehigh District. After the study was 
conducted the decision was made the best alternative long term would be running an extension from 
Linda Lane and running it to the existing water storage tank and booster pumps in Buss Acres. He 
explained the water main would come down Beck Rd to Route 29 and come down the east side of 
Route 29. They have been talking to PennDOT, who is making improvements on Route 29 and Ed 
DiMond said it would be outside of their project limits but within the right of way. The water line would 
come down close to the junction of Route 100, and an easement would be acquired along the rear 
property line of the former Ice Cream Facility. It would continue up Route 100 to Stag Drive, down 
Stag Drive and tie into an established well water line that leads to the storage tank and booster 
pumps. 
  
Supervisor Zgura asked what size line they would run? Ed DiMond answered that the minimum they 
are allowed to run is 8 inches in diameter, larger than they need, but it is the minimum that DEP 
allows. They will make minor modifications to the Kohler booster station which provides pressure to 
the system. Buss Acres is at a higher elevation, so they need to increase the pressure by 5 or 6 PSI 
at the Kohler booster station. The line and improvements are intended only to service the 90-100 
customers in Buss Acres. 
 
Charles Volk mentioned the Kohler Station on Chestnut St will need the minor modification to 
increase the capacity of those pumps, replacing with new pumps that give slightly more pressure. Ed 
DiMond said there would be appropriate road opening permits for the projects, and they will work with 
PennDOT on Route 100 and Route 29. Charles explained when looking at alternatives apart from 
their analysis treatment on site where Gary Station land is a limiting factor. Long-term operating costs 
the new water line would be the preferred alternative. The two wells at Buss Acres called the Laurie 
and Gary wells would be decommissioned in accordance with DEP criteria for abandoning wells. This 
would solve the PFAS and Radon problem, which is not presently regulated but likely will be at some 
point.  
 
Supervisor Zgura asked if this was a done deal or does the Board need to approve this? He followed 
with the biggest problem he sees is that they are opening the Township farmland for development. 
Charles Volk asked which property they were concerned about? Supervisor Zgura answered it is the 
Haltzman property 42 acres and zoned as agricultural but can still be developed. Charles Volk 
explained that is not their intent. Supervisor Zgura said he will fight this and suggested they look for 
other ways to treat the PFAS up in their development.
