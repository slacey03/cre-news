AGENDA ITEM #2:     ZONING TEXT AMENDMENT  
 
Proposed Work: Zoning Ordinance Amendments to amend Article 1302.35 to add 
definition of Data Center, Article 1305.01e to allow for Data Centers as a Special 
Exception in the Industrial (IN) District, and Article 1322.03(q) to establish regulations 
regarding Data Centers. 
 
Discussion: Ms. Fletcher gave a brief overview of the proposed Zoning Text 
Amendment. Data Centers fall under the “industrial” land use category and will be 
permitted in the Industrial (IN) District under a special exception granted by the Zoning 
Hearing Board. At the time a Land Development plan is submitted, the Applicant must 

also submit; studies, evaluations, assessments and strategies showing the impact on the 
environment and their plan to protect it. Setbacks for the front, side and rear yard have 
increased to 80ft. If near residential or existing sensitive receptor buildings have a 1,000ft 
setback. The maximum height of a data center would be 80ft and lot coverage 
percentage decreased from 65%. 
 
Mr. Melosky asked for more detail on the decommission of old data centers. Ms. 
Fletcher stated the burden and cost of the decommission will be on the developer. The 
proposed Zoning Text Amendment will require the developer to submit an electronic 
waste and decommissioning plan when submitting for a proposed data center. Mr. 
Melosky asked what would occur if the noise decibels went above the allowed range. 
Attorney Deschler replied that the Developer would be issued an enforcement notice 
by the Zoning Officer and the Developer has a time frame to resolve the violation. The 
Developer has the right to appeal the enforcement notice, and the case would be 
heard by the Zoning Hearing Board. If the appeal is denied or the violation is not 
resolved, the City then could file a complaint with the magistrate, and the Developer 
will be issued a fine of $500 fines a day for up to $12,000. Mr. Melosky asked if there was 
a follow-up procedure after fines are issued; if the noise decibels were above range. 
Attorney Deschler explained that when a complaint is filed with the magistrate for a 
Zoning violation, a fine could be collected and a lien placed on the property. There are 
provisions in the Ordinance for the City to require post-construction sound studies, and 
the property owner must do additional testing at the direction of the City based upon a 
reasonable complaint or evidence of changed operations. 
 
Mr. Malozi asked for clarification on the setbacks. Ms. Fletcher stated the current 
setbacks in the IN District are 20ft and the proposed Text Amendment would require an 
80ft setback for Date Centers. Mr. Malozi asked if the 200ft setback noted was for others 
Districts outside of the IN District. Ms. Fletcher explained that Residential Districts will 
have a 1,000ft setback while a 200ft setback for non-Residential Districts. Mr. Malozi 
asked if the 1,000ft setback for Residential Districts pertains to neighboring municipalities 
Residential Districts. Attorney Deschler stated municipalities’ Zoning Ordinance does not 
have extra jurisdictional effect of other municipalities. Mr. Melosky asked if it would be 
appropriate to measure the lot line with adjoining municipalities. Attorney Deschler 
replied that a measurement from the lot line would be appropriate and added that a 
City’s Zoning Ordinance should not be based on adjoining municipalities. Mr. Malozi 
asked if the 1,000ft setback is not restrictive and can be successfully challenged. 
Attorney Deschler replied the IN District does not touch many Residential Districts and 
the 80ft and 200ft setback will be utilized more. There are large lots on the Zoning District 
that can accommodate the setbacks in the Zoning Ordinance.  
 
Public Comment: Ms. Barry, 2815 Shakespeare Road, voice her concerns of increased 
electric use by data centers and asked if solar panels of roofs could be an option. If 
solar panels are not an option, have the Developer add more green space on the 
properties. Ms. Barry also spoke of the water usage and stated water used by data 
centers does not go back into the ground compared to other facilities.  
 
Mr. Boscola stated the high-water usage of data centers is primarily to cool the systems. 
The City has adequate water resources and a water use evaluation is done for any 

large customer. Wastewater management plans must be submitted at the time of Land 
Development submission and must be approved prior to construction.  
 
Mr. Melosky stated he supports treating the data centers as a Special Exception in the 
Industrial District and the 1,000ft setback from residential districts.  
 
Mr. Fletcher stated that the Zoning Text Amendment will be heard by City Council on 
October 6th and that if Land Development for a Data Center is submitted prior to the 
Amendment, the plans would be reviewed under the old Zoning Ordinance.  
 
Motion #2: Mr. Malozi made a motion to favorably recommend the Zoning Text 
Amendment to City Council.    
Second: Mr. Burgos 
Result of Vote: The motion carried 4-0. 
 
AGENDA ITEM #3:     SKETCH PLAN 
 
Property Location: 1438 Stefko Boulevard  
Property Owner: Estate of Fred Ronca  
Developer/Engineer: 1438 Stefko Partners, LLC 
Proposed Work: The Applicants propose to demolish all structures and construct a three-
story structure containing 18 studio apartments, six per floor, ranging in size from 478-488 
SF, and defined as workforce-focused apartments. The Applicants also propose 17 off-
street parking spaces, nine head-in/back-out on Croft Street and eight head-in/back-
out on Peters Street, dumpster pad accessed from Peters Street and other site 
appurtenances. 
 
Discussion: Attorney Scomillio and Mr. Ronca gave a brief overview of the Sketch Plan. 
The proposed efficiency apartments are targeted for singles, couples, or retirees. Mr. 
Ronca stated zoning relief is needed for lot density, parking spaces and variance to not 
have commercial space on the first floor as required in the CG (General Commercial) 
District. Mr. Ronca explained 17 parking spaces are proposed because the intent of the 
apartments is for one to two people and some may not even own vehicles. If more 
parking is needed, Mr. Ronca stated there is enough on-street parking. There are also 
LANTA bus stops near the site that would be utilized by the tenants.  
 
Mr. Ronca explained that there is not a large market for commercial space for small 
businesses. If there was commercial space, there would be no parking due to the 
layout of the property.  
 
Mr. Peiffer spoke about the City’s review letter and highlighted key points. One of  the 
main comments in the letter was that density relief must be sought from the Zoning 
Hearing Board. The City commends the Applicant for the workforce style housing. Mr. 
Peiffer explained the requirements are not met for workforce housing under the 
ordinance. The City’s sanitary engineer indicated there will be a tap fee for the project 
the stormwater engineer asks for a report if more than 10,000 SF of disturbance and 
there may be a stormwater escrow fee, a study of the curbing and sidewalk must be 
conducted to see if replacement is needed, the traffic engineer requires calculations 

to determine if any additional traffic improvements are required. Stefko Boulevard is a 
main road, and the traffic engineer may ask for safety improvements. The Forester 
requires a landscape plan and the City’s electrician needs a lighting plan for lighting on 
Croft and Peter Streets and at the entry of the building. Comments from the Fire Marshal 
stated that the location of fire department connections and fire department access 
roads, and lastly if the building will be sprinkled. Mr. Peiffer explained there is dumpster 
enclosure on the plans, but the Recycling Department requires more information for 
approval. Zoning comments focused on workforce housing. Lastly, general comments 
indicate there will be a Rec Fee that must be paid, there is sidewalk on the renderings 
but do not appear of the plans. Pedestrian connectivity is encouraged as there are 
LANTA bus stops near the site.  
 
Mr. Melosky asked for an example of what the rent be for affordable housing. Ms. 
Collins explained the Department’s definition of affordable housing. If a person spends 
no more than 30% of their income on housing and from there the area median income. 
To be considered affordable housing, 80% of the median income is calculated and 30% 
of would be the annual rent. If the median income is $65,00 the affordable monthly 
housing rent would be $1,300. Ms. Collins stated there are multiple programs such as the 
low-income housing credit program, and they target even more deeply affordable 
units. Mr. Ronca agreed with Ms. Collins. Ms. Collins encouraged the Applicant to speak 
with City staff who regularly work with programs focused on affordable housing. 
Attorney Scomillio added that the dwelling units typically are priced by square footage 
and market price falls into that price without falling under the definition of affordable 
housing. Mr. Melosky asked if the Applicant is aware their proposed plan does not meet 
the requirements. Attorney Scomillio stated they are aware and will be heard by the 
Zoning Hearing Board. Mr. Melosky asked if the Applicant is willing to comply and work 
with City staff and address the comments in the review letter. Attorney Scomillio replied 
that his client will review the letter but does not believe there will be an issue with 
addressing all comments. Mr. Melosky asked about the amount of parking required and 
what is proposed. Mr. Peiffer answered 1.75 parking spots per unit are required when 
there is no off-street parking available which total 32 spaces for this site. The parking 
proposed is 17 spaces which requires Zoning Hearing Board.  
 
Mr. Malozi voiced his concerns about the density of the proposed plan and stated most 
of the zoning relief is required due to density. To rely on on-street parking tends to show 
the site is not meant to have that much density. Mr. Malozi believes limited commercial 
in that space is feasible as there are small businesses in the surrounding area. A landlord 
cannot legally stop a tenant from having more than two people in a unit. When plans 
are reviewed, it cannot be assumed a tenant will not have a car. Mr. Malozi suggests 
scaling back the building and including mixed use. Mr. Ronca stated to make this 
feasible, 18 units are needed. Mr. Ronca stated he believes that a landlord can state a 
unit can only have 1-2 tenants which is Mr. Kodxa’s intention.  
 
Mr. Pongracz talked about the parking and stated the off-street parking would be 
impeding in other neighborhoods. Mr. Ronca explained he has multiple properties that 
have tenants that do not own cars nor has he had any issues with parking throughout 
his career.  
 

Mr. Burgos asked if Croft Street should become a right turn only for safety. Mr. Ronca 
explained that is the City’s decision but believes it would be beneficial.  
 
Public Comment: Michael DeCrosta, 914 Walters Street, voiced his concerns about 
parking. He owns a business on Stefko Boulevard and leases parking. He is concerned 
those spots would be taken by tenants. A new daycare is also about to open on 
Marvine Street which may cause more parking issues if the units are built with only 17 
parking spaces. Mr. DeCrosta asked if a written objection could be admissible. Mr. 
Melosky replied that when the plan comes for final approval that would be the time to 
attend or an email can be sent in and read. Mr. Peiffer added that if Mr. DeCrosta signs 
up, Ms. Sebesta will inform him of the upcoming meetings. Mr. Ronca stated that he is 
willing to sit down with Mr. DeCrosta and others to discuss their concerns. Mr. DeCrosta 
mentioned that some of the parking places Mr. Ronca pointed out are 1 hour only 
Monday through Saturday, which can cause issues. There is a church just south of the 
site and parking is congested during that time and daycare facility with kids running 
around during the day. Mr. DeCrosta is concerned about parking and the safety of 
pedestrians.  
 
Mr. Zajacek, Grace Realty, stated HUD guidelines allow the landlord to limit a one-
bedroom apartment to only contain two people. Mr. Zajacek states there is not a large 
market for small commercial spaces and taking away the first-floor dwelling units, 
eliminates options for elderly people who cannot do stairs.  
 
Mr. Malozi asked if the proposed project was funded by HUD in any manner. Mr. Ronca 
replied that it will all be privately funded. Mr. Malozi asked if HUD regulations would still 
apply in limiting the number of tenants in a unit. Attorney Scomillio explained a landlord 
can cap the number of tenants at any time. Mr. Zajacek stated that HUD allows 
guidelines that allow a landlord to state two people per bedroom, all states must follow 
HUD guidelines even if the units are not HUD.  
 
Mr. Melosky asked if the Applicant had any issues adding conditions into the lease 
limiting the number of tenants allowed in a unit along with rent costs and a timeline. Mr. 
Ronca responded he had no issues with including conditions. Mr. Zajacek added that if 
first-floor apartments are allowed by the Zoning Hearing Board, they will likely be rented 
by elderly people who do not own cars, and he does not believe that parking will be 
an issue.  
 
Scott Culpepper, 2325 Santee Mill Road, asked if there has been an update to the 
Autumn Estates project that has been proposed. Mr. Peiffer explained that the 
Bethlehem Township Planning Commission has approved all waivers except for the 
interpretation of a cul-de-sac and the number of dwellings in the cul-de-sac. The 
decision will be made by the Bethlehem Board of Commissioners for approval in 
October.
