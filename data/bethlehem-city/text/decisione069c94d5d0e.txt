VI. Discussion 
Applicant requests use and dimensional variances from §1302.05, §1306.0l(a)(4), 
§ 1323.07 and § 1325.07 of the Zoning Ordinance. The Zoning Ordinance provides specific 
criteria that the Zoning Hearing Board must address in relation to the approval or denial of a 
variance request: 
1325.06 Powers and Duties - Variances 
(a) Upon a written appeal from a determination by the Zoning Officer, the 
Zoning Hearing Board shall have the power to approve a Variance to one or more 
specific provisions of this Ordinance for a specific property. 
(b) The power to authorize a variance from the terms of this Ordinance shall 
only be used where authorized under the Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning 
Code or in de minimus situations. As of the adoption date of this Ordinance, the 
Municipalities Planning Code provided that all of the following findings must be 
made, where relevant: 
(1) There are unique physical circumstances or conditions (including 
irregularity, narrowness, or shallowness of lot size or shape, or exceptional 
topographical or other physical conditions peculiar to the particular property) and 
that the unnecessary hardship is due to such conditions and not the circumstances 
or conditions generally created by the provisions of this Ordinance in the 
neighborhood or district in which the property is located; 
(2) Because of such physical circumstances or conditions, there is no 
possibility that the property can be developed in strict conformity with the 
5 


provisions of the Zoning Ordinance and a Variance is therefore necessary to 
enable the reasonable use of the property; 
(3) Such unnecessary hardship has not been created by the appellant; 
(4) The Variance, if authorized, will not alter the essential character of the 
neighborhood or district in which the property is located, nor substantially or 
permanently impair the appropriate use or development of adjacent property, nor 
be detrimental to the public welfare; and 
( 5) The Variance, if authorized, will represent the minimum variance that will 
afford relief and will represent the least modification possible of the regulation in 
issue. 
Applicant did not appear with counsel at the Zoning Hearing. It is Applicant's burden to 
meet the criteria in the Zoning Ordinance to demonstrate entitlement to variance relief. 
Applicant's variances include reliefr elated to the Property being in an impermissible location for 
the proposed use and dimensional relief. 
Regarding the former, residential use is permitted in the RT Zoning District. Therefore, 
the case does not involve a traditional use variance where an Applicant wishes to utilize land for 
a use that is not allowed in the zoning district. However, in this instance, the proposed lot is not 
in a location on which a principal residential use is permitted, namely with its frontage along an 
alley as opposed to a street. 
Second, regarding the dimensional relief, while Applicant suggests that he is simply 
requesting that existing nonconformities in setbacks and lot area are permitted to persist, the 
distinction is that those setback regulations are intended to regulate principal residential uses of 
property. The current building is not a residential use but instead a garage used for parking and 
storage. 
The Board ultimately determined that the relief must be denied. Its reasoning pertained 
primarily to the lack of evidence presented. In other words, the Board did not have sufficient 
6 


evidence to even weigh whether the vanance requirements are met. Simply put, relevant 
evidence on the variance criteria and the related issues based upon the unusual historic use and 
development of the Property is not of record. First, there remains the issue of the recorded 1991 
plan which appears to eliminate use of the Property for a building lot. That said, the Board does 
recognize that a structure already exists on the property and is in use. Further, the Board had no 
evidence before it regarding the meaning of the restriction from use of Property as a building lot. 
also, it appears that even though the lot is separate and separated by a street from 624 Shields 
Street, the 1991 plan suggests it is part of 624 Shield Street and restricted to use for parking at 
624 Shields Street. Without any explanation of these facts the Board was constrained to deny 
relief. 
Next, an applicant needs to demonstrate that there exists a physical hardship that prevents 
reasonable use of the Property. Based upon the facts before the Board, it appears that a three-car 
parking garage is already a reasonable use of the Property, particularly one that is substandard in 
area and accessed only by an alley. Therefore, Applicant failed to demonstrate that a hardship 
existed. 
In addition, while not stated the City certainly had reason why the City's Zoning 
Ordinance does not permit a principal residential use to be constructed with an alley as its 
frontage. The Board understood the neighbors' suggestion that doing so creates an overuse of the 
Property and places a residence (instead of simply a parking area) in closer proximity to their 
single family home that does meet the requirement of fronting on a street. 
For all of the above reasons, it is clear that the variance standard is not met. (The Board 
recognizes, however, that Applicant may be able to seek relief in the future at which time other 
evidence can be presented to speak to the issues that resulted in this denial.) 
7 


VII. Conclusions of Law 
1. Applicant failed to articulate a hardship sufficient for the grant of a variance. 
2. Applicant failed to demonstrate why the property cannot be reasonably developed 
under the current regulations, particularly in light of the fact that it is already developed for 
beneficial use. 
VIII. Decision of the Board 
Based upon the foregoing, by a 3-0 vote the Zoning Hearing Board denied all requested 
vanances.
