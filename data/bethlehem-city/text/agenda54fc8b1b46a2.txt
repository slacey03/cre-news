ZONING

1.

The allowable height of the building is determined “from the average elevation of the proposed finished
grade at the front of the building to...top of parapet wall of a flat roof...” [ZO § 1302.55]. The Avenue B
frontage is the declared Front Yard; the north elevation is where the building’s height is determined. The
‘Screen’ extending up from the top of the walls meets the definition of a parapet. ZO section 1306.01(b)6
limits the maximum height of buildings in the PI (Planned Industrial) zoning district to 60’; the west
elevation proposes 63’. Applicant notes that ZO section 1318.20(b) provides an exemption for “parapet walls
which extend not more than 4 feet above the limiting height”. However, this exemption limits “Any such
structures shall not have a horizontal area greater than 25 percent of the roof area of the building” . The area
enclosed by the parapet exceeds 25%.

1306.06(a) requires “a minimum of 20 percent of the lot area shall be landscaped with trees and shrubs.” The
referenced section states “trees and shrubs;” the lawn areas shall not be credited as contributing to this
requirement. Using a scale, a bank of trees along the western property line was determined to be
approximately 16,000 SF, and an area in the southeast portion of the property was determined to be


approximately 15,400 SF. Per Applicants calculations, 59,233.4 SF area of “trees and shrubs” are required.
The landscaping plan shall define and dimension these areas to verify their calculations.

3. 1319.02(0) requires “[Bicycle] Racks shall be within 30 feet of building entrance.” This section is interpreted
to require exterior bicycle racks that are readily available to all visitors to the facility. While it is
acknowledged that the proposed scheme satisfies the desired goal of “Covered parking is encouraged where
possible,” requiring visitors to travel ~60’ through the vestibule, lobby and loading dock in order to secure a
bicycle cannot be approved; therefore, the applicants shall modify the rack location.

4. The building is accessed by a long, un-dimensioned ramp, and a utility stair appears to intrude into the path
leading to the ramp. Applicants shall verify the length of runs of the ramp, mid-slope landing dimensions and
pathway width to comply with UCC requirements in order to verify the accuracy of the submitted design
footprint.
