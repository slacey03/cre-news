Ill. DISCUSSION

The grant of a variance js governed by 1325.06. The Board may grant a variance where

the criteria defined thereunder, as relevant to the case, is satisfied. The Board finds the following

to be relevant to this appeal:
There are clearly unique physical circumstances and conditions on the property. The
property is an existing commercial use and has historically been used as a small neighborhood

market for an extended period of time. The architecture and design of the front portion of the

building suggests its’ commercial purpose. This is not a traditional residential building.



Although the Applicants seek to expand the building’s permitted uses to add a residential
unit as an apartment and keep the attached commercial use, the existing residential space in the
building is substantial and most likely ioo substantial to be a rental unit with any reasonable market
demand. The pictures demonstrate the sizeable interior of the residential portion of the building.
Apartment | which is on the second floor has a kitchen and two (2) bathrooms; the lower-level
Apartment 2 has a kitchen and a full bathroom. The units are easily separated and it appears that
at one time they were separated or at least separately used. There is an entrance on the side of the
building for the lower level. The residential portion of use does not have the open-air ambience
between floors which a typical two-story single-family home normally enjoys.

Parking does not mitigate against approval. There are four (4) spaces available in front of
the garage. Each residential unit will have access to sufficient off-street parking. Street parking
is also reasonably available in the neighborhood as not just evidenced by the Applicants’
photographs but seemingly confirmed by the lack of neighbor interest in the request.

The property is one of the larger properties in this area. The RT Zoning District is a high-
density residential district. Although various Ordinance provisions are implicated in separating
the residential portion of the structure, most of the relief requested is associated only with
recognizing dimensional nonconformities which already exist and will continue to exist. These
are physical conditions which cannot be reasonably changed.

A past proprietor of the store apparently occupied the large residential portion of the
structure as a family. The universe of potential occupiers looking to own a property with a small
market and a large space to house his family is likely to be extremely limited. This renders the

property unique and limits demand for single ownership.


What this appeal comes down to is in the final analysis is whether it is reasonable to
Separate a residential area which is over four thousand four hundred square feet (4,400 sq. ft.) on

separate floors into two (2) units of an excess of two thousand two hundred (2,200 sq. ft.) in the

absence of any resulting detriment. Given that this building likely will only have demand as a

rental, and because of the hardship created by the commercial building to the front of the structure,

it is reasonable to separate the residential space to render it more conventionally usable.

IV, DECISION

1. The Applicants’ request for a variance from 1306.01 (A) (4) to reduce minimum

tract size, six thousand Square feet (6,000 sq. ft.) required, five thousand forty square feet (5,040

sq. ft.) existing and proposed, to reduce lot area per dwelling unit, three thousand square feet (3,000

sq. ft.) required, two thousand five hundred twenty square feet (2,520 sq. ft.) proposed; to reduce

minimum lot width sixty feet (60 ft.) required, thirty-eight feet (38 ft.) existing and proposed and

to reduce required side yard setback of four feet (4 ft.) required each side five (S) feet (5 ft.)

diminishing to zero feet (0 ft.) existing and proposed (west) and +/- three feet (3 ft.) existing and

proposed (east) is granted,

2. The Applicants’ request for use relief from 1304.01 (B) (1) to create two (2)

dwelling units with an attached commercial use is granted.

3. The Applicants’ variances are conditioned upon the Applicants complying with

their zoning application, zoning exhibits, testimony and evidence presented to the Board in support

of the appeal.

4, The Applicants shall obtain all required reviews, approvals and permits under any

other applicable Code or Ordinance.
